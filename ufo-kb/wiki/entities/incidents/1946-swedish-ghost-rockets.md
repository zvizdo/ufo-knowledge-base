# 1946 Swedish Ghost Rockets

Wave of unidentified aerial phenomena observed primarily over Sweden and Scandinavia in 1946, described as resembling rockets — possibly modified versions of the German V-2 — flying at speeds and with propulsion capabilities that could not be attributed to any known post-war technology.

## Scale and Response

[[claus-svahn]] (AFU) counts at least **1,400 documented** rocket and cigar-shaped objects that descended on Norway and Sweden. The sightings were significant enough in scale and in the seniority of witnesses that **Eisenhower sent General James Doolittle** (a famous WWII aviation figure) to Europe to investigate. This represents one of the earliest cases of the US military treating aerial anomalies as a matter of urgent national security — before the famous 1947 Kenneth Arnold sighting that launched the American UFO wave.

The Swedish government formed a **specialized military investigative unit** in response. Military personnel searched multiple lakes where objects were reported to have entered the water and found only **indentations at the bottom** — not a single piece of debris was recovered.

## Water-Only Landing Pattern

A defining feature of the Swedish ghost rockets, emphasized by [[claus-svahn]]: **every single documented object entered water**. Not one crashed on land. Every observation ends with a crash into or landing in a lake — never a flyby, never a land impact. This is anomalous from a conventional debris-recovery standpoint.

Svahn's assessment: "I don't really understand why should those objects crash that often." He is skeptical of the simple crash-retrieval model — the pattern of deliberate water-targeting may suggest intentional behavior (refueling? concealment?) rather than equipment failure.

> ⚠ Water-only pattern vs. conventional crash hypothesis: If these were Soviet test vehicles failing over Scandinavia, some should have hit land. The exclusive water-entry pattern is inconsistent with random failure and requires explanation.

## Possible Origins

[[nick-cook]] discusses the Ghost Rockets in the context of the broader European post-WWII UAP environment. The rocket-like description invites the hypothesis of:
1. **Soviet testing** of captured German V-2 technology — rockets that looked modified and exceeded contemporary public knowledge
2. **Continuation of advanced German programs** — the Kammler-Stab and related programs had produced rockets far beyond the publicly known V-2; their successors may have remained operational
3. **Genuine unknown phenomenon** — the object characteristics in some reports exceeded what either hypothesis cleanly accounts for

## Significance

The Ghost Rockets bridge the Foo Fighters (1942–45) and the 1947 American flying saucer wave, suggesting a continuous thread of unidentified aerial phenomena in the military community's attention that predates the modern UFO era's official starting point.
