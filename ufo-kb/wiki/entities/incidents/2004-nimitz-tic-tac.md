# 2004 Nimitz Tic-Tac Encounter

The single most credentialed UAP encounter in the modern documented record. During operational exercises off the coast of Southern California in November 2004, the USS Princeton (CG-59) had been tracking anomalous contacts on its SPY-1 radar for approximately two weeks. [[david-fravor]] and his weapons system officer, plus a second F/A-18F crew, were vectored to investigate.

## Account

At the reported location, Fravor observed a white, Tic-Tac shaped object approximately 40 feet long with no wings, no exhaust, and no visible propulsion system, hovering over a disturbed circular patch of ocean. When Fravor descended in a spiral to intercept, the object mirrored his maneuver, then accelerated away at extreme speed and disappeared. A separate crew subsequently recorded the [[tic-tac]] on FLIR, producing the footage released as part of the 2017 NYT disclosure.

Three additional aviators corroborated Fravor's sighting.

## Evidence

- **FLIR footage**: Recorded by a separate crew; the footage (sometimes called "FLIR1") was released alongside Gimbal in December 2017
- **Princeton radar track**: Two weeks of SPY-1 radar contact; the Princeton's CIC watched the object accelerate to a pre-briefed rendezvous point after Fravor's intercept attempt — suggesting intentional behavior
- **Multi-witness**: At least four aviators; additional radar operators aboard Princeton

## Aftermath

The FLIR footage was eventually declassified and released by the Pentagon in April 2020. The encounter is the central reference point for [[marik-von-rennenkampff]]'s pro-anomaly position and the entry case that converted him from skepticism. [[mick-west]] considers the FLIR1 footage somewhat more explainable than Gimbal but acknowledges the overall case is the most substantive in the modern record.

## Plasma Hypothesis Analysis (Eric Haseltine)

In [[jMCavr3dVP0]], [[eric-haseltine]] (NSA Director of Research, developed free-air plasma voxel displays at Disney) specifically assesses the plasma hypothesis for the Nimitz Tic Tac:

- **Conclusion**: Plasma does not fully explain the Tic Tac visual signature. Laser-induced plasma in free air tends to form a "rope" of ionized energy, not the clean white-plastic-looking Tic Tac shape.
- **Fravor's "breathing" observation** adds a quality that plasma cannot account for — David Fravor noted the craft appeared to be "breathing" or alive.
- Haseltine's assessment: "those Tic Tacs — there's something real going on there." The multi-sensor corroboration (radar, FLIR, visual, non-motivated observers) is too strong to explain away.
- For glowing orbs (separate from Tic Tacs), free-air plasma is a more plausible mechanism — Haseltine distinguishes clearly between the two UAP types.
