# 1981 Black Sea Soviet Army UFO Sighting

A mass military UFO sighting during Soviet Army exercises in 1981, followed by systematic suppression. Firsthand account from [[kirsan-ilyumzhinov]], who was then serving as a conscript in Soviet Army special forces communications troops.

## Account

During exercises on the Black Sea (year: 1981), **"several dozen unidentified craft flew in directly."** Multiple soldiers — "several dozen boys" from Ilyumzhinov's unit — observed the craft. Those with personal cameras photographed them.

**Suppression sequence:**
1. A commission arrived from Moscow — from the central headquarters
2. All cameras were confiscated and destroyed
3. Everyone present was required to sign an agreement "that nobody would say anything, that nobody saw anything in the sky, nothing at all"
4. Ilyumzhinov signed one of these agreements

Ilyumzhinov: **"Such agreements still exist to this day."** He frames this as evidence of systematic military secrecy around the UFO issue — one he participated in personally and therefore knows to be real.

## Context

Ilyumzhinov was serving in special forces communications troops. The unit had "classified communications equipment, space stations." The exercise setting and Black Sea location are consistent with Soviet Navy and air defense exercises regularly conducted in this region during the Cold War.

This sighting occurred 16 years before Ilyumzhinov's 1997 contact experience. He had also witnessed a UFO as a child (a "saucer hovering over the football field" while playing), during his time at Artek summer camp near Mount Ayu-Dag in Crimea, and during his Soviet Army service. The 1981 Black Sea incident is the most documented of these pre-1997 experiences due to the suppression response it triggered.

## Analytical Notes

The suppression sequence — commission from central headquarters, camera confiscation, secrecy agreement — is structurally identical to documented US military UFO suppression procedures (ref: [[ufo-secrecy-cabal]]). The parallel implies a bilateral doctrine of suppression for military UFO encounters.

The KGB under [[yuri-andropov]] (chairman 1967–1982) was running a dedicated UFO and USO research department during this same period. The Black Sea sighting may have been one of the events feeding that department's files.
