# 1975 Northern Tier UAP Wave

A sustained series of UAP incursions over US nuclear missile facilities along the Canadian border (Maine to Montana) in 1974–75, referred to by investigators as "the Northern Tier case."

## What Happened

- A string of Cold War ICBM facilities — "aimed at Moscow and other places" — was visited one after another by unknown aerial objects.
- Objects appeared "right over the missiles." Witnesses initially thought they were mystery helicopters or lights in the sky.
- In "a couple of cases they disabled the missiles. If we had gone to war, we wouldn't have had those missiles being able to fire."
- "There are some indications that the launch control codes were interfered with" — beyond simple physical disablement.
- The US military scrambled fighter jets: "The things go away — we can't catch them."
- The pattern is consistent with the broader [[ufo-nuclear-nexus]] of UAPs demonstrating capability to neutralize nuclear weapons.

## Official Record and Documentation

- The Washington Post broke the story in the 1970s after obtaining documents via FOIA.
- George Knapp: "This happened. Oh yeah, yeah."
- [[robert-hastings]] documented the Northern Tier cases in depth in his book *UFOs and Nukes*, interviewing nuclear missile officers who were witnesses.
- [[aawsap]] specifically investigated the Northern Tier cases, re-interviewing "some of the nuclear missile officers and security personnel" who described the same events decades later.
