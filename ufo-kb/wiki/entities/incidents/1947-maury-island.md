# 1947 Maury Island Incident

The Maury Island incident (June 21, 1947) occurred three days before [[kenneth-arnold]]'s famous sighting over Mount Rainier that launched the modern "flying saucer" era. Harbor patrolman Harold Dahl reported seeing six doughnut-shaped craft over Puget Sound near Maury Island; one appeared to malfunction and ejected slag-like debris that injured Dahl's son and killed his dog.

## The First Deaths of the Modern UFO Era

[[fred-chrisman]] — who supervised and allegedly witnessed the event — collected samples of the slag. Two US Army Air Force intelligence officers, Captain Lee Davidson and First Lieutenant Frank Brown, flew to Tacoma to investigate and collected the material. Their plane exploded and crashed on August 1, 1947, killing both officers while they were carrying the Maury Island slag samples.

[[peter-levenda]] identifies Davidson and Brown's deaths as the first fatalities of the modern UFO era — establishing from the very beginning a pattern of lethal consequences for official investigators of the phenomenon.

## The Kenneth Arnold Connection

Kenneth Arnold, already famous for his June 24 sighting, flew to Tacoma at the request of civilian interest and met with Dahl and Chrisman. He later co-authored a book about the incident. Arnold's presence creates a direct connection between the most famous early UFO sighting and the first UFO-related deaths.

## The Intelligence-JFK Network

[[fred-chrisman]]'s role as supervisor of the incident is significant because Chrisman later surfaced in [[jim-garrison]]'s investigation of the Kennedy assassination through his known relationship with [[clay-shaw]]. [[guy-banister]], stationed as FBI special agent in the Pacific Northwest in 1947, was reporting UFO sightings in the region to [[j-edgar-hoover]] via "Mission X" telegram designations during this same period. Both Chrisman and Banister later appear in the New Orleans JFK investigation network.

## Skeptical Assessment

The Maury Island incident is widely considered a probable hoax — Dahl and Chrisman later recanted. But Levenda treats the network connections (Chrisman → Shaw → Garrison; Banister → Camp Street → JFK) as significant regardless of whether the sighting itself was genuine.

## Aftermath

The incident was investigated by Project Sign/Grudge and largely dismissed. The death of the two intelligence officers was attributed to an engine fire unrelated to the slag, though the timing remains a recurring subject of speculation among researchers.
