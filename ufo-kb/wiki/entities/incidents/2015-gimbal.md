# 2015 Gimbal Video

FLIR thermal infrared video recorded by a US Navy F/A-18F crew during USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier strike group operations approximately 300 miles off the coast of Jacksonville, Florida. Released to the public via the December 2017 New York Times story co-authored by [[leslie-kean]] and [[ralph-blumenthal]]. The video shows a dark, oval-shaped object rotating smoothly while traveling at speed, accompanied by the audio remark: "Look at that thing, dude! It's rotating!"

The Gimbal video is the most technically contested UAP evidence in the modern era.

## The Core Dispute: [[gimbal-rotation-debate]]

### Mick West's Position
[[mick-west]] argues the rotation is an optical or digital camera artifact — either a glare phenomenon produced before the sensor, or the result of a de-rotation algorithm in the FLIR pod (referenced in a patent) compensating for aircraft bank. The object is, in his view, a distant jet; the shape is a camera artifact. He notes an air-to-air infrared expert thought the object looked like an SR-71, which he points to as evidence the "expert opinions" cited by Merrick were preliminary impressions.

### Marik von Rennenkampff's Position
[[marik-von-rennenkampff]] argues the rotation is genuine physical motion and the shape is a real craft morphology. He cites: (a) air-to-air infrared experts who called the rotation "bizarre," "never seen before," and inconsistent with known glare or blooming artifacts; (b) the object growing ~15% larger as the aircraft closes distance, consistent with real perspective change; (c) visual similarity to the [[2008-iraq-gimbal]] video as evidence the morphology is physical; (d) [[aaro]]'s failure to officially endorse West's explanation.

### The Expert Opinion Dispute
The episode includes a detailed back-and-forth about what infrared experts actually said. Both sides agree experts were consulted; they disagree about whether those experts endorsed West's position. Merrick says they explicitly rejected the glare/artifact theory; West says one thought he was "right but for the wrong reason" and they were offering preliminary impressions, not formal assessments. The expert opinions were shared by Merrick anonymously (without the experts' permission).

### The Blooming vs. Glare Terminology Dispute
Merrick argues the correct technical term is "blooming" (a well-known FLIR artifact) not "glare," and that blooming does not produce rotation. West acknowledges the terminology but maintains his artifact interpretation is functionally compatible with blooming.

## Historical Context

The video was recorded alongside [[2015-gofast]] during a series of encounters by the USS Roosevelt air wing. [[aaro]] has not issued a definitive public determination on Gimbal as of late 2024.
