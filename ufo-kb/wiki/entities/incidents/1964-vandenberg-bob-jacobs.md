# 1964 Vandenberg UAP Incident

In 1964, USAF officer **Bob Jacobs** (later Dr. Robert Jacobs, professor) operated a telescope-camera tracking system at a site near **Big Sur, California**, filming test missile launches from Vandenberg Air Force Base. The test involved an Atlas missile (ICBM) carrying a dummy warhead traveling at approximately 9,000–12,000 mph.

According to Jacobs and his commanding officer **Major [[florenz-mansman]]**, the film captured a **saucer-shaped UAP** that flew alongside the re-entry vehicle, fired beams at it from multiple angles (described as beam-like interactions), and disabled the warhead. The warhead test failed.

Jacobs stated CIA personnel arrived, confiscated the film, and instructed both him and Mansman never to speak of it. Jacobs was effectively erased — the government initially denied he had ever worked at Vandenberg. He later experienced threats including to his mailbox.

The incident is cited by [[robert-hastings]] in his [[ufos-and-nukes]] documentation as one of the most evidentially significant UAP-nuclear incidents because it was **instrumented capture** — a military telescope-camera system, not naked-eye witness only.

## Witnesses

- **[[bob-jacobs]]** — filmed the event; primary firsthand account; experienced retaliation and denial of his government service
- **[[florenz-mansman]]** — Major Mansman, Jacobs's commanding officer; initially went along with the denial, later corroborated that Jacobs did work at Vandenberg and managed ~130 personnel; implicit corroboration of the incident
- **[[lue-elizondo]]** — states publicly that he has seen the Vandenberg film in a classified setting and that he possesses backup copies; has urged public release

## Evidence

- Military telescope-camera tracking footage (confiscated by CIA; Elizondo claims copies exist)
- Jacobs's sworn testimony
- Mansman's eventual corroboration of Jacobs's employment and team size
- Structural context: the same pattern (UAP disabling nuclear warheads/missiles at test ranges) documented in September 19, 1962 AVCO MarkV incident and October 1962 [[1962-bluegill-uap-shootdown]]

## Aftermath

The government's initial denial — claiming Jacobs never worked at Vandenberg — collapsed when it became untenable in the face of documentation and Mansman's corroboration that Jacobs managed ~130 people. AARO's current official position is that Jacobs was "confused about which test date" — contested by [[kirk-mcconnell]] and [[jesse-michaels]] who argue the September 1962 and 1964 incidents corroborate each other as instances of the same phenomenon.

Elizondo's claim to have seen the film and possess backups is one of the most specific outstanding whistleblower obligations in UAP disclosure — the film would constitute among the strongest physical evidence in the public record if released.

## Concurrent 1964 Vandenberg CE3

In September 1964 — the same month as the Jacobs filming — USAF air policeman [[richard-barth]] at D-sector site D5 claims a CE3: paralysis by an approaching humanoid NHI, abduction aboard a hovering saucer, and direct NHI communication that they needed "data" from missile guidance electronics and would not allow nuclear war. Barth had also observed a UAP "tagalong" tracking an Atlas missile launch in May 1964. Barth and Jacobs were introduced by [[robert-hastings]] after both came forward; neither knew of the other at the time. Both speculate their experiences may involve the same craft or coordinated NHI activity.
