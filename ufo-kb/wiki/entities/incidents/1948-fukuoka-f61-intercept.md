# 1948 Fukuoka F-61 Radar Intercept

On October 15, 1948, a F-61 Black Widow interceptor crew (pilot and radar observer) operating out of Fukuoka, Japan, conducted a 1+ hour airborne radar pursuit of an unidentified object — the first documented case in the Project Sign corpus where an investigator explicitly hypothesized UAP active radar-detection capability.

## Details

- F-61 crew (rated pilot plus radar observer) tracked object on airborne intercept radar
- Made **6 separate close-approach attempts** over 1+ hours
- Each time the F-61 closed to approximately 12,000 ft, the object **suddenly accelerated and dove** out of radar range — identical behavior repeated across all 6 attempts
- Object also demonstrated near-vertical ascent capability
- Speed "much greater than F-61 at full throttle"
- Documented as Incident 183 in [[usaf-blue-book-era-incident-summaries-box-7]]

## Investigator Hypothesis

The USAF investigator's written conclusion: "The object may have been aware of F-61's location — possibly carrying radar detection equipment." This represents primary-source 1948 documentation that USAF investigators were explicitly considering adversarial sensor awareness as a capability of the observed objects.

The six-repetition consistency rules out chance coincidence; the evasion threshold (12,000 ft = specific radar lock-on range) suggests a fixed trigger criterion rather than random variation.
