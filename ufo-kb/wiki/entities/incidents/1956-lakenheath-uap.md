# 1956 Lakenheath UAP

**Date**: 13–14 August 1956  
**Location**: RAF Lakenheath and RAF Bentwaters, Suffolk, England  
**Witnesses**: Multiple radar operators (ground stations), visual ground observers, RAF Venom aircrew

## Observation

Multiple ground radar stations at RAF Lakenheath and Bentwaters tracked an unidentified object making anomalous maneuvers. Ground personnel made visual confirmations. An RAF Venom interceptor was scrambled; the pilot made visual contact and attempted pursuit. The object turned and appeared to follow the Venom before the aircraft's radar locked on. The pilot was unable to shake or intercept the object. The engagement lasted several minutes across multiple radar contacts.

## Dual Confirmation

Simultaneous radar confirmation across multiple stations (USAF + RAF systems), visual sighting, and aircrew engagement — the full tri-level confirmation favored in the [[cometa-report|COMETA Report]]'s evidence hierarchy.

## Official Assessments

- **Condon Report** (Scientific Study of Unidentified Flying Objects, 1969): Edward Condon's own analysis team assessed the Lakenheath case as genuinely unexplained after analysis.
- **AIAA** (American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics): endorsed the Lakenheath case as one of the best-documented radar/visual UFO encounters.
- **DIA** later described the Lakenheath case as meeting "all conditions required for a legitimate study of the UFO phenomenon" — the same phrase used for the Tehran 1976 case.

## Significance

Featured as the primary British case in COMETA Chapter 2. Philip Klass attempted to debunk the case; COMETA characterizes his analysis as "reducing disinformation."
