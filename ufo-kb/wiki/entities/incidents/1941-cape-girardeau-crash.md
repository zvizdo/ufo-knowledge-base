# 1941 Cape Girardeau Crash

## Account

A reported UFO crash near Cape Girardeau, Missouri, approximately six years before the more famous [[1947-roswell]] incident. The primary account originates from a reverend (or Catholic priest) who was reportedly called to a crash site to administer last rites — suggesting that at least one of the occupants was still alive at the time.

Key reported elements:
- **Diminutive non-human beings** found at the crash site — small in stature, not human
- At least **one surviving being**, prompting the call for last rites
- **Military personnel** (Army Air Corps) subsequently arrived, removed the debris, and secured the site
- The reverend/priest's account is the primary surviving testimony; no independent corroboration is documented in this source

[[john-blitch]] personally drove to Cape Girardeau during a cross-country relocation to Wright-Patterson / AFRL (Dayton, Ohio) and walked the ground. He assessed the account as credible: "I find zero inconsistency... and the fact of the matter is abduction books don't sell."

## Radar-Crash Hypothesis Connection

Blitch specifically uses Cape Girardeau as a primary supporting case for his [[radar-induced-uap-crashes]] hypothesis. If early Army Air Corps radar installations were operating within several hundred kilometers of Cape Girardeau in 1941, the craft may have been brought down by high-power radar pulses, not by deliberate weapons fire. The geographical proximity of Cape Girardeau to Wright-Patterson (~5-hour drive) is also consistent with a rapid military response from a facility already aware of crash-retrieval protocols.

## Significance

- **Pre-Roswell crash wave**: One of several reported crashes (alongside [[1933-magenta-crash]] and others) suggesting the retrieval apparatus predates the more famous 1947 events
- **Clergy witness**: The involvement of a priest/reverend to administer last rites is a distinctive and unusual detail — suggesting a degree of government cooperation with civilian religious authorities in the immediate aftermath, possibly before full secrecy protocols were established

## Aftermath

Military personnel removed all evidence. The location became part of the pre-Roswell crash canon documented by researchers including Stanton Friedman, Don Schmidt, and others cited by Blitch.
