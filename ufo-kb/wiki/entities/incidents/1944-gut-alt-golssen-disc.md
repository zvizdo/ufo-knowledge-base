# 1944 Gut Alt Golssen Disc Observation

Sole named eyewitness: [[wladyslaw-krasuski]], a Polish POW working the agricultural estate Gut Alt Golssen approximately 30 miles east of Berlin. Reported to the FBI in November 1957 (Case 100-26505).

## Observation Details

- **Craft dimensions**: ~75–100 yards in diameter, ~14 feet total height
- **Structure**: dark gray stationary top section (~5–6 feet), stationary dark gray bottom section (~5–6 feet), 3-foot rapidly rotating middle section (blurred during operation)
- **Sound and EM signature**: high-pitched whine from rotating band caused Krasuski's tractor engine to stall on two occasions — an [[electromagnetic-effects]] pattern consistent with later UAP vehicle-interference reports
- **Launch**: craft rose vertically from inside a circular earthwork enclosure (~100–150 yards diameter, walls of 50-foot tarpaulin) then moved slowly horizontally
- **Ground infrastructure**: uninsulated copper cables, 1.5–2 inches diameter, ran from the enclosure to a nearby concrete column — suggesting an external power supply or control tether
- **Personnel**: SS guards present throughout; ~16–18 Russian, French, and Polish POWs observed the event
- **Post-war findings**: upon Krasuski's post-war return, the cables and concrete column had been removed and the enclosure area was flooded — deliberate evidence concealment

## Context

The observation occurred during the height of German advanced weapons development (1944). The enclosure and copper cable infrastructure are consistent with ground-based test-launch facilities. The disc's size (~75–100 yards / ~68–91 meters diameter) substantially exceeds any documented WWII German experimental aircraft; the Horten Ho 229 wingspan was ~16 meters. The rotating middle section producing EM interference has no analog in known German propeller or jet designs of the period.

The 1957 reporting trigger was the Levelland, Texas UFO wave, in which multiple vehicles experienced engine failure when a luminous object passed overhead — the same [[electromagnetic-effects]] signature Krasuski described from 1944.
