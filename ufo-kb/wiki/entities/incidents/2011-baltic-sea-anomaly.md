# 2011 Baltic Sea Anomaly Discovery

The accidental discovery, on the night of June 19, 2011, of an anomalous 60-meter-diameter circular object on the Baltic Sea floor at 90 m depth, by [[ocean-x]] via sidescan sonar. This event launched a 15-year investigation still ongoing as of 2025.

## Account

[[dennis-asberg]], [[peter-lindberg]], and a third crew member named Flores were approximately one week into a Baltic Sea expedition, tired and disappointed after failing to locate a targeted champagne wreck. They had relocated to a new search area and were running sidescan sonar at 1–2 AM when the anomalous object appeared on screen.

Asberg: *"This huge thing. We're talking about 60 meters in diameter and it's round and you have this track at the bottom."* Lindberg laughed and said *"UFO, Dennis."* They both looked more carefully and agreed they had never seen anything like it. After returning to harbor, they spent days consulting friends and experts worldwide — none had seen anything similar.

One week after discovery, Asberg went public through *Expressen* (Stockholm), Sweden's leading tabloid. The story became international news within days, with Asberg's phone ringing constantly day and night.

## Evidence
- Original sidescan sonar image showing circular ~60 m object with track beneath it
- Subsequent ROV footage, diver reports, sub-bottom profiling, and temperature readings from multiple expeditions
- Stockholm University multi-beam 3D scan

## Aftermath
Led to multiple Ocean X expeditions (2012, 2019, 2025, and others). See [[baltic-sea-anomaly]] for the full documented investigation. The BSA is now publicly known and has been the subject of institutional scientific investigation.

## Villarroel's Scientific Assessment (lbGE3EC6StE)

[[beatriz-villarroel]], speaking directly in [[lbGE3EC6StE]], provides the most substantive public scientific commentary on the BSA:
- The object is covered in **burned biological material** — she notes this is anomalous because there is no volcanic activity in that region
- The object **cannot be sampled**: it was so hard that researchers could not extract a piece from it
- The investigating scientists describe it as an **artificial structure** of unknown origin — not a crashed UFO per se, but something that doesn't fit natural categories
- There is evidence suggesting the object **slid into the ocean** (a track)
- Fox News published a "just a rock" dismissal, but the scientists making that claim never studied actual samples; they issued a media statement without any published paper or open data: *"There's just a claim to the media by those scientists. And I'm just like, this is not a way of analyzing something."*
- Villarroel: *"I still think it's something that should be analyzed because it might be possibly the best candidate non-human artifact that you can find."*

> ⚠ The "Fox News rock" scientists' claim is disputed by Villarroel on methodological grounds — no samples, no paper, no open data.
