# Apollo 13

NASA's seventh crewed lunar mission (April 11–17, 1970), famous for the catastrophic failure of oxygen tank 2 in the Service Module on April 13. The explosion forced abort of the lunar landing; crew James Lovell, Jack Swigert, and Fred Haise used the Lunar Module *Aquarius* as a lifeboat to return safely. The mission became a celebrated example of NASA mission recovery engineering.

**Established facts**: The oxygen tank explosion was caused by damaged insulation on stirring fan wires that arced during a routine stir operation. Investigation revealed that the tank had been dropped during ground testing, creating a hairline fracture, and that the heater thermostats had been damaged, allowing over-pressurization when the heater was activated. NASA redesigned subsequent missions.

**UAP-adjacent claim (contested)**: In [[iPxbILOMQ08]], researcher Coburn hypothesizes that the Apollo 13 mission carried a nuclear warhead (intended for seismic experiments on the Moon) and that the mission's failure was associated with UAP/alien intervention — framing the Atomic Energy Commission's (AEC) reaction to the mission failure as disproportionate and suggesting classified awareness of an NHI-related cause.

**Epistemics**: The nuclear warhead claim is not supported by publicly available mission records. Apollo missions did carry radioactive RTG units (Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generators), and the Apollo 13 RTG was deployed in the Pacific Ocean after the abort. The KB treats this as a speculative claims-thesis, not an established fact.
