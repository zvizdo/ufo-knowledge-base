# 1994 Air France Flight 3532 UAP

**Date**: 28 January 1994  
**Location**: Over the Loire Valley, France (~39,000 ft altitude), en route Nice → London  
**Platform**: Air France commercial aircraft (Flight 3532)  
**Witnesses**: Captain [[jean-charles-duboc]] + copilot Valérie Chauffour

## Observation

At approximately 39,000 ft altitude over the Loire Valley, both crew members independently observed a large disc-shaped or lenticular craft visible for approximately 50 seconds. Duboc estimated the craft's diameter at approximately 250m based on angular size and estimated distance. The object changed color as it rotated. It disappeared instantaneously — not by flying away — directly in front of the aircraft.

## Radar Confirmation

The observation coincided with a primary radar track at the Cinq-Mars-la-Pile Control and Detection Center (CDC), a French Air Force detection center whose primary radar (unlike secondary/civilian radar) could detect non-transponder targets. The radar track was recorded. This dual-mode confirmation (dual cockpit visual + primary military radar) is the feature that elevates the case to the top of the [[cometa-report|COMETA Report]]'s evidentiary hierarchy.

## SEPRA Classification

[[geipan|SEPRA]] (then the GEPAN successor under [[jean-jacques-velasco|Vélasco]]) investigated and classified the sighting as **Category D (UAP-D)** — unexplained after expert analysis — its highest designation for genuine anomalies.

## Significance

This is the primary French domestic case in the COMETA Report (Chapter 1) and is used to anchor the claim that credible radar-visual UAP encounters occur over densely monitored French airspace.
