# 1946 USS Mory USO Sonar Contact

A US Navy bathymetric survey ship, the USS Mory, was mapping the Pacific Ocean floor using a primitive single-beam echo sounder — the state of the art at the time — when the sonar registered a very large, hard contact at shallow depth.

## Account

- The USS Mory was a post-WWII bathymetric survey ship tasked with ocean floor mapping in the Pacific
- During surveying, the echo sounder registered a hard contact — distinct from soft biological scatterers (plankton, marine life) — spanning more than a football field in diameter at relatively shallow depth
- The ship's crew logged the contact in the official Navy log
- When the Mory returned to the same location to re-examine the contact, it was gone
- No explanation was recorded or investigated further at the time; the data was not followed up

## Documentation

[[richard-dolan]] cites this incident in his forthcoming unpublished book on USO cases (~600 cases compiled from records going back to the 1700s), sourcing it directly from the USS Mory's Navy log. [[tim-gallaudet]] had access to Dolan's draft and also referenced this incident in his Sol Foundation paper "Beneath the Surface: We May Learn More About UAP If We Look in the Ocean."

## Significance

This is one of the earliest examples of a hard-sonar USO contact in the official military record. The distinguishing features:
- **Hard echo** (consistent with a solid object, not a biological scatterer or thermal layer)
- **Size** (football-field scale — far larger than any known 1946-era submarine)
- **Disappearance** (not explained by vessel movement, depth changes, or biological behavior)

Gallaudet uses this case to illustrate the pattern of UAP/USO data being collected by the Navy without systematic analysis or follow-up — the anomaly is detected, dismissed or ignored, and the data is archived without investigation.
