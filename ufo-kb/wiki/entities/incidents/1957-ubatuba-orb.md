# Ubatuba Orb Incident

A fisherman at [[ubatuba-brazil]] on the São Paulo coast witnessed a glowing object that suddenly exploded over the ocean. The fisherman collected fragments of the debris — pieces of magnesium. Those fragments eventually made their way into [[jacques-vallee]]'s sample collection and were subsequently analyzed by [[garry-nolan]] using the NanoSIMS secondary ion mass spectrometer at Stanford's engineering department.

## The Analysis

[[garry-nolan]] ran mass spectrometry on the fragments using NanoSIMS — a secondary ion mass spectrometer that measures not just elemental composition but isotope ratios at the individual-element level.

**Finding**: The magnesium isotope ratios in the primary fragment are **not consistent with naturally occurring magnesium on Earth**. Nolan: *"not even close to being natural."* Producing such ratios requires isotope separation by centrifuge — expensive, technically demanding, and without obvious motivation for a hoaxer given when the event allegedly occurred.

**Second fragment complication**: A second piece allegedly from the same event has *correct* Earth magnesium isotope ratios. The two pieces together exhibit inhomogeneous composition — like a "chocolate and vanilla ice cream swirl." The material mixing itself is anomalous: no known terrestrial metallurgy produces this mixture. This inhomogeneity suggests either:
1. Manufacturing process using isotopically separated feedstock that was not fully blended, or
2. Different provenance for the two fragments (mislabeled as same event)

The magnesium itself burned easily when the orb exploded, which Nolan notes is strange — raw magnesium is highly flammable, suggesting the intact object must have had additional components stabilizing it.

## Significance

This is one of a small number of alleged UAP debris cases where isotope ratio analysis provides a falsifiable, measurable departure from naturally occurring terrestrial matter. Unlike visual sighting reports, the isotopic signature is a physical property that can be independently measured and replicated or refuted.

Nolan notes the analysis raises the question of whether the anomalous isotope ratio is:
- A **material property** intentionally engineered into the object (the ratio achieves some functional purpose), or
- A **byproduct** of a propulsion or energy effect that incidentally separates isotopes
