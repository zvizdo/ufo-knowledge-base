# 1825 HMS Blonde USO Incident

The earliest documented USO sighting in [[richard-dolan]]'s database. Recorded by [[andrew-bloxom]], the ship's naturalist on the HMS Blonde during a voyage from Hawaii to the Cook Islands and toward South America.

## Account

From Bloxom's published diary, at **3:30 a.m. on August 12, 1825**, the Night Watch of the HMS Blonde reported the following:

> "The Night Watch reported this orange spherical object rise from the ocean at 7°. [It] lit the deck of the ship so bright you could pick a pin off the deck."

Additional characteristics:
- Described as **"red hot like a cannon shot"**
- Apparent size approximately equal to the moon or sun — Bloxom explicitly notes there was no moon that night, ruling out confusion
- Object **descended back into the water**
- Object **rose a second time**, then **descended again**
- No further observations

The diary entry is embedded within otherwise meticulous scientific and navigational observations, without editorializing. After this passage, the diary returns immediately to normal records.

## Evidential Value

This is not oral tradition or retroactively collected testimony. Bloxom's diary was **published contemporaneously** and has been available in print for 200 years. It is accessible today as a PDF. The scientific background of the observer (ship's naturalist), the specificity of the time and date, and the precision of the description (7° elevation, disk/size comparisons, the candlestick detail) give it unusual credibility for a historical case.

## Pattern Match

[[richard-dolan]] pairs this case with the [[1971-uss-jfk-orb]] incident — a glowing spherical orb above/beside a US naval carrier 146 years later. He notes that the orb/sphere shape appears in a large proportion of USO cases and is also the shape most commonly associated with [[uso-em-interference]] patterns.
