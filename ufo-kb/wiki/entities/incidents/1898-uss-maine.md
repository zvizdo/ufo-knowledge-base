# USS Maine Explosion

The USS Maine was a US Navy second-class battleship that exploded and sank in Havana Harbor on February 15, 1898, killing 266 of her 354 crew members. The explosion — cause unknown — was attributed to Spanish sabotage by the American yellow press (William Randolph Hearst's New York Journal most prominently), and "Remember the Maine!" became the rallying cry for the Spanish-American War, which began two months later.

**Cause contested**: Three major investigations have examined the Maine explosion:
- 1898 Naval Board of Inquiry: concluded external mine, blamed on Spain
- 1911 Naval investigation: confirmed external explosion
- 1974 Admiral Hyman Rickover investigation: concluded internal ammunition explosion (spontaneous combustion of coal bunkers adjacent to powder magazines)
- 1999 National Geographic study: inconclusive but favored internal explosion

No investigation has produced definitive evidence of Spanish sabotage.

**Historical false-flag precedent**: The Maine is cited in KB discussions as a documented historical case of an ambiguous or accidental event being exploited as a pretext for military action. Whether Spain actually sabotaged the Maine is less important than the documented pattern: an incident with ambiguous cause, political actors with motivation to exploit it, press amplification, and rapid escalation to armed conflict. This pattern — alongside [[gulf-of-tonkin]] and [[operation-northwoods]] — is cited as evidence of the US government's demonstrated historical willingness to use genuine or manufactured incidents to justify military action.

**[[operation-northwoods]] parallel**: The Operation Northwoods documents proposed staging incidents similar in structure to the Maine explosion — fake attacks on US assets to justify intervention. The Maine serves as the historical precedent demonstrating that such pretexts work.
