# 1949 Forrestal Death at Bethesda

On May 22, 1949, [[james-forrestal]], the first US Secretary of Defense, died after falling from a 16th-floor window at Bethesda Naval Hospital. The official ruling was suicide. [[richard-dolan]] is "absolutely convinced" it was murder.

## Timeline

- **January 1949**: Truman fires Forrestal; public farewell ceremony
- In the limousine after the ceremony: [[stuart-symington]] (Sec Air Force, Forrestal's enemy) rides with Forrestal; Forrestal emerges from the limo in a dissociated state, repeating "You are a loyal fellow" in his office for hours
- Forrestal is flown to Hobe Sound, Florida for several weeks; reportedly attempts to hang himself with a belt there (fails)
- Returned to Bethesda Naval Hospital; placed on the **16th floor** against the advice of his attending physicians (who recommended a ground floor for a potentially suicidal patient)
- **National security personnel** ordered the top floor — not physicians
- Regular duty corpsman (Forrestal's "pal" who had bonded with Forrestal) **goes AWOL without explanation** on the final night; a replacement takes the shift
- Forrestal's primary attending physician is out of town at a conference
- Forrestal's brother Henry calls the hospital the evening of May 21: "If my brother is not out by tomorrow, I'm going to raise holy hell"
- **1:30–1:45 AM, May 22**: Replacement corpsman checks on Forrestal; sees him calmly in bed, reading, apparently transcribing Sophocles' *Ajax* and taking notes; Forrestal declines a sedative
- Shortly after: Forrestal "is gone"
- Falls 16 floors; dies

## Why the Official Story Fails

The alleged suicide method: tied bathrobe cord tightly around neck, tied the other end *loosely* around a small hallway radiator, hung himself out the 16th-floor window. Dolan: "No one has ever tried to kill themselves that way." The cord apparently "slipped" off the radiator, causing the fall.

Dolan's reconstruction: "Couple of big tough Navy guys grab him and throw him the hell out the window. I'm absolutely convinced that's what happened. Then tied the bathrobe cord to the radiator after the fact."

## Cover-Up Indicators

- The **Will Cutts Review** (official investigation) interviewed the duty corpsman for approximately 15 minutes — "Like, thank you for your testimony, young man. You can go." No critical examination.
- Forrestal's diaries were seized and expunged before publication
- He was denied access to his spiritual adviser, Father Sheehy, throughout his entire hospitalization — an unusual restriction on a patient in a hospital
- His first words to visitor Robert Lovett were: **"Bob, they're trying to kill me."**

## Notable Visitors at Bethesda

Truman; Forrestal's successor Lewis Johnson; Lyndon Baines Johnson (young senator); John Fitzgerald Kennedy (young congressman)
