# 1972 SRI Quark Detector Session

## Account

In 1972, [[hal-puthoff|Hal Puthoff]] — then at SRI doing laser work after completing his PhD — invited [[ingo-swann|Ingo Swann]] to spend a weekend at the lab. Swann had been forwarded a copy of Puthoff's proposed algae-culture consciousness-affects-matter experiment by polygraph researcher [[cleve-baxter|Cleve Backster]] at a New York cocktail party, and had written to Puthoff suggesting he work with a human subject instead.

Puthoff's available test instrument: an ultra-shielded quantum chip being used to search for quarks. The chip was nested inside layers of electrical shielding, magnetic shielding, and superconducting shielding, fully acoustically isolated from the external environment. Output was a slow ~30-second oscillation that the team interpreted as evidence the system was working as designed.

Puthoff asked Swann to affect the device. Swann did so unequivocally: the slow oscillation stopped. Asked to do something else, Swann then made the device oscillate fast — the moment Puthoff says "the graduate student sort of went berserko" (the student's PhD work depended on the chip being unaffected by external influences). After Puthoff confirmed nothing was wrong with the instrument, Swann reproduced the effect on demand.

Asked how he knew what to do, Swann replied: "Well, I didn't know what to do, so I just looked inside through all this shielding." Swann then drew an accurate schematic of the device's internal contents — a diagram which had never been published.

## Evidence

- Puthoff's contemporary write-up, circulated to other physicists shortly after the session, is the document that brought the CIA to his office within weeks
- Swann's hand-drawn schematic of the device's internals (never publicly published prior to the session)
- The on-demand reproduction of the oscillation perturbation under controlled lab conditions

## Aftermath

The CIA arrived at SRI within weeks. Background check on Puthoff identified his prior service as a Naval Intelligence officer at NSA between his master's and PhD — i.e., he already held high-level clearances. CIA's stated worry: if Swann could read through that level of shielding, Soviet psychics might be reading classified material out of superconducting safes. An initial small ($50–60K) contract was authorized; the program that grew from it ran more than 20 years at the highly classified level, eventually under the [[stargate-program|Stargate]] name (and earlier program names including Gondola Wish).

The session also fixed Swann's role as the program's founding remote viewer. He continued to participate at SRI for years and contributed the protocol innovations (coordinate-based viewing, viscera-not-imagery training) that scaled the program beyond his own participation.
