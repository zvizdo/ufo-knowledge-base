# 1980 Cash-Landrum Incident

On December 29, 1980, Betty Cash (51), Vickie Landrum (57), and Colby Landrum (7, Vickie's grandson) were driving near Huffman, Texas, when they observed a large diamond-shaped craft hovering over the road, emitting intense heat, flames, and light. The craft was surrounded by approximately 23 Chinook-type helicopters. The witnesses stopped and exited the vehicle; the car hood was hot to the touch afterward.

**Injuries**: All three witnesses suffered acute symptoms consistent with radiation exposure: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, burning skin, blistering, and hair loss. Betty Cash, who had spent the most time outside the vehicle, suffered the most severe injuries — blistering on her face, scalp hair loss, and progressive health deterioration. She was hospitalized multiple times. Colby Landrum developed eye problems and lesions. All three suffered long-term health consequences attributed to radiation exposure.

**Betty Cash's death**: Betty Cash died on December 29, 1998 — exactly 18 years after the incident — of illness her physicians linked to radiation exposure effects.

**Investigation**: The witnesses filed a claim against the US government, alleging the craft was a military vehicle. The government denied any responsibility; no military craft matching the description was identified. The FBI investigated. The case remains unresolved.

**Significance**: The Cash-Landrum incident is the most severe documented case of UAP-related radiation injury in the US record. Unlike the [[1967-falcon-lake]] case (single witness), it involves three independent witnesses including a child.
