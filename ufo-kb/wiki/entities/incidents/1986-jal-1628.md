# 1986 JAL Flight 1628 Encounter

The most radar-documented UAP encounter in the public record. A Japan Airlines 747 cargo freighter (transporting Beaujolais Nouveau wine) on a trans-Pacific route encountered multiple UAPs over Alaska on November 17, 1986. The event was tracked by military height-finding radar and the full data set was secretly preserved by FAA official [[john-callahan]], later becoming the basis for rigorous physics analysis by [[kevin-knuth]] and [[daniel-koumbi]].

## Account

**Initial encounter**: Rectangular craft with lights scanning vertically appeared in front of the 747, shining light into the cockpit. Crew could feel heat from the light. This lasted some time before the craft departed.

**Main encounter**: An enormous walnut-shaped object — estimated at roughly four 747 fuselage lengths, approximately aircraft carrier dimensions — appeared and paced the plane for ~45 minutes. When positioned in front of the 747, captain [[kenju-terauchi]] said he "couldn't see anything but the craft." The object would shift from one side of the plane to the other over the course of radar sweeps (~12-second intervals). Crew were in contact with air traffic control but the object was not showing on civilian radar. Military height-finding radar was tracking it.

At one point the plane did a 360° turn to attempt to shake the object; the maneuver is visible on radar. The object followed.

Terauchi was the first witness to report to ATC. His testimony was covered on NBC's Nightly News (Tom Brokaw and Connie Chung anchor broadcast). He was later fired and had his captain's license revoked — reinstated several years later.

## Evidence

- **Military height-finding radar records**: Captured the object alongside the 747 for 45 minutes; show the plane's 360° maneuver.
- **FAA data**: [[john-callahan]] — then FAA chief of accidents and investigations — received and reviewed the case. Reagan's scientific team and CIA arrived to collect all data. Callahan covertly copied everything and put it in a box under his desk; held it for ~20 years until retirement, then made it public.
- At the handover meeting with Reagan's team, officials said: **"We've never had 45 minutes of radar data from one of these things before"** — implying prior shorter radar contacts existed.

## Physics Analysis

**Knuth & colleagues** (published paper): Estimated object acceleration by tracking its position changes in 12-second radar sweep intervals. Found minimum acceleration of ~5,000 Gs for Tic-Tac-class maneuvers; initial estimates for JAL 1628 were in the same extraordinary range.

**[[daniel-koumbi]]** (Niels Bohr Institute): Later obtained the actual radar data and performed a more rigorous analysis. Found three separate acceleration events each exceeding **9,000 Gs**; one reached ~**11,000 Gs**. Estimated top speed: **~250,000 mph** — sufficient to reach the moon in approximately **54 minutes**. The craft was aircraft-carrier-sized at these accelerations.

For context: F-35 wings fail at 13 G; maximum missile frame survivability ~60 G. An object the size of an aircraft carrier accelerating at 11,000 G is outside every known physics of materials and propulsion.

## Aftermath

- Terauchi's sketch of the craft was broadcast on national television.
- JAL pressured Terauchi internally; he was initially fired.
- Callahan's 45-minute radar data set is the longest single-event UAP radar record in the public domain.
- [[kevin-knuth]] calls JAL 1628 his "highest-conviction" UAP case, prioritizing it over even the Nimitz encounter due to the duration and quality of the radar evidence.
