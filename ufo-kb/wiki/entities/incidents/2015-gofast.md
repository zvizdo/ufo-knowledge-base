# 2015 GOFAST Video

FLIR thermal infrared video from USS Theodore Roosevelt operations (2014–2015), showing an object moving rapidly and apparently skimming the ocean surface. Released publicly in March 2020 (later than [[2015-gimbal]], which appeared in the December 2017 NYT story). The crew audio captures the exclamation "Woah, got it! Woo-hoo!" as the object streaks across the field of view.

[[mick-west]] and [[marik-von-rennenkampff]] mention it alongside Gimbal as part of the same cluster of Roosevelt-era encounters. GOFAST is less technically contested than Gimbal — analysis has focused on whether the object's apparent speed is real or an artifact of the parallax from the camera platform's own motion. West has argued the object is slower than it appears when altitude and parallax are accounted for.

## Four-Object Formation — New Detail (2026)

[[ryan-graves]]'s close friend — the weapons system officer (WSO) who was in the back seat when this video was recorded — privately told Graves that the tape captured only **one of four objects in line-abreast formation**, each approximately one mile apart. The four maintained precise relative spacing throughout the engagement, implying inter-craft digital coordination or a shared guidance system. The other three objects were not framed by the camera and did not appear in the publicly released footage.

This detail has not been included in any prior public release or official statement. It changes the evidential weight of the GOFAST video: rather than a single isolated object, it is one member of a coordinated multi-craft formation operating off the East Coast.
