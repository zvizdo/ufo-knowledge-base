# 2004 Nimitz Tic-Tac Incident

On November 14, 2004, Commander [[david-fravor]] and Lt. Cmdr. Alex Dietrich of VFA-41 Black Aces encountered a white, oblong, approximately 40-foot object off the coast of San Diego during a training exercise. The object displayed performance characteristics — instant acceleration, hypersonic speed, no visible propulsion — that no known aircraft can match.

The USS Princeton (guided missile cruiser) had been tracking anomalous objects on its SPY-1 radar for approximately two weeks prior to the visual encounter. After Fravor's interaction, the object reappeared on radar at a pre-briefed rendezvous point within seconds — 60 miles away.

The Navy FLIR1 ("Tic Tac") gun camera footage was declassified and released in 2017, triggering the New York Times story and the modern UAP disclosure era.

## Key Evidence

- USS Princeton SPY-1 radar tracks (2+ weeks of anomalous objects)
- Visual confirmation by two experienced naval aviators
- FLIR1 gun camera footage showing object evasion
- USS Roosevelt subsequent encounters (2014–2015)

## "Conscious Craft" Description (dzTZbSNsKV8)

[[garry-nolan]] relays Fravor's description of the object's behavior in [[dzTZbSNsKV8]]:

> *"Looking at the UFO and it's almost as if the thing sees that he's looking at it and is conscious and almost breathing. They're all going against the wind. The wind's 120 knots west. But if there's a thing, it's rotating."*

The craft's rotation against a 120-knot headwind eliminates aerodynamic explanations; the apparent awareness of being observed adds the possibility of biological rather than mechanical propulsion. Nolan uses this as evidence for the hypothesis that some UAP are biological entities rather than technological craft.
