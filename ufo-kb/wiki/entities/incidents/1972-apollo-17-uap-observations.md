# 1972 Apollo 17 Anomalous Observations

Three distinct anomalous-observation sequences from the Apollo 17 mission (launched 1972-12-07):

## 1. Rotating Distant Object (~MET 02:18:47)

CDR [[gene-cernan]] observed a bright object at ~10–12 Earth diameters distance, rotating rhythmically with a bright flash and a dull flash per cycle. He described it as "something physical in the distance" clearly distinct from nearby debris particles. Mission Control requested attitude references to try optical tracking.

LMP [[jack-schmitt]] provided the resolution: the two-flash pattern matched the S-IVB upper stage (rotating with broad side then engine bell facing the crew). No independent optical confirmation from Mission Control is recorded in this excerpt.

**Best assessment**: S-IVB stage. Cernan's initial framing as anomalous is the notable datum — a trained commander's honest first-response before identification.

## 2. Cosmic Ray Phosphenes (Sleep Period 1)

CDR Cernan reported peripheral streaks and an intense headlight-like flash between his eyes during the first sleep period. Standard deep-space radiation effect (galactic cosmic rays ionizing retinal cells). ALFMED experiment was active. Not anomalous in a UAP sense; notable for documenting the crew's sensory reporting accuracy.

## 3. Lunar Surface Flash near Grimaldi (MET ~03:15:38)

LMP [[jack-schmitt]] observed a brief bright flash on the lunar surface just north of Grimaldi crater during orbital pass. Mission Control checked seismometers; no confirmation recorded in this transcript excerpt.

**Best assessment**: Small meteorite impact (most common cause of observed lunar flashes) or Transient Lunar Phenomenon (TLP). Schmitt's geologist background makes his observation highly credible.
