# 1967 Falcon Lake Incident

On May 20, 1967, amateur geologist Stefan Michalak was prospecting near Falcon Lake, Manitoba, Canada when he encountered two elliptical craft that landed nearby. One craft opened a hatch from which Michalak could see figures inside. After the craft closed and prepared to depart, Michalak touched the hull surface and was knocked down by an exhaust blast through a grid of holes on the craft's side.

**Injuries**: Michalak suffered a grid-pattern burn on his chest matching the spacing and pattern of the exhaust port holes on the craft. He developed acute nausea, vomiting, and other symptoms consistent with radiation exposure (lymphopenia, elevated lymphocyte counts). The burn pattern was documented by physicians. He continued to suffer health effects — including periodic recurrence of symptoms, weight loss, and immune dysfunction — for years afterward.

**Investigation**: The case was investigated by the Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP), the Canadian Department of National Defence, and the US Air Force Condon Committee. No conventional explanation satisfying the physical evidence was found. The Condon Committee classified it as "unexplained." The site was inspected for radiation; elevated readings were found.

**Medical documentation**: The Falcon Lake incident is one of the most medically well-documented UAP injuries cases. Physician reports, hospital records, and the grid-burn photographs constitute physical evidence that distinguishes it from purely testimonial cases.
