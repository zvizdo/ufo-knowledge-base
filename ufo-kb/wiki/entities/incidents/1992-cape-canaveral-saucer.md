# 1992 Cape Canaveral Saucer Sighting

The 1992 Cape Canaveral Saucer Sighting is a firsthand account by Dr. [[greg-rogers]] — then chief of aerospace medicine at the [[45th-space-wing]], [[cape-canaveral]] / [[patrick-air-force-base]] — of observing a reverse-engineered saucer craft via a closed-circuit television feed at Cape Canaveral Air Force Station. The event occurred during an [[egng]]-escorted tour of buildings on the base, with the showing arranged spontaneously by an unnamed USAF major.

## Account

### Context

Rogers was on an EG&G-supervised tour of multiple building sets on Cape Canaveral as part of his oversight role as the medical group's liaison to EG&G (the prime contractor). At a clean-room exit, his EG&G escort departed slightly ahead of him — creating a ~60–90 second gap during which Rogers was alone.

A USAF major (who recognized Rogers from a prior flight physical) intercepted him and invited him to "see something even you have never seen." The major led Rogers to a computer console room, locked the door, and closed all louvered blinds before turning on a video display.

### The CCTV Feed

The display was a **closed-circuit television feed** with no identification markings — no classification level, no location, no timestamp, no source designation. What appeared on screen:

- **The craft**: a saucer approximately **20 feet across**, shaped like a modified egg (symmetric, not offset). Surface completely smooth — no rivets, no seams, no windows, no visible hardware.
- **Color**: pearly white throughout.
- **Markings**: black-painted rectangles at the beam line (~60% above, 40% below) at the 12:30–2:30, 3:30–5:30, 6:30–8:30, and 9:30–11:30 positions. A vertical rectangle at the 3:00 position. These appeared to be motion-reference markers.
- **Personnel**: two figures in lab coats (engineer types) in the lower right of frame; three figures in Tyvek suits standing and talking; the craft stood between them.
- **Markings on craft**: as the craft rotated, Rogers could clearly read **"US Air Force"** and an Air Force flight insignia.

### Craft Behavior

A warning tone sounded, personnel cleared the area, and the craft activated:

1. Lifted off the floor **"light as a feather"** with no perceptible mechanism — no jet wash, no rotors, nothing
2. Hovered approximately 3 feet above the floor
3. Rotated clockwise 360°, paused
4. Rotated counterclockwise 360°, returning to exact original orientation
5. Moved left, right, forward, and backward — all motions smooth and precise, "like a video game aircraft testing controls"
6. Throughout activation: **audible and visible electrostatic discharges** across the entire surface — described as "like static discharge," consistent with a plasma envelope

Three umbilical lines were attached to a pole at the apex of the craft. Rogers interprets these as external power/control feeds, indicating the craft was a prototype lacking full internal power and control systems.

### The Key Statement

When Rogers asked why the craft was designed in such an aerodynamically unconventional form, the major's response:

> **"We got it from them."**

Rogers: "I don't have to figure out what that means, but that was exactly what he said."

### Interruption and Cover Story

Approximately 15 minutes into the session, a lieutenant colonel and two captains knocked and entered. The major immediately powered down the screen. When challenged on what was happening, the major told them Rogers had been checking a potentially cancerous skin lesion — a plausible medical cover given Rogers' role.

All eyes turned to Rogers. He confirmed the story and left, choosing not to report the incident. His reasoning: no classification marking appeared on the feed; the incident was ambiguous enough that reporting it would create severe career risk for himself and potentially others, with uncertain outcome. He notes that in 1992, unreported UAP sightings were common among pilots and astronauts.

### Rogers Never Returned

Rogers never went back to that building, never saw the major again, and carried the observation for ~30+ years before disclosing it publicly in May 2024.

## Location of the Craft

Rogers has no direct knowledge of where the CCTV feed was sourcing from. His speculation:
- [[lockheed-skunk-works]] (top candidate given his belief a contractor owned the craft)
- [[area-51]] (top candidate given EG&G's area management role)
- [[wright-patterson]] (top candidate historically for recovered craft)
- [[dugway-proving-grounds]]
- Another undisclosed location on Cape Canaveral itself

## Craft Characteristics and Interpretation

Rogers' aeronautical background gives his analysis technical grounding:

- **No aerodynamic advantage**: The saucer form has no conventional aerodynamic benefit. Rogers: "No vehicle I have ever known of could obtain a 45° angle of attack without moving." The design makes sense only if the propulsion principle is electromagnetic/gravitational rather than aerodynamic.
- **Not USAF-owned**: Rogers distinguishes between USAF-owned and contractor-owned prototype (analogous to "Have Blue" — Lockheed's F-117 predecessor, which bore USAF markings but was owned by Skunk Works until delivered).
- **Umbilicals = early-stage prototype**: The external feeds indicate the craft was not yet self-sufficient — an early enough stage that internal power/control had not been miniaturized into the hull.
- **Plasma envelope**: The electrostatic discharges across the surface were continuous during activation; Rogers suggests these were the active electromagnetic field that enabled the propulsion/levitation mechanism.

## Comparison to Bob Lazar's Sport Model

Rogers voluntarily compares his description to [[bob-lazar]]'s claimed Sport Model at [[s4]]:
- Both smooth, pearly-white discs with no visible seams or windows
- The primary difference: Rogers' craft had a more purely modified-egg form; Lazar's sport model had some cockpit protrusion and geometric corners
- Rogers: "If you take away the oddness to the corners and smooth it out as I've described with this egg, it would be very much like his sport model. I don't know if they're at all related, but boy, it would be a lot like it."

## Corroboration Context

Rogers explicitly came forward to corroborate [[david-grusch]]'s 2023 congressional testimony. Grusch noted no witnesses to reverse-engineering programs had yet personally come forward. Rogers watched government officials deny "we have never had a craft like that in our inventory" and responded: "What are you talking about? I saw it."

> ⚠ **Open question**: Rogers' CCTV observation is secondhand in the sense that he saw the craft via video rather than in person. However, his observation of the craft's performance characteristics (levitation, plasma discharge, motion range) is direct visual/auditory evidence — not hearsay.

## Aftermath

Rogers reported nothing to his chain of command. The story remained private until his 2024 retirement. His first public disclosure was through UK Daily Mail journalist [[josh-boswell]] (May 4, 2024), followed by interviews with [[chris-leto]] (Letto Files) and [[jesse-michaels]] (American Alchemy). Rogers is on the board of the [[international-ufo-bureau]] (Oklahoma City).
