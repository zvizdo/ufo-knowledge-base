# Wow! Signal

On August 15, 1977, astronomer **Jerry Ehman**, reviewing data printouts from the Big Ear radio telescope at Ohio State University, circled a sequence of characters and wrote "Wow!" in the margin — giving the signal its name. The signal, recorded as `6EQUJ5`, was a narrowband burst at 1420.4556 MHz (the hydrogen line, long identified as the most likely frequency for intentional interstellar communication) that lasted the full 72 seconds the source passed through the telescope's fixed beam.

## Signal Characteristics

- **Frequency**: 1420.4556 MHz — the 21cm hydrogen line; [[frank-drake]] and others had identified this as the most likely SETI frequency
- **Intensity**: 30 standard deviations above background noise — extraordinarily strong
- **Duration**: 72 seconds — the full window allowed by Big Ear's fixed-beam design
- **Bandwidth**: Less than 10 kHz — consistent with a transmitting antenna, not natural broadband sources
- **Direction**: Consistent with deep space (Sagittarius constellation); no known natural object in that direction

## Non-Repetition

Despite numerous subsequent monitoring campaigns (including multi-year targeted efforts), the signal has never been repeated. This non-repetition is the central evidentiary problem: a genuine extraterrestrial transmission would presumably be either continuous or on a detectable cycle; a one-time event is consistent with both an artificial source and with terrestrial interference or instrumental artifact.

## Proposed Explanations

- **Extraterrestrial origin**: The signal's characteristics match an intentional narrowband transmission. The hydrogen-line frequency suggests deliberate choice.
- **Cometary origin** (2017, Antonio Paris): Hydrogen clouds from two comets passing through the telescope's field of view at the time. This explanation has been contested; critics note the comets' hydrogen signatures would be broadband, not narrowband.
- **Classified satellite or experimental transmitter**: Terrestrial interference at the right frequency cannot be fully ruled out, though the signal's characteristics are inconsistent with known transmitter types of the era.

## Relevance to UAP/SETI Discourse

The Wow! Signal appears in this KB via [[beatriz-villarroel]] and [[frank-drake]]'s work on SETI and non-terrestrial signals. It is the strongest candidate for an extraterrestrial radio contact in recorded SETI history. Its relevance to UAP discourse is indirect but real: if a signal of this character was received and never explained, it is a data point consistent with the presence of technological civilizations at interstellar distances — which in turn contextualizes the UAP/NHI hypothesis.

The signal is also cited in discussions of [[astronomical-data-suppression]] — the claim that anomalous astronomical data is systematically under-investigated or de-emphasized by institutional forces.
