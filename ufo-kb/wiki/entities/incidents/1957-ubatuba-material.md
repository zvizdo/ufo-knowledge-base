# Ubatuba Material

Small metallic fragments — primarily magnesium — allegedly recovered from a UAP that exploded or disintegrated over the ocean near Ubatuba, Brazil, in September 1957. The fragments were reportedly collected by witnesses on the beach and subsequently forwarded to Brazilian UFO researcher Ibrahim Sued at the newspaper *O Globo*.

**Laboratory analysis**: The fragments were analyzed by the Brazilian National Laboratory for Mineral Production (LNPM) and subsequently sent to other laboratories. The analyses found extremely high-purity magnesium with isotopic ratios differing from natural magnesium. Some analyses reported magnesium isotope ratios significantly different from the terrestrial standard — specifically, the ratio of magnesium-26 to magnesium-24 was claimed to be anomalous. If accurate, this would indicate non-terrestrial manufacture, since magnesium isotope ratios are determined by nucleosynthesis conditions not reproducible by known industrial processes.

**Battelle analysis**: The transcript cites Battelle Memorial Institute as having analyzed the material. Battelle was a key contractor in early UAP materials analysis (also involved in Roswell-era materials analysis controversies) and was associated with Project Blue Book. An alleged Battelle analysis finding isotopic anomalies would carry significant evidentiary weight.

**Garry Nolan possession**: Per [[d9tdJ2SkBKQ]], [[garry-nolan]] reportedly has possession of a magnesium-bismuth piece with characteristics consistent with Ubatuba-type material — a contemporary continuation of the chain of custody for alleged anomalous UAP materials.

**Epistemics**: Chain of custody for the original fragments is disputed; the original analyses were never fully replicated with the rigor required for scientific publication. The fragments are not available for comprehensive modern isotopic analysis at a public laboratory.
