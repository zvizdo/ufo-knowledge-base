# Moscow Embassy Microwave Incident

## Account

Beginning no later than the early 1970s, the Soviet Union continuously beamed the US Embassy in Moscow with microwave radiation. This was eventually confirmed at the diplomatic level: Henry Kissinger confronted Soviet Ambassador Anatoly Dobrynin about the practice at the 1976 SALT talks.

US Ambassador Walter Stoessel developed a rare blood cancer attributed to the sustained microwave exposure. He died in 1986. Multiple US Embassy personnel were affected.

## Significance

This incident establishes a decades-long precedent for:

1. **Russian willingness to use directed energy against US diplomatic personnel**
2. **US government suppression of the attribution for political reasons** (acknowledging it as an attack would have escalated the Cold War)
3. **The mechanism for Havana syndrome**: sustained microwave exposure causing physiological damage

[[eric-haseltine]] cites this incident explicitly in his [[2016-havana-syndrome]] investigation as evidence that:
- Russia has the technical capability and historical intent
- The US has a documented pattern of suppressing attribution to avoid war
- The attacks may have been ongoing since the 1980s under different guises
