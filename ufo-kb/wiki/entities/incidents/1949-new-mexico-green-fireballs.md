# 1948–1950 New Mexico Green Fireballs (17th OSI District Corpus)

> *Comprehensive incident-cluster entity. The 209+-sighting catalog in [[dow-uap-d17-sandia-base-1948-1950]] is the primary documentary record. Most individual sightings are NOT broken out as separate incident pages; the catalog itself is the canonical reference.*

## Key Facts

- **Date range**: 5 December 1948 (first formally-cataloged green fireball: Sighting #6 ~2200hrs, Vaughn NM) through 1 May 1950 (Sighting #209 Kirtland AFB cylindrical silver object)
- **Total catalog size (per 25 May 1950 summary)**: 209+ sighting entries; 72 accepted as "green fireball category" (per LaPaz Seventh Report); these constitute less than 5% of total unscreened reports received
- **Geographic concentration**: "Los Alamos, Las Vegas [NM], and West Texas triangle" (per Mandelkorn 16 Feb 1949 conference report) plus extensions to Arizona, Oregon, and (Sighting #1) European observation along Brest-Kiev line
- **Phenomenological hallmarks** (LaPaz Seventh Report enumerated 11 points distinguishing from meteors):
  - Bright vivid green color (laboratory: λ=5218 Å = copper-salts emission)
  - Nearly horizontal paths (contra meteor random-compass distribution)
  - Low altitude ~8-12 miles
  - Velocities 8-14 mi/sec (slower than meteors, faster than V-2 rockets / jets)
  - Silent — no shock-wave noise even at low altitudes that should produce audible booms (basis for Edward Teller's "not material objects passing through the air" conclusion at the 16 Feb 1949 Los Alamos conference)
  - Instant full brightness (not gradual brightening like meteors)
  - North-biased compass arrival directions
  - 20:30 local-time sighting-frequency maximum (distinct from meteor maximum ~03:00 and meteorite maximum ~16:00)
  - Friday-Monday day-of-week concentration
  - ~2-second durations (longer than visual meteors, shorter than meteorite falls)
  - No train of sparks or dust cloud
  - Curious association with established meteor showers (LaPaz: "**might indicate an attempt to render the green fireballs less conspicuous**" — implying intelligent agency)

## Marquee Individual Events

- **12 December 1948 9h 2m, Starvation Peak (Bernal NM) + Los Alamos** — first doubly-observed green fireball; 25-mile horizontal path; 8-10 mi altitude; 8-12 mi/sec; trajectory backward-extension passes "almost centrally across the Los Alamos reservation."
- **20 December 1948 8h 54m, Los Alamos area** — observed by four named [[atomic-energy-security-service|AESS]] Inspectors (William D. Wilson, Buford G. Truett, Clifford E. Strang, George S. Skipper); LaPaz analysis: 7.5-mile path, 3.75-7.5 mi/sec, trajectory forward-extension passes 6 miles north of Los Alamos town.
- **30 January 1949 5:54 pm MST, El Paso TX → Roswell NM → Alamogordo NM → Ft Worth TX** — mass-witness (estimated 200+ observers in NM, 10 in Ft Worth) bright fireball; 143 miles, 10-14 mi/sec; LaPaz personally field-surveyed; **Texas observers reported color as blue, orange, red, purple** instead of green (color outlier); possible "Udden noises" reported at Roswell and Muleshoe.
- **6-7-8 March 1949, Camp Hood TX Q-Area** — multi-night named-witness aerial-light incursions over the AFSWP/Killeen Base nuclear weapons storage area; bearings recorded with surveying instruments; lights "form a rough circle about the Q Area."
- **17 March 1949, Camp Hood TX Q-Area** — 8 lights coordinated; Q Area went on alert status; ordnance check ruled out pyrotechnic explanation.
- **24-25 February 1950, Los Alamos** — sustained 24-hour disc/saucer cluster including #178 saucer 100' across at 20-30k ft "as fast or faster than sound", #180 12-witness, #186 14-witness.
- **24 February 1950, Datil NM** — photographed by Cpl Lertis E. Stanfield of Holloman AFB; LaPaz analysis confirmed object was not Moon, Venus, planet, or out-of-focus star (the only photograph in the 116-page file).
- **16 March 1950, Farmington NM** — 10-witness multi-object event; foreshadow of the famous [[1950-farmington-armada]] of 17 March 1950.

## Institutional Response

- **Project Sign** (1948) → **Project Grudge** (Feb 1949) → **Project Twinkle** (initiated late 1949 / early 1950 via Land-Air Inc. contract at Holloman AFB) — successive USAF formal investigations
- **17 February 1949 + 14 October 1949 Los Alamos conferences** with attendees from Fourth Army, AFSWP, UNM, FBI, AEC, UC, USAF SAB, AMC Geophysical Research Division, OSI
- **16 February 1949 Los Alamos conference** — Manhattan Project leadership (Bradbury, Holloway, Manley, Reines, Teller) plus Fourth Army, USAF, UNM, Sandia
- **Teller's analysis**: "they are not material objects passing through the air. We should look to electronics and optics for an explanation."
- **Bradbury's demurral** on the electronic explanation route
- **LaPaz-Crozier-Seely scientific work**: airborne particle collection 1949 recovered anomalous copper-bearing and cobalt-spherical particles; copper is among rarest meteorite elements — LaPaz: "if these particles are truly from the green fireballs, then the fireballs are not conventional meteorites."

## Significance

This corpus is **the foundational evidence base** for the canonical US-government UAP investigation apparatus of the late 1940s. It is the primary historical seed for the [[ufo-nuclear-nexus]] thesis (sustained UAP activity over the US's most sensitive nuclear weapons sites during the immediate post-Trinity / pre-Greenhouse-test period), and it is the institutional context within which Project Sign / Grudge / Twinkle, the AEC's [[atomic-energy-security-service]] surveillance program, and the AFSWP File 333.5 collection system were all stood up.
