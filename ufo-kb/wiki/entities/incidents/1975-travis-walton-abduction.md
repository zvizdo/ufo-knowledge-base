# 1975 Travis Walton Abduction

## Account

On November 5, 1975, a logging crew working in the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest near Snowflake, Arizona, encountered a hovering disc-shaped craft as they drove home at dusk. Crew member [[travis-walton|Travis Walton]] exited the truck and approached the craft. He was struck by a beam, thrown backwards, and went down. The other six loggers — panicking — drove off arguing about whether to return. They returned shortly afterward. The craft was gone. Walton was gone.

Walton reappeared five days later wearing the same clothes, none-the-worse-for-wear, with an account of being aboard a craft, being treated medically (recovered from what would have killed him), and communicating with the entities aboard. His account has remained essentially unchanged for ~50 years.

## Witnesses

- [[travis-walton]] — Primary witness; firsthand abduction account
- Six co-worker loggers — drove away, then returned; all six independently described the beam-strike event identically and passed polygraphs administered after the disappearance. One of the loggers had been in a fistfight with Walton on the day of the event. ([[hal-puthoff]] cites this fistfight detail as evidence against motivated coordination: an antagonist who nevertheless tells the same story is structurally evidence against fabrication.)

## Evidence

- Polygraph passes by all six co-worker witnesses (Walton himself failed an early polygraph and passed a later one; details vary by source)
- Decades-long invariance of Walton's account
- Continued public availability and willingness to be interviewed
- Sustained geographical pattern: the area around the abduction has had frequent UAP sightings

## Aftermath

Walton's case became the subject of the book *The Walton Experience* and the film *Fire in the Sky* (1993). It is one of the most-cited multi-witness corroborated abduction events in the US record.

In [[Gf_tKn9TaP8]], [[hal-puthoff]] tells [[joe-rogan]]: "I think the Travis Walton story is right on. I think that's a solid story." He cites the polygraph passes — particularly the fistfight-antagonist passing — and the unchanged decades-long testimony as the structural credibility markers. Puthoff keeps a Walton bobblehead on his shelf, given to him by Walton.
