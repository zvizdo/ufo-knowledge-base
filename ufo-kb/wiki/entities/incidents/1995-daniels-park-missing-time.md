# 1995 Daniels Park Missing Time

## Account

In 1995, [[john-blitch]] — then a DARPA Program Manager recently married and living in a Denver suburb — took a meditative mountain bike ride at [[daniels-park]], an area he frequented to process the trauma of his volunteer work at the Oklahoma City bombing rubble. The sequence of events:

1. **Anomalous compulsion**: Blitch had ridden past the burial ground many times. On this occasion, he inexplicably turned around and felt an overwhelming obsession to enter it. He climbed over the fence and walked his bike inside (riding in a cemetery felt disrespectful).

2. **Nosebleed onset**: Walking down into the burial ground, a drop fell on his hand. He first assumed he was sweating — but he was going downhill, it was not hot, and he had lived at Front Range altitude for a year. The drop was red. The nosebleed escalated instantly from a drip to a "gusher." There was no physical cause — he had not fallen, was not exerting, had no prior nasal issues.

3. **Immediate loss of consciousness**: He lay down. He was unconscious "within a second." No gradual fade.

4. **Waking disoriented**: He woke freezing, shivering (consistent with lying still at high desert altitude after sunset), and profoundly fatigued — "bonked," as if he had depleted all glycogen — which was inexplicable for what should have been a half-hour 45-minute ride.

5. **Fragmented memory**: The gap contains a fuzzy, dreamlike sequence:
   - Hauling his mountain bike up through scrub oak, scratched, climbing to a mesa
   - Drawn by bright lights he rationalized as a "drilling rig" on the burial ground
   - "Daintily walking" his bike around what appeared to be a **fuel truck**
   - Looking up into the cab at approximately a 20° angle and seeing **a man in a red plaid shirt**
   - At that point, extreme anxiety overtook him — and the memory ends

The total missing time is estimated at ~5+ hours based on the temperature drop and exhaustion state at waking.

## Witnesses

- [[john-blitch]] — sole witness; recounts as "two memories I cannot dismiss as dreams or sleep paralysis"

## Evidence

- **Physiological**: Nosebleed with no mechanical cause; hypothermia-consistent physical state upon waking; extreme fatigue inconsistent with the planned ride
- **Behavioral**: Anomalous compulsion to enter the burial ground — Blitch, as a behavioral scientist, specifically flags this as outside his personal behavioral baseline
- **Physical corroboration**: None confirmed; Blitch notes he has bruises associated with the terror encounter (possibly separate incident — the praying-mantis night), but does not explicitly claim bruises from this event
- **Temporal gap**: ~5+ hours of missing time

## The "Man in the Red Plaid Shirt"

A recurring element in Blitch's account that he specifically highlights as a detail he "was not prepared to talk about in previous interviews." The figure in the cab of the fuel truck is the last conscious memory before the anxiety-triggered cutoff. The "fuel truck" explanation is itself rationalized — Blitch notes he doesn't know why a drilling rig would be permitted on a Native American burial ground, but accepted it in the moment. This is a classic pattern in close-encounter accounts: the witness applies a normalizing explanation to an anomalous stimulus.

## Aftermath

Blitch did not immediately integrate the experience as alien contact. As a behavioral scientist and combat-trained warrior, he attempted to find physical causes — altitude, exertion, blood pressure — and was unable to. Over subsequent years and decades, he placed it in context with his praying-mantis encounter and with the broader abduction literature ([[john-mack]]'s patients, [[betty-hill]], [[barney-hill]]).

He now frames it as a probable abduction consistent with tissue sampling or examination — while explicitly noting he has no memory of anything sexual or of craft.
