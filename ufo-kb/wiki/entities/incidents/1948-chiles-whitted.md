# 1948 Chiles-Whitted Encounter

On July 24, 1948, at 0230 local time over Montgomery, Alabama, Eastern Airlines DC-3 pilots Capt. C.S. Chiles and First Officer John Whitted encountered a cigar-shaped craft with unprecedented structural detail — the most consequential single case for the extraterrestrial hypothesis within [[project-sign]].

## Details

- Object approximately 100 ft long, cigar-shaped
- **Two rows of windows** visible (upper and lower deck), illuminated from within
- Blue fluorescent glow beneath fuselage
- Red-orange flame/rocket exhaust trail at rear
- Observed at 5,000 ft altitude, 20 miles SW of Montgomery
- Object pulled up sharply into cloud upon closest approach to the DC-3
- Passenger Clarence McKelvie corroborated the sighting from a different cabin position
- Multiple independent sightings reported the same night at 0230 from separate locations
- Documented as Incident 144 in [[usaf-blue-book-era-incident-summaries-box-7]]

## Historical Significance

The Chiles-Whitted encounter was the primary evidentiary basis for the Project Sign "Estimate of the Situation" — a classified 1948 document that concluded flying discs were of interplanetary origin. Gen. Hoyt Vandenberg rejected the Estimate and ordered it destroyed. The two-rows-of-windows structural detail was specific enough that it could not be attributed to misidentification of a known aircraft or natural phenomenon.
