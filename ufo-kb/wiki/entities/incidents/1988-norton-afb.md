# 1988 Norton AFB Classified Aerospace Display

## Account

In 1988, industrial designer / technical illustrator **[[brad-sorensen]]** was admitted to a classified aerospace technology demonstration at [[norton-air-force-base]] (San Bernardino, California) in the company of a high-level official (possibly Frank Carlucci or a sitting congressman — not confirmed). The event was organized as a tiered show with public and classified sections.

### Public Section

- Advanced hovercraft technology
- B2 competitor airframe
- **Pulsar** (Lockheed): claimed 120 nuclear warhead capacity, scramjet propulsion — a conventional next-generation weapons platform

### The Curtained Section — "Mama, Baby, and Papa Bear"

Beyond a black curtain, Sorensen observed **three saucer-shaped craft**:

**Physical description:**
- Shape: acorn / inverted jello-mold dome; roughly 30–40 ft diameter
- Finish: metallic, visibly rough — rivets, machining slag, chips
- Construction quality: "poor man's interpretation" of 1947 crash materials, built by scientists, not aerospace engineers
- Referred to informally as "mama, baby, and papa bear" (three size variants)

**Bottom section:**
- Tesla coil-like central mechanism
- Copper plate arrays
- Rotating arm assembly

**Top section:**
- Removable crew cabin
- F-4 Phantom jump-seat controls (retrofitted conventional interfaces)

**Video demonstration shown on-site:**
- Craft hopping across a dry lake bed
- Shooting vertically into the air with zero dust disturbance

### Key Quote

Sorensen's characterization (via [[mark-mccandish]]'s retelling): the craft was described as a "poor man's interpretation" of 1947 crash materials — implying the builders had access to the original recovered craft as a template but could not fully replicate its materials or manufacturing tolerances.

## Witnesses

- **[[brad-sorensen]]** — primary witness; industrial designer admitted to the event; described the craft in detail to McCandish and to Aviation Week journalist Bill Scott
- **[[mark-mccandish]]** — not present but received the full technical description from Sorensen; created formal line art (the **Flux Liner / ARV illustrations**) subsequently presented at the 2001 National Press Club Disclosure event

## Evidence & Corroboration

1. **1990 Aviation Week interview**: Aviation Week editor **Bill Scott** conducted a full interview with Sorensen — the first complete documented account, predating McCandish's 2001 NPC presentation. This interview was independently confirmed in [[nTiFs8LudUo]].
2. **McCandish's technical illustrations**: Published widely and presented at the 2001 NPC Disclosure event alongside other witnesses under [[steven-greer]]'s organizing.
3. **X-Files design contract**: [[mark-mccandish]] was later contracted by Chris Carter to design alien craft for the X-Files pilot — he used the same XF-131 Super Sentinel design, which he had derived from descriptions given by Northrop/Lockheed engineers who described the same program.
4. **[[eric-davis]] refutation**: Davis explicitly disputed ARV claims in a separate interview ([[LnAiNChnuEQ]]), stating he received a classified briefing that no ARVs have been built. This is treated as a contested counter-claim, not a definitive refutation, given Sorensen's 1990 interview pre-dates and is structurally independent of Davis's briefing.

## Aftermath

- Bill Scott (Aviation Week) published the 1990 Sorensen interview — this constitutes a contemporaneous record
- McCandish died in **2020** from an apparently self-inflicted gunshot wound; circumstances remain contested
- The documentary editor involved in releasing related interview materials received **Men-in-Black-type threats**
- [[eric-davis]] publicly disputed ARV claims; [[uap-gerb]] and [[jesse-michaels]] treat Sorensen's account as the stronger evidentiary base given its chronological priority
