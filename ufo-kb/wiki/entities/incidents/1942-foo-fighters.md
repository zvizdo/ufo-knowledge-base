# WWII Foo Fighters

UFO sightings by both Allied and Axis pilots during World War II (approximately 1942–1945), describing small, seemingly controlled luminous objects flying in formation with aircraft. Named "Foo Fighters" after the Smoky Stover comic strip character ("where there's foo, there's fire").

## Characteristics

- Glowing ball-like or disc-like objects
- Flew in formation with aircraft, apparently tracking them
- Neither side claimed them as their own; each side initially assumed they were the enemy's secret weapon
- No reported weapons use by the objects against aircraft
- Described as exhibiting controlled flight — not random atmospheric phenomena

## German Technology Hypothesis

[[nick-cook]] discusses Foo Fighters in the context of the [[kammler-stab]] secret weapons programs. German technology in this era included a device called **"Wind Hound" (Windhund)** — designed to detect the air stream left in the exhaust trail of bombers, allowing tracking of a bomber stream. Cook notes you could assemble a package of contemporary German technologies that could produce a Foo Fighter-like device, "but even doing that today would be challenging."

However, Allied pilot sightings persist without a fully satisfying conventional explanation.

## 1946 Swedish Ghost Rockets

Directly following the Foo Fighter era, [[1946-swedish-ghost-rockets]] (1946) represented a transition to more missile-like unidentified aerial phenomena, suggesting a possible technological evolution — or a different phenomenon altogether.

## Significance

Foo Fighters represent the earliest large-scale, multi-witness UAP sightings in the modern era. They set the preconditions for the wave of flying saucer reports beginning in 1947. The fact that **both sides** encountered them rules out easy attribution to either Allied or Axis programs.
