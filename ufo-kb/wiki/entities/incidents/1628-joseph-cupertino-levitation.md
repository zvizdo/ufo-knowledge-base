# 1628 Joseph of Cupertino Levitation

## Account

In 1628 the Spanish ambassador to Italy visited [[joseph-of-cupertino|Joseph of Cupertino]] in his monastic cell and was sufficiently impressed that he returned with his wife. Joseph entered the church where the couple were waiting. Upon seeing the statue of Mary elevated 10 ft into the air, Joseph "flew over the crowd to the statue, prayed, flew back to the door, and returned home."

The event is described to [[joe-rogan]] by [[hal-puthoff]] in [[Gf_tKn9TaP8]] reading from a written account displayed on screen.

## Witnesses

- Spanish ambassador to Italy (1628) — name not specified in Puthoff's account
- The ambassador's wife
- Other church-goers present in the church that day
- Joseph of Cupertino himself

The Church subsequently took depositions from a number of people who were present that day. Puthoff says the depositions are consistent with one another.

## Evidence

- Multiple consistent witness depositions taken by the Catholic Church
- Records preserved in Inquisition-era ecclesiastical archives that Puthoff says he and colleagues have looked into
- The Catholic Church's institutional behavior — sequestering known levitators in monasteries to keep them out of public view — Puthoff treats as a back-door authentication, since the Church found levitation events disruptive rather than promotion-worthy

## Aftermath

Joseph of Cupertino was eventually canonized in 1767. The 1628 event is one of the most cited specific instances within the broader historical record of his levitation episodes. Puthoff cites it as the highest-quality singleton case because of the social rank of the witnesses (ambassador) and the church-deposition follow-up.

In Puthoff's physics framing (Gf_tKn9TaP8): the only mechanism currently consistent with the physics we know is access to [[zero-point-energy|vacuum energy]] via coherence — i.e., cohering the otherwise-random vacuum fluctuations through some consciousness-state-mediated coupling. "That guy didn't have a nuclear power pack on his back." This places the Cupertino case in the same physics-engineering box as the metric-engineering hypothesis for UAP propulsion.
