# 1973 Sary Shagan Green Concentric-Ring UAP

> *Date approximation: "late Summer 1973" per source. The 15 August 1973 placeholder above is a midpoint for ordering; the document does not specify the exact day.*

## Key Facts

- **Date**: Late summer 1973 (exact day not specified)
- **Location**: [[sary-shagan-test-range]], Site 7 (housing/admin compound, not the launch sites at Site 4 or Site 35)
- **Witness**: Single former Soviet citizen (named in original CIA cable, redacted in released version); the same source later debriefed in Germany as a CIA HUMINT asset
- **Activity at time**: Stepped outside while watching a Canada–USSR televised sport competition (consistent with 1972 Summit Series rebroadcasts or 1973 hockey/figure-skating contest; not specified)
- **Object morphology**: "Sharp (bright) green circular object or mass" — naked-eye observation
- **Bearing/elevation**: West of Site 7 at ~70° angle of sighting
- **Altitude**: Undeterminable to witness; witness "believed that the green mass would have been higher than cloud level" (no clouds present that evening)
- **Behavior**: Stationary point; within 10–15 seconds the green circle widened; "within a brief period of time several green concentric circles formed around the mass"; color faded within minutes
- **Audio**: Silent — "no sound, such as an explosion, associated with the phenomenon"
- **Resolution**: Unresolved; "Source had no opinion as to what this phenomenon was. There was no resultant rumors."

## Significance

- **Eastern counterpart to the green fireballs**: Geographically and politically far from the Lincoln LaPaz [[green-fireballs]] cluster over New Mexico nuclear facilities (1948–1951), but the same green-luminous morphology at the most sensitive Soviet strategic-weapons range. Notable as a Cold War cross-bloc parallel.
- **Co-located with Soviet laser-weapons rumor**: The same CIA report paragraph notes hearsay about laser experiments with "powerful antennas" at the range (later confirmed Terra-3 / 5K17 program). The phenomenon may correlate with — but the report does not assert — an early laser-air-ionization test. Concentric-ring formation could be consistent with a high-altitude beam-induced plasma, but the visible-light power required for the observed ring expansion is not characteristic of any publicly-documented 1973 Sary Shagan system.
- **Only known Sary Shagan UAP report in the US-IC public record (1973)**: Earlier and later Soviet UAP reporting exists in the corpus (cf. [[1994-kazakhstan-uap]] DoS cable, soviet-uap-program corpus generally) but this is the earliest CIA-collected, range-located Soviet UAP datum currently public.
