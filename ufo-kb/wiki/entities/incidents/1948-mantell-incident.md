# 1948 Mantell Incident

On January 7, 1948, National Guard P-51 pilot Thomas F. Mantell (flight leader) climbed in pursuit of a "metallic object of tremendous size" and crashed — the first aviation fatality attributed to a UAP pursuit in US military records.

## Details

- Object described as stationary, white, and "metallic of tremendous size" visible simultaneously across multiple Kentucky locations
- Control tower witnesses at Godman Field: Col. Hix (base CO), Lt. Orner, T/Sgt Blackwell, Capt. Carter, Capt. Duesler — five military witnesses including the commanding officer
- Object tracked from Elizabethtown to Owensboro, Kentucky; corroborated by wide-spread civilian reports
- Mantell's last radio transmission: "I can see the object — it's metallic and of tremendous size"
- Aircraft crashed; debris field analyzed; no recovery of object
- Documented across seven sub-cases (Incidents 33–33g) in the [[usaf-blue-book-era-incident-summaries-box-7]] source record

## Official Explanation and Controversy

The Air Force's eventual official explanation attributed the sighting to the planet Venus and the crash to hypoxia from exceeding operational altitude. Astronomical analysis indicates Venus was at 33° elevation at the time — implausibly bright and distinctive for experienced military pilots to mistake for a "metallic object of tremendous size." The Venus explanation was contested within [[project-sign]] at the time.
