# 1948 Aztec, New Mexico Crash

The Aztec crash refers to an alleged UAP retrieval event said to have occurred on March 25, 1948, near Aztec, New Mexico — approximately four months after the [[1947-roswell]] event and roughly 600 miles from Roswell in the same state.

> Note: This page uses the slug `1947-aztec` to match existing wikilinks across the KB. The actual reported date is 1948.

## The Claim

The primary public source is **Frank Scully's 1950 book** "Behind the Flying Saucers," the first commercially published book to claim the US government had recovered crashed saucers. Scully's sources were primarily **Silas Newton** (a con man) and **Leo GeBauer** — both later convicted of fraud in connection with a related scheme.

The standard claim:
- A disc approximately **99 feet in diameter** came down near Aztec
- The craft was found largely intact with the crew dead from pressure decompression (a porthole was cracked)
- **16 small humanoid entities** (3.5–4 feet tall) were recovered dead
- The craft was removed to [[wright-patterson-air-force-base]] via flatbed truck
- [[detlev-bronk]] (MJ-12 member and biophysicist) allegedly performed autopsies of the recovered entities

## Credibility Assessment

The Aztec case is among the most contested in UFO literature:
- **For authenticity**: Scott Ramsey's multi-year investigation ("The Aztec Incident," 2016) found corroborating witnesses and physical evidence suggesting something unusual did occur
- **Against**: The primary 1950 sources were con artists; the Newton/GeBauer fraud casts doubt on all claims originating from them
- **Detlev Bronk connection**: The autopsy claim comes from the MJ-12 documents (authenticity disputed) and UFO lore, not from Aztec-specific witness testimony

The Aztec crash sits in the KB primarily as a node connecting [[detlev-bronk]], [[majestic-12]], and [[nhi-biologics]] — the claim that a biophysicist of Bronk's caliber was tapped for alien autopsies illuminates the MJ-12 membership logic.
