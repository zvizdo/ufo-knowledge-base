# 1945 USS Delarof USO Incident

The USS Delarof incident is the opening case in [[richard-dolan]]'s USO database — the event that first drew him into the research. The USS Delarof was a US transport ship on a return voyage from Japan to Seattle in summer 1945, passing through the Aleutian Islands near [[adak-island]] (approximately 500 miles from Hawaii in the Pacific, not to be confused with Midway — Adak is the westernmost of the Aleutians).

## Account

Primary witness: [[robert-crawford]] (crew member). Crawford did not himself see the object emerge from the water, but other crew members did — they saw it rise from the sea before he became aware of it.

Crawford's account:
- Object emerged from the water
- Disc-shaped, approximately 200 feet in diameter
- **Circled the transport ship twice** in complete silence
- High-wind conditions prevailed — the object was utterly unaffected, "rock solid, totally stable"
- Departed to the east/southeast at extreme speed
- As it left, a visible flash of light was observed

## Significance

This case establishes several patterns that recur across Dolan's USO database:
- **Water emergence**: the object came from (not merely flew over) the ocean
- **Circumnavigation behavior**: the object circled the vessel — suggesting intentional observation or reconnaissance
- **Atmospheric stability at extreme speed**: unaffected by wind conditions
- **Naval proximity**: a US military transport is the observation platform
- **Silent operation**: no acoustic signature despite the scale

Crawford also spoke with [[james-mcdonald]], a highly credentialed UAP researcher, adding an additional layer of documentation to his account.

## Context

The summer of 1945: the European war had ended; the Pacific war was still ongoing. The ship was transiting from Japan — which implies the Pacific War operational theater. The Aleutians had been a theater of operations against Japan in 1942–1943. Navy vessels were numerous in these waters.
