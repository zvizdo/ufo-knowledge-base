# Skylab Red Light UFO Encounter (~1977)

During a Skylab mission (approximately 1977, likely Skylab 4 or an associated mission), the crew observed a red flashing light that followed their craft for part of their orbit before disappearing. Astronaut [[alan-bean]] photographed the object. The photographs are reportedly publicly accessible, though quality is poor (handheld, through a window, blurry).

[[kevin-knuth]] learned of this account in a personal conversation with Alan Bean.

## Account

A red flashing light appeared in proximity to the Skylab station and paced its orbit. The crew photographed it. The light eventually disappeared.

Knuth's analysis of why this is anomalous: "Nobody puts lights on satellites. First you don't need them and second it's more weight." Additionally, satellites in that era followed fixed predictable orbits — they didn't conduct proximity operations or maneuver alongside other spacecraft. A red flashing light actively following a space station does not match any known satellite behavior.

## Evidence

- Photographs: reportedly public but of poor quality (handheld camera, spacecraft window, motion blur)
- Firsthand account from Alan Bean (Apollo 12 astronaut; 4th human on the Moon) directly to Kevin Knuth

## Omission from Official Record

This encounter was **not included in the NASA UAP Commission report**. Kevin Knuth cites this alongside the Gemini 11 battery drain incident and Story Musgrave's "snakes" report as examples of known astronaut UAP encounters that the official NASA inquiry simply ignored.
