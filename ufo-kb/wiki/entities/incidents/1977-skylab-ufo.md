# Skylab Red Light UFO Encounter (1973, SL-3)

During **Skylab 3** (July–September 1973), approximately one week before splashdown, the SL-3 crew observed an unidentified rotating bright reddish object in a very similar orbit for ~10 minutes. Astronaut [[alan-bean]] (commander) later described this to [[kevin-knuth]] informally (Knuth's recollection placed it "~1977," which is a misremembering of the 1973 mission year). The primary source is the official SL-3 crew debrief, October 4, 1973.

**Primary account from [[owen-garriott]]** (on record, [[nasa-uap-d7-skylab-technical-crew-debriefing-1973]]):
- "Much brighter than Jupiter or any of the other planets"
- Reddish color even well above the horizon
- Rotating: brightness varied with a 10-second period
- Observed ~10 minutes until sunset; followed Skylab into darkness 5-7 seconds later
- Deduced ~30-50 nautical miles from the station; drifted only 10-20 degrees from the wardroom window over 10 minutes (near-identical orbit)
- Never observed on any prior or subsequent orbit
- Identification explicitly requested on the mission record; never publicly provided

**[[alan-bean]]**: "We tried monitors and everything on it but we could never make it into anything other than a bright light."
**[[jack-lousma]]** (first to notice it): "It never did take the shape of an object but it was always brighter than any other star or planet in the night sky."

[[kevin-knuth]] learned of this account in a personal conversation with Alan Bean.

## Account (Knuth's Analysis)

Knuth's analysis of why this is anomalous: "Nobody puts lights on satellites. First you don't need them and second it's more weight." Additionally, satellites in that era followed fixed predictable orbits — they didn't conduct proximity operations or maneuver alongside other spacecraft.

## Evidence

- Photographs: reportedly public but of poor quality (handheld camera, spacecraft window, motion blur)
- Firsthand account from Alan Bean (Apollo 12 astronaut; 4th human on the Moon) directly to Kevin Knuth

## Omission from Official Record

This encounter was **not included in the NASA UAP Commission report**. Kevin Knuth cites this alongside the Gemini 11 battery drain incident and Story Musgrave's "snakes" report as examples of known astronaut UAP encounters that the official NASA inquiry simply ignored.
