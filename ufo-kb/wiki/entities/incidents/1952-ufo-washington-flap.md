# 1952 Washington UFO Flap

July 12–29, 1952. Washington DC and surrounding region. The most prominent and politically consequential UAP wave in US history, occurring directly over the nation's capital and secured airspace around the White House, Congress, and the Pentagon.

**Key events**: On the night of July 19–20, multiple unidentified objects appeared simultaneously on radar screens at Washington National Airport (now Reagan National), Andrews Air Force Base, and Bolling Air Force Base. Intercept jets were scrambled. The objects were seen by civilian and military pilots and tracked on radar. The following weekend (July 26–27), the objects returned and were again tracked on multiple radar systems with visual confirmation.

**Scale and documentation**: The wave produced a multi-sensor observation record (radar + visual + pilot testimony) across multiple independent installations. The Air Force held what was then the largest press conference in its history to address the sightings, offering the explanation of temperature inversions — a conclusion widely contested by radar operators who noted the objects' behavior was inconsistent with temperature inversion returns.

**Consequences**:
- The [[robertson-panel]] (CIA, January 1953) was convened directly in response to the political embarrassment of the Washington flap. Its recommendations included a public debunking campaign and surveillance of civilian UFO organizations — a policy that shaped US UAP secrecy for decades.
- The event influenced [[project-blue-book]]'s analytical posture and the trajectory of official UAP policy.

**In the KB**: The 1952 Washington flap is noted in the context of [[the-nine]] contact claims, which began in 1952; the year's UAP activity (including the flap) is cited as the environment in which those claims emerged.
