# 1979 Vela Incident

On September 22, 1979, US Vela satellite 6911 detected a characteristic "double-flash" — the distinctive signature of a nuclear detonation — over the South Atlantic, near the Prince Edward Islands south of South Africa.

## Key Facts

- Vela 6911 was a nuclear-detection satellite; double-flash detection is the standard atmospheric nuclear test signature
- Location and timing were consistent with a South Africa or Israel nuclear test — or a joint test under the framework of the South Africa-Israel nuclear cooperation widely documented in intelligence records
- A US government panel ultimately concluded the double-flash was probably an instrumentation artifact; critics argued this conclusion was politically motivated to avoid confronting an undeclared ally's test
- The incident has never been definitively attributed or resolved officially
