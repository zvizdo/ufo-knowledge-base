# Worldwide Church of God

Fundamentalist Christian denomination founded by Herbert W. Armstrong in 1934 (originally as the Radio Church of God). Its distinctive doctrines included Saturday Sabbath observance, British Israelism (Anglo-Saxons as the lost tribes of Israel), and apocalyptic prophetic frameworks. At its peak in the 1960s–1980s it had hundreds of thousands of members globally and operated Ambassador College in Pasadena, California. After Armstrong's death in 1986, the church underwent major doctrinal reforms and eventually rebranded as Grace Communion International.

## Role in the Oscar Wolf Account

[[oscar-wolf]] and his commanding officer were both members of the Worldwide Church of God. This shared membership is the trust mechanism that enabled the extraordinary event: in a classified military context where discussing UAP experiences was career-ending and potentially criminal, the commander chose to bring Wolf — a lower-ranking clerk typist — to [[s4]] because he trusted him through their shared religious community.

For Wolf, the experience of encountering a being behind a one-way mirror at S4 was filtered through Worldwide Church of God eschatology:
- The denomination taught that supernatural evil was real and active in the world
- A technologically capable non-human being communicating via telepathy could be categorized as demonic within this framework
- Wolf refused to enter the room — in his account, specifically because he thought the being was demonic
- He maintained silence for decades, never even telling his wife, because the experience disrupted his theological framework in ways he could not reconcile

The WWCOG's apocalyptic framing (end times imminent; secular power structures are Babylon) would also have made Wolf deeply reluctant to tell any authority — secular or religious — what he had seen. The denomination's members were taught to be skeptical of government institutions.
