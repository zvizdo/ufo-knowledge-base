# House Select Committee on Assassinations (HSCA)

The House Select Committee on Assassinations (HSCA) was a special committee of the US House of Representatives established in 1976 and reporting in 1979. It investigated two assassinations: President John F. Kennedy (November 22, 1963) and Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. (April 4, 1968).

**JFK investigation**: The HSCA concluded that President Kennedy "was probably assassinated as a result of a conspiracy" — the only official US government body to reach this conclusion. The committee's conspiracy finding was based primarily on acoustic evidence from a motorcycle dictabelt recording that analysts interpreted as containing four gunshots, including one from the grassy knoll (a second shooter). However, subsequent scientific review (particularly a 1982 National Research Council study) concluded that the acoustic analysis was flawed, and the disputed finding has not been authoritatively resolved.

**MLK investigation**: For the MLK assassination, the HSCA found that James Earl Ray fired the shot that killed King but concluded there may have been a broader conspiracy that Ray was part of — without identifying other specific conspirators.

**Dick Billings connection**: [[dick-billings]] was an investigative journalist who worked with the HSCA as a staff investigator. The committee's investigation is cited in the KB through Billings's involvement and through the broader Kennedy assassination intelligence nexus (CIA involvement, Oswald's Cuba/Soviet connections, the [[1963-jfk-assassination]] evidence).

**Significance**: The HSCA's partial conspiracy conclusion represents a rare instance of Congress officially questioning the Warren Commission's lone-gunman finding, even if the specific acoustic evidence was later challenged. It is regularly cited in both conspiracy theory contexts and serious historical analysis of the Kennedy assassination.
