# NOAA — National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration

US federal agency within the Department of Commerce, responsible for oceanography, weather forecasting, atmospheric science, and ocean resource management. Referred to colloquially as "Noah" by some speakers. The US's primary civilian ocean agency.

## Overview

NOAA runs the country's ocean observation infrastructure: six oceanographic survey ships (under the Oceanographer of the Navy's command during Navy operations), weather satellites, the National Weather Service, and ocean fisheries management. Only ~25% of the US Exclusive Economic Zone seafloor was mapped to modern standard when Tim Gallaudet led NOAA; a National Ocean Mapping Strategy (approved by the White House during the Trump administration) was launched to increase that to ~50%.

NOAA also funds external ocean exploration through partnerships — including [[robert-ballard]]'s Ocean Exploration Trust and NOAA's own Ocean Exploration and Research Program, which operates a dedicated ship and deep-diving ROV.

## UAP Relevance

Gallaudet proposed to NOAA leadership that the agency survey its government ocean data sets for anomalous activity (UAP/USO). NOAA declined: "Too many other things on our plate." This is notable given that NOAA's ocean sensor networks — including sonar-equipped ships, satellite ocean imagers, and hydrographic data — are among the world's most comprehensive ocean observation systems. The refusal means one of the best-positioned agencies to study underwater UAP has not done so.

Gallaudet separately notes that NOAA's data, combined with Navy sonar and NASA ocean-observing satellite imagery, may already contain UAP signatures in the record — they simply have not been searched for systematically.
