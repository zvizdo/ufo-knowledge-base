# Savoia-Marchetti (SIA-Marchetti)

Italian aircraft manufacturer with origins in the 1910s; known for its flying boats, seaplanes, and bomber designs. The Savoia-Marchetti SM.79 "Sparviero" torpedo bomber was one of Italy's primary WWII attack aircraft. The company reorganized multiple times; eventually became SIAI-Marchetti, producing training aircraft and helicopters well into the postwar era.

**1933 Magenta crash storage**: In the UAP/historical context — specifically the [[1933-magenta-crash]] claim documented in the KB — SIA-Marchetti's aerospace hangars near Vergiate (Varese province, Lombardy) are alleged to have been the storage location for the non-human craft recovered by the Italian government following the Magenta crash. After [[benito-mussolini]]'s [[gabinetto-rs33]] (Cabinet RS-33) investigated and recovered the craft, it was reportedly stored at the SIA-Marchetti facility because: (a) the site had appropriate industrial-scale hangars capable of concealing an unusual aircraft-sized object, (b) the aerospace facility provided natural cover for the presence of unusual craft, and (c) SIA-Marchetti had existing relationships with the Italian military and Fascist government.

**Project McGregor target**: The alleged storage of the Magenta craft at SIA-Marchetti's facility made those hangars the target of the OSS [[project-mcgregor]] covert retrieval operation in 1945 — one of the final major OSS operations of WWII. The OSS under [[allen-dulles]], operating from the Swiss station in Bern, had reportedly identified the craft's location through Italian intelligence contacts and sent a team to retrieve it before German forces could evacuate it.

**Chain of custody**: If the SIA-Marchetti account is accurate, the chain of custody of the Magenta craft runs: Italian government (1933) → SIA-Marchetti hangars (1933–1945) → OSS retrieval (1945) → incorporation into US postwar program (post-1945). This is one of the chains the KB documents as potentially connecting the pre-WWII Italian retrieval to the post-WWII US UAP program infrastructure.
