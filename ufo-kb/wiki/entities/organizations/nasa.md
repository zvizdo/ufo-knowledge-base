# NASA

The National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) is the U.S. civilian space agency, established in 1958 and responsible for civilian space exploration, aeronautics research, and Earth science. NASA operates human spaceflight programs, planetary science missions, and a large network of Earth-observing sensors.

**Official UAP stance**: NASA's historical position was institutional distance — neither investigating nor publicly engaging with UAP beyond occasional dismissals. This shifted in 2022 when NASA commissioned an independent study team of 16 scientists to assess what data NASA held that could bear on UAP and how a scientific approach could be applied. The team's September 2023 report found no evidence for extraterrestrial origin but argued NASA's sensor assets (Earth-observing satellites, aircraft instrumentation, future telescopes) are underutilized for systematic UAP study. NASA appointed its first UAP research director, Mark McInerney, though with limited resources.

"Tyler"), described in [[diana-pasulka]]'s *American Cosmic* as a NASA Mission Controller with Space Force connections, is a key KB figure at the civilian-military space interface. This dual reality — NASA's public "no extraterrestrial evidence" stance coexisting with individual NASA personnel who are apparently read into UAP programs — is characteristic of the broader secrecy architecture.

**Carl Wolfe**: [[carl-wolfe]], a NASA technician who worked at Langley, reportedly told colleagues he saw photographic evidence of artificial structures on the moon before dying in an unexplained accident prior to planned public testimony. He represents a pattern of NASA insiders with anomalous knowledge being silenced before disclosure.

**Sensor infrastructure**: NASA's Earth-observing satellite constellation, the Deep Space Network, and the nascent Artemis lunar program all constitute sensing infrastructure that would collect UAP-relevant data. Whether this data is shared with DoD's classified UAP programs is not publicly known.
