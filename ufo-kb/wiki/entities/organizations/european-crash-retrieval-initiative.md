# European Crash Retrieval Initiative

Private citizen-driven crash retrieval team based in Sweden, run under the [[vasco]] infrastructure as a hobby project by [[beatriz-villarroel]]. Project leaders are **Thomas Pinder** and **Alex Tal**.

## Overview

Deliberately stays within Europe for legal simplicity. Maintains a database of tips and cold cases — potential UFO crash sites across European territory — with the aspiration of eventually responding to a "hot case": a fresh crash report allowing real-time investigation.

Villarroel describes it as independent, unfunded (members invest their own money), and deliberately structured to avoid commercial or governmental pressure that could compromise findings or methodology.

## Cold Cases

As of April 2026, the team is working on at least one cold case that Villarroel describes as "super cool, super scary" but will not discuss publicly. She states they have something "particularly interesting right now."

## Methodology

- Crowdsourced tips from members of the public (cold cases)
- Active investigation of candidate sites
- Target: physical material — crash debris, recovered hardware — rather than witness testimony alone
- Hypothesis: European UAP crashes have occurred but documentation has been systematically ignored; investigation by a non-governmental team may surface what official programs have buried

## Significance

ECRI represents a direct, citizen-initiated parallel to government crash retrieval programs (like the alleged US programs Grusch describes). Villarroel's argument: *"We don't want to wait for governments to open up about materials or UFOs. I simply believe that this is a question everyone can work with."*
