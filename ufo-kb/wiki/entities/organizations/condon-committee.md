# Condon Committee

The Condon Committee (formally the Scientific Study of Unidentified Flying Objects, also called the Colorado Project) was a USAF-funded scientific investigation of UFO reports conducted at the University of Colorado (1966–1968), led by physicist Edward U. Condon. The resulting *Scientific Study of Unidentified Flying Objects* (the "Condon Report," 1969) concluded that UFO study offered no scientific value, providing the Air Force with justification to close [[project-blue-book]] in December 1969.

**Mandate and funding**: The committee was commissioned by the Air Force following congressional pressure and public criticism of Project Blue Book's superficial investigations. Physicist Edward Condon, a former Manhattan Project scientist and NIST director, was selected as director. The $525,000 contract was awarded to the University of Colorado in 1966.

**Condon's conclusions vs. case findings**: The Condon Report's famous contradiction: Condon's executive summary concluded that UFO study was not scientifically worthwhile, while approximately 30% of the 90 cases actually analyzed by the committee's scientists were left as "unexplained." The gap between the summary's dismissal and the casework's anomaly rate has been a persistent criticism.

**Falcon Lake case**: The [[1967-falcon-lake]] incident (Stefan Michalak's grid-burn radiation injuries, May 1967) was submitted to the Condon Committee and classified as "unexplained" — one of the cases where the committee's investigators found no conventional explanation satisfying the physical evidence.

**Saunders and Gillmor**: Committee member David Saunders was fired after sending committee documents to UFO researcher James McDonald, who was publicly critical of the committee. Saunders later co-authored *UFOs? Yes!* arguing the committee had predetermined its conclusions before investigation.

**Legacy**: The Condon Report effectively ended official USAF UFO investigations for three decades. Its methodology and executive summary are widely cited in UAP discourse as an example of politically-driven scientific fraud — the [[uap-suppression]] mechanism operating through nominally independent academic review.
