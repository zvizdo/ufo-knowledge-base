# Atomic Energy Commission (AEC)

US civilian agency created by the [[mcmahon-secrecy-act]] (Atomic Energy Act of 1946) to control the development, production, and use of atomic energy and nuclear weapons outside of military control. It was dissolved in 1974 and its functions split between the [[doe]] and the Nuclear Regulatory Commission.

## Classification Regime

The AEC's classification framework — established by the McMahon Act — is the legal structure that [[david-grusch]] and [[bob-maguire]] independently identify as the statute that *automatically classifies* all recovered UAP material. The mechanism: if recovered technology is classified as involving atomic energy or its applications, the AEC (now DOE) classification framework places it beyond normal congressional oversight.

[[edward-condon]] — who later led the [[condon-report]] UFO study — co-authored the McMahon Act.

## EG&G as Prime Contractor

[[egng]] (EG&G) was the AEC's prime contractor, running the Nevada Test Site and providing classified technical services across AEC facilities. This is the root of EG&G's later presence at [[area-51]] — the AEC-to-DOE lineage made EG&G the natural operational arm for the most sensitive black programs.

## Successor

The AEC was succeeded by the [[doe]], which inherited its classification regime and, according to disclosure research, its custodianship over UAP-related recovered materials.
