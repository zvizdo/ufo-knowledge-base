# Lear Corporation

Aerospace company founded by aviation pioneer [[bill-lear]] — inventor of the first practical car radio (for Motorola), the Lear autopilot (which became the standard in commercial aviation), and eventually the Lear Jet, the first commercially successful private business jet. Lear was a singular figure in 20th-century American aviation: serial inventor, Air Force contractor, and close associate of the aerospace contractors named in the [[1971-australian-jio-memo]].

**Anti-gravity connection**: Lear Corporation is named in the [[1971-australian-jio-memo]] — Australian Joint Intelligence Organisation's [[harry-turner]] assessment — as one of the major US aerospace companies actively involved in anti-gravity research programs. Turner's list places Lear alongside other prime contractors as participants in what he characterized as a deeply concealed US program operating behind [[project-blue-book]]'s public UFO-debunking facade.

**Proximity to Townsend Brown**: In the 1950s, Lear Corporation's Santa Monica offices were directly across the street from [[thomas-townsend-brown]]'s Guidance Technologies laboratory. [[bill-lear]] personally witnessed Brown's gravitator experiments — the high-voltage disc experiments that Brown claimed demonstrated a gravitational coupling to electric fields. Lear's geographic and professional proximity to Brown during the critical period of anti-gravity research development makes Lear Corporation a node in the civilian-contractor layer of that program.

**Bill Lear's broader significance**: Bill Lear held the view that anti-gravity research was real and that its results were being suppressed. His witnessing of Brown's experiments gave him a firsthand basis for this belief. The Lear family's UAP interest continued through his son [[john-lear]], who became one of the major figures in 1980s–90s UAP lore — claiming that the US government had retrieved extraterrestrial craft and was concealing their occupants.

**Organizational structure**: Lear Corporation eventually evolved and was absorbed into other aerospace entities; the Lear Jet division was ultimately acquired by Bombardier. The 1950s Lear Corporation of the anti-gravity era is distinct from the later corporate lineage.
