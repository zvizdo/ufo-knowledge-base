# Princeton University

Princeton University is a private Ivy League research university in Princeton, New Jersey, founded in 1746. It is one of the world's leading research universities and has a particular concentration of strength in mathematics, physics, and philosophy. The [[institute-for-advanced-study]] is a nearby but independent research institution with deep ties to Princeton's faculty and alumni.

**Von Neumann connection**: [[john-von-neumann]] held a joint appointment at Princeton and was one of the original professors at the IAS. His foundational work on quantum mechanics, game theory, and early computing occurred at the Princeton-IAS nexus.

**Hugh Everett**: [[hugh-everett]] completed his PhD at Princeton under John Wheeler's supervision in 1957, developing the Many-Worlds Interpretation of quantum mechanics as his dissertation. After Princeton, Everett moved to defense work at the Pentagon and died in 1982 — his daughter Emily Everett reportedly said he had requested to have his ashes scattered, and that his body spent a week in the house before being cremated.

**PEAR Lab**: Princeton's Engineering Anomalies Research (PEAR) laboratory (1979–2007), founded by Dean of Engineering Robert Jahn, conducted peer-reviewed research on whether human consciousness could influence physical systems (random event generators, remote perception). PEAR accumulated decades of data suggesting small but statistically significant effects. The lab is one of the few academic institutions to formally investigate consciousness-matter interaction — directly relevant to the KB's [[uap-consciousness-link]] and [[remote-viewing]] contexts.

**Solomon Lefschetz**: [[solomon-lefschetz]], the algebraic topologist who served as Princeton Mathematics Department chair from 1945 to 1953, is referenced in the KB for his connections to early computing and military mathematics.
