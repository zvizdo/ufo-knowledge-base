# Peoples Temple

The Peoples Temple was an American religious organization led by Jim Jones, founded in Indianapolis in the 1950s and later relocated to San Francisco. It attracted a large multi-racial congregation with a social-justice focus in the 1960s–70s before moving its membership to a compound in Jonestown, Guyana. On November 18, 1978, 918 members died at Jonestown — the largest mass casualty event involving American civilians prior to 9/11 — in a combination of murder and suicide orchestrated by Jones.

**CIA and mind control connections**: Researchers including [[michael-meiers]] and others have argued that Jonestown was more than a religious tragedy — that the compound functioned as an intelligence-sponsored experiment in mass behavioral control, consistent with [[mk-ultra]] research programs into social manipulation, sensory deprivation, and drug-induced compliance. The presence of Leo Ryan (the only US Congressman assassinated in the line of duty) and the systematic nature of the deaths have fueled this analysis.

**Patty Hearst nexus**: The Peoples Temple appears in the [[patty-hearst]] account because her boyfriend, Steven Weed, had been observed at a Peoples Temple location in Ukiah, California, months before the SLA kidnapping. This geographic and social proximity connects two of the most extreme behavioral control cases of the 1970s in the same Northern California radical milieu, which was also a target of [[cointelpro]].

**Sensory deprivation and control**: Jonestown operations included sleep deprivation, public confessionals, punishment structures, and pharmaceutical administration — methods that parallel documented [[mk-ultra]] and sensory deprivation research programs. Whether this was intentional convergence or independent development has not been established.

**Political intelligence**: Jim Jones cultivated relationships with San Francisco political figures including Mayor George Moscone (assassinated days after Jonestown) and Lieutenant Governor Mervyn Dymally. The Temple's intelligence dossiers on members were extensive.
