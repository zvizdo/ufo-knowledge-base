# Apollo Program

NASA's Apollo program (1961–1972) accomplished the first crewed lunar landings, placing 12 astronauts on the Moon across six successful missions (Apollo 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17). The program was initiated under President Kennedy's 1961 commitment to reach the Moon before the end of the decade, motivated partly by Cold War competition with the Soviet Union.

**UAP-adjacent claims**: The Apollo program appears in the KB primarily through claims that astronauts and mission controllers observed anomalous objects during lunar missions, and that NASA suppressed or classified evidence of non-human structures or activity on or near the Moon. These claims range from witness testimonies (Buzz Aldrin's accounts of observations en route to the Moon) to claims about classified photographs withheld from the public record.

**Farooq El-Baz connection**: [[farooq-el-baz]] served as Secretary of the Apollo Site Selection Committee and Supervisor of Lunar Science Operations, overseeing the geological selection of landing sites and managing image archives from the lunar surface. His nickname "the King" — reflecting his control over Apollo imagery — is cited in UAP contexts as meaningful given claims about anomalous content in withheld lunar photography.

**Apollo 13**: The [[1970-apollo-13]] mission — aborted after an oxygen tank explosion on April 13, 1970 — appears in the KB in connection with a contested claim about a nuclear device carried for subsurface seismic experiments and the mission's anomalous survival.

**Lunar seismic experiments**: Apollo missions deployed seismic instruments on the lunar surface. When spent rocket stages were deliberately crashed into the Moon, the seismic instruments recorded the Moon "ringing like a bell" for an unusually long duration — cited by hollow-Moon and anomalous-Moon proponents as evidence of structural anomaly.
