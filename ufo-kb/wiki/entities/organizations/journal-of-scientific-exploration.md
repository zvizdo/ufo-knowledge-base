# Journal of Scientific Exploration

Peer-reviewed academic journal published by the Society for Scientific Exploration (SSE), founded in 1987. The JSE covers empirical research on anomalous phenomena that fall outside the mainstream academic literature — including UAP/UFO research, parapsychology, consciousness anomalies, unusual biological phenomena, and other areas where mainstream journals routinely reject submissions regardless of methodological quality.

**Editorial structure**: The JSE uses standard peer review by a board of credentialed scientists. It maintains academic standards for methodology and citation while being open to anomalous research topics that other journals reject on topic grounds alone. This makes it the primary peer-reviewed venue for rigorous research on topics like remote viewing, UAP physical evidence, and anomalous cognition.

**SSE connection**: The SSE itself was founded by scientist Peter Sturrock (Stanford astrophysicist) and colleagues who believed anomalous phenomena deserved serious scientific attention. The organization holds annual meetings and publishes the JSE.

**KB context**: The JSE is cited in [[iPxbILOMQ08]] in connection with Roger Leir's implant removal research — Leir and colleagues attempted to publish implant findings in the JSE; initial editorial enthusiasm was followed by reversal, which Leir attributed to external pressure. The JSE thus appears in the KB as both an institutional node for anomalous research and as an example of how even peer-reviewed anomalous research venues can face suppression pressure.
