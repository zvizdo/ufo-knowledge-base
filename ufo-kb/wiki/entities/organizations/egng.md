# EG&G

EG&G (Edgerton, Germeshausen and Grier) was a private defense contractor that emerged from the innermost circle of the Manhattan Project and subsequently became the operational gatekeeper for the most sensitive US black programs — including, by multiple accounts, the programs most directly connected to recovered non-human technology.

## Origins and Manhattan Project Ties

The company was co-founded by **[[harold-edgerton]]** (Doc Edgerton), a brilliant MIT scientist and associate of [[vannevar-bush]] — the figure most associated with [[majestic-12]] and the architecture of early post-war secrecy. Edgerton pioneered stroboscopic high-speed photography, a technique that became indispensable for capturing the microsecond dynamics of nuclear detonations — moments during which all electronic sensors are destroyed by electromagnetic pulse.

EG&G was therefore present at, and held proprietary access to, the earliest American nuclear tests — at exactly the moments when [[tag-along-orbs]] are most commonly reported. Their films constitute the primary visual record of nuclear detonations and, implicitly, of anomalous objects monitoring them.

By the 1950s, EG&G was woven into the fabric of the [[atomic-energy-commission]] (AEC), becoming its prime contractor and later the same for the Department of Energy. They essentially ran the Nevada Test Site.

## Bluegill Triple Prime and UAP Filming

During **Operation Dominic** (1962), EG&G crews aboard KC-135 aircraft filmed the [[bluegill-triple-prime]] and related high-altitude nuclear detonations — the tests during which a UAP "tag-along" was allegedly downed by the X-ray pulse and recovered by the US Navy (see [[1962-bluegill-uap-shootdown]]).

EG&G was also deeply involved in managing Sandia National Laboratories, which per [[harold-malmgren]]'s account is where recovered debris from such events was transferred. The combination of proprietary film access + debris management facilities makes EG&G the most plausible single organization with complete knowledge of the nuclear-UAP nexus.

## Cape Canaveral: Prime Contractor

At [[cape-canaveral]], EG&G served as prime contractor responsible for ground maintenance, building management, healthcare clinics, and contractor support services. Every contractor operating at Cape Canaveral — Boeing, Raytheon, Martin Marietta, Lockheed — leased facilities and support through EG&G.

[[greg-rogers]], as chief of aerospace medicine for the [[45th-space-wing]] (1989–~1994), had to oversee EG&G's medical chief and visited roughly 80% of the buildings on the cape under EG&G escort. This arrangement gave EG&G unmatched visibility into all contractor activities — and the ability to compartmentalize them.

Rogers' 1992 sighting of a saucer craft via CCTV feed at Cape Canaveral (see [[1992-cape-canaveral-saucer]]) occurred during an EG&G-escorted tour. Rogers rules EG&G itself out as the craft's owner but acknowledges their fingerprints were everywhere on the cape.

## Area 51 / S4: Operational Gatekeeper

In the early 1960s, EG&G's Special Projects division — based in Las Vegas — was trusted to handle:
- Personnel screening and site access for Area 51 / [[area-51]]
- Transportation and daily operations
- Management of the commuter airline ("Janet flights") flying workers in and out
- Security badge office and access lists

If an employee was hired for any black program at Area 51, their official paperwork and pay often ran through EG&G even if they were working for the CIA or Air Force. EG&G were the operational spine of the facility.

This structure is consistent with [[bob-lazar]]'s account: Lazar states he was hired through [[edward-teller]]'s recommendation and his actual employment was routed through EG&G, who flew him on Janet flights to S4. EG&G has "no records" of Lazar — consistent with the systematic record erasure documented elsewhere in his case.

## Wilson-Davis Meeting at EG&G HQ

In 2002, scientist [[eric-davis]] met Admiral [[thomas-wilson]] (Director of DIA, formerly head of J2/Joint Chiefs) in the parking lot of **EG&G Special Projects headquarters in Las Vegas**. Wilson expressed extreme frustration that a covert UAP reverse-engineering program was being hidden from him despite his position overseeing all US military technical capabilities.

The choice of location — EG&G's own HQ — is notable. The [[wilson-davis-memo]] documenting this meeting is one of the most significant UAP documents in the modern disclosure canon.

## Alfred O'Donnell Testimony (2001)

In 2001, a **senior EG&G manager named [[alfred-odonnell]]** informed [[george-knapp]] that EG&G had recovered a flying saucer in New Mexico and had a live being in possession — a being with humanlike features. This claim, if accurate, confirms EG&G's direct role in crash retrieval handling rather than merely peripheral logistics.

## Underground Operations and Reynolds Electronics

In 1991, a caller to a Las Vegas radio show claimed he was hired (through Reynolds Electronics — an EG&G subsidiary) to run electricity **3,000 feet underground** at a test site. The caller further stated that military personnel were working alongside beings that "resembled aliens." The account is unverified but consistent with the underground-base hypothesis that surfaces repeatedly in EG&G-adjacent testimony.

## Civilian Researcher and Contractor Corroboration (2001)

In 2001, a former Lockheed and CIA contractor went public claiming that Lockheed was working on UFO technology, and that scientists contracted with EG&G were organizing work in private labs in Utah.

## Corporate Trajectory

EG&G was acquired by URS Corporation in 1999. Its special government services division was subsequently sold to URS Federal Services and later became part of DXC Technology / other successors. The institutional knowledge, personnel, and contractual relationships survived the corporate restructuring.

## Lazar Hiring Process (d9tdJ2SkBKQ)

[[edward-teller]] directed [[bob-lazar]] to EG&G Special Projects by phone in 1988. At Lazar's interview at the EG&G office on the McCarran Airport tarmac (Las Vegas), the interviewers initially appeared to be offering one job — then, mid-interview, Lazar stepped out briefly and returned to find the offered position had switched to an S4 assignment. Lazar's Janet flight to S4 departed from the EG&G tarmac at McCarran Airport.

EG&G's "no records" of Lazar is consistent with the systematic credential and employment erasure documented across his case. The company ran the Janet flights that transported all S4 workers from Las Vegas to the facility.
