# Aerospace Corporation

A **Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC)** that primarily supports the **[[nro]]** (National Reconnaissance Office). Aerospace Corporation's campus in Colorado Springs includes secure facilities (SKIFs — Sensitive Compartmented Information Facilities) that house NRO tenant programs, including the specific classified UAP program that [[david-grusch]] and his anonymous government customer (GS-15) operated within.

## Role in UAP Disclosure Chain

[[eric-davis]] was flown to Colorado Springs by Aerospace Corporation's contracting apparatus (Aerospace holds contracts with NRO) to deliver a classified briefing. The audience included:

- [[jay-stratton]] (UAP Task Force leadership)
- [[david-grusch]] (NRO liaison to UAPTF)
- An anonymous GS-15 government customer who oversaw the program (later promoted to SES-1)
- Aerospace Corporation personnel supporting the NRO office

Davis describes the physical arrangement in [[LnAiNChnuEQ]]: a building with SKIFs on entire floors, and office suites built inside SKIFs inside larger SKIFs — indicating highly compartmentalized nested security architecture.

> "It's not one building now it's two and in that one building they have skips built in entire floors and then office suite that are built inside of Skips."

This briefing was the mechanism by which Davis transferred his complete classified dataset (NIDS, BASS, AAWSAP-era, CIA-interaction data) to Grusch, who then used it to identify 40 witnesses.

## General FFRDC Role

As an FFRDC, Aerospace Corporation occupies a hybrid position: it is a non-profit funded primarily by government contracts but operates as a private entity. This structure enables it to:
- Support classified programs (NRO, USAF, others)
- Hire specialized scientists without full civil service constraints
- Conduct research in areas like advanced aerospace, orbital mechanics, and classified reconnaissance
