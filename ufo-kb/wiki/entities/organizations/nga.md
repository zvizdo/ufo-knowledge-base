# National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency

The National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA) is a US intelligence community member agency responsible for geospatial intelligence — the analysis and exploitation of imagery and geospatial information from satellites, aircraft, and other collection platforms to support national security decision-making.

**Mission**: NGA produces maps, charts, imagery products, and geospatial analysis for the US military and intelligence community. It manages massive archives of satellite imagery covering every part of the world at various resolutions, as well as the classified imagery from NRO satellite programs.

**UAP relevance**: NGA's satellite imagery archives would contain any imagery of UAP or anomalous phenomena captured by overhead reconnaissance assets. If classified UAP events have been observed from orbit or from high-altitude reconnaissance aircraft, NGA would manage that imagery. The organization is one of the intelligence agencies that would have been included in any systematic UAP evidence collection.

**[[area-6]] connection**: NGA is referenced in the [[area-6]] context — the classified Nevada test range associated with the UAP-adjacent testing of advanced aircraft and possibly recovered technology. NGA's geospatial function includes analysis of test range activities.

**[[aaro]] interface**: The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office, established by the 2022 NDAA, interfaces with all IC agencies including NGA to access relevant imagery and geospatial data for UAP analysis. NGA's participation in AARO's all-source fusion mission is part of the contemporary UAP oversight framework.
