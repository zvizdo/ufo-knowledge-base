# USS Princeton (CG-59)

US Navy Ticonderoga-class guided-missile cruiser assigned to the USS Nimitz Carrier Strike Group during the November 2004 operations off the coast of Southern California. The Princeton's Aegis SPY-1 radar tracked anomalous contacts — later identified as the Tic-Tac object — for approximately two weeks before the key intercept by [[david-fravor]].

Princeton radar operators provided real-time guidance to Fravor's F/A-18, vectoring him to the intercept point. After Fravor's engagement, Princeton CIC tracked the object as it accelerated from the intercept location to the pre-briefed CAP point — a pre-planned rendezvous coordinate known only to the pilots, suggesting the object had access to the classified flight plan or the ability to intercept communications.

Princeton's radar corroboration is central to the credibility of the [[2004-nimitz-tic-tac]] case: independent ship-borne tracking across a multi-week window rules out pilot misidentification as the sole explanation.
