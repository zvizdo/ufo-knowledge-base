# Wall Street Journal

Major U.S. newspaper. In the context of this KB, notable for a 2024 article by [[joel-shectman]] sourced from former [[aaro]] director [[sean-kirkpatrick]], claiming a non-nuclear EMP test device caused the 1967 Malmstrom missile shutdowns.

## The 2024 Article

The article advanced two explanations for all UFO mythology at nuclear sites: (1) a secret EMP test device; (2) Air Force hazing rituals. Primary sources include [[sean-kirkpatrick]] and [[susan-goff]] — a Booz Allen Hamilton psyops consultant whose conflict of interest was not disclosed.

[[robert-salas]] filed a 14-point rebuttal demonstrating that:
- The cited EMP model didn't exist until 1971–73
- The sequential (not simultaneous) shutdown pattern is physically incompatible with EMP
- Boeing engineers found no explanation even for a single guided-system failure via external signal
- The missiles came back online — inconsistent with EMP physics

[[jesse-michaels]] treats the article as a paradigmatic example of how [[ufo-secrecy-cabal]] operates through legitimate media: a credible outlet + a named government source = a citable debunking that can be referenced indefinitely, regardless of its factual accuracy.

## Suppression of the Carl Nell Op-Ed

While simultaneously publishing the Shectman article, the WSJ's opinion section rejected an op-ed co-authored by [[carl-nell]] (Army UAP Task Force representative) and an unnamed former cabinet-level official, expressing frustration at the lack of civilian oversight of UAP programs. [[jesse-michaels]] met with WSJ managing editor [[lena-bell]] to advocate for the piece; she was apparently "powerless" to advance it. [[kirk-mcconnell]] (Senate Intelligence Committee staffer) is also aware of this op-ed.

## David Spergel as De Facto UFO Gatekeeper

[[lena-bell]] told Michaels that [[david-spergel]] — astrophysicist, Simons Foundation president, NASA UAP working group chair — is the WSJ's de facto adviser on UFO topics. Spergel's stated position ("space trash, light reflections, temperature inversions") is functionally identical to [[donald-menzel]]'s 1950s debunking formula.
