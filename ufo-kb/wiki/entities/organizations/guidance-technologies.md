# Guidance Technologies

[[thomas-townsend-brown]]'s Santa Monica, California-based anti-gravity and electrostatics research company. The company was funded exclusively by [[floyd-odlum]] — a prominent aerospace lawyer and investor who was also the majority owner of Northrop (before its merger with Grumman).

## Operations

During its operation, Brown used Guidance Technologies as the base for his most intensive anti-gravity electrostatics research. While running the company, Brown met and discussed his work with:
- [[bill-lear]] (aviation pioneer; had adjacent Santa Monica offices)
- Former Air Force Chief of Staff [[curtis-lemay]]
- The Rand Corporation
- Other aerospace Titans

## Closure and Aftermath

In fall 1967, Brown **shut down Guidance Technologies with no public explanation**, stating he had obtained "no results." Three months later — winter 1968 — Northrop (Odlum's primary corporate interest) published a paper on **"Electro Aerodynamics and Electrogravitics and Its Implications for Aviation"**. The term "electrogravitics" was at that time used exclusively to describe Brown's work.

[[nick-cook]]'s interpretation: Brown's IP was **covertly transferred to Northrop**, and the company's closure was the mechanism by which that transfer occurred while maintaining plausible deniability for Brown. Odlum's ongoing monthly payments of $2,500 to Brown's wife Josephine "for the rest of her life" suggest a continuing contractual obligation — either licensing or confidentiality.

## Significance

Guidance Technologies represents the apparent handoff point between Brown's independent electrogravitic research and Northrop's classified development of what [[bill-gunston]] later identified as the B2 stealth bomber's anomalous propulsion system.
