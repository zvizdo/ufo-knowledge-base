# National Security Council

The National Security Council (NSC) is the principal executive branch forum for considering national security and foreign policy matters with the President. Established by the [[national-security-act]] of 1947, it is chaired by the President and includes the Vice President, Secretary of State, Secretary of Defense, and other senior officials. The National Security Advisor leads the NSC staff and coordinates interagency policy.

**Structure**: The NSC itself is the cabinet-level body; the NSC staff (several hundred officials) supports it. The National Security Advisor (currently or recently: [[jake-sullivan]], before him H.R. McMaster, Michael Flynn, Susan Rice) is the President's primary national security coordinator.

**UAP relevance**: Any executive branch decision about UAP disclosure would flow through or be blocked at the NSC level. The NSC has the authority to task intelligence agencies for briefings, coordinate interagency responses, and recommend policy to the President. The claim that [[jake-sullivan]] attended UAP briefings and chose not to act situates the disclosure decision explicitly at the NSC level — below the President but above any individual agency.

**NSC meetings with tech sector**: The Andreessen-Horowitz account of a senior NSC official acknowledging classified physics programs ([[andreessen-horowitz]]) places the NSC as the executive node closest to any acknowledged classified UAP/physics program.
