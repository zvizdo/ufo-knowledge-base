# The Galileo Project

The Galileo Project was founded in July 2021 by Harvard astrophysicist Avi Loeb following the government's release of the UAP Task Force Preliminary Assessment. Loeb argued that the scientific community had failed its responsibility by ceding UAP investigation to governments and intelligence agencies, and that a rigorous, hypothesis-neutral, instrument-based program was needed. The project is headquartered at Harvard University and funded entirely by private donations, explicitly to avoid government entanglement and classification constraints.

The project operates on three research tracks: deploying a network of ground-based sensor stations (wide-field cameras, audio detectors, radio receivers) to detect and characterize UAP passing overhead; analyzing the trajectory and properties of interstellar objects like 'Oumuamua and Borisov for signs of artificiality; and retrieving and analyzing physical material from potential interstellar meteor impacts. The first Galileo Project observatory was installed on the Harvard campus in 2022. Loeb's approach is explicitly distinguished from advocacy: he proposes hypotheses and gathers data rather than asserting conclusions, which has drawn both praise from mainstream scientists and criticism that the project legitimizes fringe claims.

## Sensor Stack and Technical Details (_bM9eAtCOGw)

From Loeb's direct description in [[_bM9eAtCOGw]]:

- **Sensor types**: optical, infrared, radar, audio, magnetic — five independent detection channels
- **Location**: mounted on the roof of Harvard College Observatory (the Harvard campus observatory)
- **Staffing**: 100+ scientists engaged as of the time of the interview
- **Funding**: thousands of emails of support; privately funded (explicitly to avoid government entanglement and classification constraints)
- **AI classification**: algorithms classify detected objects as bird, drone, or "something else" — the third category is what they're looking for
- **Data policy**: fully public, fully transparent — distinguishing factor from all government programs

The array runs as a continuous passive observatory. The government has sensors monitoring US airspace constantly but they are not designed for scientific investigation and their capabilities are classified. The Galileo Project fills the gap with open, instrument-grade data.

The project's three research tracks:
1. Ground-based sensor detection and classification of UAP overflights
2. Interstellar object characterization (anomalous objects like [[2017-oumuamua]])
3. Recovery and physical analysis of interstellar meteorite material
