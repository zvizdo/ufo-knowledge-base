# False Memory Syndrome Foundation

The False Memory Syndrome Foundation (FMSF) was founded in 1992. On the board were [[mk-ultra]] psychiatrists [[jolly-west]] and [[martin-orne]]. The publicly stated mission was to combat the perceived epidemic of false memories of childhood sexual abuse being induced by therapists, particularly in the context of dissociative identity disorder (DID) cases.

## The Mixed Motives (per [[colin-ross]])

Ross identifies at least five distinct groups whose interests converged in the FMSF:

1. **Memory researchers** who believed dissociative amnesia was inconsistent with their scientific model of memory
2. **Wrongly accused individuals** — parents who had been falsely accused of incest and needed an advocacy organization
3. **Actual perpetrators** needing a legal defense ("it was false memories")
4. **Anti-Freudian academics** who wanted to shut down what they saw as pseudoscientific therapy trends
5. **[[jolly-west]] and [[martin-orne]]**: covering up the Manchurian Candidate programs, because therapists were beginning to hear stories from DID patients about **being taken to military bases, being spun in vestibular disorientation devices, undergoing experimental alter-creation procedures** — and documenting this in clinical settings

## Why the Cover-Up Was Necessary in 1992

By 1992, the MK Ultra record had been partially exposed (Church Committee, 1975; Rockefeller Commission), but the ongoing clinical manifestations of the programs were starting to surface in therapist offices:
- DID patients with elaborate military-location memories
- Reported vestibular disorientation experiments
- Accounts of laboratory procedures for creating alternate personalities

The FMSF provided a mechanism to discredit these accounts as "false memories" induced by overzealous therapists, without requiring specific denial of specific events.
