# Andreessen Horowitz (a16z)

Major Silicon Valley venture capital firm co-founded by [[mark-andreessen]] and Ben Horowitz in 2009. One of the most influential VC firms in technology, with investments in Meta, Twitter/X, GitHub, Lyft, Airbnb, and hundreds of other companies. Known internally as a16z (for the 16 letters between A and Z in "Andreessen").

**NSC meeting — classified physics admission**: In the KB (via [[LpLFWdsIU7M]]), Marc Andreessen attended a meeting with senior National Security Council officials. In that meeting, a senior official acknowledged to Andreessen that "entire physics areas had been classified before" — in the context of a discussion about advanced technology. Physicist david pines (who cites this account) treats it as the most significant on-record admission from a senior official confirming that classified physics programs exist beyond the publicly known scientific literature.

**What the admission implies**: If entire physics areas have been classified, the public scientific literature is not a complete record of what is known. The [[reverse-engineering-hypothesis]] requires exactly this: that recovered non-human technology has generated a classified physics knowledge base that has diverged from public science since at least the 1950s. An acknowledgment at NSC level — to a tech billionaire rather than a formal disclosure proceeding — suggests the classification is acknowledged within government even while publicly denied.

**KB significance**: The a16z NSC meeting account is part of a pattern in which senior government officials make semi-informal admissions to high-status civilian tech figures rather than through formal disclosure channels. This parallels the [[soft-disclosure-civilian-observation]] pattern — knowledge trickling through private-sector connections before any official acknowledgment.

**Silicon Valley–government nexus**: a16z operates at the intersection of technology investment, AI policy, and national security. The firm has been publicly engaged with US government technology policy, DARPA relationships, and dual-use technology investment. This makes Andreessen's NSC-level access unsurprising and the classified physics acknowledgment plausible as an informal briefing context.
