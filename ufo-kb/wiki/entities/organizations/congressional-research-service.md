# Congressional Research Service

The Congressional Research Service (CRS) is a federal legislative branch agency that provides nonpartisan policy, legal, and analytical research to members of Congress and congressional committees. Operating since 1914 under the Library of Congress, the CRS produces confidential memos and publicly available reports covering virtually every area of law and policy, written to be accurate, objective, and politically neutral.

As UAP became a legislative priority following the 2017 [[aatip]] revelations and the 2021 UAPTF Preliminary Assessment, the CRS produced several analyses covering the legal framework for UAP disclosure, the history of DoD UAP investigation programs, the scope of congressional oversight authority over classified special access programs, and the provisions of the National Defense Authorization Acts (NDAAs) that mandated [[aaro]]'s creation and reporting requirements. These reports have been used by congressional staffers and members to frame legislation and oversight hearings.

**Significance in the disclosure context**: The CRS's involvement signals that UAP has matured from a fringe topic into a mainstream legislative policy area requiring serious legal and budgetary analysis. CRS reports are written for congressional members who are not specialists — they provide the legal and historical background needed to ask informed questions in hearings and draft effective legislation. When the CRS begins producing UAP reports, it means the legislative branch's professional research staff has concluded that UAP oversight is a durable responsibility, not a temporary media cycle.

**Oversight authority**: CRS reports on UAP have specifically addressed congressional authority over classified Special Access Programs (SAPs), which is the relevant mechanism: if UAP programs exist under SAP classification, Congress has statutory authority to compel access. The CRS analyses help establish that legal framework for members pursuing oversight.
