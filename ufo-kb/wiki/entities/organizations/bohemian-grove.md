# Bohemian Grove

A private retreat in Monte Rio, Northern California, owned by the Bohemian Club of San Francisco. Every summer, ~2,000 prominent American men — business leaders, politicians, policy decision-makers — gather for approximately two weeks. The Grove is notable for extreme secrecy and for hosting the annual **Cremation of Care** ritual.

## Cremation of Care Ritual

The Cremation of Care is a midsummer theatrical/ritual performance staged at the artificial lake inside the Grove. Participants in robes carry an effigy to a large stone owl (commonly identified as "Moloch") where the effigy is burned. The official framing is that the ritual symbolizes burning away worldly cares for the duration of the summer gathering.

[[alex-jones]] infiltrated the Grove in 2000 and captured the ritual on video. His firsthand account (in [[KhmUixSXxDo]]):

> "They do this composite pagan ritual. It was a fusion of Moloch worship — the bull out of the Middle East, Tyre and places — and Faustian occultism and Druidic occultism. The men had really crazy looks on their faces. I would call it satanic."

Jones explicitly rejects the "it's just a scapegoating of pagan ritual by Christianity" framing, saying the participants were "happily indulging" in it and taking it "extremely seriously."

## Elite Membership

Members and attendees have historically included US presidents, cabinet members, military officials, CEOs, and media executives. The Grove operates on a strict no-women, no-reporting, no-phones basis. The Manhattan Project was allegedly planned there in 1942.

> ⚠ The Grove's own framing (displacing pagan tradition with Christian one) directly contradicts Jones's firsthand characterization of sincere engagement with the pagan ritual.

## Connection to Secret Society Network

Jones places Bohemian Grove in a nested hierarchy of elite coordination alongside [[bilderberg-group]] and [[world-economic-forum]]. He does not claim all attendees are malevolent ("most of them are good"), but does characterize the steering group and ritual as reflecting values at odds with child-protective, civilization-building ethics.
