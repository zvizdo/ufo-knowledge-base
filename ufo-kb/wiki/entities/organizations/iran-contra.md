# Iran-Contra Affair

The 1986–1987 Reagan-era scandal in which National Security Council staff secretly arranged weapons sales to Iran (circumventing an arms embargo) and diverted the proceeds to fund Nicaraguan Contra rebels (circumventing the Boland Amendment's prohibition on such funding). A central case in [[danny-sheehan]]'s legal career.

The [[christic-institute]] filed the Iran-Contra case as a **RICO civil complaint** — the first application of the Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations statute to a national security matter. The complaint named [[theodore-shackley]] and 28 other CIA officials and private contractors as a criminal enterprise. The case was ultimately dismissed and sanctions of $1.2 million were imposed by Judge James Lawrence King, forcing the Christic Institute into bankruptcy.

Sheehan views the sanctions as a coordinated institutional response: the RICO structure threatened to produce discovery of documents exposing a network of covert operators — documents the [[controlled-disclosure-campaign-act]]'s subpoena mechanism now seeks through legislative means.
