# Warren Commission

The government commission established by President Lyndon Johnson on November 29, 1963 to investigate the assassination of President John F. Kennedy. Chaired by Chief Justice Earl Warren, with a membership that included future president Gerald Ford, Senator Richard Russell, and — most controversially — [[allen-dulles]], the CIA Director Kennedy had fired after the Bay of Pigs disaster.

The Commission concluded in September 1964 that Lee Harvey Oswald acted alone and that there was no evidence of conspiracy. Its methodology and conclusions have been disputed by subsequent investigators, the House Select Committee on Assassinations (1979), and the ongoing release of classified JFK assassination files under the JFK Records Act.

**Core conflicts of interest**: Dulles, fired by Kennedy and with institutional motivation to cover CIA involvement, was placed on the commission investigating Kennedy's death. The CIA withheld information about its relationships with Oswald, its anti-Castro operations, and the 1963 assassination plots against Castro that were active at the time of the Kennedy assassination.
