# SRI International

Independent research and development organization based in Menlo Park, California. Formally separated from Stanford University in 1970. Covers a wide range of scientific and policy research; in the UAP/psi context, SRI International is primarily known as the site of the government-funded remote viewing research program of the 1970s and early 1980s.

**Remote Viewing Program (1972–1985)**: Under [[hal-puthoff]] and [[russell-targ]], SRI International hosted CIA and DIA-funded research into remote viewing — the alleged ability to describe distant locations or events without conventional sensory access. The program operated under various names (SCANATE, GONDOLA WISH, GONDOLA PRIZE) before its results were taken operational by the Army's Intelligence and Security Command (INSCOM) as Project STARGATE.

**Key figures developed at SRI**: [[ingo-swann]] was the primary experimental subject who developed the operational remote viewing protocols; [[joseph-mcmoneagle]] was trained through SRI before becoming the Army's lead operational viewer. The SRI research produced the structured methodology that operationalized remote viewing for intelligence collection.

**Doug Engelbart connection**: [[doug-engelbart]] — inventor of the computer mouse and hypertext, developer of NLS — worked at SRI International; his Augmentation Research Center was the laboratory where ARPANET's first node was connected. The co-location of Engelbart's human-computer interaction research and Puthoff's psi research at the same institution is noted in the KB as part of the broader pattern of advanced consciousness research at DARPA/CIA-adjacent institutions.
