# Institute for Field Physics

The Institute for Field Physics at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill was established through the patronage of industrialist [[agnew-bahnson]], who was deeply interested in anti-gravity and gravity modification research in the 1950s. Bahnson funded the institute and organized the 1957 gravity research conference at Chapel Hill — one of the most significant gatherings of anti-gravity-oriented physicists in the early Cold War period.

**1957 gravity conference**: The conference brought together physicists, engineers, and defense-adjacent researchers to explore the possibility of gravity modification and propulsion. The gathering was part of a broader 1950s movement — involving [[thomas-townsend-brown]], defense contractors like Glenn L. Martin Company, and aerospace researchers — to investigate gravity control before the more conservative mainstream physics community definitively rejected [[electrogravitics]] as a serious research direction.

**Bahnson's fiction parallel**: [[agnew-bahnson]] wrote the novel *[[stars-are-too-high]]* — which describes rogue Nazi scientists building man-made flying saucers using anti-gravity to stage a fake alien invasion. The institute, the conference, and the novel together make Bahnson an unusually self-aware node in the anti-gravity/UAP nexus: he was simultaneously funding genuine research, engaging in speculative fiction, and apparently anticipating the same strategic questions that would occupy UAP disclosure researchers decades later.

**Physics community reception**: The 1950s gravity research wave was largely dismissed by mainstream physics as pseudoscience. The mechanisms proposed (electrogravitics, unified field theory in Bahnson's framework) were not validated experimentally. However, the institutional interest from defense contractors suggests the military applications were taken seriously even while the physics remained unresolved.
