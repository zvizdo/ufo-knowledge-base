# Convair

Consolidated Vultee Aircraft Corporation, later operating as the Convair Division of General Dynamics. Major US aerospace and defense manufacturer based in San Diego and Fort Worth, active from 1943 through the Cold War period. Built the B-36 Peacemaker (first intercontinental bomber), B-58 Hustler (first supersonic bomber), Atlas ICBM (first US operational ICBM), and F-102/F-106 interceptors.

**UAP relevance**: In 1971, [[harry-turner]] — head of the nuclear division of Australia's [[australian-joint-intelligence-organisation]] — authored a classified internal assessment concluding that the United States was concealing anti-gravity and UAP programs behind [[project-blue-book]]'s public debunking facade. Turner explicitly named Convair, along with [[bell-aircraft]], [[lear-corporation]], and [[martin-corporation]], as participants in what he described as an "anti-gravity Manhattan Project" analog — a secret US program to understand and replicate UAP propulsion.

**Context**: Convair's deep involvement in both propulsion (Atlas ICBM) and aerodynamics (supersonic flight) gave it the technical profile consistent with participation in advanced propulsion research. Whether Turner's assessment was accurate or speculative is unknown; no Convair internal documents corroborating anti-gravity research have been made public.
