# Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory

The Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory (SAO) is a research institute of the Smithsonian Institution, established in 1890 and headquartered in Cambridge, Massachusetts (co-located with Harvard University). In 1973, SAO merged with the [[harvard-observatory]] (Harvard College Observatory) to form the Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian — one of the largest astrophysics research organizations in the world.

**Operation Moonwatch connection**: The SAO played a central role in organizing [[operation-moonwatch]] (1956–1975), a worldwide civilian volunteer network for optically tracking satellites. The program — initiated before the first Sputnik launch to establish baseline satellite-tracking capacity — was one of the largest citizen science programs in US history and provides a documented precedent for civilian-network sky surveillance that UAP researchers cite as proof of concept for contemporary citizen observation programs.

**Fred Whipple**: [[fred-whipple]], director of SAO from 1955 to 1973, was the primary architect of Operation Moonwatch and a central figure in satellite-tracking policy. His administrative role bridged the Harvard astronomical tradition and the Smithsonian's national-observatory mission.

**Modern role**: Today, the Center for Astrophysics (CfA) at Harvard-Smithsonian is a primary institutional home for astronomical survey programs, high-energy astrophysics, and — through [[avi-loeb]] and the [[galileo-project]] — one of the few major astronomy institutions to have formally engaged with the UAP question.
