# GEIPAN

France's official government UAP investigation body, housed within CNES (the French national space agency). Vallée describes it in JRE #2288:

> "France has had an official project on UFOs for a long time that takes reports from the public and investigates them. It's a very small team but they have access to all the resources of French research — weather data, radar, everything."

The agency operated under three names: **GEPAN** (1977–1988), **SEPRA** (Service d'Expertise des Phénomènes de Rentrées Atmosphériques, 1988–2005), and **GEIPAN** (2005–present). [[jean-jacques-velasco|Jean-Jacques Vélasco]] headed SEPRA and co-authored the 1999 [[cometa-report|COMETA Report]] that drew heavily on SEPRA's 4-category classification system and its 500+ aeronautical UAP case database.

GEIPAN is unique among Western government UAP bodies in that it is officially funded, publicly acknowledged, and has published declassified investigation files. Its scope is limited but its access to government infrastructure (meteorological services, radar data, military reports) gives it investigative tools unavailable to civilian researchers.

Vallée was not officially part of GEIPAN but was connected to French UAP research. The Valensole case ([[1965-valensole-case]]) was investigated by five French government agencies — partly through mechanisms like GEIPAN's predecessor.
