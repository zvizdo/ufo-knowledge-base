# JANET Airlines

Informal name for the classified commuter airline operating under contract to the US Air Force that transports government employees and contractors between Las Vegas Harry Reid International Airport (formerly McCarran) and classified Nevada Test and Training Range facilities — primarily [[area-51]] (Groom Lake). Officially operated by AECOM (formerly EG&G); flights depart from a restricted, unmarked terminal on the west side of the Las Vegas airport. Aircraft are unmarked white commercial jets with a distinctive red stripe. "JANET" is unofficially said to stand for "Just Another Non-Existent Terminal" or similar — the government did not officially acknowledge its existence until after decades of public speculation.

**Operational structure**: JANET flights are scheduled daily operations — a regular commuter service for the thousands of workers who maintain Area 51's facilities and classified programs. Workers cannot discuss their destination, and flight schedules are not published publicly. The existence of a dedicated classified commuter airline operating from a major commercial airport with unmarked aircraft is itself an indicator of the scale of classified activity at the facilities served.

**Bob Lazar corroboration**: [[bob-lazar]]'s specific claim that he commuted to [[s4]] via JANET from Las Vegas — a detail he described in 1989 before JANET's existence was publicly acknowledged — was a key element of his credibility assessment. Skeptics at the time could not verify or refute this specific operational detail without security clearances. When JANET's existence was subsequently confirmed through aviation research, accident records, and partial government acknowledgments, it validated that Lazar knew accurate operational details of classified Nevada facility transport. This is the [[credibility-frameworks]] pattern: a specific, verifiable operational claim proven accurate before general acknowledgment.

**Scale and geography**: JANET also serves [[dugway-proving-grounds]] and [[nellis-test-range]] facilities. The operation of a classified airline as a routine mass-transit service reflects the scale of classified workforce at these sites — the programs are too large to conceal individual commuters and instead require industrial-scale logistics that are themselves classified.
