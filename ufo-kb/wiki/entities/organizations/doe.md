# Department of Energy (DOE)

The US Department of Energy was established in 1977, absorbing the functions of the [[aec]] (Atomic Energy Commission, 1946–1974) and its successor the Energy Research and Development Administration. The DOE controls the US nuclear weapons complex — including [[los-alamos]], Sandia, Lawrence Livermore, and Oak Ridge — through its semi-autonomous National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA).

## Role in UAP Classification

The DOE's predecessor AEC created the [[atomic-energy-act-classification]] framework in 1946. The Atomic Energy Act (1946, revised 1954) classifies all "restricted data" — broadly defined as any information about nuclear materials, nuclear weapons design, and — crucially — *any other phenomenon that utilizes nuclear processes or produces effects consistent therewith*. This last clause is the legal hook cited by researchers (including [[bob-maguire]] and [[danny-sheehan]]) as the mechanism for classifying recovered UAP materials: if a recovered propulsion system involves processes that resemble nuclear or sub-nuclear physics (which virtually any exotic physics would), the AEC/DOE classification framework can be applied without explicitly acknowledging the UAP origin.

## Internal Structure (Larson Firsthand, 2005–2008)

[[ralph-moat-larson]], DOE Director of Intelligence and Counterintelligence 2005–2008, describes DOE as three functionally distinct pillars:

1. **Energy** — Renewables, nuclear power, advanced energy research including gravity and magnetics
2. **Pure Science** — National Laboratories doing basic and theoretical research; chaos theory, quantum computing, exotic physics (Larson: "I've met some of the most fascinating people who do things like chaos theory")
3. **NNSA (National Nuclear Security Administration)** — Semi-autonomous republic within DOE; manages the nuclear warhead stockpile and nuclear weapons complex. NNSA was given quasi-independent status within DOE due to a period of dysfunction

The National Laboratory complex serves all three pillars in varying proportions.

### Larson's UAP Testimony

Despite his role as Director of Intelligence — which he describes as the apex of intelligence access within the agency — Larson says he was **never briefed on UAP incursions at nuclear sites** during 2005–2008. He offers two possible explanations:
- The information was held in a separate compartment that he wasn't individually read into ("someone will tap you on the shoulder")
- There is less to it than claimed

He explicitly confirms the deep-compartment model: "There are very few people that know a lot about all the most important things because they're divided." He accepts in principle that UAP programs could exist within DOE without an intel director knowing.

Notably: **NNSA scientists attended the Soul Foundation** (a Stanford-affiliated UAP research organization) — suggesting even official actors within the nuclear complex are engaging with UAP questions outside official channels.

## Battelle Memorial Institute Connection

The DOE's predecessor AEC contracted [[battelle-memorial-institute]] for materials analysis throughout the Cold War. Battelle is alleged to have conducted early analysis of recovered Roswell-related materials ([[1947-roswell]]) under AEC classification. The DOE/Battelle relationship is documented in unclassified contracts; the alleged Roswell analysis is not confirmed in primary sources.
