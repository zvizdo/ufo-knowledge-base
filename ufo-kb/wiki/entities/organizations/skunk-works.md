# Lockheed Skunk Works

The Skunk Works is the advanced development programs division of [[lockheed]] (now Lockheed Martin), founded in 1943 by aeronautical engineer Clarence "Kelly" Johnson in Burbank, California. It is the prototype institution for classified aerospace development — small teams of top engineers with minimal bureaucracy and maximum access to classified resources. The name comes from the "Skonk Works" moonshine still in the Li'l Abner comic strip.

**Major programs**: The Skunk Works produced some of the most advanced aircraft in history, often decades ahead of publicly known capabilities:
- **U-2 spy plane** (1955): high-altitude reconnaissance aircraft whose overflights of the Soviet Union led to the 1960 Gary Powers incident
- **SR-71 Blackbird** (1966): Mach 3+ reconnaissance aircraft that remains one of the fastest air-breathing aircraft ever built
- **F-117 Nighthawk** (1981): world's first operational stealth aircraft, kept classified until 1988 and operated from [[tonopah-test-range]]
- **F-22 Raptor** and **F-35 Lightning**: fifth-generation fighters
- **Various classified programs**: ongoing black-budget aircraft development

**UAP relevance**: The Skunk Works is central to UAP reverse-engineering claims for two reasons. First, its demonstrated history of producing aircraft with capabilities far beyond anything publicly acknowledged provides a template for how classified technology development at contractor level could remain hidden for decades. Second, multiple UAP researchers and whistleblowers have specifically named Lockheed Skunk Works facilities as locations for UAP material storage and reverse engineering.

**Ben Rich statement**: Lockheed Skunk Works director Ben Rich (Kelly Johnson's successor) reportedly made statements near the end of his life suggesting that "we have the means to take ET home" and that "anything you can imagine we already know how to do." Rich died in 1995; the authenticity and meaning of these statements is disputed.
