# Caroline Group

A private intelligence and finance consortium that operated from the yacht **Caroline** — owned by **Eldridge Reeves Johnson**, whose Victor Talking Machine Company was the predecessor of RCA Records. The group functioned as "a private enterprise intelligence operation formed between people with very high-level corporate capital interests to foster across the globe."

## Origins and Structure

The Caroline made port in Nassau, Bahamas — which served as both a base of operations for MI6 spy [[william-stevenson]] ("Intrepid") and as a recurring meeting location for [[thomas-townsend-brown]] and [[robert-sarbacher]]. When the yacht docked in Nassau, Stevenson would come aboard for dinner. A 1933 stop in Nassau during a Smithsonian deep-sea expedition (on which Brown served as radar/sonar engineer) hints that the group's intelligence function predates the conventional understanding of its scientific purpose.

## Connection to Townsend Brown

A colleague of Brown's codenamed "Morgan" in [[paul-schatzkin]]'s biography stated that Brown was "basically CIA research" but was "pulled away a couple of times for espionage work." Morgan believes Morgan worked for Stevenson or his equivalent in a private security contract, with CIA training at Camp Perry.

A colleague codenamed "O'Reilly" confirmed that "a couple of Brown's other inventions were handed over to other entities" and that "this Consortium seems to have something to do with it." This points to the Caroline Group as the mechanism by which Brown's classified IP was transferred from his hands into black aerospace programs without any public paper trail.

The 1957 NSG/Nassau meetings between Brown and Sarbacher are consistent with this model — Sarbacher had direct access to the UAP secrecy infrastructure, and Nassau was where the handoff of classified knowledge occurred.

## The Ben Rich Connection

In late life, Skunk Works director [[ben-rich]] told John Andrews that the technologies locked in black projects "would take an act of God" to release. More significantly, Rich stated that in his final month of life he began to worry that "the international corporate board of directors dealing with the subject of UFOs could represent a bigger problem to the citizen personal freedom under the United States Constitution than the presence of off-world visitors themselves." Jesse Michaels raises the hypothesis that this "international corporate board" and the Caroline Group are the same entity — or that the Caroline Group evolved into it.
