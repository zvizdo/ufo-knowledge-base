# TRW

Major US aerospace and defense contractor (Thompson Ramo Wooldridge). Acquired by Northrop Grumman in 2002.

## Role in Crash Retrieval Programs

[[eric-davis]] states in [[LnAiNChnuEQ]] that TRW was the **principal investigator (principal contractor) for crash retrieval programs from the early 1960s** — "practically 29 years" — until the CIA shut the programs down in 1989.

> "TRW was the principal investigator for the first roughly what almost 30 yeah practically well 29 years."

As principal contractor, TRW served a leadership role: other companies involved in the crash retrieval work knew TRW was involved (at the "scuttlebutt level" via government customer communications) but could not communicate directly with TRW due to compartmentalization.

A second company disputed TRW's principal investigator status — a senior VP of that company (whom Davis interacted with during AAWSAP) believed their company was the primary principal contractor. Davis attributes this confusion to compartmentalization: the companies knew what the government customer told them, which may have been different from TRW's understanding of its own role.

## FFRDC Spin-Off

One FFRDC is described by Davis as a spin-off of TRW, established not too long after TRW began work (early 1960s). Davis won't name this FFRDC but says it "started out as a contractor in 1960s" and is distinct from RAND, MITRE, or the other commonly cited FFRDCs.

## 1989 Shutdown

The CIA shut down the crash retrieval programs in 1989 after "several decades of best efforts" with no progress. Materials that program contractors held at that time were supposedly returned — but at least one company ([[lockheed-martin]]) still retained materials as of the AAWSAP era (post-2007), surprising [[glenn-gaffney]] when this was discovered.
