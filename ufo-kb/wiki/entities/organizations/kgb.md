# KGB

The KGB (Комитет государственной безопасности — Committee for State Security) was the Soviet Union's primary security and intelligence agency from 1954 until the dissolution of the USSR in 1991. It was succeeded by the FSB (Federal Security Service) in Russia.

## UAP / UFO Involvement

The KGB maintained internal UAP investigation programs — parallel to the US Air Force's Blue Book and successor programs — while the Soviet government publicly dismissed UFO reports. After the USSR's dissolution, partial records were released, revealing sustained institutional interest.

Key UAP-adjacent KGB figures in this KB:

**[[yuri-andropov]]** — KGB director (1967–1982) and later General Secretary. Andropov reportedly told Gorbachev about a conversation between Nixon and Brezhnev involving a shared UFO folder — a claim relayed through [[mikhail-gorbachev]] → [[kirsan-ilyumzhinov]] (source: [[IFaXi-NrPV0]]). Epistemic status: hearsay × 3.

**[[1981-black-sea-ufo-sighting]]** — A KGB-adjacent incident; Kirsan Ilyumzhinov reports being present at a sighting near the Black Sea coast.

## Nixon-Brezhnev UFO Folder

The claim relayed through Andropov → Gorbachev → Ilyumzhinov holds that Nixon and Brezhnev shared UAP information in a classified bilateral folder, and that this agreement was linked to the simultaneous halt of both US and Soviet moon programs in the early 1970s. The policy outcome (actual halt of lunar programs) is historically documented; the UAP-as-cause is unverified hearsay.
