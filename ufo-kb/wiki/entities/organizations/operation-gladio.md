# Operation Gladio

Operation Gladio was the Italian branch of a NATO-wide network of CIA-supported covert "stay-behind" paramilitary organizations created across Western Europe during the Cold War to conduct guerrilla operations in the event of Soviet invasion. The program was revealed publicly in Italy in 1990 when Prime Minister Giulio Andreotti disclosed its existence to the Italian parliament.

**Structure**: Gladio and its equivalent networks in other NATO countries (with different code names) were run by national secret services in coordination with the CIA and the British Secret Intelligence Service. They maintained caches of weapons, explosives, and communications equipment hidden across the country, with personnel who would be "activated" in the event of Soviet occupation.

**Strategy of Tension (Italy)**: The most consequential revelation was that Italian Gladio elements had been involved in the "Years of Lead" political violence of the 1970s — bombings and attacks attributed to far-left groups that were actually carried out by or with support from far-right Gladio-connected networks as a "Strategy of Tension" (Italian: *strategia della tensione*) to frighten the population, discredit the Left, and prevent the Communist Party from gaining power through elections. The 1980 Bologna railway station bombing (85 dead) is the most cited example.
