# Ways to Well

Functional medicine clinic founded by [[brigham-buhler]] in Austin, Texas. The clinic operates explicitly outside the insurance-driven healthcare mainstream, offering treatments that Buhler and [[jesse-michaels]] argue are suppressed by the FDA/AMA system because they cannot be patented and monetized at scale.

## Treatments Offered

- **Stem cell therapy** — stigmatized by FDA despite significant clinical interest; [[jesse-michaels]] receives his first stem cell treatment at Ways to Well during this episode
- **Hyperbaric oxygen therapy** — breathing pure oxygen under elevated pressure; used for wound healing, traumatic brain injury, and inflammation
- **Red light therapy** — photobiomodulation; refuels cellular ATP by penetrating to the cellular level; a Nobel Prize for light therapy was awarded in 1907; UK studies show 5 minutes three times/week can restore degenerative eyesight
- **Hormone optimization** — comprehensive monitoring of hormonal biomarkers with regular adjustment; focus on preventing the biological decline of the "decade of decline" (40s)
- **Peptide compounding pharmacy** — compounds peptides that the FDA has fought to restrict; regulatory battles with the federal government over access

## Alien/UAP Aesthetic

Buhler decorated the clinic with alien-themed art — an alien hand reaching for a DNA double helix, an alien mural featuring a woman. The AI healthcare assistant is also alien-themed. Buhler's stated reason: to make healthcare "fun and approachable and futuristic" — and because he genuinely believes the UAP question and the health question are connected (an advanced NHI intelligence would presumably have solved human health optimization).

## Model

Ways to Well is explicitly an alternative to the standard insurance/pharmaceutical model. Biomarker testing includes 70+ markers compared to the sick American average (not the sick-fish baseline of conventional labs). The clinic emphasizes preventative medicine, wearables data integration, and LLM-assisted biomarker analysis across patient populations.
