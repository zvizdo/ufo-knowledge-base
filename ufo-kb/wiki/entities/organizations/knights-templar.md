# Knights Templar

The Poor Fellow-Soldiers of Christ and of the Temple of Solomon (Knights Templar) — a medieval Christian military-monastic order founded ~1119 CE, suppressed and dissolved by order of Pope Clement V and King Philip IV of France in 1307–1312.

## Esoteric Role (Per Carlson)

[[randall-carlson]] characterizes the Templars as custodians of two things:

1. **Advanced knowledge**: Pre-Christian knowledge encoded in the Grail romances and related esoteric traditions, transmitted through Templar networks alongside the great cathedral-building movement of the 12th–13th centuries.
2. **A bloodline claim**: The possibility they protected a bloodline connected to Christ, which Carlson frames as "another possibility" alongside the hidden-knowledge hypothesis.

Carlson places the Templars within a broader "underground river" of esoteric tradition that periodically surfaces in civilization (troubadours, Kabbalah schools in Spain, Catharism, cathedral Gothic architecture, Grail romances all emerging simultaneously ~12th century).

## Shroud of Turin Provenance

The [[shroud-of-turin]] appears in history via the Templar lineage:
- **Jacques de Molay** — last Grandmaster of the Knights Templar, executed 1314
- **Geoffrey de Charney** (de Charné) — de Molay's associate (possibly grandmaster's nephew or close associate); first recorded possessor of the Shroud
- The Shroud passed through successive owners until reaching **Turin**, where it remains in the Cathedral of Saint John the Baptist

The Templar provenance gives the Shroud unusual historical weight in Carlson's account: the Templars would have known the difference between a forgery and something extraordinary.
