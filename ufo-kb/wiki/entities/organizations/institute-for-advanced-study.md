# Institute for Advanced Study

The Institute for Advanced Study (IAS) in Princeton, New Jersey is an independent research institute founded in 1930, distinct from Princeton University though geographically adjacent to it. The IAS provides permanent and visiting appointments to scholars in mathematics, natural sciences, social sciences, and historical studies — with no teaching obligations and no formal coursework. It is the premier institution for pure advanced research in the world.

**Notable members**: The IAS's permanent faculty and long-term members have included Albert Einstein (1933–1955), [[john-von-neumann]] (1933–1957), Kurt Gödel (1940–1978), J. Robert Oppenheimer (director, 1947–1966), [[freeman-dyson]], Edward Witten, and dozens of Fields Medalists and Nobel laureates. [[abraham-pais]], the physicist and Einstein biographer, was a long-term member.

**Von Neumann connection**: [[john-von-neumann]]'s IAS appointment places him at the intersection of the most elite pure mathematics and physics community of the mid-20th century with the JASON Group (which drew heavily on IAS and Princeton alumni), the Manhattan Project, and early computer science. Von Neumann's simultaneous participation in pure mathematics, weapons design, and early computing at the IAS exemplifies the institution's relationship with classified national security work.

**Physics-intelligence nexus**: The IAS served as an informal gathering point for the scientists who moved between pure research and classified defense work during and after WWII — the JASON Group's social network overlaps substantially with the IAS community. This makes the IAS an institutional node in the classified physics nexus that UAP researchers point to.
