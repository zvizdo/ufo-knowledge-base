# Scientific Coalition for UAP Studies (SCUAP)

Nonprofit scientific organization comprising physicists, engineers, and researchers committed to rigorous empirical investigation of UAP phenomena. Founded in 2017; publishes peer-reviewed technical analyses in its own journal and coordinates scientific approaches to UAP evidence evaluation across disciplines.

**Aguadilla case analysis**: SCU's most prominent published analysis covers the 2013 Aguadilla, Puerto Rico incident — a case in which a Coast Guard thermal infrared camera at the Rafael Hernández Airport captured a pink/whitish object moving at 80–100 mph over the airport perimeter, transitioning into and out of the ocean without deceleration, and apparently splitting into two objects. SCU's analysis documented the object's movement characteristics, thermal signature, and behavior, concluding that the performance envelope was inconsistent with any known aircraft, drone, or natural phenomenon. The Aguadilla case is one of the SCU's strongest evidence anchors because the footage is unambiguous and the analysis is methodologically documented.

**Camera evasion research**: SCU has also published analysis of a recurring pattern in UAP observation: the tendency of UAP to move outside camera capture windows or to appear more clearly on sensors not pointed directly at them — documented in the [[uap-camera-evasion-problem]]. This behavioral pattern suggests either intentional camera awareness (implying some form of sensing and response) or propulsion effects that create optical/thermal signatures outside the object's direct line of travel.

**Institutional positioning**: SCU occupies the gap between the fringe civilian research community and the government-adjacent research ecosystem ([[galileo-project]], [[aaro]]). It is not government-funded and not academic (no university affiliation) but maintains peer-review standards and technical rigor that set it above typical civilian UFO organizations. Its analyses have been cited in Congressional briefings.
