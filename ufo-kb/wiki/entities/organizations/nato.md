# NATO

Intergovernmental military alliance founded by the 1949 North Atlantic Treaty. Headquarters in Brussels; 32 member states as of 2024. Established as a collective security framework against Soviet expansion, NATO coordinates member-state military planning, air defense, intelligence sharing, and joint operations — making it the institutional infrastructure through which Cold War-era UAP encounters involving allied air forces were potentially reported, shared, and classified.

**UAP information sharing**: NATO's integrated air defense network — including early-warning radar systems across Europe and the North Atlantic — generated radar contacts with unidentified objects that were reported through the same channels as Soviet aircraft intrusions. The institutional question is whether NATO maintained a parallel reporting and classification system for anomalous objects that operated separately from individual member-state programs, or whether UAP reports were simply classified under national-security protocols by each member state separately.

**Rendlesham Forest**: The [[1980-rendlesham-forest]] incident occurred at RAF Bentwaters/Woodbridge, a US Air Force installation on British soil under NATO status-of-forces agreements. The USAF's response (including the Halt Memo and subsequent official silence) demonstrates how a UAP incident at a NATO-positioned base was managed: through US chain of command rather than British or NATO oversight, even though the incident occurred on UK soil with UK military personnel also present.

**Five Eyes and beyond**: NATO's intelligence-sharing structures overlap with but are not identical to [[five-eyes]] (the UK–US–Australia–Canada–New Zealand signals intelligence sharing agreement). NATO potentially extends UAP-relevant information sharing to continental European members (Germany, France, Italy) who are not in Five Eyes — creating a broader institutional network for both encounter reporting and potential concealment.

**Allied intelligence corroboration**: Multiple NATO member states have released UAP files: France (GEIPAN), the UK (Project Condign/MoD files), Denmark, Brazil (under NATO partnership). The pattern of allied-nation disclosure provides cross-national corroboration for UAP as a real phenomenon without requiring US file releases.
