# Center for UFO Studies (CUFOS)

The Center for UFO Studies was founded in 1973 by astronomer [[j-allen-hynek]] following his disillusionment with the Air Force's [[project-blue-book]] program, which he had served as scientific consultant. After the Condon Report effectively closed Blue Book, Hynek argued publicly that the UFO phenomenon deserved serious scientific attention rather than dismissal, and founded CUFOS as a civilian research institution to pursue that agenda.

**Origins**: Hynek had spent nearly two decades serving as the Air Force's skeptical consultant on UFO reports — his job was to explain them away. By the late 1960s, he had concluded that a genuine unexplained residue existed in the data and that the Air Force was not interested in investigating it honestly. After Blue Book's closure in 1969, he published *The UFO Experience* (1972) making the public case for scientific study, then founded CUFOS the following year.

**Research mission**: CUFOS collected, catalogued, and analyzed civilian UFO reports with scientific rigor, publishing *The International UFO Reporter* and sponsoring research. The organization maintained an archive of case reports and correspondence that constitutes one of the largest collections of UFO witness testimony from the 1970s–1990s.

**[[northwestern-university]] connection**: Hynek was an astronomer at Northwestern, and the organization had ties to the university's scientific establishment through his presence there, though CUFOS operated as an independent nonprofit.

**Legacy**: CUFOS is still operational as of 2020s, though at reduced activity. It represents the first sustained attempt by a credentialed scientist to build a permanent civilian institution for UFO research — predating [[seti-institute]] framing debates and post-AATIP organizations like the [[sol-foundation]] by decades.
