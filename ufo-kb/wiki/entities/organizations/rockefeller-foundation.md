# Rockefeller Foundation

U.S. private philanthropy founded in 1913 by John D. Rockefeller Sr., the Standard Oil titan. Among the largest and most influential private foundations in American history. Funded major 20th-century programs in public health, biomedical research, agricultural science (the Green Revolution), and social sciences.

**Flexner Report**: The Foundation funded Abraham Flexner's 1910 report (*Medical Education in the United States and Canada*) that restructured American medical education. The Flexner Report led to the closure of half of U.S. medical schools, the near-total elimination of homeopathic and eclectic medical schools, and the reorientation of medical education around pharmaceutical-allopathic models. Critics argue this was gatekeeping by a foundation whose interests were aligned with the emerging pharmaceutical industry — establishing the pattern of foundation-funded institutional capture that appears throughout the KB.

**Eugenics funding**: The Foundation was a major funder of eugenics research in the 1920s–30s, including programs at Kaiser Wilhelm Institute in Germany that influenced Nazi racial policy. This history connects the Foundation to the broader network of elite American philanthropies with documented ties to the pre-war German state.

**UAP-disclosure family split**: The Foundation represents the institutional face of Rockefeller power. In the 1990s, [[laurance-rockefeller]] — one of John D. Rockefeller Jr.'s sons — funded the [[1995-rockefeller-ufo-initiative]] against the apparent preferences of his brother [[david-rockefeller]], who reportedly opposed the disclosure push. The split illustrates that even within a single power family, the UAP question generated disagreement.
