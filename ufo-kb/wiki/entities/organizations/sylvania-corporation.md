# Sylvania Corporation

US electronics manufacturer (1901–1993); produced light bulbs, vacuum tubes, television sets, and semiconductors; eventually acquired by GTE (as GTE Sylvania) and later merged into Osram. A significant mid-century US defense electronics contractor with government contracts spanning radar and communications components.

**Condon Commission role**: Sylvania supplied [[lewis-chase]] — a company engineer — as a field investigator for the [[condon-commission]]'s field investigation team (1967–68). The commission, formally the University of Colorado UFO Project, hired field investigators from industry and government to conduct on-site interviews with witnesses. Chase was assigned to investigate cases in the Malmstrom Air Force Base area — precisely the region where the [[1967-echo-flight]] incidents occurred.

**Selective reporting**: Lewis Chase's field work brought him into contact with [[robert-salas]]'s and [[walt-feele]]'s accounts (though probably through intermediaries, as NDAs limited direct access). Per [[robert-salas]]'s account, Chase actively denied documented evidence to commission investigator Roy Craig — consistent with the [[condon-commission-whitewash]] thesis that field investigators were selective about what they reported upward. Chase filed reports inconsistent with what he observed in the field, producing a commission conclusion of "no evidence for anything unusual" from a selective evidence base.

**Structural significance**: Sylvania's role illustrates how defense contractors — rather than independent researchers — were placed in field investigator roles for the commission. An engineer from a defense electronics company with government contracting relationships was sent to investigate UAP incidents at a nuclear missile base. The selection of such a person, and his subsequent behavior, is consistent with the KB's thesis that the Condon Commission was designed to produce a predetermined conclusion through controlled information flow.

**Corporate lineage**: Sylvania was one of the "Big Four" US vacuum-tube manufacturers of the Cold War era alongside RCA, GE, and Raytheon — all of which held significant defense contracts. Its investigator being placed on a government UAP commission is part of the broader pattern of defense-industry personnel embedded in official UFO investigation.
