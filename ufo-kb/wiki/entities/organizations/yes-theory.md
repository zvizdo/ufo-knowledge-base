# Yes Theory

YouTube channel and content collective founded in 2015 by four friends (Thomas Brag, Matt Dajer, Ammar Kandil, and Derin Emre) around the philosophy of seeking discomfort and saying yes to experiences outside the comfort zone. Grew to over 8 million subscribers; best known for adventure, travel, and social experiment content. Expanded into mainstream-adjacent UAP coverage in the late 2010s and 2020s, conducting field-investigation episodes and interviewing figures from the disclosure ecosystem.

**UAP coverage**: Yes Theory's UAP-related episodes brought disclosure-movement content to an audience primarily attracted by adventure and self-development content rather than UAP-specific channels. This crossover dynamic — a major mainstream creator platform publishing UAP investigations — is a structural feature of how UAP moved from the specialized research-and-enthusiast media ecosystem to mass-audience platforms during the 2017–2023 disclosure wave.

**Structural significance**: Yes Theory is referenced in [[1f16VvXaSSE]] (the Elizondo documentary context) as a representative of the mainstream-civilian-creator class that has expanded the audience for serious UAP coverage beyond legacy podcasts and specialized outlets. The [[soft-disclosure-civilian-observation]] concept identifies this class of creator — large-platform, mainstream-audience channels that independently investigate UAP — as a potential managed-disclosure vector. Whether these creators were directed to UAP content or arrived at it organically is unknown; the result is the same: millions of viewers who didn't seek out UAP content were introduced to it.
