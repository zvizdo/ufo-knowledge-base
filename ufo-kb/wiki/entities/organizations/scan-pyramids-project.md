# ScanPyramids Project

An international, multi-institutional non-invasive scanning project focused on mapping the interiors of Egyptian pyramids using muon tomography, thermal imaging, photogrammetry, and 3D laser scanning. Partners include Egyptian Heritage Innovation and Preservation (HIP Institute), Cairo University, and Japanese universities.

## Key Findings

- **Big Void (2017)**: The most significant discovery — a large previously unknown void (~30 m long) detected above the Grand Gallery in the [[great-pyramid]] using muon detectors. Published in *Nature*, peer-reviewed. This was the first major new architectural discovery inside Khufu's pyramid in modern times.

## Methodology (vs. Beyond Protocol)

Per [[filippo-biondi]] in [[zrmG2jBckgk]], the key difference between ScanPyramids' muon approach and his [[beyond-protocol]] SAR Doppler tomography:

- **ScanPyramids**: Uses cosmic-ray muons — sub-atomic particles from space that pass through rock. Muon detectors must be physically placed inside or adjacent to the pyramid to scan **between the surface and the detector**. Effective for mapping voids inside the pyramid body but limited to the zone above the detector.
- **Beyond Protocol**: Uses satellite-based SAR and phonon detection. No physical presence required. Can scan **below the surface to great depth** (~1 km) using seismic surface vibrations.

The two methods are therefore **complementary rather than competing**: SAR Doppler can scan deep underground; muons can scan the pyramid interior above a placed detector. Biondi explicitly calls for collaboration: "We don't have to be separated, but work together."

A potential joint experiment: placing muon detectors inside a cleaned shaft to scan upward from underground — giving unprecedented depth-plus-interior coverage.
