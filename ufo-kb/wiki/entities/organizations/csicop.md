# CSICOP

The Committee for the Scientific Investigation of Claims of the Paranormal (CSICOP), later renamed the Committee for Skeptical Inquiry (CSI), was founded in 1976 by [[james-randi]], Carl Sagan, Martin Gardner, Paul Kurtz, and others as an organization explicitly dedicated to applying scientific methodology to paranormal, pseudoscientific, and UFO claims.

**Founding context**: CSICOP emerged partly in response to the 1970s growth in public interest in the paranormal — Uri Geller's spoon-bending claims, the Bermuda Triangle, astrology, and UFO contactee movements. The founders believed existing scientific institutions were not adequately pushing back on unsubstantiated claims and founded CSICOP to fill that gap.

**Publications and influence**: CSICOP published *Skeptical Inquirer*, which became the flagship publication of organized skepticism. The organization had substantial academic membership and media reach, and its positions on UAP, parapsychology, and related topics significantly shaped mainstream scientific opinion through the 1980s–2000s.

**Relationship to UAP research**: CSICOP's institutional stance was that UAP reports were explicable by conventional means (misidentification, hoax, psychological factors) and did not require novel explanations. This position put it in direct opposition to researchers like [[j-allen-hynek]] and [[hynek-cufos]], and later to organizations like the [[sol-foundation]]. Critics argue CSICOP's approach was often more committed to debunking than to inquiry.

**James Randi role**: [[james-randi]] was CSICOP's most prominent public face, pursuing exposure of psychic claimants like Uri Geller and testing homeopathy and faith healing claims. His approach — setting up controlled tests — was methodologically sound in many cases but also applied with adversarial framing.

**Rebranding**: CSICOP was renamed the Committee for Skeptical Inquiry (CSI) in 2006, following parent organization restructuring under the Center for Inquiry.
