# AC Gravity LLC

Private research company founded by [[ning-lee]], a University of Alabama Huntsville physicist who had published experimental results claiming measurable weight loss above spinning superconductors. The company was formed after Lee left UAH.

## Formation and Institutional Credibility

Lee's physics department chair at UAH, **Larry Smalley**, left the university to join Lee at AC Gravity — an unusual institutional vote of confidence. Smalley subsequently appeared on a **2004 NASA-funded patent** from the Marshall Space Flight Center for a novel barrel-shaped asymmetric capacitor — connecting Lee's work to both the [[biefeld-brown-effect]] / [[thomas-townsend-brown]] lineage and to NASA's Marshall engineering infrastructure.

## DOD Contract and Classification

AC Gravity quickly won a **DOD contract**, and Lee was awarded a **security clearance**. At the time the contract was awarded, Lee's son George noted a stark change in her demeanor — increased secretiveness and somberness. She appeared to go effectively dark from public research.

[[nick-cook]] personally interviewed Lee at a 2003 Washington DC conference on high-frequency gravitational waves (organized by MIT Corporation). Cook recalls she "disappeared... within about a year" of their meeting, and "no one knew what happened to her."

## CCP Pressure and Final Years

In 2008, Lee was approached by the CCP to continue her gravity research in China. She refused. As retaliation, she was banned from entering China — including to attend her own mother's funeral. Cook's account frames this as evidence of her patriotism: she chose US classification obligations over family and Chinese offer.

In 2014, Lee was hit by a car; the trauma of the accident gave her husband a lethal heart attack and left Lee with brain impairment. She died in 2021.
