# Soul Foundation

Research and advocacy organization associated with [[helen-mccall]], a financial professional who approached the UAP disclosure question through the lens of systemic financial risk rather than national security or consciousness studies. The Soul Foundation produced at least one white paper arguing that UAP disclosure, if handled abruptly, poses an untracked systemic risk to global financial markets — a framing unusual enough to receive citation in the context of sophisticated disclosure discussions.

**The financial-stability thesis**: McCall's paper argues that disclosure would constitute a major information shock to markets — comparable to other sudden paradigm-disrupting events — for which financial institutions have made no preparation. Unlike a natural disaster or a technological disruption, UAP disclosure would simultaneously affect multiple sectors (aerospace, energy, religion, insurance, real estate) in ways that conventional stress-testing frameworks do not model. The argument is structurally parallel to how [[controlled-disclosure]] advocates frame the risk of unmanaged disclosure: the danger is not the fact itself but the unpreparedness of institutions receiving the shock.

**Framing significance**: The Soul Foundation/McCall argument represents a specific sub-category of [[disclosure-narratives]]: the economic and financial case for managing disclosure carefully. Most disclosure-management arguments focus on social stability, religious disruption, or national security. The financial-systemic-risk framing extends the risk analysis to the monetary system itself — an argument that would resonate with the [[matthew-pines]] type of analysis connecting UAP to power-structure questions.

**Citation context**: The Soul Foundation and McCall's white paper were referenced by [[jesse-michaels]] and [[matthew-pines]] in the context of cataloguing sophisticated institutional responses to the UAP question — organizations that are taking the phenomenon seriously as a policy problem rather than as fringe speculation.
