# VFA-41 Black Aces

US Navy carrier-based strike fighter squadron flying F/A-18F Super Hornets, based at NAS Lemoore, California. One of the Navy's most historically distinguished fleet squadrons — its lineage traces to WWII carrier aviation, and it has accumulated combat deployments across Korea, Vietnam, and the Gulf Wars. Described by [[marik-von-rennenkampff]] as "a storied squadron out on the West Coast."

**2004 Nimitz deployment**: VFA-41 was [[david-fravor]]'s command during the USS Nimitz Carrier Strike Group's November 2004 deployment off San Diego when the [[2004-nimitz-tic-tac]] encounter occurred. Fravor — then Commander, VFA-41 — and his WSO (Weapons Systems Officer) were the first aviators to get close to the Tic-Tac object during the initial intercept attempt. A second crew from VFA-41, Lt. Alex Dietrich and her WSO Lt. Cmdr. Jim Slaight, witnessed the same object from above and to the side simultaneously with Fravor's intercept.

**Credibility significance**: Fravor's position as Commanding Officer of a prestigious fleet squadron — rather than a junior pilot or weapons-systems trainee — is central to the [[credibility-frameworks]] analysis of his testimony. COs of fleet strike fighter squadrons are among the most operationally experienced and professionally scrutinized officers in US naval aviation; they have passed through multiple command selection boards and accumulated thousands of flight hours. A career Navy officer at this level has maximally strong professional incentives to not fabricate or exaggerate encounters that would damage his credibility.

**Multiple witnesses**: The significance of VFA-41 in the Nimitz encounter is that Fravor's sighting was not isolated — Alex Dietrich (also VFA-41) was a simultaneous independent witness who confirmed the Tic-Tac's appearance and behavior from a different vantage. Multi-crew, multi-aircraft, carrier-platform corroboration from a decorated fleet squadron is as high a standard of military witness corroboration as exists.
