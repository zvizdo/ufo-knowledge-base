# Los Alamos National Laboratory

Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL) is a US Department of Energy (DOE) national laboratory located in Los Alamos, New Mexico. Founded in 1943 as the Manhattan Project's central weapons design laboratory (Project Y), it designed the first nuclear weapons tested at the Trinity Site (July 1945) and dropped on Hiroshima and Nagasaki. LANL remains the primary laboratory for nuclear weapons design and stockpile stewardship, operating under a classified DOE contract.

**Manhattan Project origins**: LANL was established under the leadership of [[j-robert-oppenheimer]] as the site where the scientific teams from across the Manhattan Project — Oak Ridge, Chicago Metallurgical Laboratory, Hanford — converged for weapons design and assembly. The remote mountain site in New Mexico was chosen for security and isolation.

**Current mission**: LANL continues to design and certify nuclear weapons under the DOE's National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA), and conducts research in nuclear physics, materials science, computational science, and national security applications. It is managed by a consortium including the University of California.

**Bill Richardson connection**: [[bill-richardson]], Clinton-era DOE Secretary and New Mexico Governor, is associated with LANL through his federal oversight role and his state's relationship with the lab. Richardson's connections to Jeffrey Epstein also included contacts who had LANL-adjacent research ties — cited in KB documents discussing the Epstein network's penetration of classified research institutions.

**Classified physics nexus**: LANL, along with Sandia National Laboratories and Lawrence Livermore, constitutes the core of the US classified physics infrastructure. In UAP discourse, these laboratories are cited as the institutions most likely to have the classified physics knowledge relevant to understanding anomalous propulsion — and, in some claims, as the institutions actually holding relevant data.
