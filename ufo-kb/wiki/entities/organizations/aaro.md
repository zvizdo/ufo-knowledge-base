# AARO — All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office

Pentagon office established in 2022 to investigate UAP reports across all domains. Former director [[sean-kirkpatrick]]. Congress created AARO as part of its push for structured UAP accountability.

## Overview

[[robert-salas]] gave AARO a 2.5-hour presentation with supporting documents. AARO complimented him "in writing and verbally" but declined his request to follow up with the Air Force to verify his account. Their stated reason: lack of prior cooperation from the Air Force on other witness cases. Salas and Michaels frame this as evidence AARO is not performing its core mandate.

## Key People

- [[sean-kirkpatrick]] — former director; allegedly sourced the WSJ EMP hit piece
- Jon Kosloski — current director (as of late 2024); former NSA scientist with engineering/physics background; publicly stated he has seen "true anomalies" he cannot explain and knows of no one who can; called for academic and public collaboration; pledged to release the most anomalous footage; stated at least two firsthand witnesses of unreported UAP programs have approached AARO; approximately 1,600 cases as of last count

- Congressional briefings

## UAP Disclosure Act — The Missing Piece (Graves)

[[ryan-graves]] identifies AARO's structural limitation clearly: as a DoD organization, AARO answers to the Pentagon, not to the American public. It cannot declassify information on its own authority. The legislative solution was the **UAP Disclosure Act**, which would have established an independent **review panel** to receive AARO's output, assess national-security risk, and declassify information for public consumption. Graves describes this panel as the critical mechanism AARO cannot substitute for.

Graves's biggest disappointment: that panel provision was **removed** from the first version of the UAP Disclosure Act. Without it, the loop between official investigation and public accountability remains broken.

## Michael Herrera's AARO Testimony (D2tKCFmJjks)

[[michael-herrera]] testified to AARO in **April 2023**. Shortly after, Black Hawk and CH-47 helicopters began hovering over his home, his father's residence, and his business — which Herrera interprets as legacy program surveillance in response to his disclosure. Herrera views early AARO under [[sean-kirkpatrick]] as a front for identifying and silencing witnesses rather than investigating them.

**Jon Kosloski** (Herrera: "John Kuzowski") — current director; formerly NSA scientist. Herrera notes that people dealing with Kosloski directly say he "may be for the right issue," but the verdict is still out.

**Sean Kirkpatrick** — Herrera describes him in strongly negative terms as a bad-faith actor.
