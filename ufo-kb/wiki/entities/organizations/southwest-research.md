# Southwest Research Institute

Independent nonprofit research and engineering organization based in San Antonio, Texas, founded in 1947 by Tom Slick (also connected to early Bigfoot and anomalous phenomena investigation). SwRI employs roughly 3,000 scientists and engineers across a range of applied research domains: propulsion, geoscience, instrumentation, defense systems, and space science. It holds contracts with NASA, the DoD, and the Department of Energy — making it a government-adjacent contractor with the clearances and lab capabilities to analyze anomalous materials.

**Strieber implant analysis**: In the UAP context, SwRI is best known for analyzing [[whitley-strieber]]'s alleged [[nhi-implants|NHI implant]] — an object surgically removed from behind his ear in 1989. SwRI's analysis found the object to be metallic with cilia-like structures inconsistent with standard medical implant design; the device reportedly emitted a signal on a classified frequency. The analysis was never fully declassified. Strieber's experiences occurred in San Antonio, where he grew up — the same city where SwRI is based, creating geographic continuity with [[randolph-air-force-base]] and the [[1952-randolph-skinner-box]] case.

**Contractor profile**: As an FFRDC-adjacent independent nonprofit contractor with government clearances, SwRI occupies the same structural niche as [[mitre-corporation]], [[rand-corporation]], and the [[aerospace-corporation]] — civilian technical organizations with sufficient clearances to receive, analyze, and classify anomalous findings without going through normal military chain of command. This is the organizational template the KB associates with UAP material analysis under [[ffrdc-uap-architecture]].

**Tom Slick connection**: SwRI's founder Tom Slick was himself interested in anomalous phenomena — he funded expeditions searching for Bigfoot and other cryptids in the 1950s. The founder's interest in the unexplained and the institution's later role analyzing Strieber's implant create an unusual lineage for a mainstream engineering organization.
