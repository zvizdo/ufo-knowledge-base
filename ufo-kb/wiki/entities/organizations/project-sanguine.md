# Project Sanguine / ELF

US Navy Extremely Low Frequency (ELF) communication system for reaching deeply submerged nuclear submarines. ELF signals penetrate seawater to depths impossible for higher-frequency radio. The project used underground antennas buried in Wisconsin's stable bedrock — the low electrical conductivity of the crystalline shield rock making it a natural ELF transmission medium.

**Technical specifics**: The operational ELF system (sometimes called Project ELF) transmitted at 76 Hz — a frequency that penetrates seawater to depths of several hundred feet. The transmitter at Clam Lake, Wisconsin, used an antenna of approximately 56 miles of buried cable. The system transmitted one-way (submarines receive but cannot reply in ELF) very slowly — a few characters per minute. It was used to signal submarines to come to shallow depth for higher-bandwidth communications.

**Operational history**: The Navy operated Project Sanguine/ELF from the 1980s until 2004, when the system was decommissioned as newer satellite communication systems made it redundant.
