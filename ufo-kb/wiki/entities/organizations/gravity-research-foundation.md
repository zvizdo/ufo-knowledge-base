# Gravity Research Foundation

A private research organization founded in New Hamptons (now based in Gloucester, Massachusetts) by Roger Babson, a businessman who lost relatives in drowning accidents and became obsessed with "overcoming gravity." Established 1949.

## Significance

The GRF's annual essay prize — awarded for serious theoretical work on gravity — legitimized gravity research within mainstream physics during the 1950s. Prize winners have included major physicists, and the prize continues today (winners include work by Stephen Hawking and Frank Wilczek). This created an institutional opening for the anti-gravity research wave of 1954–1957.

The foundation's existence helps explain why the U.S. military's [[anti-gravity-manhattan-project]] of the mid-1950s could draw on willing academic talent: the GRF had normalized gravity manipulation as a research direction, lowering the stigma barrier that would otherwise have kept mainstream physicists away from classified applications.

## Connection to UAP Research

[[hal-puthoff]] explicitly places his [[polarizable-vacuum-model]] work in the intellectual lineage of 1950s gravity research — the era when the GRF, [[thomas-townsend-brown]]'s [[biefeld-brown-effect]] work, and classified military programs overlapped. The post-1957 [[physics-lockdown]] is partly the story of what happened when this openly pursued research direction was classified and the GRF-facilitated academic openness was reversed.

## Brandenburg and the GRF Tradition (p0S0BfoZy0w)

[[john-brandenberg]] explicitly places his [[gem-theory]] in the lineage of taking gravity and electromagnetism seriously as unifiable. The GRF's essay tradition created the institutional space that normalized this direction before the [[physics-lockdown]] shut it down. Brandenburg's presentation at an APS gravity-modification session and his DARPA-funded GEM work represent a continuation of what the GRF normalized in the 1950s — pursued now in the margins after the mainstream suppression.

Brandenburg also notes the Talbert newspaper series (ca. 1950s, *Miami Herald*) reporting that aerospace firms suddenly started getting grants for anti-gravity research, with top-level physicists interested — then "all sort of quieted down." This is the same wave the GRF catalyzed.
