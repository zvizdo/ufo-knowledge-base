# Aerospace Research Laboratory (WADC)

The Aerospace Research Laboratory at Wright-Patterson Air Development Center (WADC) was a US Air Force research facility engaged in advanced theoretical and applied physics research in the late 1950s through the 1960s. It is referenced in the KB primarily through [[josh-goldberg]], a theoretical physicist who worked in general relativity and gravitational wave theory while at the ARL.

**Anti-gravity research context**: In the [[avrocar-project]] and broader WADC anti-gravity research narrative, the ARL's maintenance of theoretical physics work on general relativity and gravitation is cited as part of the Air Force's institutional investment in understanding gravitational physics — concurrent with the Avro Canada saucer program and with WADC's sponsorship of the [[unc-chapel-hill]] gravity conference in 1957. These parallel activities suggest the WADC had an institutional interest in both applied flying saucer aerodynamics and the theoretical physics of gravity manipulation.

**Josh Goldberg**: Goldberg worked on general relativity at the ARL during this period. His presence — a serious theoretical physicist in a classified Air Force laboratory specifically working on gravitational theory — illustrates the overlap between the post-WWII classified aerospace research environment and mainstream physics.

**Wright-Patterson significance**: [[wright-patterson]] was the central node for post-WWII UAP program activities: the alleged storage site for Roswell and other recovered materials, the home of the Foreign Technology Division, and the base where multiple witnesses claimed to have seen non-human bodies. The ARL's theoretical physics work on gravity occurs in this same institutional context.
