# Lockheed Martin

Major US aerospace and defense contractor; formed by the 1995 merger of Lockheed Corporation and Martin Marietta. Its Skunk Works division (Advanced Development Programs) is the most storied advanced aerospace R&D group in US history.

## Crash Retrieval Material Custody

[[eric-davis]] provides a firsthand account in [[LnAiNChnuEQ]] of Lockheed holding crash retrieval materials:

> "The Aerospace defense firm that we were working we were trying to work with during the assap had Direct interaction with [Gaffney] as well as he was a senior VP and the executive VP of the corporation had they had to have a conversation with Glenn Gaffney."

The company had retained crash retrieval materials "since before [the program] got shut down in '89." When AAWSAP (under DIA) attempted to get Lockheed to share this material with BASS/EarthTech, [[glenn-gaffney]] (CIA Deputy Director of Science and Technology) intervened:

1. Gaffney was surprised that Lockheed still had the material — it should have been returned in 1989.
2. Gaffney blocked the transfer, saying "absolutely not we're not going to do that."
3. The classified setting for the transfer would have required SAP-level access (AAWSAP was TSSI at the time).

> ⚠ Note: This is Davis's firsthand account but does not name Lockheed explicitly — Davis refers to "the Aerospace defense firm." Given context (crash retrieval materials, senior VP-level involvement, Gaffney as gatekeeper), and the existing KB entry on [[glenn-gaffney]] which also cites congressional testimony about Lockheed material, this is the strongest candidate.

## ARV Claims Context

[[mark-mccandish]] and [[brad-sorensen]]'s ARV claims are often associated with Lockheed Skunk Works programs. Davis dismisses these claims. The Lockheed crash retrieval program described above is about analysis/custody, not about ARV construction — which Davis explicitly says did not happen.

## Coulthart's Senior Executive Source (V00WcEiKRAY)

[[ross-coulthart]] reports that a very senior Lockheed Martin executive called him after hearing Coulthart accurately characterize a situation. The executive implicitly confirmed Lockheed holds alien technology: **"It's not us. It's the agency. We want to do it."** ("It" = disclosing the technology.)

This is the most direct corporate-level implicit admission in the KB. Coulthart links it to the Lockheed → [[bigelow-aerospace]] spacecraft transfer attempt (the real substance behind the 2017 New York Times "metamaterials" reference). The craft may be the [[1953-kingman-crash]] vehicle.

> ⚠ Executive not named; account is secondhand from Coulthart to Jesse Michaels.
