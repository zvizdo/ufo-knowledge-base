# Bayer

German pharmaceutical manufacturer and one of the world's largest life sciences corporations. Founded in 1863 and best known for aspirin, Bayer has a documented history of participation in large-scale human harm — from Nazi concentration camp experimentation to the deliberate export of HIV-contaminated medications — that has continued across corporate restructurings and acquisitions.

## Nazi-Era Experimentation

During the Third Reich, Bayer — then part of the IG Farben conglomerate — participated directly in the crimes of the Nazi regime. Documented corporate correspondence shows:
- Jewish concentration camp prisoners were shipped to Bayer as experimental subjects for pharmaceutical compound testing
- Letters acknowledge the death of entire cohorts (e.g., all 150 women shipped for Phase 1 trials) and request additional prisoners as replacements
- The research was treated as commercially valuable regardless of the means of obtaining it

Post-war, Bayer reconstituted itself as an independent entity. Key IG Farben executives were tried at Nuremberg but received relatively light sentences; the corporate knowledge base and pharmaceutical pipelines continued.

## HIV-Contaminated Hemophilia Medications (1980s)

In the mid-1980s, when HIV was a death sentence, Bayer produced hemophilia medications (Factor VIII clotting concentrates) that were subsequently found to be contaminated with HIV. Documentary evidence shows Bayer:
- Had records demonstrating the contamination
- Made a deliberate decision to ship the contaminated product to third-world countries rather than destroy it
- Did so to avoid losing revenue on the existing product lots

Tens of thousands of hemophiliacs contracted HIV from these medications. Bayer paid damages in settlements but faced no criminal liability.

## Acquisition of Monsanto (2018)

In 2018, Bayer completed a multi-billion dollar acquisition of [[monsanto]] — the agrochemical company that:
- Was a primary supplier of Agent Orange during the Vietnam War, which contained dioxin — a persistent organic pollutant causing cancer, birth defects, and epigenetic mutations across generations
- Developed Roundup (active ingredient: glyphosate), linked to cancer and found throughout the global environment and human bloodstream
- Has faced massive litigation over Roundup's cancer links

The acquisition united two companies with parallel histories of profiting from products that cause severe and generational harm.
