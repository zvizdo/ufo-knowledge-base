# Theresa May

British Conservative politician who served as Home Secretary of the United Kingdom from May 2010 to July 2016, and as Prime Minister from July 2016 to July 2019. Her relevance to UAP/disclosure topics is narrow and specific: she was the government minister who ended the decade-long legal battle over the extradition of [[gary-mckinnon]] to the United States.

## Background

May represented the Maidenhead constituency in Berkshire from 1997 until her retirement from the Commons in 2024. She was the second woman to serve as British Prime Minister, after Margaret Thatcher. As Home Secretary, she was responsible for immigration, policing, and extradition decisions.

## The McKinnon Extradition Decision

### Context

[[gary-mckinnon]] was arrested in March 2002 by the UK National High-Tech Crime Unit following his unauthorized access to 97 US military and NASA computer networks. The US sought his extradition under a 2003 UK-US extradition treaty that required no prima facie evidence of criminal intent from the requesting state — a provision critics argued had been drafted with McKinnon's case in mind.

By 2012, McKinnon had been fighting extradition for a decade. He had been diagnosed with Asperger's syndrome. Multiple psychiatric experts submitted reports to the Home Office documenting an acute risk of suicide if extradition proceeded. The case had become a cause célèbre in the UK, with widespread public and political support for McKinnon.

### The Decision

In October 2012, May announced in the House of Commons that she was blocking McKinnon's extradition to the United States. Her stated grounds:

1. The risk of suicide was "very high" and medically documented
2. Extradition would therefore be incompatible with McKinnon's **Article 3 rights under the European Convention on Human Rights** (the prohibition on inhuman or degrading treatment)
3. The UK's jurisdiction over the offence was appropriate and prosecution in the UK remained possible (though the Director of Public Prosecutions ultimately decided not to prosecute)

May noted that she had received and considered medical evidence, legal advice, and representations from McKinnon and his family — including his mother Janis Sharp, who had campaigned publicly for years.

### Legal and Diplomatic Significance

The decision was significant beyond the individual case. It:

- Established a precedent that Article 3 ECHR rights could bar extradition even to a close ally
- Demonstrated that the Home Secretary retained substantive discretion in the extradition process, not merely procedural oversight
- Caused diplomatic friction with Washington, which had pressed for extradition for over a decade
- Led to changes in the UK's subsequent review of the 2003 extradition treaty, which was criticized as asymmetric

## Relationship to UAP Topics

May has no known interest in or connection to UAP/UAP-related matters. Her relevance to this knowledge base is entirely defined by the McKinnon case. She was not briefed on the substance of McKinnon's claimed findings as a condition of her decision — the decision was made purely on human rights grounds.

McKinnon himself has expressed gratitude toward May. The extradition block preserved his freedom and allowed him to continue speaking publicly about his findings.
