# Pablo Amaringo

Peruvian ayahuasca shaman and visionary artist, known for paintings that depict elaborate spirit-world scenes including "flying sorcerers" — objects that closely resemble flying saucers or UFOs. [[graham-hancock]] obtained Amaringo's permission to reproduce a number of his images in *Supernatural* (US retitle: *Visionary*).

Hancock specifically asked Amaringo what the flying sorcerers in his paintings represented. Amaringo's answer — described as "very matter of fact" — was:

> *"Those are vehicles for entering and leaving the spirit world."*

This single sentence is, for Hancock, among the most illuminating framings of the UFO phenomenon: not visitors crossing interstellar space in advanced rockets, but technology (biological/neurological or otherwise) for traversing dimensional boundaries. The shaman did not need the nuts-and-bolts hypothesis; the spirit-world hypothesis was obvious to him.

## Significance

- Provides an indigenous Amazonian framing that bridges ayahuasca DMT entity encounters and UFO sightings under a single mechanism: access to parallel dimensions/spirit realms.
- Hancock uses this as a counterpoint to the "ancient alien" hypothesis — Amaringo does not say "visitors from another planet built the pyramids"; he says these are craft for crossing between layers of reality.
- The "vehicles for entering/leaving the spirit world" framing aligns with [[pablo-amaringo]]'s testimony and [[andrew-gallimore]]'s directed-DMT-world model in [[8XD1ZiuhXoY]]: DMT commandeers the brain's world-modeling machinery to allow access to the NHI layer.
