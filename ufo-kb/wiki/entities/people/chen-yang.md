# Chen-Ning Yang

Chinese-American theoretical physicist who shared the 1957 Nobel Prize in Physics with Tsung-Dao Lee for the parity violation hypothesis in weak interactions. His later work produced the Yang-Mills gauge theory (1954, with Robert Mills) — the mathematical foundation on which the Standard Model of particle physics is built — and the Wu-Yang dictionary (with [[jim-simons]]), which demonstrated that the Standard Model's mathematical structure is equivalent to the classical differential geometry of fiber bundles and connections.

In the KB, Yang appears through [[eric-weinstein]]'s analysis: the Wu-Yang dictionary was a critical insight revealing that underneath physics lies pure geometry — a finding that, Weinstein argues, should have unified geometry and physics but instead was absorbed into the establishment without triggering the paradigm shift it implied. Weinstein's observation that "the world's greatest living theoretical physicist" was working at a state campus (SUNY Stony Brook) rather than an elite private institution is cited as evidence of institutional mis-allocation of physics talent — consistent with the broader [[physics-lockdown]] pattern.

The Wu-Yang dictionary is adjacent to the UAP physics cluster because it demonstrates that the geometric structures relevant to [[electrogravitics]], [[hal-puthoff]]'s extended electrodynamics, and Eric Weinstein's [[geometric-unity]] all derive from the same underlying mathematical framework that Yang helped identify.
