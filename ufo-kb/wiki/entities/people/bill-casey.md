# Bill Casey

## Background

William J. Casey served as Director of Central Intelligence under Ronald Reagan from 1981 until his death in 1987. Casey was a longtime OSS-lineage intelligence operator and a major architect of Reagan-era CIA expansion.

## Notable Claims

### Five-Hour Stargate Briefing

[[hal-puthoff]] (Gf_tKn9TaP8) cites his briefing of Casey as an instance supporting the working hypothesis that very-top-of-the-food-chain decision-makers are unusually receptive to psi-related material. Casey was scheduled for a 45-minute briefing on the [[stargate-program|Stargate program]]. He became sufficiently absorbed that he dismissed the rest of his afternoon calendar; the briefing ran approximately five hours.

Puthoff connects this to a parapsychological study of 67 corporate CEOs in which the CEOs whose firms were performing well scored above chance on a next-day random-number prediction task, while those whose firms were failing scored at or below chance. The interview consensus from the successful CEOs: "When I trust my gut instinct, I'm usually right." Puthoff's inferred mechanism: high-performing decision-makers may be operating in part on a sub-conscious psi channel without recognizing it as such.
