# Tom O'Neill

Investigative journalist who began a routine magazine assignment in 1999 on the 30th anniversary of the Manson murders and spent the next 20 years pursuing the story. Published *Chaos: Charles Manson, the CIA, and the Secret History of the Sixties* (2019). The book presents the case — without claiming a smoking gun — that [[charles-manson]] was a product of [[mk-ultra]] behavioral modification research conducted by [[jolly-west]] and that the Manson murders functionally served [[operation-chaos]]'s goal of destroying the counterculture movement.

## Background

O'Neill was working as a freelance journalist when he took the Manson anniversary assignment for a magazine. His original angle was: "how did Manson convert young women to killing in under a year?" This question led him to [[mk-ultra]], which he had not previously known about.

## Key Findings

### Jolly West Papers at UCLA

O'Neill obtained access to West's papers at UCLA and found West using an alias to write to [[sidney-gottlieb]] (CIA's MK-Ultra chief) about experiments in San Francisco during 1967 — the exact period and location where [[charles-manson]] was building the Family. West's movement from San Francisco to Los Angeles tracked Manson's movement.

### The Censored Document

A 1955 West report to the CIA stated he had successfully developed the ability to implant false memories and remove true memories using drugs and hypnosis, permanently. The version submitted to Congress during the Church Committee hearings had this paragraph removed. O'Neill possesses West's original document.

### The Bugliosi Suppression

Prosecutor [[vince-bugliosi]] compiled the definitive Manson narrative (*Helter Skelter*, still the best-selling true crime book of all time). O'Neill found that Bugliosi actively erased from his prosecution records a half-dozen CIA-connected peripheral figures who were present around the Manson Family during 1967–1969. Freedom of Information requests for two or three individuals O'Neill was certain were CIA-affiliated returned a "Glomar response" (neither confirm nor deny).

### Reeve Whitson

[[reeve-whitson]] — a mysterious figure who told people he worked for an unnamed government agency — was, according to his own deathbed disclosure, an undercover infiltrator of the Manson Family. He told his daughter and lawyer he was at the Tate crime scene the night of the murders (after the killings, before police arrived) and that his greatest regret was not preventing the murders.

## Credibility Assessment

O'Neill explicitly does not claim to have proven CIA involvement beyond doubt. He presents a documented evidentiary case: document trails, FOIA responses, archival materials, and witness accounts that together make the coincidences difficult to explain without government involvement. His 20 years of primary-source research places him in the category of serious investigative journalism rather than conspiracy speculation.
