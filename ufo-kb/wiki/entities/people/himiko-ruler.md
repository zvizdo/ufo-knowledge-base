# Himiko

Japan's only documented female Empress (~249 AD, Yayoi period). Ruled approximately 76 years in peace. Historical records (Chinese Wei Zhi chronicle) and Japanese legend describe her as a shaman with psychic abilities, including apparent mind-reading. Her mother and grandmother were also shamans with their own temples.

## Historical Role

Himiko is credited with converting Japan from a hunting-and-gathering economy to rice culture — a shift that bound warlords to land and effectively ended inter-clan warfare. When warlords attempted to cause trouble, Himiko reportedly appeared before them, having already perceived their intentions psychically, nullifying the threat before it materialized.

This combination of economic transformation and shamanic governance produced 76 years of relative peace in a previously fragmented, violent society.

## McMoneagle Remote Viewing Session

[[joe-mcmoneagle]] was tasked to remote view Himiko as part of a historical verification exercise (context: a class at a northern Japan island, shown on Japanese television). Without prior target knowledge, he described:

- **Summer Palace** — location and structural details
- **Winter Palace** — distinct from Summer Palace
- **Temple** — associated with Himiko's ritual functions
- **Mother's Temple** — separate from Himiko's own
- **Burial Mountain** — site of interment

The session was filmed, broadcast to 31 million Japanese viewers, and produced profound emotional responses in the audience. Local historians considered the described locations consistent with the known or conjectured archaeological record.
