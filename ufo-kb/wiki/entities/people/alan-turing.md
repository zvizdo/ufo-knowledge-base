# Alan Turing

British mathematician, logician, and computer scientist (1912–1954). Widely regarded as the father of theoretical computer science and artificial intelligence. Named [[martin-shkreli]]'s pharmaceutical company **Turing Pharmaceuticals** after him.

## Key Contributions Relevant to This KB

### Cracking the Enigma Code

Turing led the effort at Bletchley Park to break the Nazi Enigma cipher during World War II, working with Polish cryptographers who had pioneered the foundational analysis. Winston Churchill ordered the Enigma machines destroyed post-war. The machines have since entered collector markets; Shkreli owned one.

### The Turing Test and "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" (1950)

The 1950 paper proposes the Imitation Game (Turing Test) as an operational definition of machine intelligence. Less discussed: Turing enumerates candidate refutations of the test's validity. The refutation he assigns the **most credence** to is parapsychology:

> If a person has ESP ability — specifically the capacity to influence random event generators with their mind — an experimenter could affect the randomness of the test's output. This would create uncontrollable confounds that could not be distinguished from machine performance.

[[martin-shkreli]] in [[2Mb6NPPjCyg]] emphasizes this passage as evidence that Turing, far from being a strict materialist, took seriously the possibility of mind-over-matter effects. Shkreli endorses the ESP hypothesis himself, calling his position "heretical."

### Turing's Syllogistic Poem

Shkreli quotes a three-line poem attributed to Turing:

> "Turing believes machines can think. Turing lies with man. Therefore machines can think."

Shkreli interprets this as encoding Turing's views on machine sentience and his homosexuality into a syllogistic form — asserting that human-as-machine and machine-as-thinking are equivalent propositions.

### Turing on Child AI

Turing argued that before attempting to build a human-equivalent AI, one should first build a six-year-old AI. Shkreli cites this in [[2Mb6NPPjCyg]] to criticize the transformer/corpus-dump approach to AI development as skipping the developmental grounding that actual intelligence requires.
