# Flavio Estrada

Peruvian archaeologist associated with the [[peruvian-ministry-of-culture]]. His statements became the primary basis for the Western media "debunking" of the Nazca mummies. Jesse Michaels argues he is a bad-faith actor.

## Background

In 2023, Estrada was present when wooden tourist figurines (made by craftsman Manuel Caceras to sell to tourists as mummy replicas) were confiscated at Lima airport. He publicly stated "that one is fake" — referring to the figurines. Reuters and other major media outlets reported this as a debunking of the actual mummy bodies, conflating the carved wooden tourist trinkets with the anatomically consistent whole bodies. This conflation became viral in Western media.

Later, after the tourist-figurine narrative was established, Estrada made a separate claim: that the M-type mummy fingers were deliberately mutilated and the remaining digits lengthened — a different allegation using materials analysis (cotton joints, synthetic cyanoacrylate adhesives).

## Notable Claims

- Figurines confiscated at Lima airport are "craftsmanship, constructed, built" — factually correct but irrelevant to the real mummies.
- M-type fingers were mutilated and artificially lengthened using cotton joints and synthetic adhesives.

## Credibility Assessment

Jesse and other researchers consider Estrada a bad-faith actor who deliberately conflated wooden figurines with the real bodies to create a debunking narrative. The Ministry of Culture's pattern of raiding press conferences and attempting to seize the bodies from the [[university-of-ika]] adds institutional context for this assessment. The subsequent materials-analysis claim about cotton joints and adhesives has not been publicly rebutted with CT scan evidence — though three independent forensic teams found no mutilation evidence. See [[credibility-frameworks]].

> ⚠ Conflict: [[john-mcdow]], [[jim-caruso]], [[david-ruiz]], and [[jose-zalce]] all looked specifically for mutilation evidence and found none. Estrada's later claim of deliberate mutilation is unresolved.
