# Jimmy Carter

39th President of the United States (1977–1981). One of only two U.S. presidents to publicly report a UFO sighting (the other being Ronald Reagan). His presidency represents a serious but ultimately unsuccessful attempt to use executive authority to access classified UAP information.

## UAP Sighting — 1969

Carter saw a UFO in 1969 while governor-elect of Georgia, in the company of 11 other witnesses. He filed a report with NICAP (National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena). The sighting was described as an object that changed brightness and color and moved in unusual ways.

## Presidential Disclosure Attempt

Upon entering the White House, Carter made obtaining UAP information a genuine priority:
- In his **first week in office (early November 1976, Plains, Georgia)**, while still president-elect, Carter requested a CIA briefing on UFOs.
- The outgoing CIA Director [[george-hw-bush]] provided a briefing that [[danny-sheehan]] characterizes as deliberately incomplete — Carter was given managed information rather than genuine access.
- Carter commissioned the **Congressional Research Service** Science and Technology Division (directed by [[marsha-smith]]) to conduct a systematic study of the UAP phenomenon as a precursor to potential disclosure. This study hired Sheehan as special counsel.
- Sheehan's work on the CRS study led directly to his January 1977 viewing of classified [[project-blue-book]] photos showing a crash retrieval — see [[1977-sheehan-blue-book-photos]].

## The Access Denial Pattern

Carter's experience is a key data point for [[controlled-disclosure]] — that U.S. presidents do not automatically receive full UAP briefings and can be stonewalled by the intelligence community. This pattern recurs: Bill Clinton similarly described being denied access; Obama made public statements consistent with not knowing.
