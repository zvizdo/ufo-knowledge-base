# John Norsine

Former Navy pilot, weapons designer, and neuroengineer at Lockheed Martin who worked at the intersection of biometrics, neuroweaponry, and information security. He developed concepts related to **neural pattern recognition** — specifically the idea that humans have unique brain-signature "prints" analogous to fingerprints or iris patterns. He died September 27, 2007 while on business for Concurrent Technologies; circumstances remain unclear.

## Work

Norsine's research areas:
- **Biofusion** — merging biological sensing data with weapons/reconnaissance systems
- **Biometrics** — identification via unique biological signatures
- **Neuroweaponry** — weapons designed around manipulation of neural states; described by a close friend as involving "manipulation of the mind" — what would now be categorized as cognitive warfare
- **Neural pattern recognition / brain prints** — his signature concept: just as iris or fingerprint patterns are unique to individuals, brain wave signatures could identify people uniquely

His work at Lockheed Martin pushed into territory that overlaps with what the military now calls directed influence or cognitive operations against adversaries.

## Connection to the Allagash Abductions

Norsine claimed to have been present at the Allagash Wilderness (Maine) in August 1976 during the documented abduction account of four artists — at the same time the alleged abduction occurred. He told a researcher he was there conducting a **TTR (tag, trace, retrieve) exercise** with a subcutaneous device that monitored physiological data of field agents (GPS location, blood pressure, heart rate) — testing implant-based tracking technology.

This claim connects Norsine to both the classified military implant program world and to one of the more well-documented multi-witness abduction cases on record.

## Brain-Pilot Patent

Norsine held a **locked patent** for using biofeedback from the brain to remote-pilot a craft — effectively mind-controlled flight. This concept was directly connected to his "brain prints" research: if each person has a unique electromagnetic brain signature, that signature could serve both as an identification key and as the control interface for a craft designed around that specific neural pattern.

Jesse Michels describes him as "well ahead of his time." The patent is described as "locked" — suggesting it was classified, held under secrecy order, or otherwise restricted from public access.

## Allagash TTR Claim (QkuDvTFN7ms)

Norsine personally told [[charlie-foltz]] (one of the four [[1976-allagash-abduction]] witnesses) that he had been at the Allagash Wilderness in August 1976 — the same time as the documented abduction event — and had read the witnesses' incident report filed with the park ranger on the same day it was filed.

His stated purpose: conducting a **TTR (tag-trace-retrieve) exercise** testing subcutaneous bio-telemetry implants in field agents. The devices monitored GPS location, blood pressure, and heart rate — early implanted bio-telemetry for field-agent tracking. This places Norsine at the intersection of classified military implant programs and one of the most documented multi-witness abduction cases.

Jesse Michels frames this as either government surveillance of the witnesses (if they were flagged in advance), or parallel institutional interest in the same location and event.

## Death

**Date:** September 27, 2007  
**Circumstances:** Died while on business for Concurrent Technologies. He sent [[charlie-foltz]] a coded email before his death that, in Charlie's account, predicted his own death. The nature of the death remains unclear. Michels speculates that if a legacy crash retrieval program exists, Norsine was either a participant in it or being tracked by it.
