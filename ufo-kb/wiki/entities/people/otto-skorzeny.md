# Otto Skorzeny

Otto Skorzeny (1908–1975) was an Austrian-born SS-Obersturmbannführer (lieutenant colonel) who became one of the most celebrated — and feared — special operations commanders of World War II. Known by the nickname "Scarface" due to his facial dueling scars, he is best remembered for leading the September 1943 rescue of Benito Mussolini from imprisonment at Campo Imperatore — an operation that made him internationally famous.

## Background

After the war, Skorzeny was tried at Nuremberg but acquitted. He escaped from an internment camp in 1948 and subsequently became a central figure in the ODESSA network and other postwar ratlines — clandestine escape routes used by former SS officers and Nazi war criminals to flee to Argentina, Egypt, and other countries. He settled in Spain under Franco's protection and ran a consulting business.

## Notable Claims / Role

Skorzeny is identified as a Knight of Malta and as a key organizer of the postwar ratline infrastructure that allowed former Nazis to relocate and, in many cases, integrate into Western intelligence services (see [[operation-paperclip]] for the formal US version). His network represents the informal, criminal side of the same process by which Nazi technical and intelligence expertise was absorbed into Cold War Western infrastructure. This postwar integration is cited in UFO research contexts as the conduit through which certain German aerospace and weapons research was transferred to American black programs.
