# Elaine Pagels

Professor of Religious Studies at Princeton University. Pagels is one of the foremost scholars of early Christianity and **Gnosticism**, particularly the Nag Hammadi texts discovered in 1945. Her work on the **Gospel of Thomas** and related Gnostic gospels argues that early Christianity was far more diverse — and esoteric — than the canonical tradition acknowledges.

## Gospel of Thomas Connection

[[diana-pasulka]] cites Pagels in [[tS_64sTN5AU]] while discussing the hypothesis that the Last Supper was a **mystery ritual** — perhaps involving a sedative (hyssop), a staged death and resuscitation, and the transmission of secret knowledge. Pagels's key contribution:

- The **Gospel of Thomas** opens: *"These are the secret sayings which the living Jesus spoke and which Judas Thomas the Twin wrote down. And he said: Whoever finds the interpretation of these sayings will not taste death."*
- The Gospel records that Jesus **took Thomas aside and whispered secret words to him** — a classic mystery-initiation structure.
- Pagels's book *Beyond Belief* engages with this tradition and argues the Gospel of John was partly written as a polemic *against* the Thomas tradition.

This places the early Christian community within the framework of **mystery-religion initiation** — analogous to the Eleusinian Mysteries, Hermeticism, and the kind of esoteric transmission [[diana-pasulka]] sees recurring in the space-program's oral traditions.

## *Beyond Belief*

Pagels's book *Beyond Belief: The Secret Gospel of Thomas* (2003) argues that the canonical four Gospels represent a political selection — the suppression of a more diverse, experiential Christianity in favor of creedal orthodoxy. She and Pasulka share the scholarly stance of studying heretics (Pasulka notes: "She studies heretics... and so do I, in a sense").
