# Chad Underwood

US Navy F/A-18 pilot, member of VFA-41 Black Aces, who filmed the Tic-Tac object during the [[2004-nimitz-tic-tac|2004 Nimitz Tic-Tac encounter]]. Underwood was not part of the initial intercept flown by [[david-fravor]]; he flew a subsequent sortie to the CAP (Combat Air Patrol) point where Fravor had lost the object.

When Underwood arrived at the CAP point, the Tic-Tac object was already there — having apparently moved from the intercept location to the pre-planned rendezvous point, a point that was part of the classified mission briefing. Underwood recorded the object on his F/A-18's Forward Looking Infrared (FLIR) system, producing what became the primary visual evidence from the Nimitz case.

Underwood subsequently stated on a podcast that the acceleration when the object departed was instantaneous — "no ifs, ands, or buts" — and that he had thousands of hours of cockpit time as context for how unusual this was. He also reported that his weapon system could not achieve a targeting lock on the object and described the experience as consistent with active interference rather than a low radar cross-section.

Christopher Mellon confirms that "there was interference" with Underwood's systems at the CAP point — meaning the targeting system was actively disrupted, not merely unable to detect a small or slow-moving target.
