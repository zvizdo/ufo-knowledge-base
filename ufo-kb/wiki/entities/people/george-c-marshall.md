## Background

George Catlett Marshall served as US Army Chief of Staff throughout World War II, making him the senior uniformed military officer in the United States for the duration of the conflict. After the war, he served as Secretary of State under President Truman, where he authored the European Recovery Program (the Marshall Plan), and later as Secretary of Defense. He was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1953.

Marshall's career placed him at the intersection of military command, intelligence infrastructure, and postwar institutional architecture — precisely the nodes through which early UAP knowledge was managed and compartmentalized.
