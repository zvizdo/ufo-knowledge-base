# Floyd Odlum

Aerospace investment lawyer and one of the most significant financial links between [[thomas-townsend-brown]]'s electrogravitic research and the institutional aerospace establishment. Odlum was:
- A prominent aerospace investor and lawyer
- The **majority owner of Northrop** (before its merger with Grumman to form Northrop Grumman)

## Teller → Odlum Chain

According to [[nick-cook]]'s account in [[G7Ns4Aq1tVc]]:
1. Brown demonstrated his fan precipitator to [[edward-teller]] at Teller's Berkeley home in 1965
2. Teller was stunned — saying "I don't know how this works" to his wife
3. Teller's astonishment was noticed and relayed to Odlum, "a prominent aerospace investor and lawyer"
4. Odlum investigated and became the **sole investor** in Brown's Santa Monica company, [[guidance-technologies]]

## Guidance Technologies → Northrop Transfer

Brown ran anti-gravity electrostatics research at Guidance Technologies while meeting with [[bill-lear]], former Air Force Chief of Staff [[curtis-lemay]], the Rand Corporation, and other aerospace Titans.

In fall 1967, Brown shut Guidance Technologies down with "no explanation," saying he got no results.

Then, just **three months later** (winter 1968), Northrop — Odlum's primary corporate interest — published a paper on **Electro Aerodynamics and Electrogravitics and its Implications for Aviation**. "Electrogravitics" at that time was used exclusively to describe Brown's work.

## Payments to Brown's Wife

After Brown's death, Odlum made monthly payments of **$2,500** to Brown's wife Josephine "for the rest of her life." Brown's daughter confirmed this, stating: "Mr. Odlum forced them to sign a contract with my dad that was open-ended... after daddy died it was what kept my mother going." This suggests a continuing financial obligation — consistent with either compensation for licensed IP or payment for confidentiality.

## Significance

The Odlum pathway is the strongest circumstantial case for [[townsend-brown-b2-connection]]:
- Common investor between Brown and Northrop (Grumman)
- Suspicious 3-month timing between Guidance Technologies closure and Northrop's electrogravitics paper
- Ongoing financial relationship with Brown's family after his death
- Northrop later produced the B2 stealth bomber, which Aviation Weekly described as using a "Byfield Brown effect"
