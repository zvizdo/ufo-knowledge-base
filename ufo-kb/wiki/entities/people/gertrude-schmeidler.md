# Gertrude Schmeidler

American parapsychologist active in the 1950s–1970s, best known for originating the **sheep and goats paradigm** — a finding with significant epistemological implications for how parapsychology experiments are designed and interpreted.

## Sheep and Goats Paradigm

Schmeidler's methodology: before running parapsychology studies, she would interview participants and classify them as:

- **Sheep**: those who believe in psychic phenomena
- **Goats**: those who don't believe in psychic phenomena

She then compared their performance in controlled parapsychology studies. **Finding**: Sheep consistently produced better effect sizes than goats.

The critical extension, invoked by [[herb-metz]] in [[eQIMantuasQ]]: if subjects' *prior belief* affects outcomes, then so does the *experimenter's* prior belief. A skeptical experimenter (a "goat") running a psi study may be actively suppressing the effect through their own intentional state — making a priori skepticism methodologically self-defeating rather than a neutral position.

[[herb-metz]] cites this as context for why figures like James Randi or Michael Shermer entering an REG experiment to debunk it might ironically affect the results through their own skeptical intention — "and then you end up in this kind of tautological impossible loop where it's very hard to say well yeah they're skeptical and that's actually affecting the experiment."

## Significance

The sheep/goat effect is one of the most replicated findings in parapsychology and has never been satisfactorily explained within a purely physicalist framework. It suggests that:
1. Belief and intention are causal variables in consciousness-linked experiments
2. The conventional assumption that skeptical methodology is epistemically neutral does not hold in psi research
3. A paradigm shift in how "rigorous" experimentation is defined may be required for this domain

This connects to the broader pattern documented across the KB: the mechanisms that legitimate science (peer review controlled by skeptical editors, replication demands by skeptical researchers) may be systematically biased against detecting genuine psi effects.
