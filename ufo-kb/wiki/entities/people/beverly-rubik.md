# Beverly Rubik

American biophysicist who, with physicist Harry Jabs, published a re-examination of the original Michelson-Morley (1887) experiment — the canonical "null result" that is cited as having disproved the existence of the luminiferous ether and thereby opened the door to special relativity. Rubik and Jabs argued the data actually showed a small but real effect in the expected direction, and that the "null" characterization was a methodological and interpretive overstatement.

## The Michelson-Morley Re-Analysis

The 1887 Michelson-Morley experiment attempted to detect the ether by measuring whether light traveled at different speeds in different directions relative to Earth's motion. The conventional interpretation is that no difference was found, ruling out the ether. Rubik and Jabs contend:

- The effect was not truly null; a small signal was present
- The magnitude of the effect was within the range expected for a physically real ether given the experimental precision
- The subsequent consensus was built on the null interpretation rather than the actual data

Jesse Michaels presents this in [[0qlppHc3-gg]] as part of a broader argument that the ether — and [[scalar-waves]] — are making a scientific comeback, consistent with quantum electrodynamics' vacuum energy (zero-point field), which can be read as a physical ether-equivalent.

## Significance

If Rubik and Jabs are correct, the empirical foundation for ruling out an ether is weaker than assumed. This would rehabilitate a class of physical theories — including extended electrodynamics and scalar wave theory — that have been dismissed precisely because they presuppose an ether-like medium.
