# Lou Baldin

Lou Baldin is a self-described contactee and author whose two books — *In League with a UFO* (1997) and *A Day with an Extraterrestrial* (published ~10 years later) — [[richard-dolan]] regards as uniquely difficult to classify. Dolan has returned to the books multiple times and cannot determine whether they are exceptional fiction or genuine insider accounts.

## Books

**In League with a UFO** (1997, subtitle includes Roswell): Claims to describe insider knowledge of the US flying saucer study program in the aftermath of the Roswell crash. The book presents the study program's activities from a perspective that suggests either deep research or actual participation.

**A Day with an Extraterrestrial**: Describes a meeting with an ET named Milton. Dolan describes it as "Alice in Wonderland for UFOlogy" — surreal, internally consistent, and unlike any other contactee account in its texture. The NHI figure Milton is given a distinct voice and perspective that Dolan finds more convincing than most fictional attempts.

## Dolan's Assessment

> "If this is fiction, this is the best damn fiction in UFOs anywhere... If Lewis Carroll were to write about UFOs, this is the book you would write. It's a great read and I find myself returning to it over and over again."

Dolan refuses to categorize Baldin definitively: not as a fraud, not as a proven contactee. He treats the work as genuinely epistemically ambiguous — an unusual stance for a researcher who has developed strong positions on most UAP figures.

## Significance

Baldin's status as an ambiguous figure in Dolan's assessments is itself informative. Dolan is skeptical of most contactee claims; his inability to dismiss Baldin suggests the books contain details or structural characteristics that are difficult to fabricate convincingly.
