# Ken Wilson

American theoretical physicist (1936–2013). Nobel Prize in Physics 1982. Worked at CERN in its early years and later Cornell. Developed the **renormalization group** — a mathematical framework for understanding how physical laws change when viewed at different energy scales.

## Renormalization and Phase Transitions

Wilson's renormalization shows that by zooming in on a system (increasing energy), superficial lower-energy physics strips away and deeper, more fundamental structures emerge. Near **critical points** (phase transitions — e.g., the temperature at which water turns to ice), new universal behaviors appear that apply across a wide range of systems — the underlying dynamics become legible only at these extremes.

[[riz-virk]] invokes Wilson's framework in [[aKZ_MUbuk_Q]] to argue that **high-energy events reveal deeper layers of ontological reality** — specifically:
- Nuclear detonations at sufficient energy output could literally be ripping through the simulation's abstraction layers, exposing the raw information substrate.
- CERN's 13 TeV collisions and the Cosmotron at Brookhaven are other examples of energy regimes where "strange things happen" and simulation-layer phenomena (anomalous objects, beings through portals) become observable.

Wilson also worked on **exotic vacuum objects** (along with physicist Ken Shoulders), which are plasma-state phenomena that appear at extreme energy levels. The "plasma orbs" documented around NASA space shuttles and the "foo fighters" of World War II Germany may be objects whose plasma state reflects a deeper ontological reality than their lower-energy material state.
