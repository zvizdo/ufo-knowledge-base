# Joel Shectman

Wall Street Journal reporter who authored a 2024 article claiming that the 1967 Malmstrom AFB missile shutdowns were caused by a non-nuclear EMP test device — sourced primarily from [[sean-kirkpatrick]], former director of [[aaro]].

## The 2024 WSJ Article

The article advanced two explanations for all UFO mythology involving nuclear sites: (1) a non-nuclear EMP test device secretly wheeled up to Malmstrom and fired at live missiles; (2) Air Force hazing rituals.

**Primary sources (alleged):**
- [[sean-kirkpatrick]] — former AARO director
- [[susan-goff]] — Booz Allen Hamilton psyops consultant; LinkedIn confirms lead role in DoD psychological operations practice; conflict of interest not disclosed

**Why the EMP claim fails** (Salas 14-point rebuttal + Michaels analysis):
- The cited EMP device model was not operationally ready until 1971–73 — four years after the 1967 incidents
- The sequential (not simultaneous) shutdown pattern is physically inconsistent with EMP
- Boeing engineers investigated in 1967 and found no explanation even for a single-guidance failure via injected signal
- The missiles came back online; EMPs cause irreversible damage to electronics
- [[strategic-air-command]] was "extremely puzzled" and paid Boeing to investigate
- A 60-foot test stand visible to all base personnel would have been required

**Why the hazing rituals claim fails:**
- Cannot explain 1949 Air Force emergency meeting documents (with FBI, CIA, Army CIC, ONI present)
- Cannot explain the 1947 [[nathan-twining]] memo declaring UFOs "not visionary or fictitious"
- Cannot explain international sightings at civilian nuclear sites (Fukushima, [[1995-bariloche-argentina]])
- Cannot explain 57 years of documented witness harassment ([[bob-jacobs]] mailbox bombing; etc.)

[[jesse-michaels]] frames the article as a behavioral fingerprint of [[ufo-secrecy-cabal]]: a credible outlet deploying an official cover story through named government sources, creating a citable reference that survives factual refutation.
