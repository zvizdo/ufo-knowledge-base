# Boris Sakov

Colonel in the Soviet military who headed the USSR's formal UAP investigation program from 1978 to 1988. Sakov's program was established under a military directive following a series of incidents in which Soviet aircraft attempted to intercept unidentified aerial objects; the objects consistently outmaneuvered the interceptors.

The program documented approximately **40 UAP chase incidents** involving Soviet military aircraft. In at least three incidents, Soviet planes were credited as having been destroyed by or during pursuit of UAPs; two pilots died in those encounters. Following the deaths, Sakov's program issued internal guidance to Soviet military command: "leave them alone" — an official acknowledgment that engagement was not viable and further losses were unacceptable.

When the Soviet Union collapsed and archives briefly opened in the early 1990s, Sakov made a deliberate choice to provide [[george-knapp]] with the classified Soviet UAP files. Knapp traveled to Russia in 1993 for initial meetings and returned in 1996 to physically remove the documents. Knapp subsequently delivered the Soviet material to US government contacts and later to [[aawsap]]; [[robert-bigelow]] hired Russian-speaking analysts to process and translate the documents.

The Soviet program's conclusions — that UAPs are real, non-human, and capable of neutralizing pursuit aircraft — matched what US investigators were finding independently, providing an adversary-nation corroboration chain with no incentive for fabrication.
