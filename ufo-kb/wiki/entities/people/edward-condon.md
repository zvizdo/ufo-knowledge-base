# Edward Condon

Physicist who served as the second-ranking scientist under Oppenheimer on the [[manhattan-project]] before clashing with General Leslie Groves over transparency and being asked to resign ~6 weeks in. A J. Edgar Hoover letter to Congress labeled him "nothing less than a Russian spy" (Salas's paraphrase), and he eventually lost his security clearance during McCarthy-era proceedings. He later became a professor at the University of Colorado.

Around 1966, Lou Branscom (a figure connected to the Jason Advisory Group and US defense establishment) approached Condon and suggested he apply to regain his clearance — framing the [[condon-commission]] appointment as a vehicle for doing so. Condon went on to head the Air Force-funded UFO study, which produced a 1968 report concluding there was nothing of scientific interest.

## Background

Per [[robert-salas]] (and Hoover's 1949 letter), Condon was politically compromised and potentially an ideal figurehead for a pre-determined null result study. His deputy [[robert-low]] wrote an internal memo before the study began stating it would *appear* scientific while reaching a pre-determined conclusion. In an American Institute of Physics oral history interview, Condon was evasive when asked why he needed a security clearance for a study he characterized as dealing with non-classified material.

## Notable Claims

None of his own in this source. The Condon Commission's conclusion — no UFO activity of national security concern — was used by the Air Force to shut down [[project-blue-book]] in 1969.

## Additional Context from 5udx_SDdL3Y

[[jesse-michaels]] adds several layers to Condon's role in [[5udx_SDdL3Y]]:

### Göttingen and Oppenheimer
Condon studied at Göttingen in Germany in the 1930s alongside [[j-robert-oppenheimer]]. He grew up in Alamogordo — in the orbit of what would become [[los-alamos]] — and helped *pick Los Alamos* as the Manhattan Project site. He wrote the **Los Alamos Primer**, the document all new Los Alamos employees were required to read upon hiring.

### The McMahon Act (1946)
Condon wrote the **McMahon Secrecy Act of 1946** — the legal precursor to the Atomic Energy Act of 1954 ([[atomic-energy-act-classification]]). The AEC Act (which automatically classifies anything with radiological properties, including UAP) is built on the legal framework Condon created.

### The Security Clearance Loss: A Different Reading
The nominal reason for Condon's 1953–54 security clearance loss was political (Berkeley connections, labor sympathies, socializing with socialists). But Michels notes the structural irony: Condon wrote the McMahon Secrecy Act (the AEC's legal ancestor) — the law that would have governed classification of UAP material — and then, a decade later, led the committee tasked with convincing the public that UAP weren't real. Whether the clearance loss was causally connected to UAP programs is unknown, but the network is tight.

### Rigged Coordination
Robert Hippler ([[robert-hiper]]), an Air Force Colonel, wrote Condon a memo instructing him to show that all UFO research was "a waste of money." Michels describes this as documented evidence that the Condon Committee was in active coordination with the Air Force to reach a predetermined null result.

## The Guy Hottel Blackmail Network (TH10mD9hQas)

[[jesse-michaels]] introduces a new layer in [[TH10mD9hQas]]: while Condon's security clearance was under investigation, FBI field agent [[guy-hottel]] was building a dossier of derogatory field reports on him in the background.

The structural significance: Hottel was the author of the [[hottel-memo]] (1950) — describing an Air Force officer's account of three crashed flying saucers with humanoid crews, the most-read document on the FBI website. An agent who definitively knew about crash-retrieval events was simultaneously accumulating leverage over the man who had written the secrecy law governing those events.

The inference Michels draws: Condon may not have been simply a willing institutional soldier but a coerced one. His clearance was stripped in the early 1950s; it was mysteriously restored in 1966 precisely when he was placed as lead debunker on what became the [[condon-commission]]. The timing raises the question of whether the restoration and the appointment were a package deal.

> This is circumstantial — no document directly establishes Hottel's file as operational blackmail. But the network is tight: the man who wrote the secrecy law, surveilled by an agent who knew the secret, later placed to kill public UFO research.

See [[debunkers-as-atomic-insiders]] for the broader claims-thesis.

## Lou Branscomb's Role (lbGE3EC6StE)

[[beatriz-villarroel]] and [[jesse-michaels]] name **[[lou-branscomb]]** as the specific person who gave Condon his security clearance back in 1966. Branscomb was a PhD student under [[donald-menzel]] at Harvard and was connected to the Jason Advisory Group and the US defense establishment. This is the first source in the KB to name Branscomb explicitly as the clearance-restoration agent.

Jesse Michaels: *"It was literally Menzel's protege giving Condon his clearance so that he could completely kind of put the kaibosh once and for all on UFOs."*
