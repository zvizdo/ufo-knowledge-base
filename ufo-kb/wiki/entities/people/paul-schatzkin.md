# Paul Schatzkin

Author of *[[the-man-who-mastered-gravity]]*, the first comprehensive biography of [[thomas-townsend-brown]]. Schatzkin spent years assembling the primary source record — interviewing Brown's daughter [[linda-brown]], tracking down key witnesses including aging French Air Force officer [[jacque-corneau]], and using Freedom of Information Act requests and archival research to surface previously classified details.

## Research Contributions

Schatzkin's biography represents a decisive shift in the public record on Brown, moving the story from "obscure forums on the dark corners of the internet" toward documented, cross-corroborated history. Key archival finds:

- **1943 FBI file** confirming Brown as the Navy's foremost radar expert — undermining the "amateur quack" narrative.
- **Stevenson telegram** from the Rockefeller Plaza building (William Stevenson's HQ) to Brown's wife, confirming Brown was on an MI6-coordinated mission to Germany and was wounded, recuperating in an English hospital.
- **Jacque Corneau interview** (April 2008, Pennsylvania): the 98-year-old French Aerospace veteran confirmed the 1956 Montgolfier vacuum experiments showed thrust "several orders of magnitude larger than residual ambient ionization could account for."
- Documents showing that when the Navy was asked for Brown's service records under FOIA, they first denied his existence, then provided records for the wrong "Townsend Brown," then finally gave Linda Brown an obviously edited version — with the hint that "a lot of this work was classified; we can't even tell you it's classified."

## The Vindication Thesis

Schatzkin's framing is explicitly vindicatory: Brown was not a crank; his experiments produced anomalous results witnessed by the highest levels of American government; his work was deliberately suppressed and absorbed into black aerospace programs. The book argues this represents a missed paradigm for physics.
