# Brenda Dunne

## Background

Brenda Dunne managed and co-led the [[princeton-parapsychology-lab|Princeton Engineering Anomalies Research (PEAR)]] laboratory at Princeton University from its founding in 1979 (one year after the Puthoff/Targ IEEE paper opened the field) until its closure in 2007. Her partnership with engineering-dean [[bob-jahn|Robert Jahn]] built the most extensive academic replication corpus of the SRI [[remote-viewing]] protocol and developed the random-event-generator (REG) consciousness-effect experiments.

## Notable Claims

### PEAR's Two-Track Program

[[hal-puthoff]] (Gf_tKn9TaP8) describes the Dunne-Jahn program as having two tracks:
1. **Remote viewing replication** of the SRI protocol over ~20 years — a complete confirmation of the SRI findings under independent academic conditions.
2. **Quantum-driven random-event generators** — operators attempting to influence the output of quantum-mechanical random sources by intention alone. The aggregate effect is small per trial but statistically robust over many trials.

Puthoff frames PEAR as the institutional bridge that moved the remote-viewing question from the classified CIA program into peer-reviewed academic science.

### Time-Decoupled Variants

Per Puthoff, Jahn (with Dunne managing the program) ran time-decoupled remote-viewing trials in which the outbound target was generated *after* the viewing — replicating the central protocol with reversed temporal ordering. Hit rates matched standard runs. Published in the IEEE Proceedings ~1978.
