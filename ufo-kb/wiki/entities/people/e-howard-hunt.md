# E. Howard Hunt

Everette Howard Hunt (1918–2007) was a CIA operations officer whose career spanned from the 1950s through the early 1970s. He is best known publicly as one of the operatives convicted in the 1972 Watergate break-in. In UFO and deep state research contexts, Hunt is significant as a liaison figure connecting CIA paramilitary operations, anti-Castro Cuban exile networks, and the broader covert infrastructure sometimes called the [[s-force]].

## Background

Hunt served in the OSS during World War II and subsequently joined the CIA, where he became a specialist in political operations and propaganda. He played a role in the 1954 CIA-backed coup in Guatemala and was deeply involved in the planning of the Bay of Pigs invasion. His liaison role at the Everglades paramilitary training base connected him to the Cuban exile paramilitary networks that overlapped with the CIA-Mafia assassination programs.

## Notable Claims / Role

Hunt's role as liaison for the Everglades training base places him at the operational center of the anti-Castro paramilitary apparatus. The same network — Cuban exiles, CIA handlers, mob-connected figures — that trained in the Everglades is identified by researchers as the operational pool from which the [[s-force]] drew for operations including the Kennedy assassination. Hunt himself, shortly before his death, made partial admissions about knowledge of a plot against Kennedy, recorded by his son Saint John Hunt.

## Deathbed Claim: JFK and the Alien Presence

According to [[douglas-caddy]] (Hunt's lawyer), Hunt told Caddy on his deathbed that JFK was assassinated over *"the alien presence."* Caddy relayed this to Dark Journalist. [[peter-levenda]] considers this "a distinct possibility" — credible but not 100% verifiable.

This claim is separately documented in [[jfk-assassination-ufo]] alongside [[harold-malmgren]]'s firsthand assessment and the [[jfk-letter-mccone]] documentary evidence. Hunt's proximity to the CIA's Bay of Pigs operation and its aftermath — the same institutional network connected to the assassination — gives his deathbed statement structural weight even if it cannot be independently confirmed.

Hunt also wrote three occult novels during his later years and reportedly lived on "Witches Island" — an unusual biographical detail for a CIA officer that Levenda notes as potentially significant given the broader intersection of intelligence and occult traditions documented in the KB.
