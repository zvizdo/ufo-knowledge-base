# Joseph Campbell

## Background

Joseph Campbell (1904–1987) was an American professor of literature at Sarah Lawrence College and the preeminent comparative mythologist of the 20th century. His most influential work, *The Hero with a Thousand Faces* (1949), identified a universal pattern — the monomyth or "hero's journey" — underlying myths across unconnected cultures worldwide.

## Relevance to UAP/Consciousness Discourse

In [[9QMrhcpJq8I]], [[matthew-pines]] invokes Campbell's framework in the context of consciousness exploration and the confrontation that UAPs force upon humanity. Pines characterizes UAP disclosure as the civilizational equivalent of a hero's journey — a confrontation with the unknown that forces ontological transformation. Campbell's dictum "Follow your bliss" and his concept of the "Soul's High Adventure" are referenced as frameworks for understanding why consciousness exploration (including via mystery traditions, DMT, meditation) is not escapism but genuine epistemology.

The deeper connection: Campbell's cross-cultural mythological evidence supports the idea that ancient traditions were encoding **real transpersonal experiences** — the same consciousness phenomena that UAP-adjacent researchers like [[andrew-gallimore]] and [[rick-strassman]] approach via psychedelic science. Campbell's mythological data provides the cross-cultural anchor for claims about non-local consciousness.
