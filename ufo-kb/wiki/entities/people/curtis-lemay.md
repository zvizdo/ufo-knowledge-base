# Curtis LeMay

## Background

General Curtis Emerson LeMay was one of the most powerful and feared military figures of the Cold War era. He served as Commander of Strategic Air Command (SAC) — the nuclear strike force — from 1948 to 1957, and later as Air Force Chief of Staff. He was the primary architect of the US nuclear deterrence posture.

LeMay is infamous for his hawkish positions during the Cuban Missile Crisis in October 1962, where he reportedly pushed hard for preemptive nuclear strikes against Cuba and the Soviet Union. [[harold-malmgren]], who was present in the situation room at age 27, describes LeMay's calculus as monstrously casual: stating that a nuclear exchange would result in "10 to 20 million killed tops depending on the brakes" — a remark that stunned the room.

## UAP Connection

LeMay is identified in multiple UAP research contexts as having had direct access to and active control over UAP material and information. [[stanton-friedman]] and others have alleged he was a key gatekeeper who blocked civilian and presidential access to UAP files. His institutional control over [[strategic-air-command]] and therefore over nuclear sites — precisely the locations where UAP "tag-alongs" were most active — placed him at the nexus of [[ufo-nuclear-nexus]].

Malmgren's account of being present at the highest levels of Cuban Missile Crisis deliberations, and his later disclosure of UAP knowledge, situates LeMay within the same closed circle of Cold War insiders who possessed both nuclear command authority and UAP awareness.
