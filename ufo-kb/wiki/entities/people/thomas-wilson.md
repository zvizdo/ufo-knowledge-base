# Thomas Wilson

Vice Admiral (later Admiral) Thomas Wilson served as the Director of Intelligence for the Joint Chiefs of Staff (J2) and subsequently as Director of the Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) — positions that made him, in theory, the senior official with oversight of all US military technical intelligence, including any classified non-human technology programs.

## The Wilson-Davis Meeting — Timeline Clarification (xnxasfyHtfo)

[[eric-davis]] clarified in [[xnxasfyHtfo]] that the **initial Pentagon briefing of Wilson by Davis occurred in summer 1997** — not 2002. The parking lot meeting at [[egng]] Special Projects HQ in Las Vegas occurred later (2002, per the leaked memo). The 1997 meeting is where Wilson first engaged with Davis on UAP intelligence.

At the time of the 1997 meeting, Wilson had discovered that a **WOSAP** (Advanced Aerospace Weapon Systems Application Program variant) was being funded from his DIA director's budget line — money he was nominally responsible for — without his knowledge or access. The program had **"hundreds of people"** and was working with material "that doesn't seem to be of human origin." Wilson was denied access despite his J2/DIA oversight role.

The facilitation chain for Wilson's meeting with Davis included [[john-alexander]] and Davis's contacts at the [[nnsa]] Nevada site — both the Director of Intelligence and Director of Counter-Intelligence at NNSA Las Vegas, who were AFIO (Association of Former Intelligence Officers) members alongside Davis.

## The Wilson-Davis Meeting (Las Vegas, 2002)

In 2002, Wilson met with physicist and aerospace scientist [[eric-davis]] in the **parking lot of [[egng]] Special Projects headquarters in Las Vegas**. Davis had previously briefed Wilson in the Pentagon (1997) on UAP intelligence.

During the Las Vegas meeting, Wilson expressed extreme frustration that a private contractor consortium was operating a UAP **reverse-engineering program** and had successfully denied him access despite his seniority. Specifically:
- Wilson traced a relevant Special Access Program (SAP) through channels
- He was told by the program's security officer that he did not have a "need to know" — despite his J2 oversight role
- He was explicitly told he was not "read into" the program and would not be
- He expressed belief that the program was real and was being hidden from appropriate oversight

This meeting is documented in the **[[wilson-davis-memo]]** — a contemporaneous record of the conversation that Davis maintained and which became public in 2019. It is one of the most significant UAP documents in the modern era.

The choice of EG&G's Special Projects HQ as the meeting location is significant: [[egng]] was itself the operational manager of the most sensitive black programs, making their headquarters a symbolically (and perhaps practically) appropriate location for a conversation about access being denied by a private contractor.

## Significance

Wilson's account — if accurate — establishes that:
1. A private contractor consortium was operating a UAP reverse-engineering program as of at least the late 1990s
2. The program had security arrangements that could block access even to the J2/DIA level
3. A senior military official with institutional motivation to be skeptical of such claims believed the program was real

[[greg-rogers]]' 1992 sighting at [[cape-canaveral]] (see [[1992-cape-canaveral-saucer]]) provides a physical correlate: a reverse-engineering test in progress — consistent with the program Wilson was denied access to five years later.
