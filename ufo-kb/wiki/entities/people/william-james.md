# William James

Philosopher, psychologist, and physician. Professor at Harvard. Founding figure of American pragmatism and functional psychology. Co-founder of the American Society for Psychical Research (ASPR) in 1885; collaborated with the British [[society-of-psychical-research]] (SPR). Extensively investigated medium Leonora Piper and concluded, after years of controlled sittings, that her abilities could not be explained by fraud.

Referenced in [[0qlppHc3-gg]] as part of the argument that serious academic investigation of telepathy and anomalous consciousness phenomena has a legitimate 150-year history — predating and contextualizing the current work of [[diane-powell]] and [[ky-dickens]].

## Background

1842–1910. MD from Harvard Medical School; never practiced medicine but taught physiology, psychology, and philosophy at Harvard for 35 years. Author of *Principles of Psychology* (1890), *The Varieties of Religious Experience* (1902). His psychical research work was conducted seriously but remained peripheral in his published legacy.

## Notable Claims

- Telepathy and other psychical phenomena deserved rigorous scientific investigation, not dismissal.
- The SPR methodology — controlled experiments, standardized protocols, systematic case collection — was the appropriate response to anomalous reports.

## The Spectral Revolution Prediction

[[jason-reza-jorjani]] in [[ABw7EsWh2Us]] cites James's prediction that parasychology would be "the next great scientific revolution" — a term Jorjani calls the **[[spectral-revolution]]**. James made this prediction while serving as president of the Society for Psychical Research in the 1870s. Jorjani pairs him with [[henry-bergson]] (SPR president in the 1910s, who debated Einstein) to establish that psychical research once had genuine elite academic legitimacy before being deliberately marginalized.

Jorjani's thesis: the Spectral Revolution "didn't happen... because of the extremely serious psychological and sociological implications of mainstream acceptance of things like telepathy precognition clairvoyance." Not a failure of evidence — a deliberate suppression.
