# Yevgeny Primakov

## Background

Yevgeny Primakov was a senior Soviet and Russian intelligence official who served as head of the SVR (foreign intelligence), Foreign Minister, and Prime Minister of Russia. He was a key architect of post-Soviet Russian foreign policy and a central figure in Russia's intelligence establishment for decades.

## UAP Connection

In [[09KP8XVf5nY]], [[harold-malmgren]] describes a personal back-channel relationship with Primakov spanning years. This back-channel was reportedly shut down by the Obama administration in 2009. The termination of this channel is presented as part of the broader pattern of suppressing unofficial diplomatic networks — particularly those that might carry information touching on UAP awareness shared between the superpowers.

The Soviet/Russian parallel awareness of the [[ufo-nuclear-nexus]] is an open question raised by Malmgren's account: if both superpowers were monitoring UAPs at nuclear test sites during the Cold War, back-channels between senior officials on both sides would have been the logical vector for discreet mutual acknowledgment.

### Larson-Primakov Back-Channel (1993)

In [[ATJwqp5twAg]], [[ralph-moat-larson]] describes a personal back-channel with Primakov that operated approximately weekly in 1993 as part of post-Soviet intelligence cooperation work. Larson says Primakov was "a brilliant man" who he believes would have become Prime Minister earlier had he not died of health problems before Putin. 

Primakov proposed a joint secret operation: "You have your means [CIA]. We have our means [SVR] in the Middle East. CIA keeps Rabin alive. SVR keeps Arafat alive." Larson submitted this idea to Langley — "they thought I was insane" and it was never implemented. Rabin was assassinated in 1995 (by a Jewish extremist, not an Arab actor); Arafat died in 2004. Larson offers no UAP content in this back-channel context; it is included as context for the kind of unofficial diplomatic creativity that back-channels could carry.

This is a second, independent confirmation that Primakov was willing to engage in unconventional back-channel diplomacy on major geopolitical questions — consistent with the Malmgren account.
