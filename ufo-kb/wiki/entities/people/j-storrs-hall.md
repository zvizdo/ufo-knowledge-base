# J. Storrs Hall

## Background

J. Storrs Hall is a nanotechnology theorist whose 1989 paper and a 1993 paper presented at a NASA conference introduced the **utility fog** concept — a swarm of ~100-micron robots ("foglets") that link arms in 12 directions to form programmable solid matter. Hall has continued to publish on molecular nanotechnology, advanced fabrication, and the institutional barriers to its public development.

## Notable Claims

- **Utility fog design.** Each foglet is armed with rudimentary local compute, can share power and data with neighbors, and can rearrange to form arbitrary objects on command. A cubic inch of foglets packs on the order of **16 million processors**.
- **Pixel-mode upgrade.** In follow-on interviews, Hall described enhancements (light-emitting antennae, optical-matching layers) that would let the outer skin of a foglet aggregate function "basically as a 3D TV screen" — supporting environmental camouflage that could mimic rock, sky, or other surfaces.
- **Holodec analogue.** Hall's stated motivation: a foglet environment could realize "Star Trek's Holodeck — one that could actually cut you and make you bleed" — physical interaction, not just visual illusion.
- **Suppression claim.** Publicly stated that significant nanotech research was actively suppressed, contributing to the visible civilian lag behind capabilities held in classified hands.
