# Dorit Hofflight

Assistant to [[donald-menzel]] at the Harvard Observatory in the early 1950s. Later became a well-respected astronomer in her own right. In her memoirs she documented witnessing [[donald-menzel]] ordering the destruction of one-third of the Harvard photographic plate collection — directed at his secretary, not at any astronomer — and the destruction of observational log books.

## Background

At the time of the events, Hofflight was in a junior position under Menzel. The fact that she later achieved standing as a respected astronomer makes her account credible within the astronomical community's own terms.

## Notable Claims

- Menzel ordered his *secretary* (not any astronomer) to go and throw away one-third of the Harvard plates.
- He also destroyed log books that tracked which plates existed and when observations were made — eliminating the metadata needed to know what had been destroyed.
- She attempted to protect some plates from disposal.
- Menzel retaliated against her following her resistance.

## Significance

Hofflight is the primary eyewitness source for the Harvard plate destruction. Her memoir account converts what might otherwise be a rumor into documented history. The episode is directly relevant to [[beatriz-villarroel]]'s findings because the Harvard plate archive from the early 1950s could have independently corroborated (or refuted) the transient observations in the [[palomar-observatory]] plates.
