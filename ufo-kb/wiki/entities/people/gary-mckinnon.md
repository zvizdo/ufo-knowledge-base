# Gary McKinnon

Scottish IT contractor and lifelong UFO enthusiast who, in 2001, used basic networking tools to scan and access approximately 97 US military and government computer networks — including the US Army, Navy, Air Force, NSA, DISA, DoD, and [[johnson-space-center|NASA Johnson Space Center]]. The Pentagon called it "the biggest military computer hack of all time." McKinnon's stated motive: personal verification that free energy and UAP propulsion technology was being suppressed.

## Background

McKinnon grew up near Bonnybridge, Scotland — a known UAP hotspot. He had one childhood sighting (reddish-orange light moving in an arc) and was a member of the British UFO Research Association (BUFORA). He had IT experience in banking and networking but no formal hacking training. His method: a Perl script scanning large IP blocks for Windows machines with blank or default passwords, then using LoftCrack for domain administrator access, and the commercial tool RemotelyAnywhere for graphical remote desktop control. At its largest, he ran this across 5,000 machines simultaneously. He used AltaVista and NiHii's IP index to map DoD subnets. Result: access to ~97 networks; total of potentially thousands of individual machines.

## Key Findings

### NASA Building 8 — UAP Photograph

Guided by [[donna-hare]]'s Disclosure Project testimony that Building 8 of Johnson Space Center processed satellite imagery containing UAP that were airbrushed before public sale, McKinnon located and accessed Building 8 machines (identified via Windows network audit commands). He found desktops with only two folders: "raw" and "processed." Using NASA's proprietary image viewer remotely, he watched a satellite image load slowly over a 56k dialup connection. A hemisphere — apparently Earth — appeared. Then a smooth, silvery, cigar-shaped object in lateral orbit (oriented horizontally relative to Earth, not descending toward it). No rivets, seams, antennae, or conventional structural features. No geodesic scale reference. Before the full image loaded, a NASA employee moved the mouse, right-clicked the LAN icon, and disconnected him.

### Non-Terrestrial Officers Spreadsheet

On a US Navy network, McKinnon found an Excel spreadsheet titled **"Non-Terrestrial Officers"** containing:
- ~30–40 names in initial-plus-surname format (none recalled)
- Ship names — none matching any known US Navy vessel
- A separate tab listing fleet-to-fleet and ship-to-ship transfers of exotic materials: **barium**, **strontium**, **molybdenum** (and possibly others)

McKinnon explicitly interprets this as a human-run space-based supply chain — not alien officers in uniform. The "non-terrestrial" designation means not Earth-stationed. The chemicals are consistent with high-K dielectric research relevant to the [[biefeld-brown-effect]]. McKinnon downloaded the spreadsheet; it was seized by the [[office-of-naval-intelligence]] along with all his hard drives at arrest.

### What He Did NOT Find

- No evidence for "Solar Warden" (which he believes is disinformation spawned after his case became public)
- No free energy technology
- No evidence of alien-government liaisons

## Arrest and Extradition

McKinnon was arrested in March 2002 by the UK National High-Tech Crime Unit (NHTCU) at his girlfriend's aunt's house. Initial UK assessment: 6 months or community service under the Computer Misuse Act 1995. After NHTCU officers visited [[office-of-naval-intelligence]] in Washington, everything changed. The US charged McKinnon with 7 counts of computer crime, each carrying a 10-year sentence. The 2003 UK–US extradition treaty — drafted in American English — removed the prior requirement for the US to demonstrate prima facie evidence of mal intent; phrases in McKinnon's charges appear nearly verbatim in the treaty language. US attache Ed Gibson told McKinnon's lawyer Karen: "We want to see him fry." The DOJ planned to try him under military order #1 — Guantanamo-equivalent conditions, no press, no family. By 2008, McKinnon had purchased potassium chloride and intended to die rather than face 70 years in an American prison. He was diagnosed with Asperger's syndrome in 2008–2009. In 2012, Home Secretary [[theresa-may]] blocked extradition, ruling it would give rise to an unacceptably high risk of suicide and therefore breach his human rights. He remains on the Interpol red list.

## Bifield-Brown Research

Independently of his hacking, McKinnon has been interested in the [[biefeld-brown-effect]] since 2007. He is building a CCTO (calcium copper titanate) capacitor experiment in a garden shed — 40mm discs, a 10-ton hydraulic press, gas and electric furnaces, high-quality silver paste electrodes — aiming to replicate the electrogravitic thrust effect and eventually test it in a vacuum. He cites [[charles-buhler]] (NASA electrostatics) and Gravitech as confirming the effect survives in vacuum. He notes the exotic chemicals from his non-terrestrial spreadsheet (barium, strontium) are precisely the high-K dielectrics that make the Bifield-Brown effect stronger.

## Alleged Implant

In 2006, McKinnon was woken from deep sleep by sharp pain in his left heel. In the morning: two perfectly circular 5mm holes with a skin flap. A double-puncture pattern identical to the injection method used by [[verichip]] (a subsidiary of Applied Digital Solutions; chips sourced from a Raytheon subsidiary). He has since developed two bumps at the site that have migrated. He suspects government tracking, not alien abduction. [[roger-leir]] is mentioned as the appropriate specialist (now deceased).
