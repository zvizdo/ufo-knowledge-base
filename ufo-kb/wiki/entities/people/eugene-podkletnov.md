# Eugene Podkletnov

## Background

Russian physicist who worked at Tampere University of Technology (Finland) in the 1990s, where he reported that a rotating YBCO (yttrium barium copper oxide) high-temperature superconductor produced a measurable reduction in the gravitational field above it — approximately 2% shielding. The claim was controversial; replication attempts produced mixed results. Podkletnov subsequently worked on an "impulse gravity generator" that allegedly produced a focused beam of gravitational force.

## Rotating Superconductor Experiments

The gravity-shielding claim was the first rigorous experimental assertion of this kind in the peer-reviewed literature (published in *Physica C*, though the paper was withdrawn before final print after press leaks). Subsequent attempts at independent replication at NASA, Boeing, and European labs were inconclusive.

The mechanism proposed is related to the rotation of the superconducting condensate: angular momentum quantization in the Cooper pair lattice produces frame-dragging effects that partially cancel local gravitational pull above the disk. This is distinct from [[salvatore-pais]]'s approach (which uses vibration rather than macroscopic spin) but shares the superconductor-as-gravity-mediator premise.

## Soviet Intelligence Lineage

When interviewed by journalist [[nick-cook]] for *The Hunt for Zero Point*, Podkletnov disclosed that **his father had been a Soviet intelligence agent** during WWII specifically tasked with retrieving [[victor-schauberger]]'s engineering files. This indicates an intelligence-level appreciation for implosion-based, rotation-based propulsion physics as early as the late 1940s — long before Podkletnov's own experimental work.

In the [[8TYMQOUDQBo]] interview, Pais discusses Podkletnov and Schauberger in the context of a lineage: Schauberger (implosion, rotation) → Podkletnov (rotating superconductor) → Pais (vibration, which Pais argues is more engineering-practical than rotation at extreme RPMs).

## Pais's Assessment

Pais acknowledges the rotating-superconductor lineage but says vibration is more engineering-practical: achieving 10⁴+ RPM in a superconducting disk is mechanically extremely challenging, whereas acoustic vibration of an electrically charged medium can achieve the same EM field amplification. The mathematics for spin and vibration are "highly similar."
