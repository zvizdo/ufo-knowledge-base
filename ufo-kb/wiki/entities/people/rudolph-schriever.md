# Rudolph Schriever

German technician recruited by SS General [[hans-kammler]] to work on disc-shaped aircraft at a facility in Prague, Czechoslovakia — part of the [[kammler-stab]] secret weapons program. Schriever is one of several researchers documented by [[nick-cook]] in [[hunt-for-zero-point]] as working on flying saucer designs under Nazi SS oversight.

## Disc Design

Schriever claimed to have worked on a **disc-shaped aircraft** in Prague. He stated its **first test flight occurred in February 1945**, reportedly achieving 40,000 feet altitude in 3 minutes. This performance claim — if accurate — would be extraordinary even by modern standards.

Cook notes this as one of the "pretty arcane stories" in the Kammler-Stab orbit, alongside [[victor-schauberger]]'s impeller and [[richard-miethe]]'s high-voltage designs.

## Context

Schriever's claim sits alongside a cluster of similar German disc-aircraft accounts from the Kammler-Stab period. Whether any of these craft achieved genuine anti-gravity or novel propulsion, or relied on conventional jet/rocket propulsion in an unconventional airframe, remains unverified. Cook treats them as significant circumstantial evidence that a serious German advanced propulsion research program existed — backed by the later Operation Paperclip absorption of these personnel into US programs.
