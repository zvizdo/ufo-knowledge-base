# Andrew Bloxom

Andrew Bloxom served as the ship's naturalist aboard the HMS Blonde during its early-19th-century Pacific voyage — [[richard-dolan]] likens him to the naturalist character in *Master and Commander*. His detailed diary of the voyage, published at the time, contains what is now recognized as one of the earliest published accounts of a USO (unidentified submersible object) sighting.

## The 1825 Account

The diary is meticulous throughout — detailed records of weather, geography, encounters, and natural history. Against this background of careful observation, the following entry stands out:

> At 3:30 a.m. on August 12, 1825, the Night Watch reported this orange spherical object rise from the ocean at 7°. [It] lit the deck of the ship so bright you could pick a pin off the deck.

Bloxom describes the object descending back into the water, rising a second time, then descending again. He compares its brightness to "red hot like a cannon shot" and estimates its apparent size as comparable to the moon or sun (with explicit note that there was no moon out that night, ruling out confusion). After this entry, the diary returns to its normal meticulous accounting, as though Bloxom simply observed and recorded without editorializing further.

## Significance

The account was published and has been in print for 200 years — it is available as a PDF. Its significance: this is not a retroactively collected oral account but a contemporaneous published record by a trained scientific observer. Dolan pairs this with the [[1971-uss-jfk-orb]] incident (also a glowing spherical USO near a naval vessel, ~150 years later) to suggest consistent behavior across two centuries.
