# Robert F. Kennedy

Robert Francis Kennedy (1925–1968), known as Bobby Kennedy, served as US Attorney General under his brother President John F. Kennedy (1961–1963), continuing in the role into the Johnson administration (1963–1964). He was elected US Senator from New York in 1964 and was the leading candidate for the Democratic presidential nomination when he was assassinated on June 5, 1968, in Los Angeles, immediately after winning the California primary.

**Attorney General tenure**: As Attorney General, RFK was the driving force behind prosecuting organized crime figures (particularly through the McClellan Committee) and enforced federal civil rights law in the South, including personally managing the Freedom Riders crisis and the University of Mississippi integration. His relationship with FBI Director [[j-edgar-hoover]] was deeply antagonistic — Hoover resented Kennedy's authority over the FBI and Kennedy's attempts to redirect its focus from Communism to organized crime.

**Assassination**: Kennedy was shot by Sirhan Sirhan at the Ambassador Hotel in Los Angeles. Sirhan's conviction and motive (Palestinian-Israeli conflict anger) have been questioned by critics who note that witness accounts suggest shots fired from a direction inconsistent with Sirhan's position, and that the number of bullet holes found may have exceeded Sirhan's gun's capacity. These anomalies feed conspiracy theories that parallel those around JFK's assassination.

**RFK Jr. connection**: [[rfk-jr]] (Robert F. Kennedy Jr.) is RFK's son. He appears in the KB primarily through his political positions, including his 2024 presidential campaign and his advocacy on vaccines, government transparency, and related issues. The Kennedy family's history with intelligence community conflicts (JFK assassination, RFK assassination, CIA antagonism) is backdrop to RFK Jr.'s positions.
