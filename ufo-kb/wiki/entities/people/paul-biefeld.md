# Paul Biefeld

An Ohio-based physicist who had studied alongside Albert Einstein in the 1920s. Biefeld is best known for his collaboration — however brief — with [[thomas-townsend-brown]], which gave the [[biefeld-brown-effect]] its name. His core contribution was the suggestion that **asymmetric capacitors** were the right experimental apparatus for generating electrogravitic thrust.

## The Capacitor Suggestion

When Brown was investigating whether electricity could induce a gravitational field, Biefeld offered the decisive experimental direction: "I think it might be a capacitor." Brown then named the effect in Biefeld's honor. This conversation established the experimental architecture — asymmetric charged plates with a dielectric between them — that Brown would pursue for the rest of his life.

## Affidavit Significance

Detractors of Brown historically claimed he never actually met Biefeld, dismissing the collaboration as retrospectively invented. The emergence of Biefeld's signed affidavit — confirming not only that they met but that he witnessed "anomalous effects unaccounted for in conventional known physics" — is described by [[paul-schatzkin]] as "the first time we have an objective observer saying that there is some anomalous effect here."
