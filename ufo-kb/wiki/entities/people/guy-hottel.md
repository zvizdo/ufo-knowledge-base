# Guy Hottel

FBI field agent best known for authoring the [[hottel-memo]] (1952), which describes an Air Force officer reporting to the FBI about a crashed flying saucer containing an alien crew — the single most-read FOIA document on the FBI's public website.

## Role in the Condon Network

[[jesse-michaels]] presents Hottel as a structural link between the UFO secrecy apparatus and the debunker-as-asset strategy. While [[edward-condon]] was being investigated for communist sympathies in the late 1940s and early 1950s, an FBI field dossier of derogatory reports was being assembled on him in the background — apparently by or under Hottel.

The implication: Hottel, who definitively knew about UFOs (as evidenced by the 1952 memo he authored), was simultaneously building blackmail material on a man who had written the [[mcmahon-secrecy-act]] (1946) — the legal precursor to the [[atomic-energy-act-classification]]. Whether this was coordinated leverage to coerce Condon into his later role as lead debunker on the [[condon-commission]] (1966–1968) is speculative but structurally coherent.

## The Hottel Memo

The [[hottel-memo]] (March 22, 1950) — officially titled "Flying Saucers — Information Concerning" — relays an Air Force investigator's account of three flying saucers recovered in New Mexico, each with a crew of three small humanoids. Hottel forwarded the account to J. Edgar Hoover. The memo became famous after being released under FOIA; it remains the most-visited document in the FBI's online Vault.
