# Luis Alvarez

## Background

Nobel Prize-winning physicist (1968) and Manhattan Project veteran. In the late 1960s he led a team that performed the first cosmic-ray muon radiography scan of Khafre's pyramid at [[giza-plateau]]. The method works because muons from cosmic rays pass through rock — denser material absorbs more, creating a shadow map of internal voids.

## Findings

The team verified their equipment by successfully detecting all four corners and the apex of the pyramid. When scanning upward from the burial chamber, they found no additional chambers above — contradicting the hypothesis of undiscovered rooms. A subsequent Stanford team in the 1970s confirmed these results upward and additionally scanned shallow bedrock around the pyramid, where they found two large cavities in the ground (at ~69 ft and ~112 ft depth).

These verified scans stand in sharp contrast to the unvalidated 2025 claims by [[filippo-biondi]], whose team never published baseline validation results.

## WWII Foo Fighters and Robertson Panel

[[kirk-mcconnell]] explicitly notes that Alvarez — like [[hp-robertson]] — was assigned to investigate [[1942-foo-fighters]] during WWII, and then appeared on the 1953 [[robertson-panel]] that created the Project Blue Book debunking mandate. McConnell calls this continuity "not a coincidence": both men investigated anomalous objects during wartime, classified whatever they found, and were then appointed to the institutional machinery that suppressed public interest in UFOs post-war.

The combination of Alvarez's WWII Foo Fighter investigation, his Manhattan Project atomic weapons work, and his presence on the Robertson Panel places him at the intersection of all three domains that [[kirk-mcconnell]] identifies as the foundation of UAP secrecy: exotic physics, nuclear weapons, and systematic debunking.
