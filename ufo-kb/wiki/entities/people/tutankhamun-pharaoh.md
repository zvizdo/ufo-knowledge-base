# Tutankhamun

## Background

Born Tutankhaten, son of [[akhenaten-pharaoh]]. Became pharaoh as a child after [[nefertiti-queen]]'s death. His advisor Ay persuaded him to reverse the Amarna revolution: he renamed himself Tutankhamun, reopened the temples, restored the Amen priesthood, and moved the capital back. Reigned only briefly before dying under suspicious circumstances.

## Death

[[bob-brier]]'s examination of his mummified body found blood pooled at the back of the skull — consistent with a sustained blunt-force injury while lying on his back. The implication is that Tutankhamun may have been murdered, possibly by those who stood to gain from removing the last link to Akhenaten's lineage.

## The Tomb and Its Curse

Buried in a small, hurried rock-cut tomb in the Valley of the Kings (not a pyramid). The entrance bore a curse inscription. [[howard-carter]] discovered the tomb in 1922–23 after years of searching, famously saying "Yes, wonderful things" when he first shone a light inside.

The tomb contained extraordinary wealth despite Tutankhamun's political insignificance — suggesting rushed burial of inherited royal treasure.

Following the excavation, nearly every person involved suffered tragic outcomes: some died from inhaling toxic air (carcinogenic paint residue after 3,000 years of containment), others from disease, accidents, and seemingly unrelated events. Carter survived the longest.

[[luke-caverns]] notes the materialist explanation (toxic paint) probably accounts for some deaths, but wonders whether an additional "cosmic element" was at play — consistent with Egyptian beliefs about the persistence of the Ka and its ability to act on the world.
