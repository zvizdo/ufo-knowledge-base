# John Hutchinson

Canadian inventor working out of Vancouver whose anti-gravity experiments attracted DOD funding, overseen by Colonel [[john-alexander]]. [[nick-cook]] visited Hutchinson's home as a follow-up lead provided by [[boyd-bushman]] during Cook's investigation for [[hunt-for-zero-point]].

## The Experiments

Hutchinson had assembled a collection of exotic equipment — oscilloscopes, Van de Graaff generators, and other high-voltage apparatus — in a room in his home. Under laboratory conditions set up by Alexander's DOD team, none of the claimed anti-gravity effects could be replicated.

However, **off-camera and outside strict laboratory conditions**, team members witnessed objects flying across the room. The critical unresolved question: was this the **equipment doing it**, or was it [[john-hutchinson]] himself doing it — via psychokinesis?

Cook notes that "John always says it's the equipment, but there are others who became relatively convinced that there was some psychokinetic kind of influence on it all."

## Significance

The Hutchinson case sits at the intersection of:
1. DOD interest in anomalous physical phenomena (consistent with the Stargate remote viewing funding pattern — 18 years of CIA support for something with no scientific explanation)
2. The psychokinesis-vs-equipment ambiguity that runs through many anti-gravity adjacent phenomena
3. The question of whether certain UAP-relevant physics is observer-dependent — tied to the [[psionics-uap-interface]] concept

## The Hutchinson Effect

The "Hutchinson Effect" refers to the anomalous levitation and disruption of objects reportedly observed in his presence — a cluster of phenomena that has been described but never cleanly replicated under controlled conditions. It remains a contested but frequently cited data point in advanced propulsion fringe research.
