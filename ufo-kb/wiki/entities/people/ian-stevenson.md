# Ian Stevenson

Psychiatrist at the University of Virginia who conducted decades of systematic field research on children who claimed memories of previous lives. His primary methodology was to travel to the locations children described (especially in India, Sri Lanka, and Lebanon) and verify whether the claimed details matched verifiable facts about deceased individuals. Collected over 2,500 cases.

In [[0qlppHc3-gg]], Stevenson is referenced as a historical precedent for how academic researchers encounter resistance when investigating phenomena that are culturally accepted elsewhere. The anecdote: Stevenson traveled to India to "prove" past-life memories in children, only to be met with bemusement — Indian subjects questioned why he was trying to prove something they already knew was real.

## Background

Chair of Psychiatry at the University of Virginia. Founded the Division of Perceptual Studies (DOPS) at UVA, which continues after his death (2007). Published *Twenty Cases Suggestive of Reincarnation* (1966) and *Children Who Remember Previous Lives* (1987), among others. His work is largely dismissed by mainstream psychiatry but taken seriously by consciousness researchers.

## Notable Claims

- Children across multiple cultures spontaneously report specific, verifiable details about lives of deceased individuals they had no normal means of knowing.
- The strongest cases involve birthmarks or birth defects corresponding to wounds on the deceased individuals.
- Cultural context shapes how such reports are received — in India, past-life continuity is a baseline assumption, not an anomaly requiring proof.
