# Chris Gibson

Royal Observer Corps observer working on an oil rig in the North Sea (between the UK and Norway/Germany) when he witnessed an unusual aircraft formation in the late 1980s. The Royal Observer Corps was a disciplined British civilian aircraft-monitoring service, making Gibson a significantly more reliable witness than an ordinary civilian.

## The Sighting

Gibson looked up and saw:
- A **dart-like, black, arrow-shaped aircraft** flying at altitude
- Escorted by what he took to be **three or four F-111 swing-wing aircraft** (US Air Force jets based in the UK at the time)
- Being **refueled mid-air by a Boeing KC-135 tanker**

He drew a sketch immediately after the sighting. His training as a Royal Observer Corps member — which demanded immediate, disciplined documentation of aircraft observations — gave the sketch and description elevated credibility.

Gibson eventually sent the sketch to [[bill-sweetman]], who published it as part of his investigative reporting on the [[aurora-aircraft-program]].

## Identity Ambiguity

[[nick-cook]] notes one possible complication: an RAF Tornado (also a swing-wing aircraft) looks similar to an F-111. Despite Gibson's expertise, he could theoretically have mistaken a swing-wing RAF Tornado for an F-111. However, Cook states he has heard from "qualified RAF military pilots" who were sworn to secrecy that the Aurora was real — contextualizing Gibson's sighting as likely genuine.

## Significance

Gibson's sighting, combined with [[bill-sweetman]]'s NASP/budget analysis and the USGS sonic boom tracking toward Area 51, forms the core of the public evidentiary case for Aurora. As a trained observer with immediate documentation, Gibson's account carries more weight than most black-aircraft witnesses.
