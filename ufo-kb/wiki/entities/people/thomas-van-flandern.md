# Thomas Van Flandern

Astronomer affiliated with NASA and the U.S. Naval Observatory who investigated anomalous gravitational data from the Pioneer 10 and 11 probes in the 1980s. Working alongside [[robert-harrington]] (chief astronomer, U.S. Navy), Van Flandern identified gravitational influences consistent with a dead stellar companion — a "black sun" — orbiting our solar system at vast distance.

Their findings were published in a 1987 science encyclopedia (only surviving documented source, per [[matt-lroy]]), and subsequently discredited by astronomer Miles Standish who called the work a "mistake." Van Flandern died of throat cancer; he was a non-smoker. [[matt-lroy]] flags the near-simultaneous throat-cancer deaths of both Van Flandern and Harrington — both non-smokers, both working on the same suppressed data — as suspicious.

## Significance

Van Flandern represents the [[astronomical-data-suppression]] thesis: credentialed researchers who found evidence for a binary stellar companion were neutralized via professional discrediting and, potentially, physical harm. Their findings feed directly into [[matt-lroy]]'s [[binary-star-companion]] thesis.

> ⚠ Conflict: The mainstream astronomical community accepts that Pioneer's anomalous acceleration was explained by thermal radiation pressure from the probes' own heat sources (the "Pioneer Anomaly" resolved ~2012). Van Flandern's binary star interpretation was not the mainstream resolution.
