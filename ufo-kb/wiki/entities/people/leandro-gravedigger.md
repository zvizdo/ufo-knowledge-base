# Leandro (huaquero)

A huaquero (grave robber) who lives behind a chicken coop near the Nazca region and conducts shamanic tours of the local area. In 2015, he stumbled upon a cave containing over 200 body parts, isolated heads, limbs, appendages, and intact bodies covered in diatomaceous earth — the [[nazca-tridactyl-mummies]] discovery site.

## Background

Met French explorer [[thierry-jamin]] in 2016, which opened the path for systematic scientific investigation. Multiple sources consider his trustworthiness questionable: [[michael-mazzola]] states outright "not even a little bit" when asked if he trusts Leandro. He reportedly tried to charge $1,500 just for an interview, and charges ~$3,000 for cave tours.

The cave he shows tourists appears too small to have held 200 body parts. Mazzola and anonymous sources believe the real discovery site is a larger subterranean tunnel system whose location Leandro keeps secret, partly for commercial reasons and partly perhaps out of fear of local cartels.

## Notable Claims

- Discovered the cave by accident while walking and tripping on the wall.
- Initial story: bodies found inside stone sarcophagi in an underground citadel-like temple. Story later changed to: they were in this cave.

## Credibility Assessment

Financially motivated, inconsistent provenance accounts, shows researchers what appears to be the wrong cave. Essential as the original discovery source but unreliable as a guide or witness. See [[credibility-frameworks]].
