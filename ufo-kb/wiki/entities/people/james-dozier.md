# James Dozier

US Army Brigadier General kidnapped by the Italian Red Brigades in December 1981 — one of the highest-profile political kidnappings of the era. His location was among the operational remote viewing taskings given to [[joe-mcmoneagle]] under the [[stargate-program]].

## Connection to Stargate

In [[RofQnByLwOo]], Jesse Michaels cites the Dozier kidnapping as one of the operational cases that demonstrates McMoneagle's remote viewing utility: McMoneagle helped locate Dozier's position. Dozier was rescued by Italian special forces (NOCS) in January 1982 after 42 days of captivity.

The inclusion in the Stargate operational record strengthens the program's case as a real intelligence collection tool: the target was a named US general with active kidnapping — not a laboratory exercise.

## Harley Trent's Roses Session

[[harley-trent]], working the Dozier target all night, produced only one output: **the smell of roses**. Of the four possible apartment entry doors, the one with a rose garden planted around it was the correct door. The NOCS rescue team entered through that door; Dozier was inside. A single sensory datum from an exhaustive overnight session proved operationally decisive.

## Italian Interference and Post-Rescue Debriefing

McMoneagle recounts (in [[hjzMV8H0YIQ]]) that the Italians mobilized **approximately 800 of their own psychics** to work the Dozier case. The first two data pulls pointed to specific addresses — raided, both turned up embarrassing situations involving an Italian senator and other notables (not Dozier). The interference created a political problem; the US Army's Stargate data had to be routed through the **State Department** to reach Italian authorities without triggering further political fallout.

After his rescue, Dozier spent **three hours reviewing his own remote viewing data** with the Stargate team. His recommendation to the Army: *"You ought to have a school to train people how to think, to help their own rescue."* Dozier had actively catalogued details of his surroundings during captivity that correlated with elements the viewers had described.
