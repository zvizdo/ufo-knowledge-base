# Carl Jung

Swiss psychiatrist and psychoanalyst; contemporary of [[sigmund-freud]] and founder of analytical psychology. Coined the term **synchronicity** to describe meaningful coincidences that resist causal explanation. His encounter with the scarab beetle is Wargo's canonical example of a self-fulfilling time loop in [[hXYdkcv5TtY]].

## The Scarab Beetle Case (Time Loop Archetype)

Jung was treating a highly rationalist, intellectually defended patient he could not reach through conventional therapy. During a session, the patient recounted a dream in which she was gifted a golden scarab beetle — a deeply meaningful image she could not interpret. As she spoke, a live scarab beetle landed on the window of Jung's office. Jung took it and handed it to her.

The event was paradigm-shifting for the patient. [[eric-wargo]] uses it as his **canonical time loop example**:

1. The patient's precognitive dream (a signal from her future) changed her attentional state
2. That attentional change affected her behavior and location (she was in Jung's office telling him about it)
3. Jung, noticing the beetle, handed it to her — producing exactly the event she had dreamed
4. The dream made the loop inevitable, yet the patient could not have consciously orchestrated it

The loop works because the dream gave partial, symbolic information that changed behavior without revealing the full causal chain — preserving the conditions for the self-fulfilling outcome.

## Synchronicity vs. Precognition

Wargo argues that what Jung called *synchronicity* (meaningful coincidence without causal connection) is better understood as *precognition*: the "coincidence" is the convergence of a precognitive signal with its fulfillment. The acausal framing was Jung's workaround in the absence of a physics model. [[quantum-brain-precognition]] provides the physics model Jung lacked.
