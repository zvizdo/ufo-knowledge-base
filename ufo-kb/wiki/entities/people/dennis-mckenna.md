# Dennis McKenna

Ethnopharmacologist and younger brother of [[terence-mckenna]]. While Terence is the more publicly visible figure, Dennis provided the scientific and botanical grounding for their shared work on tryptamines and consciousness. The phrase **"nature is drenched in DMT"** — one of the most often-cited statements about DMT's ubiquity — is Dennis's.

## DMT Ubiquity Claim

Dennis's observation: DMT is so common in the plant kingdom, being a simple two-enzymatic-step metabolite from tryptophan, that if all plants were tested, it would be found in essentially all of them (at varying concentrations). This is cited by [[andrew-gallimore]] in [[8XD1ZiuhXoY]] as evidence that DMT's presence in nature is not a coincidence — it is a "message implanted in reality," discoverable only by an organism with sufficient cognitive advancement to isolate and use it.

## La Chorrera (1971)

Dennis and Terence McKenna traveled to La Chorrera, Colombia, in 1971, where they participated in psilocybin mushroom sessions and what they later called "the Experiment" — an attempt to modulate tryptamine visionary phenomena through a specific vocal/mental technique. The results were described as a profound ontological disruption. Their collaborative theory from this period — published as *The Invisible Landscape* (1975) — attempted to ground psychedelic phenomenology in information theory and quantum physics, prefiguring later frameworks like Gallimore's.
