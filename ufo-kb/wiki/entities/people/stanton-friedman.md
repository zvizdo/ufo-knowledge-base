## Background

Stanton Friedman worked as a nuclear engineer for major US defense contractors before leaving the field to pursue full-time UFO research — a career transition that was unusual and professionally costly, but which gave him a distinctive credibility in the research community. His engineering background provided him with both the technical vocabulary to assess UAP-related claims and informal access to Q-cleared colleagues and former colleagues in the defense and nuclear industries.

Friedman authored multiple books on UFOs and the Roswell incident and was for several decades among the most publicly recognized and academically credible voices in the UFO research field. He died in 2019.

## Notable Claims

- Friedman received the testimony of [[robert-sarbacher]] — former head of Washington National Laboratories and government scientific advisor — and published it in UFO research literature. Sarbacher's statement that "UFO secrecy is classified two levels above the hydrogen bomb" became one of the most cited insider claims in the research community, in large part because Friedman's own nuclear engineering background lent authority to his judgment of Sarbacher's credibility.
- His nuclear engineering career gave him unusual access to the Q-cleared community, making him a natural conduit for classified-adjacent testimony from former government scientists.

## Strieber's Firsthand Account (ABOP8ZJsyIk)

[[whitley-strieber]] describes Friedman as "the most driven person I ever knew — he wouldn't stop." Strieber's personal interaction with Friedman centered on the [[robert-sarbacher]] introduction: Friedman brought Strieber to Sarbacher before *Communion* was published, making Friedman the critical connector between the two. The introduction led to Sarbacher's last disclosure (the Battel molecular grid) and his death the night Strieber mailed him his manuscript.

Strieber also notes that Friedman was "coy" about whether he himself had been personally "activated" — i.e., whether he'd had a close encounter. He died essentially "running through an airport" — a characterization of his relentless pace until the end.

## Richard Dolan's Assessment (GQlWf54K_7Y)

[[richard-dolan]] provides a nuanced, mixed portrait of Friedman:

**Gatekeeper behavior**: When Dolan first entered the field (2001, St. Louis conference, ~15 people in attendance), Friedman checked him out and immediately pushed the question: "What's your opinion on Bob Lazar?" Dolan didn't have one yet — he'd spent five years on 1941–1973 and hadn't gotten to Lazar. Friedman was clearly angling for Dolan to come out against Lazar. Dolan didn't take the bait: "You can't — don't bully me." They had a "fine and cordial relationship" afterward, though Dolan was never close to Friedman the way others were.

**The Lazar Problem**: Friedman was "so hardcore against Lazar" that Dolan considers it "a larger problem" in Friedman's methodology. Dolan's logic: if you believe crash retrievals happened and reverse engineering is ongoing, you *must* assign non-zero probability to the most coherent story about having worked on a recovered craft (Lazar's). To simultaneously say "I believe there's a crash retrieval program" and "Lazar is 100% a fraud" is intellectually incoherent. Friedman fixated on Lazar's claimed MIT education credentials — which Dolan attributes to MIT pride (Friedman was himself an MIT alumnus).

**Intelligence Community Proximity**: Friedman's close friendship with [[bill-moore]] — who admitted at Mufon 1989 to running disinformation operations — is noted without condemnation. Dolan speculates Friedman became aware of and attracted to the Lazar question partly through Moore, and that his hostility to Lazar may reflect the same intelligence-community influence.

**Final Assessment**: "I admire and always respected and liked Stanton Friedman and his contribution to where we now are at is extremely important." Dolan uses the Marilyn Monroe "couldn't cook" analogy: "You're going to hold that against her?"
