# Morgan Robertson

American merchant mariner and science fiction author; active around the turn of the 20th century. Known primarily through the lens of precognition research for his 1898 novella *Futility*.

## Futility (1898) and the Titanic

Robertson's novella *Futility* (later republished as *The Wreck of the Titan*) describes:
- The largest ocean liner ever built, the SS **Titan**
- Its collision with an **iceberg** on an **April night in the North Atlantic**
- On its **third voyage** between Liverpool and New York
- Elite passengers who perish because there are **not enough lifeboats**

Fourteen years later, in April 1912, the RMS **Titanic** — the largest ocean liner ever built at that time — struck an iceberg on its first voyage in the North Atlantic and sank, killing over 1,500 people, many due to insufficient lifeboats.

The correspondences are sufficiently specific (ship name, season, ocean, direction, elite passenger composition, lifeboat insufficiency) that the case is cited in nearly every book on ESP and precognition as a canonical example — and cited in [[hXYdkcv5TtY]] by [[eric-wargo]].

## Wargo's Framing

Wargo acknowledges Skeptics' argument that in a world of chance, such coincidences will occasionally occur and be selected as meaningful in hindsight. He counters that the Robertson case is merely the most famous among a dense pattern of artist precognition — which, when studied systematically, appears across countless cases involving *personal* upheavals (not just famous events), suggesting a real phenomenon rather than cherry-picked coincidence.
