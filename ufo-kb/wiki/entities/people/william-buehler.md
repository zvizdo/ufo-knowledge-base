# William Buehler

William J. Buehler was a metallurgist at the Naval Research Laboratory (NRL) in Washington, DC, who discovered [[nitinol]] — nickel-titanium shape memory alloy — in 1959. The discovery occurred while Buehler was researching heat-resistant materials for nose cone applications and noticed that a nickel-titanium alloy exhibited unusual properties: it deformed under stress but returned to its original shape when heated (the shape memory effect).

**Ambiguous answers on anomalous materials**: When subsequently pressed by researchers investigating whether nitinol's unusual properties (shape memory, superelasticity, and behavior at the molecular scale) might have been inspired by or confirmed through analysis of recovered anomalous materials, Buehler gave responses described as ambiguous — neither confirming nor denying such a connection. This ambiguity has been cited by researchers connecting nitinol to the hypothesis that its properties were first observed in recovered UAP crash materials.

**Nitinol's unusual properties**: Nitinol exhibits properties that were genuinely surprising to materials scientists: a shape memory effect recoverable through thermal cycling, superelastic behavior under loading, and unusual biocompatibility. These properties have made it commercially valuable in medical devices (stents, orthodontic wires) and aerospace. The question raised in UAP research is whether materials with these properties were observed in alleged recovered UAP debris before their synthetic discovery.

**NRL context**: The Naval Research Laboratory has been identified in multiple KB contexts as an institution with both classified materials research capabilities and potential exposure to anomalous materials. Buehler's work at NRL places nitinol's discovery in a classified-adjacent research environment.
