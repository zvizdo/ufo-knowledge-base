# Stephen Szara

Hungarian physician who, while working in communist Hungary in 1956, synthesized DMT and self-injected it — following the practice of the era where scientists experimented on themselves. His first experience: an overwhelming rush of complex geometric imagery that transported him to an alternate reality, qualitatively distinct from other known psychedelics (LSD, mescaline, psilocybin). He recognized immediately that this was something of a different order.

His subsequent studies at the hospital, injecting colleagues, showed that from the very first experiments, subjects reported not merely altered states but populated alternate worlds containing what would now be recognized as entity beings — diminutive, lively, multitudinous creatures. The machine-elf/NHI encounter motif appeared in DMT research from its very first controlled trials.

## Historical Significance

Szara's discovery is the foundational moment in DMT research. The entity encounter motif appeared immediately — not as a later cultural contamination (contra critics who argue Terence McKenna "implanted" the machine-elf idea) but from the first observations in a 1956 communist Hungarian hospital. [[andrew-gallimore]] cites this specifically to establish the pre-McKenna antiquity of the motif.
