# Ning Lee

University of Alabama Huntsville physicist whose research on spinning superconductors and gravitational wave generation was reportedly significant enough to be classified. Her story is a paradigm case of the pattern described in [[physics-lockdown]]: breakthrough research, government interest, and subsequent disappearance from public view.

## Research

Lee initially worked on **ring superconductors** in the 1990s, attempting to spin them to generate gravitational effects. The rings would break past a certain rotational threshold, pushing her toward a more solid-state approach.

Her central claims:
- **AC gravity**: An alternating gravitational current — much stronger than DC gravity, pulsed, with a helicoidal/caduceus emission geometry consistent with graviton emission
- **DC gravity**: A static gravitational effect that depends on the strength of the source's magnetic field rather than electric permittivity

[[dave-rossi]] and others correlate these two forms with [[bob-lazar]]'s "gravity A-wave" and "gravity B-wave" claims about UAP propulsion.

A paper by **Bob Baker** (MITRE Corporation, now deceased) — written for a high-frequency gravitational wave conference — reportedly states that Lee generated up to 11 kW of gravitational waves, describing both AC and DC gravity types. Only the abstract/introduction of this paper is publicly accessible.

## Disappearance

Lee reportedly sent an email claiming ~11 kW gravitational wave generation. The email was intercepted by a **West Point IP address**, and she was not heard from again in the public domain. She left the University of Alabama Huntsville shortly after. The conventional explanation would be that her work was debunked or abandoned — but [[dave-rossi]] notes:

- **Larry Smalley** (head of the UAH physics department) went and joined her — a significant institutional endorsement
- Smalley's name appears on a **2004 NASA-funded patent** involving an asymmetric barrel-shaped capacitor — directly connecting Lee's research tradition to the [[biefeld-brown-effect]] / [[thomas-townsend-brown]] lineage

This pattern — breakthrough result, government intercept, clearance, and disappearance into classified work — parallels the trajectories of researchers in [[physics-lockdown]].

## Significance for Inertial Mass Reduction

Lee's work provides an electromagnetic pathway to gravitational wave generation using superconductors. This is the proposed mechanism behind several aspects of [[gravity-manipulation-propulsion]], and is structurally connected to [[salvatore-pais]]'s patents on inertial mass reduction, which Rossi suggests may have been informed by her findings.

## Nick Cook's 2003 Interview

[[nick-cook]] personally met and interviewed Lee at a 2003 Washington DC conference on high-frequency gravitational waves (organized by the MIT Corporation). Cook's impression: Lee had a view that her science "should be for the people — very philanthropic in its nature and outcomes." She seemed to believe the technology should benefit humanity broadly, not be weaponized. Cook found her "an interesting character" who shared one of the conference sessions.

Within approximately a year of their meeting, Lee "just disappeared and no one knew what happened to her." Cook's account in [[G7Ns4Aq1tVc]] confirms this timeline aligns with the formation of [[ac-gravity-llc]] and receipt of a DOD contract with security clearance.

## Published Experimental Results

Lee's published results (multiple trials) claimed test masses lost between **0.5% and 2.1% of their weight** when suspended above rapidly spinning superconductors driven by alternating current. If accurate, this would break the conventional understanding of physics — unifying electromagnetism and gravity. As Lee said: "Nobody found any mistake in my papers... but people don't believe it. I know."

## CCP Pressure and Final Years

In 2008, the CCP approached Lee at her home and asked her to continue her research in China. She refused. In retaliation, they **banned her from entering China** — including to attend her own mother's funeral. This choice is cited as evidence of Lee's commitment to American research institutions.

In 2014, Lee was struck by a car. The shock caused her husband (who was with her) to suffer a fatal heart attack. Lee sustained brain impairment. She died in 2021.
