# B.F. Skinner

American behaviorist psychologist (1904–1990). Developed the framework of **operant conditioning** — the study of how behavior is shaped by consequences. Best known for Skinner Box experiments, the concept of reinforcement schedules, and his influence on behavioral psychology and education.

## Relevance to UAP Discourse

In [[fyX8V1XXmQM]], [[jacques-vallee]] explicitly invokes Skinner's reinforcement schedules as the theoretical mechanism underlying his [[control-system-hypothesis]].

### Reinforcement Schedule Insight

Skinner's key finding: the *schedule* of reward matters more than the reward itself for producing durable behavior change.

- **Continuous reinforcement** (reward every time): produces fast learning, but behavior extinguishes quickly when reward is withdrawn
- **Intermittent reinforcement** (reward unpredictably): produces slower initial learning, but the behavior becomes almost inextinguishable — subjects persist far longer after reward withdrawal

The classic example: a rat that receives a food pellet *every* time it presses a lever will stop pressing almost immediately when the food stops. A rat that receives pellets *randomly* will continue pressing far longer, because it never learns the reward has permanently ended.

### Vallée's Application

Vallée applies this to NHI-human contact at a civilizational scale:

> "If you want to change the behavior of somebody who drinks too much... you should not reward every time. If you do it every time you get fast learning, but then they go back to the way they were."

Applied to the UFO phenomenon: NHI appearing *sporadically, absurdly, and ambiguously* — rather than consistently and clearly — produces sustained civilizational influence. A clear, consistent landing on the White House lawn would produce brief astonishment followed by normalization. Millennia of sporadic, koan-like encounters adapted to each culture's symbolic vocabulary produces durable, near-inextinguishable effects on human belief, behavior, and narrative.

The [[1945-san-antonio-trinity-crash]] exemplifies this: a 3-ton craft observed by children, 20 days after humanity's first nuclear test, then apparently erased from official knowledge — maximally ambiguous, maximally absurd, and therefore maximally persistent as a behavioral/psychological influence.
