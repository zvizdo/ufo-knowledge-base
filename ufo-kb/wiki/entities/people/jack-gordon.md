# Jack Gordon

Head of [[lockheed-skunk-works]] at the time [[nick-cook]] was invited — in an unusual dispensation — to interview him inside the classified Palmdale headquarters building (normally journalists were kept to a sanitized "airlock" area across the railway tracks). Gordon succeeded [[ben-rich]] in the Skunk Works leadership.

## The 15 Aircraft Admission

During the interview, Gordon stated he had overseen **15 real flying aircraft** in his tenure at Skunk Works, but that he could **only talk about 12 of them**. Cook notes that "those missing three, to my knowledge, have never been revealed." This admission implies at least three acknowledged-internally-but-unacknowledged-publicly aircraft programs operating under Skunk Works as of the mid-1990s.

## The ASTRA Incident

As Cook was being escorted out of the headquarters building after the interview, he noticed an organizational chart depicting Skunk Works aircraft from the 1940s through the then-modern day. At the top of the chart — above what should have been the most recent acknowledged aircraft (either the YF-22 prototype or the Darkstar drone) — was the designation **ASTRA**.

When Cook asked his escort "What's Astra?", he was bundled out of the building immediately. Cook later confirmed with a subsequent Skunk Works head that this incident was "very well known internally" — suggesting it was significant.

Cook later noted the date of the visit was **April 1st** (April Fool's Day), leading him to wonder whether the ASTRA chart was a deliberate piece of disinformation — planted to be seen — or a genuine slip. A later Skunk Works director told Cook that ASTRA "hasn't flown yet" and that "it'll make an appearance sometime soon." See [[astra-program]].
