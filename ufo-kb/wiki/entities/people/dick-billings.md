# Dick Billings

American investigative journalist (Richard Billings); senior editor at *Life* magazine from the 1960s through the 1970s. At *Life*, Billings worked on JFK assassination-related reportage during the period when *Life* owned the Zapruder film and was conducting its own parallel investigation of the Kennedy killing. He subsequently served as **editorial director of the House Select Committee on Assassinations (HSCA)** investigation in the late 1970s — the Congressional inquiry that concluded (in contrast to the Warren Commission) that Kennedy was "probably" killed as the result of a conspiracy.

**HSCA significance**: The HSCA (1976–1979) reached conclusions significantly different from the Warren Commission: acoustic evidence (since contested) pointed to a second gunman; the committee found that the CIA and FBI had failed to adequately share information with the Warren Commission; and it concluded that organized crime figures had motive and opportunity to participate in the assassination. Billings as editorial director helped shape the investigative output of the most significant Congressional reassessment of the Kennedy killing.

**David Morales access**: Billings is specifically cited in the KB for his access to [[david-morales]] — the CIA paramilitary operations chief who made a drunken deathbed statement to friends suggesting he was involved in the Kennedy assassination ("We took care of that son of a bitch"). Billings profiled Morales and has been cited by [[danny-sheehan]] for providing context on the CIA operational network around the assassination.

**Credibility tier**: As a *Life* editor who worked the assassination beat and subsequently directed Congressional investigation reporting, Billings occupies the highest tier of documented mainstream-journalist access to the JFK operational network. His accounts are primary-source-adjacent — not a researcher constructing a theory but a journalist with contemporaneous access to the people involved.
