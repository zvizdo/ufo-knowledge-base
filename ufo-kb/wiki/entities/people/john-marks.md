# John Marks

John Marks was a U.S. State Department official who, in 1974, discovered a cache of [[mk-ultra]] financial records that had survived [[richard-helms]]'s 1973 destruction order. He brought these files to a congressman, initiating the Congressional investigations that became the Church Committee and Kennedy/Inouye hearings — the most comprehensive public exposure of MK-Ultra.

## The Discovery

When Helms ordered MK-Ultra files destroyed in 1973, the destruction was apparently not complete. Financial records — perhaps filed separately from operational records — were overlooked. Marks found these records in 1974 and recognized their significance. His decision to go to Congress rather than sit on the documents made public exposure of the program possible.

Marks later wrote *The Search for the Manchurian Candidate* (1979), the most comprehensive public account of MK-Ultra based on the surviving files.

## Significance

Without Marks's discovery, MK-Ultra might have remained permanently hidden. The program had no whistleblowers who came forward publicly, no public lawsuits prior to [[danny-sheehan]]'s civil case, and no investigative journalism trail. The entire known record of MK-Ultra traces back to the financial files Marks found.
