# Agnew Bahnson

North Carolina industrialist and aviation enthusiast who served as the primary patron of the 1957 gravity research conference at [[unc-chapel-hill]] — the gathering of top theoretical physicists ([[bryce-dewitt]], [[john-wheeler]], [[peter-bergmann]], [[richard-feynman]]) who were convening to work on gravity within the Einsteinian paradigm. The conference was simultaneously bound up with aerospace contractors: [[lewis-whitten]] of [[martin-corporation]]'s anti-gravity division attended, and the entire event was sponsored by Wright Air Development Center (see [[wright-patterson]]).

## Science Fiction and Anti-Gravity Obsession

Bahnson was sufficiently obsessed with beating gravity that he wrote a science-fiction novel titled *The Stars Are Too High* ([[stars-are-too-high]]), whose plot involves: a team of rogue Nazi scientists captured and brought to America, who collaborate with American counterparts to build man-made flying saucers using anti-gravity, then stage a **fake alien invasion** to end Cold War tensions between the US and Soviet Union.

The novel's premise — human-made saucers masquerading as alien craft, managed by a covert science program with Nazi inputs — is a barely-fictionalized account of what some researchers believe actually happened. The fact that the novel's author was simultaneously funding the real-world anti-gravity research program it describes amplifies its significance beyond ordinary science fiction.

Bahnson died in a plane crash — a detail Cook mentions in [[G7Ns4Aq1tVc]] as characteristically suspicious given his centrality to the anti-gravity ecosystem.

## Simultaneous Funding of Townsend Brown

Bahnson funded [[thomas-townsend-brown]]'s gravitator experiments at the same time he was sponsoring the UNC Chapel Hill conference. This dual investment — backing both the theoretical physics community searching for a mathematical framework for anti-gravity and the empirical experimenter who claimed to have already produced anti-gravity effects — gives Bahnson a unique structural position in the 1950s anti-gravity ecosystem.
