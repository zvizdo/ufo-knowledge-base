# Wilbert B. Smith

Canadian radio engineer employed by the Canadian Department of Transport, director of telecommunications and an expert in ionospheric research. Best known for running **Project Magnet** — Canada's official UAP research program (1950–1954) — and for a pivotal 1950 meeting with [[robert-sarbacher]] that produced one of the earliest documented official admissions regarding US government UFO research.

## The 1950 Sarbacher Meeting

Smith traveled to Washington in 1950 and met [[robert-sarbacher]] (Scientific Adviser to the Research and Development Board, atomic energy expert affiliated with Harvard's Undersea Observatory). Smith had been inspired by Frank Scully's book *The Flying Saucer Conspiracy* and sought official confirmation.

Sarbacher's reported statements (per Smith's memo to his superiors):

1. The US **is** studying flying saucers
2. The work is led by **[[vannevar-bush]]**
3. The subject is classified **two levels above the H-bomb**
4. The craft are **"not made by humans"**
5. US researchers are also studying the **"mental phenomena"** that accompany UAP encounters

This last point — that mental/psionic phenomena were under study from the very beginning — is a founding data point for [[psionics-uap-interface]].

## Project Magnet

Smith subsequently founded **Project Magnet** under the Canadian Department of Transport. His stated research aim was to use **Earth's magnetic field** for propulsion — suggesting he took Sarbacher's hints about the craft's operating principles seriously. Project Magnet ran 1950–1954 and was Canada's only official government UAP investigation.

A parallel effort, **Project Second Story**, was also established.

## Thomas Townsend Brown Connection

[[robert-sarbacher]] was later involved with [[thomas-townsend-brown]]'s 1956 Paris vacuum experiments on the [[biefeld-brown-effect]], arriving at the experiments in a black Cadillac. This suggests Sarbacher was functioning as an informal liaison between independent researchers and the US program. Smith's interest in magnetic propulsion may have had the same research lineage as Brown's electrogravitic work.
