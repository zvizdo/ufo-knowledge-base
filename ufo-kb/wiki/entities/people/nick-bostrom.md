# Nick Bostrom

Swedish-British philosopher at the University of Oxford. Founder of the Future of Humanity Institute. Best known for two works: the **simulation argument** and *Superintelligence* (2014).

## The Simulation Argument

Bostrom's 2003 paper "Are You Living in a Computer Simulation?" (and elaborated in *Superintelligence*, referenced in the transcript as his 2012 book) presents a trilemma: at least one of the following must be true:
1. Almost all civilizations go extinct before reaching technological maturity.
2. Almost no technologically mature civilizations choose to run ancestor simulations.
3. We are almost certainly living in a computer simulation.

The argument's elegance is that it requires no extraordinary physics — only that posthuman computation is feasible and that sufficiently detailed simulations of conscious beings are possible. [[riz-virk]] credits Bostrom's work as giving simulation theory its "coveted academic stamp of approval" after science-fiction authors like [[philip-k-dick]] and William Gibson had already explored the territory literarily.

The argument is noted in this transcript as sufficiently common-sense that even Neil deGrasse Tyson accepts a ~50% probability of simulation — described as Bostrom's essential thesis.

## Relevance to UAP

Bostrom himself does not engage with UAP discourse. His relevance here is as the academic foundation on which [[riz-virk]] builds — Virk "takes Bostrom's work to the next level" by pointing to quantum physics, information theory, and consciousness research as empirical evidence for the simulation.
