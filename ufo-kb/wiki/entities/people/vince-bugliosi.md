# Vince Bugliosi

Vincent Bugliosi (1934–2015) was the Los Angeles prosecutor who tried and convicted Charles Manson and his followers in 1971. He went on to author *Helter Skelter* (1974) — co-written with Curt Gentry — which remains the best-selling true crime book of all time and established the official narrative of the Manson murders.

## The Official Narrative

Bugliosi's "Helter Skelter" theory: Manson believed a race war was imminent (drawn from Beatles lyrics and the Book of Revelation), ordered his followers to stage the murders to trigger it, and chose the Tate house because of a prior connection to music producer Terry Melcher (who no longer lived there). The murders were designed to implicate the Black Panthers. The theory is compelling internally but, O'Neill argues, carefully constructed to exclude inconvenient evidence.

## The Suppression Problem

[[tom-oneill]] found that Bugliosi removed from his prosecution records the half-dozen CIA-connected peripheral figures who surrounded the Manson Family during 1967–1969. These included [[reeve-whitson]] and others whose FOIA requests later returned Glomar responses (CIA connection confirmed by neither-confirm-nor-deny). Bugliosi's prosecution records contained no mention of these individuals despite their documented presence in the Family's orbit.

O'Neill's interpretation: Bugliosi may have been instructed or pressured to exclude this evidence, or may have made a prosecutorial decision that the CIA angle would complicate conviction. The result was a narrative that served both the prosecution's needs and the CIA's interest in suppressing the [[operation-chaos]] connection.
