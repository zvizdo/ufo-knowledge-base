# Henri Bergson

French philosopher (1859–1941). Nobel Prize in Literature (1927). Professor at the Collège de France. Among the most celebrated philosophers of his era, famous for his work on time (*Time and Free Will*, *Creative Evolution*), consciousness, and intuition as a philosophical method. His *Creative Evolution* (1907) proposed *élan vital* (vital impetus) as the driving force of biological evolution — a framework explicitly at odds with mechanistic materialism.

Cited by [[jason-reza-jorjani]] in [[ABw7EsWh2Us]] alongside [[william-james]] as co-founding evidence that parasychology was not always marginal. Bergson served as president of the **Society for Psychical Research** (SPR) in the 1910s — at the same time he was considered a worthy debate partner for Einstein. This juxtaposition is central to Jorjani's argument: that parasychology was once rigorously academic and was deliberately pushed out of mainstream science for political rather than empirical reasons.

## Background

Bergson's engagement with psychical research was not peripheral to his philosophy — it was consistent with his core thesis that consciousness and intuition reveal aspects of reality inaccessible to mechanistic scientific analysis. His late work, *The Two Sources of Morality and Religion* (1932), engaged explicitly with mystical experience as data for understanding human consciousness.

His public debate with Einstein (the 1922 Bergson-Einstein controversy over the nature of time) demonstrated that he was operating at the highest level of contemporary scientific discourse simultaneously with his SPR engagement — lending credibility to the claim that the early psychical research movement was not fringe.

## Role in the Spectral Revolution Thesis

[[jason-reza-jorjani]] uses Bergson (alongside [[william-james]]) to anchor his claim that the [[spectral-revolution]] — the transformation of parasychology into an accepted science — was a real historical possibility that was deliberately foreclosed. Both Bergson and James were:
- Credentialed, elite academic figures
- Presidents of the SPR
- Operating when paranormal research was taken seriously in mainstream scientific discourse
- Convinced that telepathy and related phenomena deserved rigorous study

Their subsequent marginalization (as their tradition became "new age quackery" rather than foundational science) is Jorjani's evidence for deliberate suppression rather than scientific consensus.
