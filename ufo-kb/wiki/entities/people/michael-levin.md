# Michael Levin

Developmental and synthetic biologist at Tufts University. Known for work on bioelectricity and morphogenesis — how bodies know their shape. Most relevant to this KB: his discovery that **individual biological cells display goal-directed behaviour and intentionality**, independent of higher neural architecture.

## Cellular Intentionality

Levin's research shows that cells and simple organisms navigate toward outcomes they have not directly experienced — they appear to *want* things, not merely respond to chemical gradients. This challenges the standard picture in which intention is a cortical, brain-level property. In Levin's model, intentionality is a fundamental feature of biological information processing at all scales.

[[riz-virk]] cites Levin in [[aKZ_MUbuk_Q]] alongside [[carl-friston]]'s free energy principle to argue that **consciousness and intention are primary in biology** — not emergent side effects. Erwin Schrödinger's 1944 lecture series *What is Life?* theorized the same connection; Levin is the empirical confirmation.

In the simulation context: if intention is fundamental at the cellular level, then the simulation's physics was designed to *support* and *propagate* intention — consciousness is not an accident but a design specification.

## Flat Worm Memory Experiment

Described by [[rupert-sheldrake]] in [[wT5-hXWIkzM]]: Levin trained flat worms (planarians) to respond to particular stimuli, then decapitated them. Planarians regenerate — new heads grow back. When the regenerated worms were tested, they *retained the learned behavior* despite having entirely new heads (and new neural tissue).

**Standard interpretation**: memory was stored in the body cells and the new head accessed it — a form of whole-body cellular memory.

**Sheldrake's interpretation**: the worm's morphic field persisted through decapitation and regeneration; the new head resonated with the prior intact-form's morphic field, recovering the behavioral memory without molecular encoding. This experiment cannot distinguish between the two interpretations with current methodology.

Sheldrake uses this alongside the flat worm case to argue that Levin's work points to something beyond conventional molecular biology — and that Levin is privately more open to this interpretation than his public academic posture allows.
