# Karen Silkwood

29-year-old health and safety worker at Kerr-McGee's Cimarron nuclear reprocessing plant in Oklahoma — at the time, the sole US facility converting spent nuclear fuel rods from 103 power plants into bomb-grade plutonium powder via centrifuge separation.

## The Missing Plutonium

Silkwood discovered that approximately 40 lbs of 98% bomb-grade plutonium was unaccounted for. She initially concluded it was lost through sloppy plant management and notified the union she was helping to organize. What she did not know: the plutonium was being routed to Israel for nuclear weapons development in violation of the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty, and as a condition of that arrangement, was being shared with Iran under the Shah.

## CIA Facilitation

The CIA facilitator was [[theodore-shackley]] — at the time on the Israeli desk; former Saigon station chief who had run the assassination program in Southeast Asia (CORDS/Operation Phoenix). Shackley's involvement connects the Silkwood case to the broader continuity of CIA covert operations that [[danny-sheehan]]'s Christic Institute documented.

## Her Death

Silkwood was driving to meet New York Times reporter David Burnham with documents when her car was run off the road and she was killed. The documents were taken from the vehicle.

## Sheehan's Investigation

The [[christic-institute]] (under Sheehan) led the investigation. Multiple theories circulated about what happened; Sheehan's conclusion: pieces of two or three competing theories were true, but no single theory fully explained it. The investigation demonstrated CIA involvement in nuclear proliferation to US treaty partners.
