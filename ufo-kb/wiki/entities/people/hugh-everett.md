# Hugh Everett

American physicist (1930–1982). Princeton PhD student of [[john-archibald-wheeler]]. Formulated the **Many-Worlds Interpretation** (MWI) of quantum mechanics in his 1957 dissertation as a solution to the measurement problem in the Copenhagen interpretation.

## The Many-Worlds Interpretation

The Copenhagen problem: the wave function evolves smoothly via the Schrödinger equation until "measurement" collapses it to a single definite outcome. But the mathematics of *how* collapse happens has no derivation — it was inserted as an axiom. What happens to the other possibilities?

Everett's answer: nothing happens to them. The wave function never collapses. Instead, the observer becomes *entangled* with the observed system, and the universe branches into a superposition of all outcomes — each "branch" is a complete physical universe in which one outcome occurred. The apparent collapse is just the observer's inability to perceive the other branches.

The main objection: this proliferates universes at every quantum decision point — many times per Planck interval — which seems wildly unparsimonious. **[[riz-virk]]'s rejoinder**: if the universe is informational/computational, you don't need to copy a physical universe at every branch — you only need to copy the *information state*. Cloning information is cheap; cloning matter is not. The simulated multiverse is a computationally tractable version of MWI.

## Relevance to Simulation Theory

Virk reframes Everett's branching universe as a **simulated multiverse**: the simulator runs all branches, prunes suboptimal ones, and advances the best timeline. This transforms the "unparsimonious" objection into a feature — the proliferation of branches is the mechanism by which the simulation optimizes outcomes.
