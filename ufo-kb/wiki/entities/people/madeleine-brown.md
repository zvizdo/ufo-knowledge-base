# Madeleine Brown

Madeleine Duncan Brown (1925–2002) was a Texas advertising executive and socialite who claimed to have had a 21-year affair with Lyndon Baines Johnson beginning in 1948, a son from that relationship (Steven Mark Brown), and first-hand knowledge of the political conspiracy that killed President Kennedy.

**The Murchison mansion statement**: Brown's most significant and widely-cited claim concerns the evening of November 21, 1963 — the night before the Kennedy assassination. She claims a gathering occurred at [[clint-murchison-jr]]'s Dallas mansion, attended by [[j-edgar-hoover]], [[richard-nixon]], [[h-l-hunt]], and other Texas establishment figures. She says Lyndon Johnson arrived late and told her: *"After tomorrow those goddamn Kennedys will never embarrass me again — that's no threat, that's a promise."*

**Corroboration status**: The Murchison mansion meeting claim is among the most colorful and least independently verified in the JFK literature. No contemporaneous documentary evidence confirms the meeting occurred, and most of the alleged attendees (Hoover, Nixon) have documented alibi-level accounts placing them elsewhere or in ambiguous positions. Brown's account appears credible to some researchers based on its specificity and consistency across decades of retelling; skeptics note the absence of corroboration and the impossibility of verification.

**LBJ affair claim**: The affair itself is documented to the degree that Steven Mark Brown (born 1950) was acknowledged by Johnson's associates, though not by Johnson publicly. The nature of the relationship supports the claim that Brown had social access to Johnson's circle.

**KB significance**: Madeleine Brown is a named source in the [[jfk-assassination-ufo]] framework specifically because her account, if accurate, would establish that Johnson had foreknowledge of the assassination and that the Texas oil establishment ([[h-l-hunt]], [[clint-murchison-jr]]) was in some way informed or involved.
