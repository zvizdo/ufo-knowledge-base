# David Shindell

Retired USAF Captain at [[minot-air-force-base]], North Dakota; witness to the [[1966-minot-ufo-missiles]] incident — the most extreme publicly-documented case of a UAP apparently *triggering* (not merely disabling) a nuclear missile launch sequence. [[robert-hastings]] conducted a phone interview with Shindell and details the event in full.

## Background

Shindell came forward publicly alongside [[robert-salas]] as a corroborating witness for the broader UFO-nuclear pattern. He was at his man-post inside the launch capsule during the 1966 Minot incident.

## 1966 Minot Launch Indicator Incident

When a UAP ("objects with flashing lights, ~80-100 ft wide") flew over alpha-flight to oscar-flight giving erratic indications to launch sites:

1. Shindell received an **actual launch indicator signal** — meaning his console showed a countdown had been initiated, as if he had received a launch order from the National Command Authority
2. He immediately flipped an **inhibit switch**, which paused the countdown
3. However, a single inhibit switch only *delays* — at the end of the expired time period, the missile would launch automatically unless a **second inhibit switch** was activated
4. He and his partner "were freaking out" — if the second inhibit hadn't come in, "*that one missile would launch theoretically*"
5. This had **never happened before or after** in his experience

[[robert-hastings]] frames Shindell's specific horror: "This could be World War III if these missiles get off the ground."

## Nuclear-Risk Significance

The Soviet Ukraine case ([[1982-soviet-ukraine-missiles]]) is the only other documented incident where UAP interaction resulted in an *activation* rather than a deactivation. But in the Soviet case, the system shut down on its own. In Shindell's case, a human action was required to prevent a real launch. This makes the Minot 1966 incident arguably the closest documented near-launch caused by a UAP.

## Secrecy

After the incident, Shindell and his crew were told: "We as a crew were never to speak about this again, and we were told that essentially it never happened."
