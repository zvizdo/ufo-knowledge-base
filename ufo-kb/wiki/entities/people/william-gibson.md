# William Gibson

American-Canadian science fiction author; widely credited with coining the concept of "cyberspace" (*Neuromancer*, 1984). Referenced in [[hXYdkcv5TtY]] for *The Peripheral* (2014) as the best available narrative model for quantum temporal communication technology.

## The Peripheral — Quantum Time Communication Premise

*The Peripheral*'s premise (TV series adaptation also praised by Wargo): a video gamer in the near-future American South is piloting a drone in what she believes is a simulator — a futuristic city. She witnesses what she thinks is an NPC being murdered. She then receives a call from a publicist in 22nd-century London who explains she was piloting a real drone in future London via a **quantum computer server** and witnessed a real murder. He enlists her help cross-century.

[[eric-wargo]] treats this as the best fictional model for:
1. **Quantum computers as temporal bridges**: a server left plugged in long enough develops the ability to communicate backward to its own start-up moment
2. **3D printing from future information**: the future can send *instructions* to present-day fabricators — building future technology in the present using present manufacturing capacity
3. **Drone operation across time**: a pilot in 2125 controls hardware in 2025 via the quantum server — producing UAP-like phenomena (apparently advanced craft operated remotely by beings from another era)

Wargo notes Gibson — like most prescient sci-fi authors — is probably precognizing the actual next paradigm rather than merely extrapolating. He previously predicted cyberspace; Wargo thinks *The Peripheral*'s temporal quantum computing model is the next real thing.

## Relevance to UAP

The *Peripheral* model provides a concrete mechanism for UAP that reconciles:
- Advanced craft apparently *manufactured* in the present (using 3D-printing or equivalent technology)
- Controlled by operators in the *future* via quantum communication
- Appearing before witnesses with no conventional propulsion visible
- Producing electromagnetic and temporal anomalies consistent with time-field effects

This is distinct from the wormhole physical time machine model — the craft need not travel through time; it is *built here, now*, using instructions transmitted backward from the future.
