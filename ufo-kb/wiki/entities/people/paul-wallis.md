# Paul Wallis

Biblical scholar and author; former archdeacon in the Church of England. Best known for *Escaping from Eden* (2020) and subsequent works that reinterpret ancient texts — the Bible, Sumerian tablets, the Vedas, and other canonical religious documents — as records of historical contact with non-human intelligence rather than as purely theological or mythological material.

**Ancient texts as NHI records**: Wallis's central thesis is that texts describing "gods" (Elohim in the Hebrew, Annunaki in Sumerian, Devas in Sanskrit) should be read as accounts of interactions with extraterrestrial or non-human beings who intervened in human development. This is the ancient-astronaut interpretive framework applied with attention to primary-language analysis and theological training rather than the cruder pop-history versions. Wallis's Church of England background gives him unusual credentials for this interpretive approach — he reads the texts with the linguistic and theological competence to recognize where standard translations smooth over unusual passages.

**Lake Van research team**: Wallis is a member of [[matt-lroy]]'s interdisciplinary research team investigating the [[lake-van]] megalithic region in southeastern Turkey. His role is interpreting the textual and mythological dimensions of sites that Lroy investigates archaeologically and physically. The collaboration represents the pairing of on-site field investigation with ancient-texts analysis — combining physical evidence (megalithic structures, underwater formations) with textual traditions that may encode historical memory of the same region.

**Publication and reach**: Wallis's *Escaping from Eden* reached substantial audiences through alternative history channels and has been cited alongside Zecharia Sitchin and Graham Hancock as a mainstream-accessible ancient-astronaut-adjacent text. His Church of England background provides a credibility differential that distinguishes his work from non-credentialed authors in the same genre.
