# Frank Scully

Frank Scully was an American journalist and author who published *Behind the Flying Saucers* in September 1950 — the first commercially successful book claiming that the US military had recovered crashed flying discs with non-human occupants. The book was primarily based on accounts provided by Denver oilman [[silas-newton]] and his associate, identified in some accounts as a "Dr. Gee."

## Relationship to FBI documentation

The FBI's 62-HQ-83894 case file documents Newton's claims through intermediary Koehler in January 1950 — eight months before the Scully book's publication. This establishes that the disc-recovery narrative (three-foot occupants, near-indestructible metal, Mojave Desert location) was already circulating in FBI-documented intelligence channels before Scully published it.

The book was subsequently discredited: Newton was exposed as having a prior fraud conviction, and the disc-recovery claims were attributed to his confidence schemes. However, the book's occupant-height detail (approximately three feet tall) is consistent with the independently documented [[hottel-memo]] (March 1950), also in FBI records, suggesting the narrative had multiple independent sources beyond Newton's testimony.

## Significance

*Behind the Flying Saucers* was widely read and shaped the public's expectation that the US government was concealing recovered disc technology and occupants. Whether or not Newton's specific Mojave Desert claims were true, Scully's book established the cultural template for disc-recovery narratives that persisted through the 1970s and into the Roswell literature of the 1990s.
