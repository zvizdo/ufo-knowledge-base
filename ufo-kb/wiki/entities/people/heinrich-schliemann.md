# Heinrich Schliemann

## Background

19th-century German businessman and self-taught archaeologist. Deeply passionate about Homer's *Iliad*, he refused to accept the academic consensus that Troy was purely mythological. Acting on personal conviction and careful reading of Homer and Thucydides, he funded and led excavations at Hisarlik (modern Turkey) in the 1870s — and found Troy.

## Significance

His story is the canonical example cited by [[luke-caverns]] and [[jesse-michaels]] of what "vigilante archaeology" can achieve: personal passion, willingness to trust ancient texts as containing historical memory, and action without institutional permission overturning the professional consensus.

He is contrasted with the "sterilized" academic model that dismissed Homeric geography until Schliemann proved it. The same argument is applied to the Younger Dryas flood narratives, Plato's Atlantis, and the Bible's Exodus accounts.
