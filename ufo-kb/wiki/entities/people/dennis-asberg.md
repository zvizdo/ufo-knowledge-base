# Dennis Asberg

Swedish entrepreneur, diver, and treasure hunter. Co-founder of [[ocean-x]], an exploration company specializing in Baltic Sea wreck retrieval and anomaly investigation. Grew up in a family with diving and exploration interests; his father was a diver.

## Background

Asberg's commercial diving career includes finding shipwrecks with valuable cargo — most notably a wreck containing 1600-vintage wine (among the oldest in the world), cognac, and champagne destined for the Russian Tsar. He also discovered a Russian Tsar-era submarine (built 1904, used in the Russo-Japanese War) in Swedish waters in 2015, which attracted Swedish security service (SÄPO) interest.

Before the [[2011-baltic-sea-anomaly]] discovery, Asberg had no interest in UFOs or the paranormal. His prior encounter — a 1996 UAP sighting over a Swedish military facility — changed his worldview completely but he kept it private for years.

## 1996 UFO Sighting

In summer 1996, at approximately 3:30 AM, Asberg woke spontaneously and saw a large disc-shaped craft hovering 1–1.5 km from his apartment window, above a nearby military installation in Sweden. The object appeared elongated from the side (cigar/hotdog shape), then rotated to reveal it was circular before accelerating away in under 2 seconds. He estimated its diameter at 600–700 m based on comparison with the familiar installation below. The sighting lasted 14–15 seconds.

He reported to [[ufo-sweden]] by email; received no reply. He contacted local military connections and local papers; found nothing. He kept the sighting private for years.

The military installation was later confirmed to involve nuclear material (plutonium enrichment). See [[ufo-nuclear-nexus]] and [[1996-dennis-asberg-sighting]].

## Discovery of the Baltic Sea Anomaly

On the night of June 19, 2011, after nearly a week at sea searching for a champagne wreck, Asberg and [[peter-lindberg]] were running sidescan sonar in a new area at 1–2 AM. The sonar returned an image of a large round object, approximately 60 m in diameter, with what appeared to be a flat track or groove beneath it. Lindberg joked "UFO, Dennis." They took the data home, consulted experts worldwide, and went public one week later via *Expressen* (Stockholm tabloid). The story became international news within days.

## Remote Viewing and Scientific Consultations

Asberg contacted [[joe-mcmoneagle]] and [[angela-ford]] for remote viewing assessments. Multiple remote viewers described the BSA as constructed, some saying it is an energy source; one said it was "made of gold." The Finnish scientific team brought their own ROV and independently assessed the object as "man-made" and described something like a "power line" or "high voltage" energy output on their instruments — on tape.

[[beatrice-varel]] became the key figure who opened academic doors; the team now works with Stockholm University scientists (who confirmed the GPS failure on their own vessel). The BSA sits in the Finnish economic zone, not Swedish waters.

## Institutional Reactions

- **Swedish military**: 6–7 NATO-nation ships filmed encircling the site during dives. Swedish military HQ met with Asberg, denied any involvement, could not explain his footage of their ships.
- **Current expedition**: Using a Stockholm University government ship, the government may be receiving the data regardless.
- **SÄPO (Swedish FBI equivalent)**: Called Asberg in about the 2015 submarine discovery; not about the BSA.

## Threats and Resistance

Asberg received explicit threats naming his children's kindergartens and schools, designed to pressure him to stop the BSA investigation. He has preserved these messages. He concluded that going public is the best protection.
