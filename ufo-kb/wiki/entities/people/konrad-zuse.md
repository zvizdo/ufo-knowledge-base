# Konrad Zuse

German engineer who built the **Z3** (1941) — considered the world's first programmable digital computer, predating Alan Turing's practical computing machines. [[jason-reza-jorjani]] describes him as "the Alan Turing of the Nazis" and states Turing was "playing catch-up" with Zuse.

## Calculating Space (Rechnender Raum)

Beyond mechanical engineering, Zuse was deeply theoretical. During World War II he developed the concept he called ***Rechnender Raum*** — **Calculating Space** or **Computing Space**. His thesis:
- The anomalies of quantum mechanics (wave-particle duality, observer effect, uncertainty principle) indicate that **we live inside a computational system**
- Physical reality is a digital information process
- The "mesh of the matrix" would become visible at **extremely high entropy events** — such as nuclear detonations

Zuse published this as a monograph in 1969. This makes him the **first person in history to formally propose the simulation hypothesis** — decades before Nick Bostrom, Elon Musk, or any modern philosopher.

## Nuclear Test Implication

The Zuse/Jorjani synthesis suggests that nuclear weapons detonations — as high-entropy events — might reveal the underlying computational structure of reality. This provides an alternative explanation for **why NHI beings (particularly Nordics) consistently demand nuclear disarmament**: not out of ecological concern or anti-war ethics, but because nuclear detonations risk **cracking the simulation** — revealing the computational infrastructure the control system runs on.
