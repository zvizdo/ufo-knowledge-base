# Stephan Schwartz

## Background

Stephan Schwartz is an independent researcher who ran [[remote-viewing]] experiments aimed at locating archaeological shipwrecks. His protocols put remote-viewing operators in physical environments — including submarines at depth — that allowed isolation of candidate physical mechanisms for psi propagation.

## Notable Claims

### Submarine-Depth Control Experiments (via Puthoff)

[[hal-puthoff]] (Gf_tKn9TaP8) describes piggybacking on Schwartz's submarine-based archaeological RV experiments to run a specific physics control: the Soviet hypothesis was that the [[remote-viewing]] effect was carried by extremely-low-frequency brain-wave-band electromagnetic propagation. Seawater's high conductivity blocks ELF EM. Putting viewers at submarine depth therefore tests whether ELF EM is the carrier.

Puthoff's group ran two such experiments inside Schwartz's protocol. Results were pristine — viewers worked at depth as well as on the surface. Conclusion: the effect is not carried by ordinary low-frequency EM. "We could strike one thing off the list."

The archaeological side of Schwartz's work — the actual location of shipwrecks — was also independently successful, per Puthoff.
