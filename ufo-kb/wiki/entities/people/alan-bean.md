# Alan Bean

NASA astronaut; member of Apollo 12 (1969), the second lunar landing mission — the fourth human to walk on the Moon. Later commanded Skylab 3 (1973). Died 2018.

## Background

Bean flew Apollo 12 to the Moon with Pete Conrad (commander) and Richard Gordon. He was known for being both a rigorous test pilot/astronaut and an artist who later painted scenes from his lunar experience.

## Notable Claims

[[kevin-knuth]] met and spoke with Alan Bean personally. Bean told him: during his time on [[skylab]] (approximately 1977 — likely the Skylab 4 mission), the crew photographed **a red flashing light** that followed them for part of their orbit and then disappeared.

Knuth's analysis: "Nobody puts lights on satellites. First, you don't need them, and second, it's more weight." Furthermore, satellites at that time followed fixed predictable orbits — they didn't do proximity operations or maneuver alongside other craft.

The photographs are apparently public, though the image quality is poor (handheld through a window in a moving spacecraft). Knuth notes this makes expert image analysis frustrating, but the object's presence is not disputed.

This account was **not mentioned in the NASA Commission's UAP report** — Knuth explicitly raises this as an example of institutional omission.
