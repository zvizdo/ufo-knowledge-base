# Robert Schoch

Dr. Robert Schoch is a geologist and associate professor at Boston University who caused controversy in 1991 when he argued, based on geological erosion analysis, that the Great Sphinx of Giza was carved at least 7,000–10,000 years ago — thousands of years earlier than the accepted 2500 BC dating. His analysis focuses on the vertical erosion patterns on the Sphinx enclosure, which he argues require thousands of years of heavy rainfall not seen in Egypt since the end of the last ice age.

Schoch's work is cited by ancient mysteries researchers including [[graham-hancock]] and [[christopher-dunn]] as evidence of a lost advanced civilization. This connects to UAP discourse via the hypothesis that the civilization predated known history and may have had contact with or been assisted by non-human intelligence.

## Hancock's Tribute

In [[9PJJiut0Iuw]], Hancock explicitly credits Schoch alongside [[john-anthony-west]] as individuals who had "the guts to stick it out" despite "horrendous attacks from the archaeological community." Hancock is careful to note that West was the catalyst — it was West who first brought Schoch to the Giza Plateau.
