# Frank Drake

Astronomer. In 1961 launched [[project-ozma]] — named after the Oz series — at the National Radio Astronomy Observatory in Green Bank, West Virginia. This was the first organized search for interstellar radio signals from intelligent extraterrestrial civilizations, using scrapped WWII radar parts. In doing so, Drake initiated what became the SETI (Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence) program, which has been running for 70+ years.

## Background

Drake also formulated the Drake Equation — a probabilistic framework for estimating the number of technologically communicating civilizations in the Milky Way. It remains the canonical framework for discussion of extraterrestrial civilization probability despite its wide uncertainty ranges.

## Notable Claims

No firsthand UAP claims. Drake represents the official SETI methodology: search for electromagnetic signals across interstellar distances. This approach contrasts with [[ronald-bracewell]]'s probe hypothesis, which argues that physical artifacts parked in local solar system orbits would be a more efficient communication strategy.

## SETI Context

Outside of the famous [[1977-wow-signal]] (discovered by Jerry Ehman at Ohio State), no repeatable alien radio signal has been detected in 70+ years of SETI. One study calculated that the volume of the galaxy scanned so far is analogous to comparing a hot tub of water to all of Earth's oceans when asking "where are all the fish."
