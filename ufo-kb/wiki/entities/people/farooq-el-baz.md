# Farouk El-Baz

Egyptian-American geologist and space scientist (born 1938). Director of the Center for Remote Sensing at Boston University. During the Apollo program, El-Baz was Secretary of the Landing Site Selection Committee and trained Apollo astronauts in geology and lunar observation — making him one of the primary scientific architects of where humans landed on the Moon.

**Nickname "the King"**: Apollo astronauts nicknamed El-Baz "the King" — a reference to both his authority in lunar landing site selection and his Egyptian origin. The connection between an Egyptian geologist selecting the landing sites for the most historically significant exploration in human history carries resonance in the KB's context of Egyptian sacred geometry, esoteric traditions, and the space program's cultural symbolism.

**Father's background**: El-Baz's father was a scholar of Islamic and Egyptian religious tradition — creating an unusual line from Egyptian sacred knowledge to the inner circle of Apollo mission planning. This lineage is cited in [[space-program-patches]] as part of the KB's broader theme of esoteric cultural influences within the US space program.

**Remote sensing**: Post-Apollo, El-Baz became a pioneer in satellite remote sensing for geological applications, including discovery of ancient river systems beneath the Sahara. His work demonstrates the practical scientific value of space program technologies applied to Earth.
