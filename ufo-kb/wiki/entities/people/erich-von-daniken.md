# Erich von Däniken

Swiss popular author whose 1968 book *[[chariots-of-the-gods]]* launched the modern ancient-astronaut hypothesis into mass culture. His core argument: the technological sophistication of ancient monuments and the descriptions of "gods" in ancient texts cannot be explained without positing visits by extraterrestrial beings.

## Position in the Discourse

Von Däniken occupies a contested but influential position in the alternative archaeology field. He is widely credited with alerting a mass audience to genuine anomalies — unexplained engineering feats, astronomical alignments, pre-Columbian contact claims — but equally widely criticized for making the explanatory leap to extraterrestrial intervention without ruling out simpler alternatives.

## Hancock's Assessment

[[graham-hancock]] in [[dfPfPB601hw]] offers a nuanced evaluation:

> "I know Eric and I think he did a good job of alerting people to mystery in the past. But I personally think that the answer he came up with — that it was ancient aliens in high-tech spacecraft who came here — I think that's an unnecessary argument in order to explain the anomalies that we confront."

Hancock's preferred alternative: a **lost human civilization of the Ice Age** destroyed in the Younger Dryas global cataclysm. For Hancock, this is both more elegant and more parsimonious than extraterrestrial intervention.

## Open Questions

- Von Däniken's evidence base overlaps extensively with mainstream alternative archaeology. The distinction between "anomaly worth investigating" and "therefore aliens" is where researchers part ways.
