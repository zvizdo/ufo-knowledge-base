# Roscoe Hillenkoetter

## Background

Rear Admiral Roscoe H. Hillenkoetter was the first Director of Central Intelligence (DCI), serving from 1947 to 1950 — the precise period that encompasses the Roswell incident. He was a career naval intelligence officer who had served as naval attaché in Paris before taking the CIA's founding directorship.

His tenure coincided with the creation of the CIA itself (National Security Act, July 1947) and the July 1947 Roswell crash. According to [[matthew-pines]] in [[9QMrhcpJq8I]], Hillenkoetter received the Roswell materials in his capacity as first CIA Director — placing the CIA at the founding layer of the UAP custodian chain.

Hillenkoetter is one of only a handful of high-ranking officials who later publicly acknowledged UAP reality: after leaving government he joined the National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena (NICAP) and made public statements affirming that the flying saucer phenomenon was real and that the government was withholding information from the public. This public disclosure after leaving office follows the pattern of deathbed-adjacent statements seen in [[howard-hunt]], [[harold-malmgren]], and others in the [[ufo-secrecy-cabal]].

## UAP Role

As first CIA Director, Hillenkoetter sits at the institutional origin of the UAP classification regime. The Roswell materials flowing to him — and then being managed by [[walter-b-smith]] (his successor) and the subsequent CIA directors — establishes the CIA custodian chain that runs through [[richard-bissell]], [[allen-dulles]], and [[james-angleton]].

## NICAP and the 1962 Hearing Collapse (GQlWf54K_7Y)

After leaving government, Hillenkoetter joined [[nicap]] (National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena) and publicly acknowledged that the flying saucer phenomenon was real — a remarkable disclosure from the first CIA Director. His affiliation gave NICAP the most credible figurehead of any civilian UFO organization.

In 1962, [[donald-keyhoe]] — NICAP's primary advocate — was building momentum toward forcing formal congressional UFO hearings. [[richard-dolan]] notes the suspicious timing of what followed:

> "Hillenkoetter suddenly resigned from NICAP in 1962 right as Keyhoe was trying to organize congressional hearings."

Hillenkoetter resigned without public explanation at the precise moment the civilian-military disclosure push was closest to institutional leverage. Dolan treats this as consistent with intelligence pressure: a former CIA director retains relationships and vulnerabilities that make him susceptible to being recalled into silence. The congressional hearing initiative subsequently collapsed.

This pattern — post-government public disclosure followed by sudden withdrawal at a critical moment — is structurally consistent with the [[flood-the-zone-disinfo]] thesis: Hillenkoetter's NICAP affiliation may have been permitted as a disclosure valve, and removed when the valve threatened to open too wide.
