# Armando Fortunato

Forensic physician based in Varginha, Minas Gerais, Brazil. His credentials: forensic medicine, public health physician, occupational physician, and a bachelor's degree in law. He served as municipal health secretary during the COVID-19 pandemic in his city. He is married to a Peruvian psychoanalyst and has two daughters who are also doctors.

Fortunato performed the official coroner's autopsy on [[marcos-charez]] — the military police officer who reportedly captured one of the Varginha alien beings with bare hands and died from a virulent bacterial infection weeks later. He testified at [[UkKwa4jU0fc]] alongside [[italo-venturelli]] and [[carlos-de-souza]].

## The Autopsy

Fortunato received the body with no context about alien involvement. The military police colonel requested the autopsy to investigate possible medical malpractice — a battalion doctor had performed a drainage procedure on a skin lesion (hydrodenitis) in Charez's left armpit area. The autopsy was ordered through the state civil police (EML — Forensic Medical Institute), linked to the criminal investigation branch.

### Findings
- Single wound: drainage incision at the left armpit
- No other lesions
- Blood and tissue samples sent to the laboratory of [[jean-bachista-janini]] (pathologist, Varginha)
- Janini's report: cause of death was pulmonary infection leading to generalized septicemia

### Bacterial Characteristics
The bacteria:
- Exists on Earth as a rare staphylococcal strain
- In this case, its virulence, speed of progression, and lethality were unlike anything Janini had seen in 45–50 years of pathology
- Charez was a young, vigorous, healthy soldier — death within 12–14 days despite antibiotic treatment
- Dr. Cesario (cardiologist who treated Charez) stated he "threw the kitchen sink" of antibiotics with zero effect

A 2024–2025 reassessment: Janini now states on the record (in a signed, authenticated document provided at this interview) that he does not rule out the bacteria being of alien origin or genetically engineered — a second hypothesis he advances alongside the standard infection progression explanation.

## The Contamination Route

Charez's sister confirmed he was involved in capturing the alien being and told her he believed his illness was caused by that contact. The being reportedly had an oily, greasy substance on its skin; Charez handled it with bare hands.

Two hypotheses (Fortunato's framing):
1. Standard complication — the left-armpit drainage procedure introduced infection that progressed unusually rapidly
2. Post-contact contamination — alien bacteria (or an Earth bacteria that the being had encountered and modified) entered through Charez's skin during capture

The [[varginha-zoo]] connection adds a third thread: several zoo animals died of unknown causes during the same week, possibly having been exposed to the alien beings, potentially creating an animal-reservoir pathway for the bacteria.

## Jean Bachista Janini

[[jean-bachista-janini]] — a reference pathologist now nearly 90 years old who came from Rio de Janeiro and established a diagnostic laboratory in Varginha serving 90% of public health patients across more than 100 cities in the region. He is a respected scientific figure and university professor. He submitted an authenticated signed document to this interview stating his current position on the bacteria. He is requesting court authorization for exhumation of Charez's remains for DNA/bacterial analysis.

## Proposed Next Steps

Fortunato, with James Fox and others, is pushing for:
- Court-authorized exhumation of Charez's remains
- DNA analysis of the bacterial remnants
- Soil analysis of the crash site by geologists and forensic specialists
- Collaboration with US institutions (Fortunato specifically invited [[hal-puthoff]] and American scientific resources)
