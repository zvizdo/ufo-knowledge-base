# Richard Firestone

Nuclear scientist at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. In 2007, Firestone co-authored with **Allan West**, **George Howard**, and others the paper that brought the Younger Dryas Impact Hypothesis into mainstream scientific debate. The paper proposed that a cosmic impact or airburst over the Laurentide Ice Sheet ~12,900 years ago triggered the Younger Dryas cooling, megafaunal extinctions, and the collapse of the Clovis culture.

## 2007 YDIH Paper

[[randall-carlson]] credits Firestone, West, and Howard with formally proposing the impact hypothesis in 2007 — *after* Carlson had independently been arguing for a cosmic trigger since his Warren Wilson College lectures in 1995. Carlson notes that the 2007 paper initially did not connect the impact to the Taurid meteor stream; that link came slightly later via [[bill-napier]].

## Proxy Evidence Documented

The 2007 paper and follow-on work documented the "lower Younger Dryas boundary" evidence:
- Iridium spike in Greenland ice cores (~12,900-year layer)
- Platinum spike
- Nanodiamond layers (require 2,000–3,000°C to form)
- Microspherules
- "Black mat layer" visible at multiple quarry sites worldwide

Carlson notes that by 2024, **eight or ten independent research teams** have replicated documentation of cosmic signatures at this boundary — a significant evidentiary convergence.
