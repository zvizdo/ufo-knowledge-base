# Riz Virk

MIT- and Stanford-educated computer scientist and serial video-game entrepreneur. Founded several video-game and tech startups before pivoting to writing and speaking on the **simulation hypothesis** — the thesis that physical reality is best understood as a quantum-computational RPG in which souls voluntarily embody as avatars. Adviser to the [[galileo-project]] and peripherally involved with the [[soul-project-stanford]] at Stanford. Personal friend and correspondent of [[jacques-vallee]], who introduced Jesse Michaels to Virk's work.

## Background

Virk's career arc — from MIT CS → Stanford → commercial video-game entrepreneur — gives his simulation theory an unusual practical grounding. His argument is rooted in how games are *actually built*: lazy evaluation, level-of-detail rendering, client-server architecture, reusable code modules — not in abstract philosophy. He wrote two books on the subject: [[the-simulation-hypothesis-virk|The Simulation Hypothesis]] and [[simulated-multiverse-virk|The Simulated Multiverse]].

## Core Theoretical Contributions

### RPG Model (vs. NPC Model)
Virk distinguishes two versions of simulation theory:
- **NPC model**: everyone is a non-player character — mere code with no external soul. He dismisses this as nihilistic and inconsistent with evidence of genuine agency.
- **RPG model**: a more fundamental, primordial soul voluntarily ports itself into a low-resolution physical avatar. It forgets its former self (anamnesis); the forgetting is a feature, not a bug — it allows genuine immersive experience. This maps onto Platonic anamnesis, Sufi veils (70,000 veils between the soul and God), and Hindu Maya.

### Quantum Code = Simulation Architecture
Virk maps standard quantum phenomena onto video-game rendering techniques:
- **Superposition = lazy evaluation**: particles don't resolve until observed, exactly as 3D MMOs render only what the avatar's camera sees.
- **Wave-function collapse = rendering trigger**: the observer is the rendering engine.
- **Many Worlds = save-state multiverse**: Hugh Everett's branching universes are simulated runs of the same game state; the simulator prunes suboptimal branches and advances the best timeline.

### Information-Theoretic Evidence
Virk cites reusable code patterns in nature as evidence:
- Fibonacci sequence + golden ratio in DNA, plant stems, spiral galaxies — copied code modules.
- DNA base pairs (A–T, C–G) as bit-pairs — binary information storage.
- Electron/positron pairs as matter/antimatter binary switches.

### Client-Server Consciousness
Drawing on [[rupert-sheldrake]]'s morphic resonance, [[bob-jahn]]'s PEAR results, and the Banister Effect, Virk proposes that individual minds are client nodes downloading from (and uploading to) a central consciousness server. New knowledge crystallised in one node becomes fractionally easier to access by all others — consistent with the first-crystallisation getting easier over time and with Roger Bannister's four-minute mile triggering a flood of sub-four-minute runs within two years.

### UFOs as Holographic Projections
Referencing [[jacques-vallee]]'s redwood-tree case (a UFO descended at 45° through 40-foot trees and left marks on the ground — witnesses omitted the tree-cutting detail for fear of being disbelieved), Virk proposes UFOs are rendered into physical reality rather than continuously present. Inside the simulation they can be semi-transparent / walk through walls; once rendered at landing they become physically solid. He frames them as "hyperobjects that exist outside of the cave, meant to be glimpsed." See [[simulation-theory]] and [[trickster-phenomenon]].

### Timeline Optimization
Virk's most speculative claim: the simulation is run multiple times; the simulator(s) save game states, run branches forward, prune undesirable outcomes, and advance the optimal timeline. Humans pick up "clues" from possible futures as intuitions — what he calls "future memories." Philip K. Dick's belief that simulators chose the Allied-victory timeline over the German-victory one he had written is cited as narrative evidence. See [[time-travel-uap]] and [[future-humans-hypothesis]].

## Connection to Jacques Vallee

Vallee told Virk he had found a flaw-free argument in his books and wrote the foreword to the French edition of *The Simulation Hypothesis*. Vallee introduced Virk to Jesse Michaels. This is the primary link connecting simulation theory to the empirical UFO-investigation tradition in this KB.

## Open Questions

- Are Virk's information-theoretic "code evidence" examples (Fibonacci, DNA) genuinely indicative of simulation, or are they post-hoc pattern recognition?
- Is the RPG soul model empirically distinguishable from the NPC model?
- What is the optimization function that the timeline pruning process is working toward?
