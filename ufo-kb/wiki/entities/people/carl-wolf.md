# Karl Wolfe

USAF sergeant and photographic equipment repair specialist. In the early 1960s — during the run-up to the Apollo missions — he was sent to repair equipment at a facility he initially believed was NASA. He was told upon arrival it was in fact an **NSA facility** processing lunar orbiter reconnaissance photographs.

## The Moon City Photos

While working on a photographic mosaic machine, a facility technician showed Wolfe **mosaic photographs** of the dark (far) side of the Moon. He described:
- A **large city-sized structure** with predominantly polygonal architecture
- Architecture at **titanic scale** — massive structures
- Construction material appeared to be **poured stone or concrete** (not metal or glass), reminiscent of Earth's megalithic architecture
- Located on the **dark side / far side** of the Moon

## Death Before 2017 Disclosure Wave

[[jason-reza-jorjani]] notes in [[EzPmG_7WhXc]] that Wolfe died in a hit-and-run accident **just before** the 2017–2018 UAP disclosure wave that began with the *New York Times* AATIP story. Jorjani draws an explicit parallel to [[john-mack]]'s fatal hit-and-run, arguing Wolfe's death was not coincidental — his testimony about NSA lunar photos would have made him a key subpoena target in any serious Congressional investigation. Jorjani also notes that [[ingo-swann]]'s remote viewing of the Moon was conducted with access to these same photographs.

## Significance

The Wolfe testimony, if accurate, implies:
1. The US government had photographic evidence of lunar structures in the early 1960s, prior to Apollo
2. This was being processed at an NSA (not NASA) facility — implying classification at the highest intelligence levels
3. Ingo Swann's remote viewing of the same location (seeing Nordic slave-drivers and a city) corroborates from an independent modality

## Credibility Assessment

**Firsthand single witness**. Wolfe testified publicly at the 2001 National Press Club Disclosure Project event. No corroborating witness has come forward. His death before the 2017 wave is noted by Jorjani as suspicious, though hit-and-run deaths are not inherently anomalous.
