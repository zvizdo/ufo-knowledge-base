# Jacque Corneau

A French Air Force officer and Technical Representative for one of France's largest aircraft companies, **Sud-Ouest**, Corneau is the most important institutional witness to the [[biefeld-brown-effect]] working in vacuum. His role was to independently facilitate [[thomas-townsend-brown]]'s experiments at the Montgolfier facility in Paris in 1956, stripping away the ion-wind explanation.

## The 1956 Paris Vacuum Experiments

The core controversy around Brown's work was always whether observed thrust was genuine electrogravitic coupling or simply **ion wind** (charged air molecules impelled between electrode plates). The vacuum test was decisive:

- Brown's experiments were conducted in a vacuum of at least **10⁻⁶ torr** — deep enough to eliminate virtually all ambient ionization.
- The observed residual thrust was **several orders of magnitude larger** than the remaining ambient ionization could account for.
- Brown's own post-experiment statement: "It appears there is strong evidence that the Biefeld-Brown effect does exist in the negative-to-positive direction in a vacuum of at least 10⁻⁶ torr."

Corneau was of possibly French-Canadian heritage; his father was an antique dealer named Lewis from Montreal. He arranged successively higher-level demonstrations, with each subsequent audience representing a higher echelon — the final audience being France's atomic energy authority.

## Final Interview

[[paul-schatzkin]] interviewed Corneau in April 2008 in Pennsylvania, just before his death. At 98 years old and still fully lucid, Corneau confirmed the Montgolfier reports' findings and the "bizarre anomalous effect" he witnessed — an effect that "flew in the face of conventional science." His affirmation was among the last firsthand testimonies from the 1950s anti-gravity research era.
