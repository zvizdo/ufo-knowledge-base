# Warren Burger

Warren E. Burger (1907–1995) served as Chief Justice of the United States from 1969 to 1986, appointed by President Nixon. Before his elevation to the Supreme Court, Burger served in legal roles connected to the US intelligence community — and it is in this capacity that he appears in [[mk-ultra]] litigation history.

**MK-Ultra cover-up**: [[danny-sheehan]]'s [[christic-institute]] successfully litigated the only civil lawsuit against MK-Ultra to reach a conclusion: a case involving a Long Island tennis professional who was dosed twice with massive amounts of LSD without consent while hospitalized ostensibly for divorce counseling, and died of a heart attack as a result. [[jacob-javits]], then serving as the Army's lawyer, covered up the death. Warren Burger — serving at the time as intelligence-community counsel — was also involved in the cover-up. When Sheehan's investigation exposed Burger's role, the presiding judge ordered the case settled rather than allow Burger's name to appear in open court proceedings.

**Deep state continuity pattern**: The Burger case is cited in [[deep-state-continuity]] analysis as an example of the rotational personnel dynamic: individuals who participated in covering up classified program abuses went on to occupy the most senior positions in American governance. A future Chief Justice was implicated in suppressing evidence of an unauthorized human experiment death — and faced no accountability, the case being settled specifically to protect him.

**Chief Justice tenure**: As Chief Justice (1969–1986), Burger presided over the Burger Court, which issued significant rulings on Nixon's resignation (US v. Nixon) and other matters. His judicial record is separate from the MK-Ultra involvement documented through Sheehan's litigation.
