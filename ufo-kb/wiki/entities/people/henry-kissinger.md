# Henry Kissinger

U.S. Secretary of State (1973–1977) and National Security Advisor to Nixon (1969–1975); Nobel Peace Prize laureate (1973, jointly with Lê Đức Thọ). One of the most powerful foreign policy figures of the 20th century. In the UAP discourse, Kissinger is alleged to be a node in the hidden UAP oversight network, connected through his roles in intelligence community oversight and his membership in elite networks including the [[knights-of-malta]].

## CIC Service and Oberammergau (1945)

After VE Day, Kissinger was assigned to **Oberammergau**, Bavaria — the location of a secret Messerschmidt weapons facility and the area where Werner von Braun and ~400 Nazi scientists had been gathered under SS General Hans Kammler. Kissinger was one of the first Allied officers to occupy the barracks there, placing him at the center of [[operation-paperclip]] recruiting and in the same CIC network that:
- Interrogated [[victor-schauberger]] for nine months on anti-gravity propulsion (considered more important than Nazi nuclear research per Nick Cook's *The Hunt for Zero Point*)

## Nixon White House and 303 Committee

As NSA and Secretary of State, Kissinger oversaw the **[[303-committee]]** — the inter-agency covert ops approval body sometimes identified as overlapping with MJ12 structures. Nixon installed [[harold-malmgren]] partly as a check on Kissinger; Malmgren had already been briefed on UAP material by [[richard-bissell]].

## Operation Moondust Telegram (January 1977)

In January 1977, days before leaving office, Kissinger sent a declassified telegram to the US Embassy in Ottawa requesting a report on "possible returned space objects." Subject line: **MOON DUST** ([[operation-moondust]]). Related Moondust documents describe UAP recoveries including a 1957 Afghanistan event sent to Wright-Patterson.
