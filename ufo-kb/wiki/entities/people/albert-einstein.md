# Albert Einstein

Albert Einstein (1879–1955) was a German-born theoretical physicist who developed special relativity (1905) and general relativity (1915), and made foundational contributions to quantum mechanics (photoelectric effect, Brownian motion, stimulated emission). He was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1921 for the photoelectric effect. After the Nazi rise to power in 1933, Einstein emigrated to the United States and joined the [[institute-for-advanced-study]] in Princeton, where he worked until his death in 1955.

**Unified field theory**: In his final decades, Einstein pursued a unified field theory — a mathematical framework that would combine electromagnetism and gravity into a single geometric description. The program ultimately did not succeed, and is considered a historical dead end, though it prefigures later attempts at grand unification that remain relevant to UAP physics speculation.

**Abraham Pais**: [[abraham-pais]], physicist and science historian, was a colleague of Einstein at the Institute for Advanced Study and wrote the definitive scientific biography *Subtle is the Lord: The Science and Life of Albert Einstein* (1982), one of the most authoritative sources on Einstein's life and work.

**UAP claims**: Einstein is cited in various UAP claims, including: that he was briefed on the 1947 Roswell crash at the request of the US government; that he worked on reverse-engineering of recovered materials; and that he knew about non-human intelligence. None of these claims have documentary support in the KB, and they are characteristic of the pattern of attaching famous scientists' names to UAP narratives for credibility.

**Relationship to UAP physics**: Einstein's general relativity — the warping of spacetime by mass/energy — is the theoretical framework within which most serious UAP propulsion proposals are situated. Warp drives, wormholes, and metric engineering are all extensions of general relativistic physics.
