# Geoff Marcy

Astronomer and pioneer of exoplanet detection. Was cancelled in a 2015 sexual harassment scandal and subsequently retired from his institutional position, though he continued publishing papers and collaborating with other scientists.

## VASCO Collaboration

[[beatriz-villarroel]] continued working with Marcy after his cancellation on the grounds that his science was excellent and that she "believes in human rights" — including the principle that individuals deserve a chance at rehabilitation and should not be condemned outside of a court process. He was never criminally convicted.

The collaboration produced a paper on the nine-transient discovery — multiple stars appearing and vanishing in a small patch of sky simultaneously (see [[pre-sputnik-uap-transients]]). The paper was accepted by a journal.

## Suspicious Timing of NAS Ejection

The **same week** the nine-transients paper was accepted, the **National Academy of Sciences** decided to eject Marcy from membership. Science and Nature published articles about the ejection. Someone called Villarroel and advised her not to issue a press release because she would "get seriously attacked."

Villarroel was subsequently:
- Kicked out of conferences
- Had registration forgotten
- Subject to social media attacks

She frames this as peer pressure motivated at least in part by the paper's anomalous content, though she cannot definitively prove causal intent. The timing — NAS ejection coinciding precisely with paper acceptance — is the load-bearing coincidence.

> Villarroel: "I cannot answer that question. And there might be it might have been a coincidence but it's anyway it happened that same time."

## Villarroel's Stance

Villarroel frames her continued collaboration as a matter of principle: she believes in science productivity and human rights. She argues the targeting was enabled by Marcy's 2015 scandal but activated by the paper on anomalous transients — a pattern consistent with the [[astronomical-data-suppression]] dynamic where institutional mechanisms are weaponized against anomalous findings.

She also credits the experience with liberating her from peer-approval dependency: after being "crucified" by the academic community, she felt free to pursue UAP-adjacent work without worrying about institutional gatekeeping.
