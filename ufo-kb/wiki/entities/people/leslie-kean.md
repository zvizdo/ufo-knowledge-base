# Leslie Kean

Investigative journalist whose UAP reporting reshaped mainstream coverage of the phenomenon. She is primarily known for co-breaking the December 2017 New York Times article ("Glowing Auras and 'Black Money': The Pentagon's Mysterious U.F.O. Program") alongside [[ralph-blumenthal]] and Helene Cooper — the article that effectively ended the stigma era and launched the modern disclosure movement.

Earlier she published the Chilean Navy UAP video (a naval helicopter thermal recording) in the Huffington Post (~2016–17), characterizing it as a compelling unexplained case. [[mick-west]] and colleagues subsequently identified it as a distant plane with jet exhaust, a conclusion both Mick and [[marik-von-rennenkampff]] accept as settled.

## Significance

The 2017 NYT article came with declassified video footage (Gimbal, later GOFAST and FLIR1) and the revelation of the [[aatip]] program. It triggered a cascade of congressional activity, subsequent AARO establishment, and the current disclosure arc. Kean's journalism is the proximate cause of the UAP discourse shift that this entire episode inhabits.

## Grusch Publication Fight

[[james-fox]] provides a detailed account of how the [[david-grusch]] whistleblower story came to run in [[the-debrief]] rather than the New York Times:

- Kean is a staff writer at the NYT and attempted to publish the Grusch story there
- NYT journalist Julian Barnes has an anti-UAP bias and blocked it: Fox says Barnes "has a bias, he hates UFOs"
- Kean sat next to Fox at the Congressional hearing; he asked her directly why it didn't run in the NYT
- The story was also nearly placed at Politico (which needed another week)
- Grusch's name was already circulating and he was concerned about timing — this pressure contributed to the decision to run with The Debrief
- Fox helped facilitate Grusch's subsequent op-ed

This account, if accurate, identifies Julian Barnes (NYT) as an institutional blocker of major UAP disclosure journalism.
