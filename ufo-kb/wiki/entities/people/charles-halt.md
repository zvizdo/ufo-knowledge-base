# Charles Halt

Lt. Colonel Charles Halt was the Deputy Base Commander at [[raf-bentwaters]] in December 1980 during the famous [[1980-rendlesham-forest]] UFO incident — one of the most extensively documented military UAP cases in history. Halt is notable for having authored the **Halt Memo**, an official document sent to the UK Ministry of Defence describing events in considerable detail.

## Background

Halt was a senior USAF officer stationed at RAF Bentwaters/Woodbridge, which at the time housed the **largest concentration of tactical nuclear weapons being stored in Western Europe** — a fact the UK government officially neither confirmed nor denied per Cold War policy.

## 1980 Rendlesham Forest Incident

On the second night of the incident, Halt personally led a security team into Rendlesham Forest to investigate ongoing anomalous activity. He described:

- Observing a glowing orb-like object moving erratically through the forest trees
- The object firing **a beam of light down at his team's feet** as they approached — causing them to jump back and question whether they were being attacked or warned
- He was **several miles from the WSA** (weapon storage area) but monitoring radio traffic; other security personnel at the WSA reported an object hovering directly above the bunkers and **sending beams of light down into them**

Halt's interviewed account, recorded on tape at the time (a recording that became public), and his subsequent Halt Memo to the MoD give the incident unusual documentary weight.

## Nuclear Dimension

[[robert-hastings]] specifically sought out WSA-related testimony and independently confirmed through a retired NATO weapons security colonel (unnamed, but whose background Hastings vouched for) that **two tactical nuclear weapons were subsequently removed from a bunker at Bentwaters**, flown on a C-5A to [[kirtland-air-force-base]] where an Air Force weapons lab analyzed them — presumably to assess whether the beams had affected their functionality or integrity.

Halt told Hastings in a 2005 interview that he was out in the woods with the security team; he did not personally witness the WSA beam activity but heard it reported over radio by personnel at the storage area.

## Notable Claims

- The beams fired at his team's feet may have been a demonstration or warning, not an attack.
- The object was observed hovering over the WSA and sending down beams — behavior consistent with the broader Hastings nuclear-nexus pattern.
