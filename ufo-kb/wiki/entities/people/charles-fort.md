# Charles Fort

## Background

American writer (1874–1932) who spent decades in the New York Public Library and the British Museum cataloguing newspaper and scientific-journal reports of anomalous phenomena: rains of fish, spontaneous human combustion, unidentified flying objects (before the term existed), and other events mainstream science dismissed or ignored. His four books — *The Book of the Damned* (1919), *New Lands* (1923), *Lo!* (1931), and *Wild Talents* (1932) — assembled this data into a sardonic critique of scientific dogma.

## "We Are Property"

The opening lines of *The Book of the Damned* include the statement:

> "I think we are property."

Fort went on to speculate that Earth and its inhabitants are owned or managed by some external intelligence — that the anomalous phenomena he documented (including airship sightings, falls of unidentifiable material, strange lights in the sky) were evidence of non-human management of this planet, analogous to how a farmer manages livestock.

This is one of the earliest explicit formulations of the [[we-are-property]] thesis — predating modern UFO discourse by three decades. In the [[8TYMQOUDQBo]] interview, [[salvatore-pais]] directly endorses Fort's framing, treating it as the most honest starting hypothesis for understanding NHI relationships to Earth.

## Significance to UAP Discourse

Fort is the intellectual grandfather of the "Fortean" research tradition that takes anomalous phenomena seriously as data points regardless of how they fit existing frameworks. His emphasis on cataloguing without forcing premature explanation anticipates [[jacques-vallee]]'s later scientific approach to UFO phenomenology.
