# Itzhak Bentov

Self-taught inventor and consciousness researcher who helped establish Israel's early rocket program, consulted on the CIA's Gateway Process (a consciousness-ascension protocol developed by Robert Monroe), and worked closely with early Stargate-era figures including [[andrija-puharich]]. He died in the crash of American Airlines Flight 191 in 1979.

## Background

Born in Czechoslovakia during World War II; escaped Nazi Germany and immigrated to Israel. Despite lacking formal scientific training, he helped invent Israel's **first rocket** — an extraordinary engineering accomplishment. His intellectual passion was consciousness: he believed the universe was composed of **vibrating harmonious atoms** and that consciousness was a resonant phenomenon of this vibration.

## Consciousness Theory

His foundational claim: consciousness is not produced by the brain but is a property of the universe's vibrating structure. The brain resonates with (and tunes into) background cosmic vibration rather than generating awareness internally. This is structurally related to [[morphic-resonance]], [[consciousness-non-local]], and Grinberg's [[jacobo-grinberg|lattice theory]].

His main book, ***Stalking the Wild Pendulum*** (1977), has become a cult classic in consciousness research despite — or because of — its extreme density. Researchers who have worked through it consistently report that Bentov appeared to have arrived at a near-complete "theory of everything" for consciousness.

## Intelligence and Research Connections

- **Stanford Research Institute (SRI)** — consulting work; SRI was the primary institutional home for the CIA's remote viewing program (later formalized as [[stargate-program]])
- **[[andrija-puharich]]** — one of the earliest architects of MK-Ultra; Bentov was a close associate, placing him at the intersection of CIA consciousness research and early remote viewing programs
- **CIA Gateway Process** — the protocol developed by Robert Monroe at the [[monroe-institute]] to achieve altered states and potentially out-of-body experiences; Bentov's vibrational consciousness model provided theoretical underpinning
- His theories are cited by researchers in the remote viewing and hypnagogic-state literature as anticipating key findings

## Death

**Date:** May 25, 1979  
**Incident:** American Airlines Flight 191 crashed shortly after takeoff from Chicago O'Hare Airport, killing all 273 people aboard — the deadliest aviation accident in US history at the time. Bentov was on the flight while traveling on business.

Researchers in parapsychology and consciousness note the timing: Bentov was, by multiple accounts, approaching a complete theoretical synthesis in consciousness — and he died at 55. The crash is not attributed to foul play by any official investigation, but within UAP-adjacent consciousness research circles, his death alongside his institutional intelligence connections is treated as structurally significant.

## Connection to Uri Geller and Puharich

Photographs exist of Bentov, Puharich, and [[uri-geller]] together in a Tel Aviv apartment. Bentov apparently played a role in connecting Puharich to Geller, and worked for Israeli intelligence — creating an interesting three-way node between Israeli intelligence, CIA parapsychology, and the world's most famous psychic.

Around the time Puharich's Ossining house was burned in a proven arson (1978), Bentov was among a circle of Puharich's associates who were being monitored and harassed. A possible Israeli intelligence connection to the arson has been raised given Bentov's documented Israeli intelligence ties.
