# Jace Callaway

Pharmacologist associated primarily with research on ayahuasca alkaloids and endogenous psychedelics. In the KB, Callaway is cited for an earlier hypothesis (attributed to him in the late 1980s/1990s context) that DMT is released by the pineal gland during REM sleep, generating the vivid imagery characteristic of dreaming.

**Endogenous DMT sleep hypothesis**: Callaway proposed that endogenous DMT production — occurring rhythmically during sleep — could be the biochemical substrate of dreaming. This hypothesis was later popularized by [[terence-mckenna]] but is treated skeptically by [[andrew-gallimore]] in the KB, who notes that dream states are phenomenologically unlike DMT states, and that there is no direct evidence of DMT elevation during REM sleep.

**Ayahuasca research**: Callaway also contributed to the pharmacology of ayahuasca, including analysis of the beta-carboline MAOIs (harmaline, harmine) that enable oral DMT activity by inhibiting monoamine oxidase in the gut.

**KB relevance**: The endogenous DMT hypothesis connects to broader KB claims about endogenous psychedelics as biological substrates for UAP/NHI contact experiences — the thesis that the brain has evolved a chemistry for contact, and that entities encountered in DMT states may have the same ontological status as NHI encountered in UAP events.
