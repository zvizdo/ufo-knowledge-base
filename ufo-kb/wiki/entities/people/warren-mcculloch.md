# Warren McCulloch

## Background

Warren S. McCulloch was a senior researcher at Northwestern University who encountered [[andrija-puharich]] when Puharich was a young medical student there starting in 1947. Malazzi describes him as "a Puharich on steroids" of that era — an extremely far-out researcher into ESP and mind-altering drugs who had to maintain a mainstream academic facade.

## Influence on Puharich

McCulloch "took Puharich under his wing" and was the first person who introduced him to what Malazzi calls "weird science" — ESP, radio frequencies, and related phenomena. Off the record, McCulloch conducted strange experiments with radio frequencies; in the academic setting he was more restrained.

McCulloch was also deeply connected to intelligence agencies. [[greg-malazzi]] and [[annie-jacobsen]] both found archives of McCulloch's correspondence showing letters from the CIA and Navy, apparently seeking to recruit his expertise. Crucially, McCulloch's letters back to these agencies recommended Puharich — identifying him as "young, very smart" and worth the intelligence community's attention. This appears to have been Puharich's initial introduction to the intelligence world, predating his Roundtable Foundation.

## Significance

McCulloch represents the hidden mentorship layer in Puharich's story — an older, established researcher who spotted potential in Puharich, introduced him to fringe science, and then actively brokered his entry into classified intelligence research.
