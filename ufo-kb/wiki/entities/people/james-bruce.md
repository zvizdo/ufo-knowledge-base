# James Bruce

Scottish explorer (1730–1794), known as "Bruce of Kinnaird." Traveled to Ethiopia (Abyssinia) and became the first European to reach the source of the Blue Nile in 1770. His seven-volume *Travels to Discover the Source of the Nile* (1790) remains a primary historical source on Ethiopian history and religious traditions.

In the ancient mysteries / UAP-adjacent discourse, Bruce is cited for his documentation of the Ark of the Covenant's custodial presence at Axum, Ethiopia — corroborating claims that the Ark was transported from Jerusalem and is still guarded there. His firsthand account of Axumite religious practices provides a historical anchor for the [[ark-of-the-covenant]] hypothesis as a "thing of fire" / radiation device.

## Confirmed as Freemason: Kill Winning Lodge No. 2

[[graham-hancock]] spent considerable effort trying to prove Bruce was a Freemason while researching *[[sign-and-the-seal]]* in the early 1990s. He tracked down the **Earl of Elgin**, whose estate was funding the restoration of Bruce's grave near Falco, Scotland. The Earl confirmed:

> "My dear boy, of course he was a Freemason — Kill Winning Lodge No. 2 in Edinburgh."

The Earl also confirmed Bruce was a **speculative Freemason** — interested in the esoteric dimension of the order, not merely the social one. Hancock's conclusion: Bruce's Ethiopian journey was Masonic in motivation, oriented toward the Ark rather than purely geographical exploration.

Bruce's grave had been in disrepair; the Obelisk that stood over it had fallen and was lying in the car park. The restoration was funded by the Earl of Elgin.

## Book of Enoch

Bruce is the reason the **[[book-of-enoch]]** exists in Western scholarship. He brought back a copy written in **Ge'ez** (the Ethiopian sacred language) from his Ethiopian travels and had it translated. The Book of Enoch had been lost to Western tradition for centuries, preserved only in Ethiopia.

Hancock in [[dfPfPB601hw]]:
> "We would not have the Book of Enoch if it hadn't been for that Scottish traveler getting a copy of it written in the Ethiopian sacred language and having it then translated."
