# Carl Compton

## Background

Karl Taylor Compton served as President of MIT from 1930 to 1948 and was one of the most influential science-policy figures in America during and after World War II. He chaired the Research and Development Board, making him a key liaison between military research and academic science.

## UAP Connection

Compton is a key node in the [[robert-sarbacher]] chain of testimony. In 1950, Compton was reportedly briefed on recovered UAP technology at Wright Air Field (later [[wright-patterson]]). This briefing was referenced in correspondence that [[stanton-friedman]] uncovered — specifically in the Sarbacher letter exchange — suggesting Compton was among the elite scientific administrators given access to crash-recovery findings.

In the [[09KP8XVf5nY]] episode, [[harold-malmgren]] describes a pivotal encounter in which Compton — or a figure in the same elite network — told a young Malmgren: "How many people of my caliber do you think in your lifetime are going to spend three or four days of your time of their time trying to understand you?" — framing the moment as Malmgren's entry into the inner custodian circle. This is likely the event after which Malmgren's name was entered in "all the important books."
