# Hans Kammler

SS General (SS-Obergruppenführer) and one of Hitler's most trusted officers in the final years of the Third Reich. Kammler oversaw the construction of several concentration camps including Auschwitz-Birkenau. His colleague Albert Speer described him as "a cold, ruthless schemer, a fanatic in pursuit of a goal, and as carefully calculating as he was unscrupulous."

As [[nick-cook]] documents in [[hunt-for-zero-point]], Kammler ran a "special projects office" called [[kammler-stab]] — essentially a black weapons program — in modern Czechoslovakia and Poland. Cook calls it "the blackest of black Nazi secret weapons projects."

## Weapons Mandate

Kammler's mandate covered:
- V-2 rockets (placed under his control)
- Jet propulsion programs
- Remote-control aircraft
- Atomic energy research
- And allegedly: disc-shaped aircraft and [[die-glocke]] (The Bell)

Researchers under his direction included:
- [[rudolph-schriever]] — designed a disc-shaped craft in Prague; first test flight reportedly February 1945
- [[victor-schauberger]] — naturalist/inventor; designed the "impeller" engine based on vortex propulsion
- [[richard-miethe]] — high-voltage physics researcher with flying saucer designs

## The Assassination Plot and Defection

By 1945, Kammler had secretly turned against Hitler and was planning his assassination. Cook reports "quite a bit of evidence that Kammler had been in negotiations with the Western Allies before the end of the war." The reported deal: all his files on the secret technologies his programs had developed, in exchange for being allowed to live. This deal reportedly resulted in multiple conflicting accounts of his death — "there are sort of six different accounts of his death, which is always suspicious."

The [[paul-mellon]] account (relayed by his grandson [[john-warner-iv]]) describes Mellon and [[allen-dulles]] viewing "a German flying disc" in a hangar in Czechoslovakia at the end of WWII — possibly part of the technology transfer Cook attributes to Kammler's defection negotiations.
