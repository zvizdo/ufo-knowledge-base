# Sean Kirkpatrick

Former director of [[aaro]] (All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office). [[jesse-michaels]] and [[robert-salas]] allege he was the primary source for [[joel-shectman]]'s Wall Street Journal article attributing the 1967 [[1967-oscar-flight]] missile shutdowns to a non-nuclear EMP test device. Salas characterizes Kirkpatrick as "collaborating very closely, probably too closely" with Shectman on what he calls a hit piece designed to discredit Salas's account.

## Background

During his tenure at AARO, the office received Salas's 2.5-hour briefing with documentation but declined to follow up with the Air Force to corroborate the account. Michaels frames Kirkpatrick as operating a "fly trap" — drawing out whistleblower information and then suppressing it — though this is speculative.

## Résumé and Atomic-Insider Profile (fzvwBBSmWYA)

[[robert-hastings]] provides a detailed examination of Kirkpatrick's background in [[fzvwBBSmWYA]]:

- At **age 17**, received a DOE grant and visited/worked at **Brookhaven National Laboratory**
- Later worked at **Oakridge National Laboratory** — one of the oldest nuclear sites in Tennessee
- His company after AARO was reportedly named "Nonlinear [something] LLC" — Hastings notes nonlinear algebra is required for gravity research and UAP physics
- Kirkpatrick's consistent public line — "we have no evidence of extraterrestrials" — is framed by Hastings as technically accurate (extraterrestrial = from another planet, which is a specific claim) while avoiding the broader UAP evidence

When [[lue-elizondo]] told [[kirk-mcconnell]] that the Big Sur film (see [[1964-vandenberg-bob-jacobs]]) was in a Pentagon safe, Kirkpatrick allegedly went to check and told McConnell it was not there. This is the single most serious specific allegation against AARO: destruction or removal of evidence.

After the 11-veteran AARO testimony sessions (facilitated by Hastings), Kirkpatrick told Senator Gillibrand's committee "we were presented with no evidence that these were extraterrestrial craft" — despite the veterans' accounts of missile shutdowns and UFO encounters.

## Lobbying Against the UAP Disclosure Act

Per [[jesse-michaels]] in [[h0hAit-KH9A]], Kirkpatrick lobbied against the [[uap-disclosure-act]] before it went to a vote. The UAP Disclosure Act would have created civilian congressional oversight of UAP programs and a presidentially-appointed review board. His active resistance to oversight is structurally contradictory to his public "nothing to see here" posture — if UFO programs don't meaningfully exist, civilian oversight of non-existent programs would be harmless.

## The 2024 WSJ Article's Hazing Claim

The article Kirkpatrick allegedly sourced included a second cover story beyond EMP: that widespread UFO mythology is the product of **Air Force hazing rituals**. This claim fails to account for: 1949 Air Force emergency meeting records; the 1947 [[nathan-twining]] memo; international civilian nuclear grid sightings (Fukushima, [[1995-bariloche-argentina]]); documented witness harassment across decades.
