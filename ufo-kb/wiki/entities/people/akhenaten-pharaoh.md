# Akhenaten

## Background

Born Amenhotep IV, son of [[amenhotep-iii]]. Became pharaoh during the 18th dynasty (~1353–1336 BC). Raised in an environment where his father was feuding with the powerful temple priesthood — particularly the Amen priesthood at Karnak — which had accumulated enormous economic and political power over centuries.

According to [[luke-caverns]]'s interpretation: Akhenaten absorbed his father's rage against the priests and, when he became pharaoh, acted on it radically — closing all temples, exiling the priests, declaring the sun disk (Aten) the sole god, renaming himself Akhenaten ("effective for Aten"), and moving the capital to a new city (Akhetaten, modern Amarna).

This was **the first recorded example of monotheism**, predating the Exodus and Moses by at least a century.

## The Elongated Skull Depictions

During the Amarna period, Egyptian art underwent a dramatic stylistic break: royal figures were depicted with elongated skulls, enlarged eyes, and unusual proportions. These depictions appear *within this one generation* and disappear immediately after the Amarna period ends. Where this iconographic shift came from is unknown.

[[luke-caverns]] speculates: did Akhenaten's contact with the Aten reflect an encounter with an actual NHI/cosmic being? Or were the elongated skull images the artistic representation of hallucinogenic visions of the divine? (See [[ancient-hallucinogens-religion]].)

## Death of the Amarna Period

When [[nefertiti-queen]] died, power passed to the young [[tutankhamun-pharaoh]]. His advisor Ay persuaded him to restore the old religion, rename himself Tutankhamun (from Tutankhaten), and reopen the temples. The Amarna experiment collapsed within one generation.
