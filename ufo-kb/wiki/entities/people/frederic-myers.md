# Frederic W.H. Myers

British poet, classical scholar, and psychical researcher (1843–1901); one of the co-founders of the Society for Psychical Research (SPR) in 1882, alongside [[edmund-gurney]] and [[henry-sidgwick]]. Myers coined the term **telepathy** and was the primary theoretical architect of what he called the "subliminal self" — a conception of the psyche that extended below ordinary consciousness and was capable of anomalous perception.

**Human Personality and Its Survival of Bodily Death**: Myers's posthumously published magnum opus (1903) synthesized decades of SPR case collection into a theory of consciousness that could accommodate apparitions, automatic writing, hypnosis, genius, and mystical experience under a single framework. The "subliminal self" thesis anticipated aspects of later psychology (the unconscious, the extended mind) while being explicitly concerned with whether consciousness survived physical death.

**Telepathy**: Myers introduced the word "telepathy" in 1882 as a more scientific replacement for earlier terms like "thought transference." The SPR's subsequent work on telepathy — statistical card experiments, spontaneous case collection, Ganzfeld precursors — flowed from his foundational framing.

**SPR methodology**: Working alongside [[edmund-gurney]] on *Phantasms of the Living* (1886), Myers helped establish the statistical and corroborative methodology that defined academic parapsychology: standardized questionnaires, independent witness corroboration, and probability calculation to distinguish anomaly from chance.

**KB relevance**: Myers exemplifies the 19th century's [[spectral-revolution]] — credentialed elite academics applying scientific method to anomalous experience. His subliminal-self framework also prefigures later models of consciousness (Huxley's transmission theory, the [[gateway-process]] model) that appear in the KB's consciousness-UAP nexus.
