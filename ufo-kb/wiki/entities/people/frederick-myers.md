# Frederick Myers

British classicist and poet who co-founded the [[society-of-psychical-research]] (SPR) in 1882 alongside Edmund Gurney and Henry Sidgwick. Myers coined the term "telepathy" in 1882 as a scientific replacement for the folk category "thought transference." His posthumously published *Human Personality and Its Survival of Bodily Death* (1903) remains a landmark in consciousness research — an attempt to synthesize all anomalous human experience into a coherent theoretical framework based on an extended concept of the "subliminal self."

Referenced in [[0qlppHc3-gg]] as part of the 150-year academic tradition of rigorously investigating telepathy and anomalous consciousness.

## Background

1843–1901. Classical scholar at Trinity College, Cambridge. Moved from orthodox academic work into psychical research in the 1870s after the death of his close friend Annie Marshall. Co-founded SPR 1882. His theoretical framework — the subliminal self as a deeper stratum of personality with access to non-normal information channels — anticipates later consciousness-field models.

## Notable Claims

- Coined "telepathy" as a scientifically neutral term for apparent mind-to-mind information transfer.
- The "subliminal self" is a broader, deeper aspect of personhood that can access information beyond normal sensory channels — and may survive bodily death.
- Systematic case collection and controlled experiments could establish telepathy as a scientific fact.
