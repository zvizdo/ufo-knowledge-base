# Admiral Byrd

United States Navy admiral and celebrated polar explorer. Led multiple Antarctic expeditions, most significantly [[operation-high-jump]] in 1946–47, which was the largest Antarctic expedition in US history.

## Operation High Jump

The expedition comprised approximately 70 ships, 33 aircraft, and nearly 5,000 men. The official mission was scientific and mapping work. Per multiple accounts (including Malmgren's indirect reference via [[james-forrestal]]), the expedition encountered UAPs that reportedly fired on the ships with energy weapons, forcing an early return. Byrd's subsequent congressional testimony referred to an "enemy" capable of flying from pole to pole with tremendous speed.

## Forrestal Connection

[[james-forrestal]], as Secretary of the Navy, managed Admiral Byrd and Operation High Jump. [[harold-malmgren]] references this chain (Forrestal → Byrd → Operation High Jump → UAP encounters) as evidence that Forrestal had direct access to firsthand UAP material — and subsequently briefed President Kennedy on the subject.

## Significance

Byrd's encounter, if accurate, represents one of the most dramatic early Cold War UAP incidents: a large military force directly engaged by unknown craft in international waters. The fact that Forrestal — the first Secretary of Defense — was managing this program places UAP awareness at the very top of the postwar national security establishment from its founding.
