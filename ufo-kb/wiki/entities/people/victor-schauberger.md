# Victor Schauberger

Austrian inventor and naturalist who developed unconventional theories of fluid dynamics and propulsion. During World War II he was associated with the Nazi SS weapons program ([[kammler-stab]]) — a secret weapons facility in Czechoslovakia (modern-day Poland) run by SS General Hans Kammler. Schauberger designed what he called an "impeller" — a flying saucer concept based on vortex propulsion principles.

Following the war, Operation Paperclip ([[operation-paperclip]]) brought Schauberger and other Kammler-Stab researchers to the United States, with [[wright-patterson]] as a primary receiving institution. This represents one of the three pathways [[jesse-michaels]] identifies for why Wright-Patterson would have had intense interest in anti-gravity research in the late 1940s and 1950s.

## Cook's Family Diary Visit (G7Ns4Aq1tVc)

During his investigation for [[hunt-for-zero-point]], [[nick-cook]] visited the Schauberger family and was shown **Victor's diary from 1942** — the year Kammler's secret projects began. The diary's entries are damning as corroborating evidence:

- The locations Schauberger noted that he was traveling to ("a trail he says he can't talk about") are **all places which Cook had already mapped onto the Kammler Kingdom** — the documented network of secret SS laboratories, outposts, and underground facilities that Kammler built to develop his special weaponry
- Schauberger wrote to his son: **"What I am doing is secret"**
- Cook notes Schauberger was in "Yonet in Czechoslovakia" (Jonastal area) — the same Kammler-Stab operational area

This diary is the most direct documentary confirmation of Schauberger's Kammler-Stab involvement: not secondhand military records, but Schauberger's own contemporaneous writing placing himself at Kammler sites with an explicit note of secrecy.

Cook's account also includes "arcane stories about things that Schauberger had built that purportedly took off — running air and water through at very high rotation speeds." Cook notes this would still be challenging to replicate even today.

## UAPGerb's Account (D2tKCFmJjks)

[[uap-gerb]] describes an additional dimension to Schauberger's technology transfer: the **US Army Counter Intelligence Corps tricked Schauberger** into giving his propulsion research to US authorities, which then routed it to [[brookhaven-national-lab]] (via [[nick-cook]]'s reporting). This frames the transfer as coercive/deceptive rather than voluntary Paperclip recruitment.
