# John Archibald Wheeler

Princeton physicist (1911–2008); one of the most influential physicists of the 20th century. Mentored under Bohr, collaborated with Einstein at Princeton's Institute for Advanced Study, and trained a generation of physicists including Richard Feynman. In addition to his technical contributions, Wheeler attended the 1957 gravity research conference at [[unc-chapel-hill]] alongside [[peter-bergmann]], [[richard-feynman]], and [[bryce-dewitt]] — a meeting sponsored by Wright Air Development Center, placing top theoretical physicists in contact with Air Force-funded advanced propulsion research.

**Coined terminology**: Wheeler coined "black hole" (1967), "wormhole," and "quantum foam" — making him the inventor of much of the standard vocabulary of extreme physics. His terminological contributions reflect a career at the conceptual frontier of spacetime, quantum mechanics, and gravitation — the same domain as the propulsion physics the KB traces under [[anti-gravity-manhattan-project]] and [[gravity-manipulation-propulsion]].

**Participatory Universe / "It from Bit"**: Wheeler's most KB-relevant contribution is his late-career "participatory universe" thesis, which holds that at the quantum level, reality only acquires definite properties when observed — and that information and consciousness are more fundamental than matter (summarized as "It from Bit"). This is not merely philosophical: Wheeler argued experimentally that the universe is observer-created, pointing to delayed-choice quantum experiments where the observer's choice after a photon has already traveled affects what the photon was doing at the moment of emission.

**Retrocausality and time**: The delayed-choice experiment Wheeler proposed (and others confirmed) establishes that measurement can affect past events — a form of backward causation. [[jesse-michaels]] and researchers in the [[time-travel-uap]] cluster cite Wheeler's framework to argue that the apparent paradoxes of NHI physics (including apparent time manipulation) have legitimate theoretical grounding in established quantum mechanics.

**1957 gravity conference**: Wheeler's presence at the [[unc-chapel-hill]] 1957 conference alongside Air Force representatives and contractors including [[agnew-bahnson]] situates him in the same institutional moment as the alleged [[anti-gravity-manhattan-project]] effort. Whether Wheeler was aware of or contributed to classified anti-gravity research remains unknown; his public work on spacetime geometry and gravitational waves ran in parallel with classified contractor work on the same physics.
