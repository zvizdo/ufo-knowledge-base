# Maxwell W. Hunter II

Aerospace engineer; Member of the Professional Staff, National Aeronautics and Space Council (NASC), Executive Office of the President (1963). Later worked at Douglas Aircraft and became a noted advocate for SSTO (Single Stage to Orbit) launch vehicles.

**1963 alien contact policy memo**: In July 1963, during deliberations on BNSP Task I, Hunter wrote "Thoughts on the Space Alien Race Question" to Robert F. Packard at the DoS Office of International Scientific Affairs. The memo systematically analyzed three alien contact scenarios (chemical Martians, nuclear civilization, FTL civilization) and their policy implications — concluding that no meaningful preparation was possible and that policy response would be determined by "grand panic."

## NASC Career and the 1963 Memo

Maxwell W. Hunter II (1922-2001) was an aerospace engineer at Douglas Aircraft (1944-1961) where he led design work on the Nike-Zeus ABM, the Thor IRBM, and the Delta launch vehicle. In 1961 he joined the National Aeronautics and Space Council (NASC) staff under Vice President Lyndon Johnson, serving as a senior staff scientist with portfolio over national space-policy questions.

His July 18, 1963 NASC memo — released decades later as part of the gov-release PURSUE batch — argued that the executive branch should develop a Basic National Space Policy ("BNSP") addressing the contingency of confirmed extraterrestrial contact. The memo treats contact as a low-probability but high-consequence scenario warranting advance planning; it concludes with what Hunter calls the "grand panic" risk if disclosure happens without prior policy framework. Hunter later joined Lockheed (1965-1987) where he worked on the Strategic Defense Initiative.
