# George Trimble

Vice President of [[martin-corporation]] and head of their anti-gravity research division, the Research Institute for Advanced Study (RIAS). The existence of a dedicated anti-gravity division within a major aerospace contractor, led by its VP, is one of the concrete institutional data points cited in [[5udx_SDdL3Y]] to establish that the 1950s anti-gravity research boom was real rather than fringe.

## Quoted Predictions

In the 1956 *Young Men's* magazine article ([[1956-g-engines-article]]), Trimble stated that the US was going to "beat gravity" and that doing so could be accomplished "in about the time it took to build the first atom bomb." This direct comparison to the Manhattan Project framing appears in the same article in which Lawrence Bell ([[lawrence-bell]]) claimed to be working with "nuclear fuels and equipment to cancel out gravity."

## RIAS and Lewis Whitten

Trimble ran RIAS, which employed [[lewis-whitten]] as Martin Corporation's representative. Whitten attended the 1957 [[unc-chapel-hill]] gravity research conference under [[agnew-bahnson]]'s patronage — connecting RIAS directly to the theoretical physics community.

## Cook's Encounter and Trimble's Refusal

The [[1956-g-engines-article]] landed anonymously on [[nick-cook]]'s Jane's desk in the early 1990s. Cook initially filed it as intriguing but moved on. He returned to it when he ran into a Lockheed PR executive he knew well and asked about interviewing Trimble — who he had heard was still alive.

The Lockheed contact relayed Trimble's response: he didn't want to speak to Cook "now or ever" and sounded "really quite spooked." Cook reports Trimble initially seemed open to the interview but then did a complete 180 — as if something had been said or discovered that triggered a refusal rooted in fear.

Cook: "I don't mind telling you he sounded scared." This moment — a 180-degree flip from initial openness to absolute refusal with evident fear — became the catalyzing moment for Cook's full investigation.
