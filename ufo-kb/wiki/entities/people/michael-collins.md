# Michael Collins

Command module pilot for Apollo 11 (July 1969). While Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin walked on the Moon, Collins orbited alone in the command module for approximately 21.5 hours — the most isolated human in history at that moment. Collins did not walk on the lunar surface.

## Star Visibility Contradiction

[[bart-sibrel]] and [[joe-rogan]] cite a documented inconsistency in Collins' testimony:

**Apollo 11 post-flight press conference (1969):**
> "We were never able to see stars from the lunar surface or on the daylight side of the moon by eye without looking through the optics. I don't recall during the period of time that we were photographing the sonar what stars we could see. I don't remember seeing any."

**Michael Collins, *Carrying the Fire* (1974):**
Collins describes the stars as magnificent.

Rogan's note: Collins wasn't even on the lunar surface — he was in the command module. His "can't see stars" statement at the press conference is therefore either:
1. Confused/misattributed (genuine error about what applies to him vs. the surface crew)
2. Deliberate misdirection consistent with a staged mission

## Gemini 15 Spacewalk Photo

The photograph purported to show Collins conducting a spacewalk during Gemini 10 (not 15 as sometimes cited) has been identified as a training photo with the space background blacked out. This is the one confirmed case of NASA producing staged imagery. [[bart-sibrel]] uses it to establish that NASA was willing to fake photographs.
