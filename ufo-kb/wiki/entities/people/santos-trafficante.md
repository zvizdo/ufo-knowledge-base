# Santos Trafficante

Tampa/Miami Mafia boss and central link in the CIA–Mafia assassination pipeline organized by [[richard-nixon]] in 1960. Trafficante had operated casinos in Batista's Cuba in partnership with [[paul-helliwell]] (CIA front operative) and facilitated the narcotics-casino-political triangle in Southeast Asia. His attorney of record was Hank Gonzalez, a lawyer at the firm where [[danny-sheehan]] worked.

## Background

Ran organized crime operations in Tampa and Miami. Under Batista's Cuba, operated Havana casinos in partnership with Sam Giancana and Johnny Roselli through the CIA's [[paul-helliwell]] connection. After the Castro revolution, lost the Cuba gambling revenue and became a willing participant in CIA plans to reverse it.

## Role in the S Force / JFK Assassination

Per [[danny-sheehan]]'s account (sourced partly from Trafficante's own revelations to the law firm):

1. Nixon → [[howard-hughes]] → [[robert-maheu]] → [[johnny-roselli]] → [[sam-giancana]] → Trafficante: this was the chain of delegation that assembled the assassination team targeting Castro.
2. The three key operational planning meetings were held at the **Fontainebleau Hotel in Miami**, attended by Trafficante, Giancana, Roselli, and Maheu.
3. [[sheffield-edwards]] (CIA Chief of Security) greenlit the operation at a formal meeting with Trafficante.
4. When the assassination team was turned from Castro to Kennedy (after [[allen-dulles]] discovered JFK's secret disarmament letters to Khrushchev), Trafficante's network was part of the operational apparatus.
5. Trafficante revealed portions of this history to the law firm — **partially firsthand, partially disclosed under pressure of proximity to the investigation** — which Sheehan accessed through his work there.

## Heroin Trafficking Connection

Trafficante's Cuba partnerships with [[paul-helliwell]] extended to Southeast Asia. Helliwell ran Sea Supply Corporation (CIA front), and the same triangle — casinos, political protection, heroin — ran from Batista's Cuba to Southeast Asian ops. Sheehan frames this as the financial substrate that made the S Force politically possible: the Mafia had leverage over, and was owed by, the CIA.
