# Roger Leir

American podiatric surgeon and UFO researcher who became the most prominent figure in the surgical investigation of alleged extraterrestrial implants. Leir died on March 14, 2014, from complications of open-heart surgery.

## Background

Leir practiced podiatric medicine in Thousand Oaks, California. His involvement in UFO research began in 1995 when he attended a lecture by Derrel Sims — a UFO investigator who had been collecting accounts from people claiming to have foreign objects implanted in their bodies following alien contact experiences. Leir agreed to examine and potentially operate on two patients; the resulting surgeries launched a research program that consumed much of the remainder of his career.

He co-founded A&S Research with Sims and later operated independently. He authored several books including "The Aliens and the Scalpel" (1998).

## Implant Removal Surgeries

Over approximately 19 years, Leir performed more than 17 implant removal surgeries. The procedural approach:

1. Patients were identified through UFO researcher networks as reporting abduction or contact experiences
2. Pre-operative imaging (X-ray, MRI) was used to locate foreign objects in soft tissue
3. Objects were removed under sterile surgical conditions
4. Specimens were sent to multiple independent laboratories for analysis

### Reported Lab Findings

Labs used for analysis reportedly included New Mexico Tech (metallurgy/mineralogy). Findings consistently reported by Leir (and contested by mainstream scientists who were not given access to specimens):

- **Isotopic ratios**: Metallic specimens reportedly had isotopic ratios of constituent elements that differ from naturally occurring terrestrial ore sources, suggesting either meteoritic origin or artificial isotope separation
- **Alloy composition**: Some specimens reportedly contained metallic alloys — including iron and nickel combinations — in arrangements not produced by any known terrestrial manufacturing process
- **RF emission**: Several objects reportedly emitted radio frequency signals detectable before removal and, briefly, during extraction — after which the signal ceased, suggesting a powered device that stopped functioning when physically disturbed
- **Absence of inflammatory response**: Removed objects were typically found encased in a dark membrane that prevented tissue rejection; there was no foreign-body immune response despite no biocompatible coating of any known type

No full independent peer-reviewed analysis of Leir's specimens has been published in a mainstream scientific journal.

## Collaborative Network

Leir worked within the broader UFO research community alongside figures including Derrel Sims, [[steven-greer]] (Disclosure Project), and various abduction researchers. His work was discussed at conferences including the International UFO Congress and was referenced by major UAP figures as supporting evidence for physical NHI contact.

McKinnon notes with evident regret that Leir was deceased by the time of the interview, foreclosing the most technically credible avenue for independent investigation of his alleged implant. This is directly relevant to the [[verichip]] page.

The connection McKinnon draws is significant: he is not claiming alien abduction, but government surveillance via [[verichip]] technology. Leir's surgical protocol would be applicable regardless of the origin (government or NHI) of the implant.

## Critical Assessment

Implant removal surgery, while technically possible and executed under legitimate medical conditions, does not by itself constitute evidence of extraterrestrial origin. Critics note:

- No specimens have been made available to independent researchers under chain-of-custody conditions
- Isotopic anomalies, if genuine, could reflect meteoritic fragments (not uncommon as environmental contaminants) rather than artificial construction
- RF emission claims were never independently replicated with calibrated equipment by outside parties
- The patient selection process (self-reported abductees) introduces strong selection bias

The lack of peer-reviewed publication remains the central limitation of Leir's entire body of work.

## Steve Coburn Account (iPxbILOMQ08)

[[steve-coburn]] is Leir's closest scientific collaborator and provides the most detailed firsthand account of Leir's personality and methodology from within the research program. Key additions from this source:

**Origin story precision**: Leir initially called the implant subject "ridiculous" when [[derrel-sims]] raised it at a UFO Congress. A mutual friend later persuaded Leir to reconsider. Leir's offer to Sims: "Get some patients down here, we'll X-ray them, take the object out, and see what it is." This conditional, empirical approach — let's just look — became the template for all subsequent work.

**Coburn's recruitment**: Coburn walked into Leir's office as a patient claiming a possible implant; Leir was initially skeptical. Prescription for X-ray revealed "a bent piece of wire." Leir then invited Coburn to attend removals and later to analyze specimens at Coburn's workplace lab.

**Publication attempt**: Leir and Coburn drafted a paper and submitted it to the **Journal of Scientific Exploration**. The journal initially expressed enthusiasm, then reversed — telling them the paper was "ridiculous." They were not claiming alien origin; they reported "an unknown object recovered from someone's leg." Coburn is certain someone intervened. This is the primary reason Leir has no peer-reviewed publications — not lack of effort.

**Implant protocol**: Leir developed and taught Coburn a detection protocol using a stud finder (conductive object detector) plus G-meter (magnetometer). A double hit = almost certainly an implant, because implants consistently have magnetic fields.

**Object count**: Coburn specifies 17 removals from 17 different people over approximately 20 years.

**Death circumstances**: Leir died before he could meet with [[eric-mitchell-experiencer]] in Eureka Springs, Arkansas — a meeting scheduled approximately two weeks before his passing.

## Varginha Connection

Around 2013, Leir contacted [[james-fox]] urgently about his Varginha research. He said: "I've got to talk to you — I've got things I need to discuss and I need to go on camera." Fox was working 17-hour days and kept postponing. Leir, angry, said "Well, if you're not going to meet with me…" and gave Fox his Varginha-related tapes. Leir died shortly after. Fox later retrieved and incorporated the tapes into his 2025 updated film *Moment of Contact: New Revelations*. According to the Roger Leir book on Varginha, at least one other living creature survived the crash and underwent a procedure at a hospital — with the two doctors who attended it having some form of communication with it.
