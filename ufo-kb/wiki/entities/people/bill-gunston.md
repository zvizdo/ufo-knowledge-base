# Bill Gunston

Described by [[nick-cook]] as "the doyen" of aerospace journalism — a "very dogged data guy" whose technical credibility within the industry was exceptional. Gunston was another editor at [[janes-defence-weekly]].

## The B2 Performance Anomaly

Gunston published a story making a striking claim about the B2 Spirit stealth bomber: **its performance data does not add up given the power of its engines** — i.e., the aircraft should not be able to take off. He argued this implies an undisclosed propulsion mechanism.

He then invoked the work of [[thomas-townsend-brown]], describing the B2 as having "leading edges of millions of volts positive, followed by trailing edges of millions of volts negative" — precisely the capacitor configuration at the heart of Brown's electrogravitic thrust research (the [[biefeld-brown-effect]]).

## Self-Censorship: The Tower of London

Crucially, Gunston then publicly dropped the story with the explicit explanation: **"I don't want to reside in the Tower of London."** This is a veiled way of acknowledging that he believed the subject was classified and that publishing further would risk serious consequences — confirming rather than undermining the claim's significance.

Cook signs off on Gunston's account as credible, framing it as further evidence for [[townsend-brown-b2-connection]].

## Significance

Gunston's statement is significant because:
1. He was not a UFO advocate — he was a rigorous aviation data journalist.
2. His invocation of Brown's specific mechanism (positive leading / negative trailing voltage configuration) shows he had technical familiarity with the electrogravitics literature.
3. His self-censorship is itself an epistemic data point: he believed the story was true enough to be dangerous.
