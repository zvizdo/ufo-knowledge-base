# Scott Aaronson

Theoretical computer scientist at the University of Texas at Austin, specializing in quantum computing and computational complexity. Author of *Quantum Computing Since Democritus* (2013) and his widely-read Shtetl-Optimized blog. [[martin-shkreli]] calls him "probably the smartest person alive today."

## Relevance to This KB

### Matter Continuous vs. Discrete: The Simulation Debate

In [[2Mb6NPPjCyg]], Shkreli cites Aaronson as holding that **matter is continuous and infinitely divisible** — contrasting with Shkreli's own position that the Planck constant represents the "resolution of our simulator," a hard pixel limit below which the substrate doesn't compute.

The stakes of this disagreement:
- If matter is **discrete** (Planck-pixelated), the simulation hypothesis gains hard physical support: the programmer chose a minimum unit, and any civilization harnessing sub-Planck energy could potentially exploit the simulation's architecture to become "masters of space and time."
- If matter is **continuous** (Aaronson's position), real numbers are instantiated physically, irrational numbers present no paradox, and the Planck length is merely a computational / observational limit rather than an ontological one.

Shkreli uses the Pythagorean discovery of irrational numbers (√2 being incommensurable) as an argument for discreteness — if matter were truly continuous, every length should be expressible as a ratio, which fails for √2.

### NP Problems and the Limits of Neural Shortcuts

Aaronson's work on NP-completeness is referenced in the context of SHA-256 cryptographic hashing — some problems (like finding a hash preimage) admit no neural-network shortcut, only brute force. Shkreli uses this to argue that chemistry screening is a genuinely NP-like problem that requires distributed brute-force compute rather than AI cleverness.
