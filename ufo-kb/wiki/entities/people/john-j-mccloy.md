# John J. McCloy

John Jay McCloy (1895–1989) was one of the most powerful unelected Americans of the 20th century, known informally as "The Chairman of the Establishment." His career encompassed service as Assistant Secretary of War (under Stimson), US Military Governor of Germany (1949–52), President of the World Bank (1947–49), Chairman of the Council on Foreign Relations (1953–70), and — most controversially — membership on the Warren Commission, which investigated the Kennedy assassination.

## Background

McCloy's affiliation with Brown Brothers Harriman placed him in the same institutional network as [[robert-lovett]] and [[robert-anderson]]. His role in postwar Germany gave him direct oversight of the denazification program — which critics argue he deliberately undermined by shortening sentences and releasing convicted war criminals. This context is relevant to the broader question of Nazi network integration into Western intelligence structures.
