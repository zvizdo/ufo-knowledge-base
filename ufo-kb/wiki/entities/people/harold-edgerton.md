# Harold Edgerton

American electrical engineer and inventor (1903–1990), professor at MIT and co-founder of [[egng]] (Edgerton, Germeshausen and Grier). Known as "Doc Edgerton," he is the "E" in EG&G — the contractor that became the operational backbone of US nuclear testing and, by multiple accounts, one of the primary custodians of UAP secrecy infrastructure.

## Stroboscopic Photography

Edgerton pioneered stroboscopic high-speed photography — the technique of using extremely short, precisely timed flashes to freeze motion on film. This became indispensable for capturing the microsecond-scale dynamics of nuclear detonations, which destroy all conventional electronic sensors via EMP. EG&G's mastery of this technique made it the natural choice to document nuclear tests and to handle other highly sensitive photographic documentation tasks.

## Vannevar Bush Connection

Edgerton was a close associate of [[vannevar-bush]] — the OSRD director who is most strongly associated with [[majestic-12]] and the architecture of post-war UAP secrecy. This places Edgerton within the innermost circle of the post-Manhattan Project physics and secrecy network.

## EG&G's Reach

The company Edgerton co-founded became the prime contractor at:
- The Nevada Test Site (nuclear testing)
- [[cape-canaveral]] (space launches)
- [[area-51]] (classified aircraft and alleged UAP programs)
- [[kennedy-space-center]] (NASA support)
