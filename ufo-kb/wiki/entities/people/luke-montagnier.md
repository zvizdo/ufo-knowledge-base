# Luc Montagnier

French virologist who shared the 2008 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for the discovery of HIV. In his later career, Montagnier became controversial for experiments suggesting that **DNA can transmit structural information electromagnetically through water** — findings widely dismissed by mainstream science but embraced by researchers working in extended electrodynamics and scalar physics.

## DNA Water Teleportation

Montagnier's key experiment (published 2011):
- Two DNA samples placed in separate containers connected only by a wire (some versions: no physical connection at all)
- The receiving container of water, after exposure to low-frequency EM emissions from the original DNA, was able to act as a template for PCR amplification of the original sequence
- Montagnier interpreted this as the DNA emitting **electromagnetic signatures** that induce **aqueous nanostructures** in nearby water — structures that carry the same information

This was widely described as pseudoscience. However, [[dave-rossi]] and others interpret it through the lens of scalar/vector potential physics:
- The information carrier is not a conventional EM wave but longitudinal scalar waves — which do not attenuate over standard distances
- Helicoidal scalar waves (Meyl's model) are structurally matched to DNA geometry, making DNA a natural receiver and emitter
- [[peter-gariaev]]'s "linguistic wave genetics" is a parallel theory from the same direction
