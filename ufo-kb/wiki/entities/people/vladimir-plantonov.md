# Vladimir Plantonov

Retired Soviet army colonel who, in approximately 1994, gave journalist [[george-knapp]] a series of still-classified Soviet military documents about UFO activity — and allowed Knapp to take them back to the United States, effectively smuggling still-classified documents out of Russia. Plantonov's documents included an account of the [[1982-soviet-ukraine-missiles]] incident.

## Background

During [[george-knapp]]'s 1994 trip to Russia (made approximately three years after the Soviet Union's dissolution in December 1991), Knapp interviewed scores of former Soviet military personnel about UFO activity during the Soviet era. Plantonov was one of these sources and went further than any other: he produced actual Soviet military documents and permitted Knapp to photograph or take them.

## Key Disclosure

Plantonov's documents included an account of a Soviet Ukraine incident in which:
- A **huge disc-shaped object** was observed hovering over a Soviet missile base in Soviet Ukraine
- Missiles in one of the launch capsules **suddenly entered countdown mode**
- For **15 seconds** the missiles were apparently preparing to launch
- The missiles were then **deactivated without any human intervention** — in contrast to the [[1966-minot-ufo-missiles]] incident where the American operators had to manually flip inhibit switches

Had the missiles actually launched, it could have triggered a nuclear exchange and World War III.

## Significance

This incident — if the documents are authentic — is the single most alarming nuclear-UAP data point known: not missiles being disabled but missiles being *activated* and then deactivated, all without human agency. It parallels the [[1966-minot-ufo-missiles]] case (David Shindell's launch indicator), but is even more extreme.

[[robert-hastings]] treats the Plantonov documents as significant corroboration that the nuclear-UAP nexus is global and that the Soviet military was having the same experiences as the American military, independently.
