# L. Ron Hubbard

Lafayette Ronald Hubbard (1911–1986) was a prolific science fiction author who, in 1950, published *Dianetics: The Modern Science of Mental Health* and subsequently founded the Church of Scientology (1954). His relevance to this KB is concentrated at three junctures.

## Jack Parsons Connection

In 1945–46, Hubbard embedded himself in [[jack-parsons]]'s Thelemic occult household in Pasadena — the OTO Agapé Lodge. Parsons, co-founder of JPL and a key figure in early American rocketry, was performing the "Babalon Working" ritual with Hubbard as his assistant (the "Scribe"). The ritual's stated goal was to summon a Thelemic deity into a human vessel. Hubbard subsequently absconded with Parsons's girlfriend and $10,000 of his money. Parsons died in a laboratory explosion in 1952.

This intersection of aerospace engineering, occult practice, and a figure who would go on to found a religion is cited by [[lue-elizondo]] as a data point in the [[occult-aerospace-connection]] pattern.

## Scientology Cosmology and UAP Parallels

Hubbard's secret OT III materials (leaked publicly in litigation, available in court records) describe:
- An alien galactic confederacy ruler named Xenu who, 75 million years ago, transported billions of people to Earth and murdered them with hydrogen bombs near volcanoes
- The "thetans" (souls) of these murdered beings were captured, implanted with false religious memories, and clustered around human bodies
- OT auditing is meant to identify and release these "body thetans"

This cosmology sits adjacent to — but distinct from — standard UFO/NHI discourse. It predates the mainstream contactee tradition and shares structural similarities with ancient-astronaut claims.

## Intelligence Community Connections

Hubbard served in the US Navy during WWII. He claimed a decorated combat career; Navy records show a more ambiguous record. Multiple accounts allege early Scientology had CIA or ONI connections; these remain largely unverified in primary sources. What is documented: the FBI conducted extensive surveillance of Scientology, and the relationship between Scientology and the intelligence community was adversarial (Scientology's "Operation Snow White" — the largest documented infiltration of US government agencies by a private organization, 1973–1977).
