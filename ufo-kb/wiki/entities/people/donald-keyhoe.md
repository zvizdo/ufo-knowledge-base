# Donald Keyhoe

Retired US Marine Corps Major. One of the earliest and most prominent public advocates for UFO disclosure. Author of several books on the topic in the 1950s. Keyhoe's credibility rests on his military background and his connections to active-duty officers who shared information with him.

## Notable Claims

### Pre-Sputnik Artificial Satellites (1953)
Keyhoe stated that the Air Force was tracking **two unknown artificial satellites** at 400 and 600 miles above Earth in 1953 — four years before the Soviet Union launched Sputnik (October 1957). This claim is corroborated by the same-year establishment of the [[white-sands-missile-range]] Near-Earth Satellite Project under [[lincoln-lapaz]] and [[clyde-tombaugh]], which was ostensibly tasked to search for natural "mini-moons" but may have been initiated in response to these unknowns. The project's 1954 report paradoxically claimed the objects were "natural asteroids" while simultaneously concluding in its final report that there are no natural satellites of Earth.

## Significance

Keyhoe's 1953 report preceded Sputnik by four years, making the satellites definitively non-Soviet in origin if confirmed. The establishment of a dedicated tracking project at White Sands that same year, led by scientists with UFO-adjacent backgrounds ([[lincoln-lapaz]] of Project Twinkle; [[clyde-tombaugh]] who remained a lifelong UFO believer), suggests official awareness of these unknowns was real.

## Dolan's Assessment (GQlWf54K_7Y)

[[richard-dolan]] names Keyhoe as his **#1 research inspiration** — ranking him above all other figures in the field:

> "Donald Keyhoe is just — I went through all his books very carefully. He was my #1."

Dolan credits Keyhoe's Marine Corps background and his network of active-duty Navy contacts as giving him access to encounter data and institutional perspectives that no purely civilian researcher could have matched. Keyhoe had "an ear to top Navy brass" at a time when the Navy's resentment of Air Force UFO jurisdiction was creating a parallel intelligence stream.

Dolan also draws a connection between Keyhoe's efforts with [[nicap]] to force congressional UFO hearings in 1962 and the suspicious abrupt resignation of [[roscoe-hillenkoetter]] from NICAP at exactly that moment — suggesting coordinated pressure to defuse the civilian-military disclosure push.
