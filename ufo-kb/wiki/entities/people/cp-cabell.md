# C.P. Cabell

General Charles Pearre Cabell served as USAF Director of Intelligence in 1950. His September 8, 1950 directive — stating that "no publicity be given this reporting or analysis activity" regarding New Mexico aerial phenomena — is the earliest documented coordinated inter-agency UAP information suppression order.

## The 1950 no-publicity directive

Cabell's directive covered the entire reporting and analysis program around anomalous aerial phenomena near New Mexico's nuclear installations (Sandia, Los Alamos, Kirtland). Critically, the directive was simultaneously copied to:
- Director, FBI (J. Edgar Hoover)
- Director, CIA (Roscoe Hillenkoetter in 1950)

This simultaneous distribution established a three-agency (USAF + FBI + CIA) information management agreement — not a unilateral USAF suppression decision. All three agencies were separately receiving UAP-relevant information, and Cabell was coordinating across them.

## Later career and JFK assassination connection

Cabell was later appointed Deputy Director of the CIA under Allen Dulles. He was fired by President John F. Kennedy following the Bay of Pigs disaster in 1961. His brother, Earle Cabell, was Mayor of Dallas on November 22, 1963 — the day of President Kennedy's assassination. This circumstance has been noted by researchers examining possible intelligence community involvement in the assassination.
