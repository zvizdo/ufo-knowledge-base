# Jay Andrew Kissner

New Mexico state representative; a "proto-Grusch" figure, per [[uap-gerb]] on [[u7g5Sn1DJF4]].

In 1994–1995, Kissner conducted a series of interviews with firsthand legacy-program personnel spanning the Office of Naval Research, the Air Force, and other agencies. His findings documented:

- Starting from 1947: Vannevar Bush spearheaded retrieval of crash craft; [[afswp]] (Armed Forces Special Weapons Project, later [[dtra]]) was the operational unit.
- **The 1950s shift**: In the early 1950s, there was a change in how crash craft were held — custody transferred to government-owned contractor-operated laboratories (DOE/NNSA national labs: Sandia, Lawrence Livermore, Oak Ridge) as permanent custodians. This comports with UAPGerb's "1954 thesis" that the 1954 Atomic Energy Agreement gave expanded powers to NNSA national labs to house exotic materials.

UAPGerb confirmed Kissner was still alive and contactable (as of the interview), but Kissner "doesn't really want to talk."

## Legacy

Kissner briefed fellow New Mexico state representative Steven Schiff on his findings. Schiff launched a large GAO probe and investigation into Roswell — but the Air Force had lost all of its relevant files. Schiff was on record complaining that records were "rampantly" and "inappropriately destroyed."

Linda Moulton Howe presented Kissner's testimony at the 2002 or 2004 Second Annual Crash Retrieval Conference. UAPGerb contacted Kissner himself to confirm; Kissner did not deny the account but refused to discuss further.
