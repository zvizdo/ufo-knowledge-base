# Harold Abramson

Harold Alexander Abramson (1905–1980) was an allergist and physician at Mount Sinai Hospital in New York who was among the Central Intelligence Agency's principal researchers in [[mk-ultra]] LSD experimentation. He received CIA funding to study LSD's effects on psychological processes and was regarded as one of the leading figures in the CIA's psychedelic research program.

**Frank Olson involvement**: When [[frank-olson]] — the Fort Detrick biological warfare researcher who was covertly dosed with LSD by [[sidney-gottlieb]] in November 1953 — began exhibiting extreme psychological distress, Abramson was the doctor called in. The choice of Abramson — an MK-Ultra-affiliated researcher rather than a conventional psychiatrist — has been cited by Frank Olson's son [[eric-olson]] and researcher [[colin-ross]] as evidence of coordinated management. Abramson's role was to manage the situation in ways that served CIA interests rather than to provide independent psychiatric care.

**LSD research**: Abramson hosted LSD study groups at his Long Island home in the 1950s — informal sessions where colleagues, academics, and CIA-connected figures took LSD together. His research output included papers on LSD's effects on thought processes, Siamese fighting fish behavior, and human subjects, all funded directly or indirectly through CIA cut-outs.

**CIA cut-out funding**: Abramson received MK-Ultra funding through the Josiah Macy Jr. Foundation, one of the primary CIA pass-through foundations for consciousness and psychological research in the 1950s. This arrangement was revealed through the Church Committee investigations and subsequent FOIA releases in the 1970s.

**Significance**: Abramson represents the physician-researcher class that made MK-Ultra operationally possible — credentialed clinicians with academic cover who provided institutional legitimacy for human experimentation outside normal medical ethics constraints.
