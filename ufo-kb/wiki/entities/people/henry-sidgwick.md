# Henry Sidgwick

British moral philosopher (1838–1900), Knightsbridge Professor of Moral Philosophy at Cambridge, and one of the three co-founders of the Society for Psychical Research (SPR) in 1882, alongside [[frederic-myers]] and [[edmund-gurney]]. Sidgwick served as the SPR's first President and provided the institutional credibility and scholarly authority that established the organization as a serious academic endeavor rather than a spiritualist society.

**SPR founding**: Sidgwick's involvement was crucial for establishing the SPR's academic legitimacy. His position as a Cambridge professor signaled to the scholarly world that the investigation of anomalous phenomena — telepathy, apparitions, hypnosis — was an intellectually serious enterprise. The SPR's early membership included multiple Fellows of the Royal Society and other credentialed scientists.

**Research program**: Sidgwick presided over the SPR's most productive early period, overseeing the systematic collection and analysis of spontaneous cases that produced *Phantasms of the Living* (1886) and the Census of Hallucinations (published 1894). He remained skeptical throughout, insisting on methodological rigor and resisting premature conclusions.

**Sidgwick effect**: The impossibility of satisfying Sidgwick's evidential standards has been called "the Sidgwick effect" — the observation that the standards demanded of anomalous research are systematically higher than those accepted for conventional research, creating an asymmetric burden of proof.

**KB relevance**: Sidgwick represents the late 19th century's most serious academic attempt to apply empirical methodology to anomalous phenomena — the [[spectral-revolution]] — and the institutional challenges that such work faced even with elite academic sponsorship.
