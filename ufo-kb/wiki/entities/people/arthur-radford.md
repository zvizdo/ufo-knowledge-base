# Arthur Radford

Admiral Arthur Radford was a senior US Navy officer and, later, Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff (1953–1957). He is relevant to UAP history for a 1950 firsthand sighting that directly triggered an independent Navy investigation into UFOs.

## 1950 Sighting with Dan Kimball

In 1950, Radford was flying from California to Hawaii alongside [[dan-kimball]] (Secretary of the Navy). Both men were in separate aircraft. An unidentified object appeared and circled first one plane, then the other — with each aircraft crew independently witnessing the encounter.

[[richard-dolan]] describes this as particularly credible:
> "Secretary of the Navy Dan Kimball himself had a UFO sighting. He was flying from California to Hawaii with Admiral Radford. Two separate aircraft were accosted."

The encounter was so striking that Kimball immediately ordered the Navy to conduct its own independent UFO investigation — separately from the Air Force's [[project-blue-book]] program, which the Navy resented for taking jurisdictional control of UFO incidents aboard Navy vessels and at Navy installations.

## Historical Significance

The Radford-Kimball sighting is a pivot point in the [[navy-ufo-rivalry]] dynamic: it gave the Navy's civilian leadership direct personal motivation to demand institutional independence in UFO investigation. The Air Force's monopoly on official UFO investigation was a source of deep resentment within the Navy, and the 1950 sighting made it personal for Kimball — the Navy's top civilian.

Radford's later role as JCS Chairman (during the Eisenhower administration) would have placed him at the apex of the US military command structure during the critical 1953–1957 period, when the UAP secrecy architecture was being consolidated.
