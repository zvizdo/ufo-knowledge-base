# Frank Malina

Frank J. Malina (1912–1981) was an American aerospace engineer and kinetic artist who was among the founders of the GALCIT Rocket Research Project at Caltech — the group that became the Jet Propulsion Laboratory ([[jpl]]). He worked alongside [[jack-parsons]], Theodore von Kármán, and other early US rocketry pioneers in the late 1930s and 1940s.

**JPL co-founder**: Malina was instrumental in the founding and early development of JPL, contributing to the first US government-funded rocket research program (the ORDCIT program) and helping establish the institutional structure that JPL eventually became. He served as JPL's first director.

**Communist Party affiliation**: Malina was a documented member of the Communist Party of the United States, a fact that was discovered during Cold War security investigations. This affiliation effectively ended his US government-accessible career. He left the US in 1953 and settled in Paris, where he later worked for UNESCO's science division.

**Second career as artist**: After leaving aerospace, Malina became a prominent kinetic and light artist, founding the journal *Leonardo* (still published) and developing motorized luminous art installations. His trajectory from rocket engineer to avant-garde artist is one of the more unusual career arcs among early JPL figures.

**Context for Caltech/JPL**: Malina's case — alongside [[jack-parsons]]'s occultism — illustrates the unusual ideological diversity among the founders of US aerospace. The same institutional incubator (Caltech/JPL) produced a Communist Party member engineer and a Thelemic occultist rocket engineer, both central to the US space program's foundations.
