# Arthur Exon

## Background

Brigadier General Arthur E. Exon, USAF (ret.), served as Wing Commander at [[wright-patterson|Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]] — the institutional hub historically linked to the analysis of recovered UAP material. In the 1990s Exon gave on-camera video testimony stating that wreckage from the [[1947-roswell|Roswell crash]] was transported to Wright-Patterson for analysis after the event. He has since become one of the most-cited general-officer-level corroborators of the crash-retrieval narrative — both because of the rank he held and because his account is preserved on tape rather than being re-told secondhand.

He is one of two general officers (with [[ralph-debose|General DeBose]]) whose 1990s on-record statements [[david-grusch|David Grusch]] adduces in support of the post-Roswell program's continuity at Wright-Patterson. [[hal-puthoff|Hal Puthoff]] independently names Exon as one of the witnesses [[eric-davis|Eric Davis]] interviewed directly while developing the senior-source dataset for the AAWSAP / [[to-the-stars-academy|TTSA]] era.

## Notable Claims

### Roswell Wreckage Transport (1990s On-Camera)

In video interviews recorded in the 1990s, Exon stated that material from the 1947 Roswell crash was flown to Wright-Patterson for analysis. He is recorded as giving similar statements alongside General [[ralph-debose|DeBose]]. The DeBose-Exon pairing — two retired general officers, both with relevant institutional positions, both willing to go on camera — is treated by the modern disclosure community as one of the highest-rank corroboration data points on the Roswell question outside of the original 1947 press materials.

[[david-grusch|Grusch]] in [[R8TqBrrqL4U]] situates the Wright-Patterson testimony alongside the [[1933-magenta-crash|Magenta]] case to construct a continuous timeline: European retrieval (1944–45) → Roswell analysis at Wright-Patterson (1947 onward) → Lockheed hull analysis (1950s onward) → ongoing legacy programs. Exon's testimony anchors the Wright-Patterson node in that chain.

### Eric Davis Interview — "True Non-Human Intelligence Craft"

In [[Gf_tKn9TaP8]] (Joe Rogan Experience #2314, May 2025), [[hal-puthoff]] discloses that Eric Davis — Puthoff's senior scientific adviser — interviewed Exon directly. Per Puthoff: Exon, along with a second source (rendered "Deose" / "Del Roy" in casual transcript, likely [[ralph-debose|DeBose]] again), told Davis that the materials taken to Wright-Patterson were "the real deal — unidentifiable, off-planet provenance." When Joe Rogan asked what came out of the analysis, Puthoff answered: "Not that the public would hear about. Not that I could disclose."

This is significant because it places Exon's testimony not only in the 1990s public-video record but also in Eric Davis's classified-program interview corpus — i.e., the same dataset that informs current TTSA-era assessments of the Roswell event. Puthoff's stated personal conclusion that Roswell was "a true non-human intelligence craft that crashed" rests in part on Exon's account routed through Davis.

### Institutional Position as Credibility Anchor

Exon's value to the disclosure record is structural: he was not a researcher or witness, but a Wing Commander at the facility where the wreckage was reportedly analyzed. Crash-retrieval claims typically fail credibility tests because they rely on civilian witnesses or low-rank personnel. Exon's testimony is the inverse — a retired general officer with custodial authority over the relevant installation, speaking on camera, with corroboration from a same-rank peer. The [[credibility-frameworks|credibility framework]] strongly weights this combination.

## Open Questions

- The exact 1990s video interview(s) — when filmed, by whom, full transcript — would settle a substantial amount of debate about how strong his on-camera language was. Multiple researchers have referenced the footage; the original chain-of-custody for the video record is worth confirming.
- The pairing with "DeBose / Deose / Del Roy" in Puthoff's spoken account is ambiguous: most likely the same person ([[ralph-debose|General Ralph DeBose]]) but the transcript renderings differ.
- What Exon said specifically about *what happened* to the materials after analysis at Wright-Patterson — versus only that they arrived there — is the highest-leverage missing detail.
