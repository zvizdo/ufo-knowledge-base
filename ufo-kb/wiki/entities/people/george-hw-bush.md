# George H.W. Bush

CIA Director under President Ford (1976–1977); 41st President of the United States (1989–1993). Two distinct UAP-adjacent claims attach to Bush in the Sheehan account.

## JFK Assassination — Pre-Knowledge Claim

[[danny-sheehan]]'s claim (**hearsay / secondhand** — sourced from persons he does not fully name in the episode):
- Bush was present at **Clint Murchison's ranch in Dallas** the night before the JFK assassination (November 21, 1963).
- Three named witnesses were allegedly present alongside Bush at that gathering. Sheehan does not name them in this episode.
- Sheehan characterizes this as Bush having **"pre-knowledge"** of the planned assassination — not as directing the operation.
- Others allegedly present at the Murchison gathering: Lyndon Johnson and J. Edgar Hoover (per Sheehan and other researchers).

> ⚠ This is one of the more contested claims in the JFK assassination literature. The Murchison gathering is documented in certain accounts (including Madeleine Duncan Brown's claims) but is disputed by mainstream historians. Sheehan presents it as established within his research.

## Accidental CIA Director Briefing on Early-1960s UAP Event (Davis Account — LnAiNChnuEQ)

[[eric-davis]] states he had direct conversations with HW Bush "back in 2003" in which Bush described what happened when he was CIA Director. **This is Davis's firsthand report of HW Bush's firsthand account.**

A Pentagon liaison officer was briefing Bush and **wrongly assumed he had already been briefed** by CIA personnel on UAP. The liaison officer began describing "a major UAP event that took place in the early '60s":

> "Bush's eyes lit up and this is what he's told me he said what are you talking about."

Bush's reaction when told "there's a there there":

> "He said well I want to see the evidence and the evidence was in the form of a film and some documentation reports or something but the film was the primary thing."

The liaison officer then told Bush: **"No. You don't have a need to know. This is DoD intelligence."** Access would only be granted if a new UAP event occurred requiring a presidential decision, routed through the National Security Advisor.

The reason Bush was never briefed through proper CIA channels: **the CIA portfolio holder never told Bush who in the CIA was running the UAP program**, so Bush had no one to call. The Pentagon liaison assumed CIA had already handled it.

> ⚠ Note on chain: This is Davis's 2003 account of what HW Bush told him about a 1970s-era experience (Bush was CIA Director 1976–1977). It is **secondhand** in the KB — Davis → KB. The underlying event is HW Bush's firsthand account.

This account has a different character than Sheehan's account (above): the CIA Carter briefing (Sheehan) is about Bush as outgoing CIA Director briefing an incoming president; the Davis account is about Bush as CIA Director discovering the UAP program existed and being denied access.

## UAP Briefing to Carter

When [[jimmy-carter]] became president after seeing a UFO in 1969 (with 11 other witnesses, he filed a report with NICAP), he arrived at the White House with a genuine desire to understand the phenomenon and requested a CIA briefing. Bush, as outgoing CIA Director, gave Carter a briefing that Sheehan characterizes as **deliberately incomplete** — Carter received managed information rather than full disclosure.

## Iran-Contra Connection

[[theodore-shackley]], who Sheehan places at the operational center of the Miami CIA station on November 22, 1963, later appears in the Iran-Contra scandal — which Sheehan litigated through the Christic Institute. Felix Rodriguez, a CIA operative, met with a Bush aide during Iran-Contra. Sheehan argues this represents a consistent throughline: Shackley's network ran from the JFK operation through Southeast Asia assassinations to Iran-Contra, all in proximity to Bush.
