# John Callahan

Former FAA chief of accidents and investigations. The pivotal custodian of the most important UAP radar data set in the public domain.

## Background

Held the position of chief of accidents and investigations at the Federal Aviation Administration. In 1986, after the [[1986-jal-1628]] encounter was reported, the case landed on his desk for official review.

## Notable Claims / Actions

When Reagan's scientific team and CIA arrived to collect all data related to JAL 1628, Callahan cooperated — but first made a covert copy of the entire data set, which he placed in a box under his desk. He did not disclose this copy.

The moment at the handover meeting that [[kevin-knuth]] finds most significant: officials told Callahan, "We've never had 45 minutes of radar data from one of these things before." This implies prior, shorter radar contacts existed and were known to Reagan's scientific apparatus.

Callahan held the copied data for approximately 20 years before retiring and then making it public. This data set subsequently became the basis for [[daniel-koumbi]]'s analysis finding 9,000–11,000 G accelerations at 250,000 mph.

Kevin Knuth: "He's a hero, right? I mean, that's amazing."
