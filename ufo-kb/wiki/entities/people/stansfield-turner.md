# Stansfield Turner

## Background

Stansfield Turner served as Director of Central Intelligence from March 1977 to January 1981 under President Jimmy Carter. A US Navy admiral, he led CIA during a period when the agency's psychic-research contract at Stanford Research Institute — the foundation of what became the [[stargate-program|Stargate program]] — was active and producing operational results.

## Notable Claims

### The Soviet Plane in Africa Challenge

[[hal-puthoff]] (Gf_tKn9TaP8) recounts that Turner, aware of the SRI [[remote-viewing]] program, challenged it to locate a downed Soviet plane somewhere in Africa: "Why don't they find the plane for you?" Two remote viewers — one at SRI and one at [[wright-patterson-air-force-base|Wright-Patterson]] Foreign Technology Division — placed an X on a map 3 miles from where the plane (a Tu-22 bomber) actually was. The wreckage was recovered before the Soviets located it. Turner's framing of the program as a real operational asset — rather than a research curiosity — is part of why it remained funded across administrations.

### Carter Disclosure Breach

Carter himself later breached the security of the operation in a Georgia speech post-presidency, mentioning publicly that "we had some remote viewers so-called and they pinpointed where it was." Puthoff says of Turner's authorization vs. Carter's later mention: "That was a breach of security. But a president can. They're allowed to do that."
