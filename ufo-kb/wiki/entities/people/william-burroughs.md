# William S. Burroughs

William Seward Burroughs (1914–1997) was an American author central to the Beat Generation literary movement. His novel *Naked Lunch* (1959) — fragmented, non-linear, dealing with addiction, control, and the bureaucratic machinery of power — became one of the defining texts of 20th-century avant-garde literature.

**Consciousness and control**: Burroughs' work is deeply concerned with systems of control over consciousness — addiction as control, language as control, the media as control. His "cut-up" technique (slicing and randomly reassembling texts to expose hidden messages) was both a literary device and a meditation on how meaning is programmed into language. He explicitly compared his cut-up method to jamming or subverting "control systems."

**Psychedelic and consciousness experiments**: Burroughs experimented extensively with substances including heroin, yage (ayahuasca — documented in *The Yage Letters* with Allen Ginsberg), and other psychedelics. His descriptions of ayahuasca-induced entity contact and reality dissolution preceded the contemporary DMT/entity-contact research literature by decades.

**Andrew Gallimore connection**: [[andrew-gallimore]]'s forthcoming *Death by Astonishment* — a history of science's encounter with DMT — uses a phrase attributed to Burroughs (or alternatively to Terence McKenna; attribution is contested per [[jesse-michaels]]). The phrase resonates with Gallimore's framing of DMT entity contact as exceeding the cognitive frameworks available to process it.

**Terence McKenna parallel**: Both Burroughs and [[terence-mckenna]] explored psychedelic entity contact, non-ordinary states, and the information-processing limits of human consciousness — making them natural reference points for contemporary DMT/UAP consciousness research.
