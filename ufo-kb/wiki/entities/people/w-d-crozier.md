# W. D. Crozier (Dr. William Crozier)

> *Stub. Expand with biographical detail, full publication record, post-1950 career, and any additional UAP-related primary documentation as it enters the KB.*

Aerosol physicist at the [[new-mexico-school-of-mines]] in Socorro, NM (Research and Development Division). Per [[dow-uap-d17-sandia-base-1948-1950]] (specifically the August 1949 LaPaz Sixth Report at pages 8-11 of the released PDF), Crozier had been "closely associated, in 1943-44, with Dr. Crozier at New Mexico Proving Grounds while working on sabotaged and proximity fused shells and knew that he had satisfactory clearance" — establishing his cleared-status credentials for involvement in the green-fireball investigation.

## 1949 Green Fireballs Particle-Collection Program

Per [[dow-uap-d17-sandia-base-1948-1950]] pages 4-7:

- **Apparatus**: impactment-based air sampler (34 liters per minute) developed under the New Mexico School of Mines aerosol research project; collected ~90% of airborne particles >1 micron diameter onto microscope slides with glycerin-gelatin + rubeanic acid (dithiooxamide) adhesive for copper / nickel / cobalt identification.
- **Field campaign 7/25-8/1/1949**: 18 collections at 9 locations on a Monday-Tuesday trip: Socorro Campus (starting collection 10:00 a.m. 7/25), Pastura, Santa Rosa, Routes 66/84 junction, Las Vegas NM, Carmoncito, Glorieta, Pecos.
- **Results**: collection R-104L (Highway 84, 17 miles north of Highway 66) contained "**a large number of copper-bearing particles**" of granular material ~30 microns; smaller copper particles (5 micron) appeared 35-145 hours after the 24 July 1949 fireball; **three perfectly spherical cobalt-indicating 12-micron particles** in the 7/26 PM collection ("quite unique in the experience of the present"); occasional nickel particles.
- **August 8, 1949 flight**: B-25 from Kirtland Field followed the August 6 fireball event at 23,000 ft, 41 hours after the event; few copper indications, apparently surface origin.
- **Co-author**: Ben K. Seely (also of New Mexico School of Mines)
- **Conclusion** (Crozier's own): "the results of the present investigation should be regarded as negative or inconclusive" — though copper-bearing particles 35-145 hours post-fireball remained unexplained
- **LaPaz interpretation**: if the copper particles are truly from the fireballs, "the fireballs are not conventional meteorites. Copper is one of the rarest of the elements found in meteorites."

## Proposed Project Twinkle Role

In LaPaz's 23 May 1950 Seventh Report (and his cited 20 February 1950 letter to Dr. P. H. Wyckoff at Atmospheric Physics Laboratory), LaPaz proposed a four-person scientific team for what became [[project-twinkle]]:

- Dr. Fred L. Whipple (Harvard College Observatory) — photographic phase
- Dr. Peter M. Millman (Dominion Observatory) — spectrographic phase
- Dr. L. A. Manning (Stanford) — radar investigation
- **Dr. William Crozier (New Mexico School of Mines) — dust collection and identification**

Crozier's continued cooperation was specified in LaPaz's recommendation; he agreed to cooperate at the highest attainable altitudes "in Dr. Crozier's opinion, a B-36 or B-50 should be made available for such work."
