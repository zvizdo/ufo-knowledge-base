# Carl Ruck

Professor of Classical Studies at **Boston University**. In 1978, Ruck co-authored *[[the-road-to-eleusis]]* with R. Gordon Wasson and Albert Hofmann — the chemist who first synthesized LSD. The book formally proposed that the kykeon (the ceremonial drink consumed at the Eleusinian Mysteries) contained **ergot** (*Claviceps purpurea*), a grain fungus whose alkaloids are precursors to LSD.

Ruck's central question, cited by [[brian-muraresku]] as the launching point of *[[the-immortality-key]]* 42 years later: **Was Dionysus the god of wine, or the god of psychedelics?**

## The Road to Eleusis (1978)

The 1978 book was a landmark interdisciplinary collaboration:
- **R. Gordon Wasson** — ethnomycologist who documented ritual use of Amanita muscaria among indigenous Mexicans
- **Albert Hofmann** — synthesizer of LSD; applied knowledge of ergot alkaloid chemistry to the kykeon question
- **Carl Ruck** — provided the Classical philological and archaeological context

The argument: ancient Greek literary sources describe the kykeon as an "uncanny" preparation that produced experiences far beyond ordinary wine intoxication; ergot grows on wheat and barley, the primary grains of the ancient Mediterranean; and the Eleusinian rites produced effects (permanent life-transformation, sense of immortality) consistent with a psychedelic experience rather than mere religious ceremony.

## Influence

Ruck's question sent [[brian-muraresku]] down a 25-year research path culminating in *[[the-immortality-key]]* (2020). [[andrew-gallimore]] and [[jesse-michaels]] also reference the Ruck/Wasson thesis when discussing ancient DMT/psychedelic access to NHI. Muraresku describes Ruck as a founding figure of the modern academic investigation into psychedelics and ancient religion.
