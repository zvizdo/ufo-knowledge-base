# Jim Lowsky

Missiles engineer who worked at the **Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)** during the 1980s–2000s. [[eric-davis]] states that Lowsky told him directly — not through a secondhand chain — that he personally "breached the hull and walked inside of a UFO." Davis finds the claim credible: "There's no reason not to." Lowsky published a book describing his account, though he did not name the specific craft location or the contractor holding the program.

**How Davis learned the location context**: Davis's knowledge of where the event likely occurred comes from conversations with Jay Stratton (former UAPTF director) rather than from Lowsky directly. Lowsky described the personal experience; the location intelligence is a separate thread.

**Evidential weight in the crash-retrieval corpus**: In the [[xnxasfyHtfo]] transcript, Davis presents Lowsky's hull-entry as one of two first-person accounts he has personal access to. The second is a Clinton-administration four-star general who used his authority to authorize his own visit to the program site — touched the craft, spoke with employees, confirmed the program's existence. Admiral Wilson was denied access through normal channels during the same period but was told by gatekeepers: "crash retrieval, non-human technology, off-world, nine human intelligence sources." The Lowsky case and the four-star case together represent Davis's baseline for the claim that multiple individuals have had direct physical craft contact.

**Publication**: Lowsky published a book on his experience, placing his account in the self-published or limited-distribution tier of crash-retrieval testimony. Unlike whistleblowers operating through congressional channels, Lowsky's disclosure is through a book rather than sworn testimony.
