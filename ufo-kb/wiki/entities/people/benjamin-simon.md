# Dr. Benjamin Simon

Boston psychiatrist (1896–1984) who conducted the foundational hypnotic regression sessions with [[betty-hill]] and [[barney-hill]] beginning in late 1963 — over two years after their September 1961 abduction experience near Lancaster, New Hampshire. Simon had no prior connection to UFO research; he was a respected neuropsychiatrist who had used hypnosis clinically with PTSD patients returning from WWII, and the Hills came to him because they were experiencing psychological distress connected to their encounter.

**Methodology and conclusions**: Simon conducted extended hypnotic regression with both Betty and Barney in separate sessions, so neither could hear or be influenced by the other's account. Despite being conducted separately, the two accounts showed striking independent consistency on the core events: the road, the craft, the beings, the examination, and the missing time. Simon nonetheless concluded that the hypnotic material most likely represented an anxiety-driven confabulation — possibly stemming from Betty's vivid nightmares in the weeks after the encounter — rather than literal recovered memory. He was especially concerned about Betty's influence on Barney and the possibility that the accounts were unconsciously synchronized.

**Methodological legacy**: Despite Simon's own skepticism about the literal interpretation, his regression methodology — recording and documenting hypnotically recovered memories of abduction experiences — became the template for subsequent abduction research. [[leo-sprinkle]], [[budd-hopkins]], and [[john-mack]] all conducted similar sessions with later abductees, drawing on the Simon methodology. This created a body of documentation but also the confabulation critique that has shadowed the entire abduction category.

**Credibility framework**: Simon's value in the KB is as a credentialed, skeptical witness to the Hill sessions who documented extensive material without endorsing the literal interpretation. His documented skepticism makes the material more rather than less credible in some respects — the independent consistency under separate regression by a clinician actively seeking confabulation is more persuasive than consistent accounts under a true-believer practitioner.
