# Steven Aldrich

Steven Aldrich served as director of the CIA's Office of Research and Development ([[oard]]), the classified successor program to [[mk-ultra]] that continued behavioral control research through the 1970s while MK-Ultra was officially being wound down publicly. His role is documented in John Marx's *The Search for the Manchurian Candidate*, the primary source for OARD's operations in the KB.

**OARD directorship**: As OARD director, Aldrich oversaw a significant technological escalation beyond MK-Ultra's primary focus on LSD and hypnosis. OARD's expanded research included:
- Remote-controlled brain electrode implants in animal brains to control movement
- Psychosurgery (surgical alteration of brain tissue to modify behavior)
- Memory erasure research using chemical or surgical methods
- Biological weapons delivery investigations using animals as delivery vehicles

**Institutional continuity significance**: Aldrich and OARD represent the documented pattern in which publicly terminated classified programs continue under new names with upgraded capabilities and better funding. The MK-Ultra "shutdown" in the 1960s did not end the CIA's behavioral control research; it renamed and expanded it. Aldrich is the named official managing this continuation.

**Research venues**: OARD conducted experiments at Holmesburg State Prison in Philadelphia — a full decade after MK-Ultra's public termination — demonstrating that the congressional and public narrative of program shutdown was false.
