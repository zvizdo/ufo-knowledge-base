# Marc Andreessen

American software engineer and venture capitalist (born 1971). Co-created Mosaic, the first widely-used graphical web browser (1993), while at the University of Illinois. Co-founded Netscape (1994) and Andreessen Horowitz (a16z, 2009), one of the largest venture capital firms in Silicon Valley. Major investor in AI, biotech, defense technology, and consumer internet companies.

**Willow quantum computing statement**: Cited in [[ai-as-cosmic-gateway]] for a statement made on Joe Rogan's podcast, describing Google's Willow quantum chip: that if every atom in the universe were converted into a supercomputer, all those computers working together would not solve the computation that Willow solved in minutes before the universe died of heat death. Andreessen used this to suggest the multiverse may need to exist — that quantum computation is distributed across parallel universes — as a public popularization of the "many-worlds interpretation is physically real" claim. In the KB, this statement appears as part of a cluster of arguments by technologists and scientists that the implications of quantum computing point toward fundamental changes in understanding of physical reality.

**NSC classified physics meeting**: Through his position at [[andreessen-horowitz]], Andreessen attended a meeting with senior National Security Council officials in which a senior official acknowledged that "entire physics areas had been classified before" — cited by physicist david pines as the most significant on-record admission of secret physics programs. The combination of the Willow/multiverse statement and the NSC classified physics acknowledgment positions Andreessen at an unusual intersection of public AI-physics commentary and private government science briefings.

**Tech-government nexus**: a16z operates at the intersection of Silicon Valley and national security technology. The firm has been active in AI policy, defense technology investment (through its American Dynamism fund), and engagement with federal agencies. This makes Andreessen's access to NSC-level briefings consistent with his institutional position rather than remarkable.
