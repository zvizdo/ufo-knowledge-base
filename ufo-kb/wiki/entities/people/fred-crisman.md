# Fred Crisman

Fred Crisman was the supervisor of harbor patrolman Harold Dahl and a central figure in the 1947 [[1947-maury-island]] incident. He and Dahl claimed to have witnessed six doughnut-shaped craft over Puget Sound near Maury Island on June 21, 1947 — three days before [[kenneth-arnold]]'s famous Mount Rainier sighting.

## Role in the Maury Island Incident

Crisman collected samples of the alleged disc debris (a slag-like material). On July 31, 1947, at the Winthrop Hotel in Tacoma, Washington, he and Dahl transferred this material to Army Air Forces Intelligence officers Captain William C. Davidson and Lieutenant Frank M. Brown. The following day, Davidson and Brown's B-25 (#1316) crashed near Kelso, Washington, killing both officers. Classified documents were aboard the aircraft.

The FBI's investigation ultimately concluded that the Maury Island story was fabricated to promote the sale of a gravel pit slag operation. Crisman was suspected of making anonymous calls related to the case.

## Later Associations

Crisman's name resurfaced decades later in connection with investigations into the JFK assassination network; he was allegedly identified as one of the "three tramps" photographed near Dealey Plaza on November 22, 1963, though this identification has never been confirmed.

[[ray-palmer]], editor of *Amazing Stories*, paid [[kenneth-arnold]] $200 to travel to Tacoma and investigate the Crisman/Dahl claims.
