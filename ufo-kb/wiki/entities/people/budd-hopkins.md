# Budd Hopkins

American abstract expressionist painter (1931–2011) who, following a personal UFO sighting in 1964, became one of the most prominent figures in abduction research. Hopkins is credited with systematizing the concept of "missing time" — the phenomenon where abductees report a gap in conscious memory corresponding to the duration of an apparent abduction event. He used hypnotic regression extensively to recover accounts of these gaps, documenting thousands of cases over his career.

**Research methodology**: Hopkins's approach drew on hypnotic regression sessions to recover memories of abduction experiences, producing detailed narratives of alien medical procedures, craft interiors, and entity interaction. His methodology became a template for subsequent abduction researchers, including [[john-mack]] and [[david-jacobs]]. Critics including [[benjamin-simon]] raised the confabulation problem — hypnotic regression is not a reliable memory recovery tool and may generate false memories consistent with subject expectations.

**Key works**: *Missing Time* (1981) introduced the missing-time concept to a broad audience. *Intruders* (1987) documented the Kathie Davis case and argued for a genetic harvest program. *Witnessed* (1996) focused on the Linda Cortile case — an alleged abduction from a Manhattan apartment building witnessed by UN Secretary-General Perez de Cuellar's motorcade.

**Intruders Foundation**: Hopkins founded the Intruders Foundation in 1989, a nonprofit providing support and documentation for abduction experiencers.

**KB relevance**: Hopkins is the canonical bridge between the [[1961-betty-barney-hill-abduction]] methodology and the modern abduction research corpus. His framing — particularly the genetic harvest thesis — runs through much of the subsequent literature and into current NHI discourse about biological interest in humanity.
