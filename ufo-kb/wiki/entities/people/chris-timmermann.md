# Chris Timmermann

Researcher at [[imperial-college-london]] who implemented the DMTX protocol developed by [[andrew-gallimore]] and [[rick-strassman]] (2015 paper), moving it from theoretical feasibility to human trials. Timmermann's group developed a more sophisticated pharmacokinetic model than the original Gallimore-Strassman "back of the envelope" version and used it to run human DMTX experiments.

## DMTX Trial Results

- Successfully extended the DMT state to **30 minutes** of stable breakthrough experience
- No major physiological adverse effects: heart rate, blood pressure normal throughout
- Anxiety peaked at the beginning of the infusion, then settled during the extended state
- The intensity of experience remained stable across the 30-minute window
- Some subjects did not complete the full trial due to intensity, but no severe adverse reactions

This proof-of-concept opened the door for systematic scientific work within the extended DMT state. Subsequent groups extended further: a Basel group (Matias Lekai) to 90 minutes; another group to 6 hours at low dose.

## 1-Hour Sessions with MRI Scanning (Hancock Account)

[[graham-hancock]] in [[dfPfPB601hw]] describes a more advanced stage of Timmermann's work than the initial 30-minute DMTX trials:

> "They've found a way to keep people in the DMT state for an **hour**... and while doing that they put them in MRI scanners and they interview them."

Key features of this protocol:
- Subjects maintained in a **steady-state DMT experience for one hour** via IV infusion
- Simultaneous **MRI scanning** throughout
- Researchers conduct real-time interviews, though subjects can sometimes not talk until after the journey
- The extended duration allows subjects to "process the experience much more effectively" than a 10-minute trip
- "Large numbers of volunteers" have now participated

**Entity encounter consistency finding**: Across many subjects, the entity encounters are "remarkably consistent — they're encountering the same entities in the same setting." Hancock's conclusion: normally when large numbers of people agree on the same experience, we define that experience as real. Only cultural prejudice leads to dismissing these encounters as "mere hallucinations."

This represents the most serious empirical work on the phenomenology of the DMT realm currently under way.
