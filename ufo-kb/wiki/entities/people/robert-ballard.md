# Robert Ballard

Oceanographer and deep-sea explorer; discoverer of the RMS Titanic wreck in 1985. Worked with French oceanographers to find some of the first hydrothermal vent ecosystems on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge. Founded the **Ocean Exploration Trust**, which operates its own research ship and ROV systems; [[noaa]] funds Ballard's organization for ocean exploration work.

[[tim-gallaudet]] knew Ballard through their overlapping careers in ocean science and entered an agreement while Gallaudet was at NOAA for Ballard to put NOAA scientists aboard his ship and share sonar and other data collected. Gallaudet credits Ballard as a close contact ("my buddy Robert Ballard") and uses Ballard's Titanic discovery as illustrative of how the deep ocean contains verifiable surprises.

In the context of the UAP discussion, Gallaudet's relationship with Ballard is relevant because the Ocean Exploration Trust's ROV and ship capability represents a possible platform for investigating USO-related seafloor features. Gallaudet framed the ocean as an analogue to space exploration: the Titanic's existence was "known" in principle but only confirmed by a physical asset descending to that depth — the same epistemological gap applies to USO features on the seafloor.

Ballard's career includes work at the **Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution** and collaboration with the Scripps Institution of Oceanography alumni network that Gallaudet also belongs to. His Argo/Jason ROV systems pioneered remotely operated seafloor survey capability that is now standard in deep-sea exploration.
