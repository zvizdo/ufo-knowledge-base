# James Doolittle

US Army Air Forces general (1896–1993); led the famous **1942 Doolittle Raid** on Tokyo (the first US air raid on Japan following Pearl Harbor). Holder of an MIT doctorate in aeronautical engineering — one of the first such doctorates awarded — and a post-war Shell Oil executive.

**1946 Ghost Rocket investigation**: At the request of the US government and Shell Oil's intelligence apparatus, Doolittle traveled to Sweden in 1946 to personally investigate the [[1946-swedish-ghost-rockets]] flap — the phenomenon where over 1,400 rocket- or cigar-shaped objects were observed descending on Norway and Sweden, virtually all entering water rather than crashing on land. Doolittle examined the evidence and produced a classified assessment. The assessment's conclusions remain classified or were destroyed; its existence is documented.

**Significance**: Doolittle's investigation represents one of the earliest known instances of a senior US military official conducting a formal on-site examination of an unidentified-aerial-phenomena case file. His credentials — MIT aeronautical doctorate, combat aviation command, Shell Oil intelligence contacts — gave the investigation unusual technical and institutional weight. The 1946 ghost rocket episode preceded the 1947 Roswell incident and the Twining memo by one year, suggesting US government UAP interest predates the conventional origin story by at least a year.

**Context in the early-UAP assessment chain**: Doolittle's ghost-rocket work sits immediately before [[nathan-twining]]'s 1947 memo concluding that UAP "something is really flying" and the formation of Project Sign. This sequence — Doolittle (1946) → Twining memo (1947) → Project Sign (1948) → Blue Book — is the documented institutional arc of early US government UAP investigation.
