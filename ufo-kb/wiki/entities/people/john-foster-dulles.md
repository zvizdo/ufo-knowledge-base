# John Foster Dulles

U.S. Secretary of State (1953–1959) under President Eisenhower. Brother of [[allen-dulles]], CIA Director (1953–1961). Together the Dulles brothers exercised near-simultaneous control over overt foreign policy (Foster at State) and covert intelligence (Allen at CIA) — a dual-portfolio concentration of power unique in American history, spanning the entire early UAP cover-up formation period.

**Sullivan & Cromwell**: Before government service, Foster Dulles was senior partner at Sullivan & Cromwell, the Wall Street law firm that represented German industrial interests including I.G. Farben, Krupp, and other corporations that used forced labor during WWII. In the pre-war period, Sullivan & Cromwell brokered loans and legal arrangements between American capital and the Nazi state. When FDR's Treasury Department (led by Henry Morgenthau) attempted to crack down on this network through the Trading with the Enemy Act, it was Foster Dulles's network that complicated enforcement.

**Eisenhower era and UAP formation**: The 1953–1959 period — precisely Foster's tenure at State — is when the KB's core UAP secrecy infrastructure was cemented. [[majestic-12]] (if real) operated in this window. The Robertson Panel (1953) produced the CIA's debunking policy. [[operation-paperclip]] personnel were deeply integrated into defense research. Foster and Allen's joint institutional control during this period makes them foundational figures in whatever structure was established.

**Death and aftermath**: Foster died in office in May 1959. Allen was fired by Kennedy in November 1961, then placed on the [[warren-commission]] investigating Kennedy's murder.
