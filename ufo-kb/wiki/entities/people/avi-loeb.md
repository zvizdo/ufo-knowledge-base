# Avi Loeb

Harvard professor, former chair of the Harvard astronomy department. Founded the [[galileo-project]] for systematic scientific UAP data collection. Proposed that [[2017-oumuamua]] (the first detected interstellar object) might be of artificial origin — specifically a defunct lightsail. One of the most credentialed mainstream scientists to publicly advocate for treating UAP as an empirical science problem.

Tough demeanor: conducted a snowstorm interview on his porch in a suit while Jesse bundled up and borrowed gloves.

## Background

Born in Israel; drafted to the military at 18 (paratrooper training). Describes himself as a "farm boy that is curious." Entered astronomy and rose to chair Harvard's astronomy department. His 2021 book *Extraterrestrial* presented the ʻOumuamua lightsail hypothesis to a general audience. The backlash from colleagues catalyzed the founding of the Galileo Project.

His self-described stance: "I feel that this subject is so important that I don't care how many wounds I get out of the interaction with some of my conservative colleagues. Just like a soldier who puts his body on the barbed wire so other soldiers can pass through."

## ʻOumuamua and the Lightsail Hypothesis

In 2017, the Pan-STARRS telescope discovered [[2017-oumuamua]], the first confirmed interstellar object. Its anomalies — non-gravitational acceleration without a cometary tail, extreme flat shape — led Loeb to propose in a 2018 paper that it could be an artificial lightsail. He rejected all natural-object proposals as "rocks of a type we've never seen before" — the same error a non-curious caveman would make encountering a cellphone.

Partial vindication: in September 2020, Pan-STARRS found another baffling object. It turned out to be a forgotten 1966 NASA rocket booster that uses sunlight for propulsion — a human-made lightsail analogue. Loeb's point: if humans build lightsails, why can't extraterrestrials?

## The Galileo Project

Founded after the June 2021 Pentagon UAP report. Key features:
- Multi-sensor array on Harvard College Observatory: optical, infrared, radar, audio, and magnetic sensors running continuously
- 100+ scientists engaged as of the time of this interview
- Privately funded (thousands of emails of support)
- Data fully public and transparent — distinguishing it from all government programs
- AI algorithms classify objects as bird, drone, or unidentified

The Galileo Project's three research tracks: (1) ground-based sensor detection of UAP overflights; (2) interstellar object characterization (ʻOumuamua follow-ons); (3) recovery and analysis of interstellar meteorite material.

Loeb's critique of government sensors: they're military-grade, designed for the battlefield, and classified so adversaries can't know their capabilities — making open scientific instrumentation impossible within that system.

## Credibility / Instruments-Only Stance

Loeb will not write a scientific paper based on eyewitness testimony — not even from decorated fighter pilots like [[david-fravor]]. His position is not that pilots are lying, but that testimony is not the same as scientific instrumentation. "You want to detect those things with instruments." He explicitly distinguishes from John Mack's approach (interviewing experiencers) — valid social science if the subject is people, but the subject here is objects.

This contrasts him with most UAP researchers in the KB who treat pilot testimony as primary evidence. Loeb wants instrument-grade data; until then, no paper.

## AI as Interstellar Traveler

Loeb's working model for UAP/NHI: advanced civilizations send AI-equipped technological probes rather than biological beings. Cosmic radiation rules out biological interstellar travelers. Homo sapiens has been physiologically unchanged for 200,000 years; only in the last 10,000 (5%) did it develop agriculture and — now — AI. Any civilization further along this arc would have sent its "technological kids" ahead. This is the [[interstellar-probe-hypothesis]].

AI advantage for UAP classification: unlike human scientists who are ego-attached to prior theories, AI would simply say "it doesn't look like any rock we've seen before — maybe it's equipment."

## Fermi Paradox Critique

Loeb finds the Fermi Paradox question "very presumptuous." Sitting at home saying "nobody is knocking so I have no neighbors" — the correct move is to look through windows with telescopes. Or: a fishing net with holes small enough to catch fish. "If you don't use a fishing net you will not find any [fish]." The Galileo Project is his fishing net.

He recognized the metaverse announcement as a Fermi resolution: civilizations that retreat into virtual reality still must obey the second law of thermodynamics — they produce entropy/waste that escapes into space. We could detect this "technological trash" as interstellar archaeology. See [[fermi-paradox]] and [[postbiological-civilization]].

## Proxima Centauri b / JWST City Lights

Loeb and a Stanford undergraduate student wrote a paper arguing the James Webb Space Telescope could look for city lights on the dark side of Proxima Centauri b — a planet in the habitable zone of the nearest star (Proxima Centauri), tidally locked with a permanent day and night side. Artificial illumination of the night side would be an unambiguous technosignature.

## Anti-Gravity

Einstein introduced the cosmological constant (negative gravity from vacuum) to keep the universe static; when expansion was confirmed, he called it his "biggest blunder." Decades later, astronomers discovered the expansion is *accelerating* — confirming the cosmological constant's reality and, with it, the existence of anti-gravity. Loeb: "We definitely know it is possible to produce anti-gravity because we see the universe accelerating — that's a fact." Whether anti-gravity can be harvested locally remains unknown.

## Connections to Eric Weinstein and the Galileo Project

[[eric-weinstein]] joined the [[galileo-project]]. In Loeb's account, he and Weinstein speculate about UAP physics; Weinstein proposes Geometric Unity-like properties; another colleague suggests macroscopic quantum phenomena as an emergent property of underlying quantum mechanics. Loeb is agnostic on the physics until data is gathered.

## Embarrassing Humanity

A characteristic Loeb position: sending 30 grams of Clyde Tombaugh's ashes on the New Horizons spacecraft was "very embarrassing" — ashes are burnt-up DNA, destroying the very genetic information they were meant to commemorate. An advanced extraterrestrial civilization would find this primitive ritual baffling.
