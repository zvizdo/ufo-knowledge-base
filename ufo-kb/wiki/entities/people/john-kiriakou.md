# John Kiriakou

CIA Operations officer who disclosed the existence of the CIA's waterboarding/torture program to the press — and was subsequently prosecuted under the Espionage Act, entrapped by the FBI and CIA, and served approximately four years in prison.

He chose to serve his sentence: "He'd do it again."

## Relevance to UAP Disclosure

[[jeremy-corbell]] invokes Kiriakou explicitly as the model for how intelligence agencies handle both whistleblowers and operatives who become liabilities:

> "That guy was famously CIA, and FBI tried to entrap him because he told the truth to the world that we were waterboarding and torturing people. So he served out of choice four years in prison as a CIA guy for disclosure going on the news of this fact, because it was the right thing to do."

Corbell's use of Kiriakou goes beyond the whistleblower parallel. He uses the Kiriakou case to describe the IC's *selection process*: agencies choose operatives with "sociopathic tendencies — not loose cannons," because those people can be controlled and deployed for violence or coercion. When they later become inconvenient (as whistleblowers), the same controlled operatives are then targeted by the same institutional apparatus that originally selected them.

The implication for the [[arrow-organization]] pressure campaign: Arrow's covert operators were selected using the same criteria, and the entrapment methodology used against Kiriakou is the same methodology Corbell describes Arrow using against UAP witnesses.
