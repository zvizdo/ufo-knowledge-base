# Socrates

Athenian philosopher (470–399 BC). Referenced in the Atlantis discourse as the interlocutor in Plato's *Timaeus* and *Critias*, the primary ancient texts describing Atlantis. In Plato's dialogues, Socrates hears the Atlantis account from Critias, who traces it to Solon's visit to Egypt.

## The Failed Return from the Cave

In [[KRDE1i3LDuU]], [[jim-madden]] makes the case that Socrates' execution by Athens is [[plato-philosopher]]'s real-world proof of the cave allegory's darkest prediction: the prisoner who escapes the cave and returns to enlighten the others will be rejected and killed. The Athenians convicted Socrates of impiety and corrupting youth — precisely the charges that accrue to someone who has undermined the settled reality of the cave (conventional religion, conventional politics) and cannot be accommodated back into it.

Madden: in ideal cities, the philosopher owes his education to the city and should return. But Plato says the ideal city will not occur naturally; in actual cities, the philosopher should not return. Socrates' fate is the demonstration.
