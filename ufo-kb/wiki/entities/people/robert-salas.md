# Robert Salas

USAF First Lieutenant (ret.), assigned to Oscar Flight, [[malmstrom-air-force-base]], Montana as a missile launch control officer. On March 24, 1967, Salas was on duty in the underground launch control capsule when he received two calls from the topside flight security controller reporting anomalous lights. Shortly after the second call — describing a large red-orange pulsating oval object — all 10 Minuteman I missiles went offline one by one in what guidance systems reported as a simultaneous failure of guidance-and-control electronics.

## Background

Salas served three years at Malmstrom. He and his crew commander [[fred-maywald]] were assigned to Oscar Flight, near Roy, Montana (~100 miles east of Great Falls). He was a graduate of the USAF Academy. After the incident, Salas signed a non-disclosure agreement administered by an [[afosi]] agent in his squadron commander's office. He later learned he had been conflating his own Oscar Flight incident with the earlier [[1967-echo-flight]] incident (Echo Flight, 8 days prior) for years until [[fred-maywald]] clarified the distinction around 1996. He is co-author of *Faded Giant* ([[faded-giant]]) with James Klotz.

## Notable Claims

- The missiles shut down one-at-a-time sequentially, not simultaneously — a pattern inconsistent with any known equipment failure mode.
- [[boeing]] engineers, hired by SAC to investigate, could reproduce a single guidance failure via injected signal but admitted they could not explain how the signal penetrated triple-shielded (8–9 inch diameter) cables to take down all 10 missiles.
- Between September 1966 and March 1967, he counts 30 missiles lost to UFO activity across three incidents (Minot Sept '66, Echo Flight March 16 '67, Oscar Flight March 24 '67).
- A [[wall-street-journal]] article (2024) by [[joel-shectman]], sourced from [[sean-kirkpatrick]], claimed a non-nuclear EMP test device caused the shutdown; Salas wrote a 14-point rebuttal. The cited EMP model was not operational until 1971–73.
- He is aware of witnesses who cannot go public due to NDAs and fear of career/life consequences.
- Trusts a classmate's claim of having flown a reverse-engineered craft; treats this as confirmation of [[reverse-engineered-craft]].
