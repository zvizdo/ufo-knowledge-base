# George Knapp

Investigative journalist based in Las Vegas who has been one of the most consequential UAP reporters in the field for over thirty years. His 1989 KLAS-TV broadcasts introduced [[bob-lazar]] to the public and remain the foundational documentation of Lazar's claims.

## Bob Lazar Investigation

Knapp interviewed Lazar multiple times beginning in 1989, initially with Lazar's face blurred. He subsequently spent years attempting to verify Lazar's background — finding the Los Alamos phone directory entry confirming employment, a newspaper article naming Lazar as a physicist there, and documenting anomalous radiation exposure on Lazar's vehicles. He remains the most thorough journalistic defender of Lazar's account.

After Lazar's disclosure, Knapp identified at least **six witnesses** who could have corroborated Lazar's presence at S4. All six were threatened and chose not to come forward. Knapp also flew with Lazar to Los Alamos post-disclosure; Lazar still knew the names of security guards and door combinations from his time there, which Knapp verified directly. Knapp has also captured unusual UAP footage from Baja Mexico that he has not yet released publicly.

## Skinwalker Ranch

Knapp investigated [[skinwalker-ranch]] (the Bigelow-owned Utah property with alleged multi-phenomenon activity) extensively in the 1990s–2000s, co-authoring [[hunt-for-the-skinwalker]] (2005) with [[nids]] researcher [[colm-kelleher]]. His reporting on the ranch was a direct precursor to the Bigelow/AAWSAP UAP program.

He subsequently co-authored [[skinwalkers-at-the-pentagon]] (2021) with Kelleher and DIA analyst [[james-lacatski]] — the partially declassified account of AAWSAP's ranch investigations, most notable for documenting the [[hitchhiker-effect]].

## NIDS Involvement

Knapp had a close working relationship with [[nids]] (National Institute for Discovery Science), [[robert-bigelow]]'s private UAP research organization, and had access to NIDS investigators and case files.

## Russia Trips — Soviet Military UFO Study

After becoming a UAP reporter, Knapp traveled to Russia twice after the Soviet collapse. He met "Nikolai" — Boris Yeltsin's national security adviser — at a US disarmament conference at the suggestion of a US Congressman. Knapp sent Nikolai back to Moscow with a stipend to find senior ex-military sources.

Eight months later, Knapp went to Russia and interviewed former Soviet military officers, including [[colonel-borosokolov]] who revealed the **1978–1988 Soviet military UFO study** — every Soviet military unit globally required to file UAP reports for 10 years, all routed to a single Ministry of Defense desk held by Colonel Sokolop. Knapp describes it as "probably the biggest UFO study in the history of the world." Colonel Sokolop also disclosed the Ukraine ICBM incident (corroborating [[1982-soviet-ukraine-missiles]]).

Knapp also received related classified Soviet documents from [[vladimir-plantonov]] covering the same period and incidents.

## 1975 Northern Tier Case

Knapp documented the 1974–75 [[1975-northern-tier-case|Northern Tier]] UAP wave — a series of incursions over US nuclear missile bases from Maine to Montana: in multiple cases UAPs disabled missiles; launch control codes may have been interfered with. Washington Post broke the story via FOIA in the 1970s. [[aawsap]] later re-investigated these cases and found original witnesses who confirmed the accounts.

## Holmes & Narver Stenographer Witness

Knapp interviewed a woman who worked as a stenographer at Holmes & Narver (defense contractor), who attended meetings between contractors and Air Force officials discussing crashed saucers. After each meeting, tapes were destroyed. After speaking to Knapp, she was threatened by two men who referenced her daughter's LA travel and said "it'd be terrible if something happened to either one of you." Six of Knapp's Lazar-background sources were similarly visited and silenced.

## Kirk Meyer Cover-Up

Knapp's investigation into Lazar's background at Los Alamos included tracking down **Kirk Meyer** — the headhunter firm that hired Lazar. Kirk Meyer initially confirmed Lazar's employment records, then two and a half years later reversed: "He was never here." This reversal — not Lazar's claims — was what "hooked" Knapp on the story.

## Alfred O'Donnell Disclosure (2001)

In 2001, a **senior [[egng]] manager named [[alfred-odonnell]]** disclosed to Knapp that EG&G had recovered a flying saucer in New Mexico and held a live being with humanlike features. This was reported by [[jesse-michaels]] in [[TNtlzEnl8rA]] as part of a broader examination of EG&G's role in UAP secrecy. If accurate, the claim establishes EG&G as not just a logistics and management contractor but a direct custodian of crash retrieval assets.
