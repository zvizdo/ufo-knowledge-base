# Carl Friston

British neuroscientist at University College London. Creator of the **free energy principle** — the most mathematically developed framework for understanding how biological agents resist entropy and maintain their organisation.

## Free Energy Principle and Centropy

The conventional picture from the second law of thermodynamics: the universe moves from order to disorder (entropy increases). Biological systems appear to reverse this locally — they maintain organisation, learn, and complexify. Friston's free energy principle formalises this: neural systems are attracted to **low-entropy, ordered states**, minimising "surprise" (the difference between predicted and actual sensory states).

This gives rise to the concept of **centropy** — the biological/conscious inverse of entropy. Where entropy is the tendency toward disorder, centropy is the tendency toward order driven by goal-directed, intentional systems.

[[riz-virk]] invokes Friston in [[aKZ_MUbuk_Q]] to argue that Consciousness is not merely an epiphenomenon of matter but an **entropy-reducing force** — possibly the primary driver of biological evolution. Combined with [[michael-levin]]'s finding that individual cells have goals and intentions, this points to intention as a fundamental feature of living systems, not a high-level emergent property.

In the simulation framework, Consciousness as an entropy-reducer makes sense: the simulation is designed to produce and preserve conscious agency — it would have a built-in drive toward order, complexity, and awareness.
