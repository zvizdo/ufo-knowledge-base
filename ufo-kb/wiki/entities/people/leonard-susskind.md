# Leonard Susskind

American theoretical physicist (b. 1940). Stanford professor; **one of the founders of string theory** (co-developed the earliest version in the early 1970s) and primary architect of the **holographic principle** in quantum gravity (the AdS/CFT correspondence idea that a lower-dimensional boundary theory can completely describe a higher-dimensional bulk space). Central figure in the black-hole information-paradox debates with Stephen Hawking — Susskind argued for information conservation; Hawking argued for information loss. Hawking eventually conceded.

**The Weinstein critique**: [[eric-weinstein]] is the primary figure in this KB who names Susskind by name as an exemplar of the string-theory establishment's self-referential quality. In [[xnxasfyHtfo]], Weinstein recounts that Susskind appeared on a podcast and said "we have to go back to the beginning. We have to question absolutely." — and then proceeded to say we should go back to "the foundations of string theory," not question the string framework itself. Weinstein treats this as the defining illustration of the [[string-theory-as-deflection]] dynamic: infinite regress within the paradigm, never outside it.

**The string-theory-as-deflection argument**: In UAP discourse ([[iQOibpIDx-4]], [[xnxasfyHtfo]]), the argument runs that string theory — introduced ~1972 and still experimentally unconfirmed after 50+ years — functions as an "intellectual dead end maintained by social pressure" that absorbs the talent and funding that might otherwise work on propulsion-relevant exotic physics. Whether intentional or emergent, the effect is the same: [[physics-lockdown]] by institutional capture rather than by classification. Susskind is named as the field's most credentialed senior figure, which makes him the primary exhibit in this argument. He himself has acknowledged stagnation: citing string theory and saying the field has achieved "a B+" in [[0qlppHc3-gg]].
