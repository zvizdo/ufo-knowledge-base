# Colonel Borosokolov

Former Soviet military officer interviewed by [[george-knapp]] during one of his two post-Soviet Russia trips in the 1990s. A contact described by Knapp as "a gold mine of information."

## What Borosokolov Disclosed

Borosokolov revealed the existence of a previously classified **10-year Soviet military UFO study (1978–1988)**:

- "For 10 years from '78 to '88, the Russian military empire — every military unit in Russia, in their whole military globally — if they saw any ball of light, anomalous aircraft, UFO, anything weird like that, they had to report it to the KGB."
- All reports were funneled to a single desk at the Ministry of Defense, held by **Colonel Sokolop** (a distinct person from Borosokolov).
- Borosokolov told Knapp about at least 45 different incidents (on-camera account was truncated at this point in the transcript).
- Knapp's assessment: "Probably the biggest UFO study in the history of the world."

## How Knapp Reached Him

Knapp arranged the Russia trip through an intermediary — a Russian physicist named "Nikolai" (Boris Yeltsin's national security adviser) whom Knapp met at a US national lab disarmament conference at the suggestion of a US Congressman. Knapp sent Nikolai back to Moscow with a stipend and instructions to "find people who were in a position to know — high up in the military or the political government — who had never talked about this before." It took eight months.

## Significance

The 1978–1988 Soviet study represents a Soviet-corroborating parallel to US nuclear-UAP encounter documentation (see [[ufo-nuclear-nexus]]). The systematic collection architecture — with all reports routed through the KGB to a single Ministry desk — mirrors what US investigative journalism (Knapp, [[robert-hastings]]) has documented about Western nuclear facility encounters.
