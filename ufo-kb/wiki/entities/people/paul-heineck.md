# Paul Heineck

Referenced by [[michael-p-masters]] (anthropologist and author of *Identified Flying Objects*) in connection with the [[future-humans-hypothesis]]. The specific citation in the KB context is: *"Why would they find us? Because we're their ancestors. Why would they care about this planet? Because they're said to inherit it."* — a framing that attributes NHI's interest in present-day humanity to genealogical inheritance motivation.

**Role in Masters's framework**: Masters's future-humans hypothesis proposes that NHI are human descendants from the far future who have developed time travel and return to interact with their ancestral population. The Heineck citation appears to contribute a specific motivational argument: what would drive future humans to return to the present? Heineck's framing suggests the answer is inheritance — present humans are the biological and cultural ancestors of future NHI, and the planet these future humans will eventually inherit connects the two populations.

**Significance of the citation**: The inheritance framing addresses one of the challenges for temporal NHI hypotheses: the motivation problem. If future humans have access to all of human history, why specifically interact with early 21st-century humans? Heineck's argument (as cited by Masters) is that genealogical continuity provides intrinsic motivation — future humans study their ancestors for the same reasons contemporary humans engage in genealogy research, and at civilizational timescales the motivations become even stronger.

**Documentation status**: Heineck's institutional affiliation, publication record, and whether this citation reflects original research or a conversational exchange with Masters are not documented in current KB source material. This page marks the citation for expansion if additional context is located.

**Temporal NHI framework**: The broader context is the KB's [[time-travel-uap]] cluster of hypotheses — the family of proposals suggesting that NHI may be temporally displaced humans rather than spatially displaced extraterrestrials. Masters is the primary academic proponent within the KB; Heineck is one of several figures cited in that work.
