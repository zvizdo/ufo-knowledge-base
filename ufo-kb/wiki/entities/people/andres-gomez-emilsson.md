# Andres Gomez Emilsson

Mathematician affiliated with the Qualia Research Institute; deeply interested in DMT phenomenology as a site of mathematical inquiry. Met with [[andrew-gallimore]] at the Psychedelic Science Conference in Denver, where he shared observations from DMT sessions that Gallimore describes as "blowing his mind."

## The Four-Color Theorem Observation

In DMT states, Emilsson observed entities repeatedly painting complex surfaces with four colors — precisely following the **four-color theorem**: any planar map of regions can be colored with just four colors such that no two adjacent regions share a color. This is a famous result in combinatorial mathematics that is easy to state but computationally hard to execute — for a complex map, it would require a smart human mathematician several hours of work by hand.

Emilsson observed the entities doing this **instantaneously and repeatedly**, without apparent effort.

[[andrew-gallimore]]'s interpretation: this is exactly the kind of evidence the DMTX program should be designed to capture. Most people in the DMT state are overwhelmed and cannot evaluate what they observe. A trained mathematician can say: "I know why this is impossible, and they are doing it anyway." The entities cannot help but betray their non-human intelligence — you just need to send the right observers.

## Significance

This observation is cited by Gallimore as one of the most promising early results suggesting that DMT entities exhibit **genuinely non-human computational capability** — not fabricated by the human brain, which could not perform these operations at this speed. It is the empirical cornerstone of the proposed DMTX research program: sending specialists in to observe what the entities do.

Emilsson also reportedly described other geometric and topological operations at the same superhuman speeds, including calculating distances between surfaces.
