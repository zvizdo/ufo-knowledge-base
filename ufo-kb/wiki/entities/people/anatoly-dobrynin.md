# Anatoly Dobrynin

Soviet Ambassador to the United States from 1962 to 1986 — the longest-serving Soviet envoy in Washington, present through the Kennedy, Johnson, Nixon, Ford, Carter, and Reagan administrations. A central figure in back-channel US-Soviet diplomacy, including during the Cuban Missile Crisis.

## UAP Inquiry

Per [[harold-malmgren]] in [[09KP8XVf5nY]], Dobrynin actively tracked Malmgren and at one point directly asked him about UFOs. Malmgren's response: **"We only feel safe when we control."** This implies Dobrynin — and by extension the Soviet foreign intelligence apparatus — was aware of US UAP programs and probing for information.

This exchange fits the broader pattern: both superpowers were aware of each other's UAP-related activities, and the fear of losing control of the narrative (and the physical phenomenon) drove both sides toward secrecy rather than cooperation.

## Context

JFK wanted to share US UAP knowledge with the Soviets as a unifying force; the JFK-McCone letter (FOIA 2005) requests all "unknowns" data to coordinate with Russia and prevent misidentification. Dobrynin's direct inquiry to Malmgren suggests the Soviet side was equally interested in this bilateral UAP framework.
