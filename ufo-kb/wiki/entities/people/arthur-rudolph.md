# Arthur Rudolph

Nazi German rocket engineer (b. 1906, d. 1996). Member of the Nazi Party and of the SA. Operations director of the V-2 production line at the Mittelwerk underground slave-labor factory during WWII — the role that, decades later, produced the legal basis for his stripping of US citizenship. Transferred to the United States in 1945 under [[operation-paperclip]].

Cited by [[lue-elizondo]] in [[1f16VvXaSSE]] alongside [[wernher-von-braun]] as one of "129 people" who constituted "a transplant of the Nazi program" into US aerospace. At NASA's NASA Marshall Space Flight Center, Rudolph served as project director of the Saturn V program — the launch vehicle that took Apollo to the Moon — making him arguably the most operationally consequential of the Paperclip transferees.

## Background

- 1906–1996 (b. Stepferdingen, Germany; d. Hamburg).
- Engineer at Heylandt-Werke and the Heereswaffenamt (Army Ordnance Office); recruited to Peenemünde under [[wernher-von-braun]] in 1934.
- Operations director, Mittelwerk V-2 production line (1944–45); the position later cited by the US Office of Special Investigations as exposing him to war-crimes culpability.
- Transferred to Fort Bliss, Texas, under [[operation-paperclip]] (1945); subsequently to Huntsville, Alabama, with the rest of the von Braun team.
- Project director, Saturn V program at NASA Marshall Space Flight Center (1963–1968).
- Awarded the NASA Distinguished Service Medal (1969).
- 1984: agreed to renounce US citizenship and return to West Germany rather than face denaturalization proceedings over his Mittelwerk role.

## Notable Claims

- Per Elizondo's framing on [[1f16VvXaSSE]], the Saturn V program is properly understood as a continuation of the Nazi rocket program — with Rudolph and von Braun as the through-line.
- The "129 people" Operation Paperclip transferee count is recurring in Elizondo's discussion of [[occult-aerospace-connection]] — the architectural inheritance from German aerospace into the American space program.
