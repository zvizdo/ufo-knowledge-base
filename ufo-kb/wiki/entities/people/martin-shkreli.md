# Martin Shkreli

Pharmaceutical entrepreneur, investor, and public provocateur. Most publicly known for raising the price of the AIDS drug Daraprim from $13.50 to $700+ in 2015, and for a subsequent securities fraud conviction that resulted in ~five years in prison (2017–2022). In the UAP knowledge base he is a peripheral but notable figure: in a wide-ranging interview with [[jesse-michaels]], he independently arrived at several ideas that converge with core UAP discourse positions.

## Background

Shkreli studied pharmacology and finance, founded multiple drug companies (Retrophin, Turing Pharmaceuticals), and built a career as both a pharmaceutical entrepreneur and a short-seller. He is a self-described effective accelerationist on AI, a prolific reader, and a SETI@Home enthusiast from childhood. He served approximately five years in federal prison on securities fraud charges (2017–2022), during which he read several hundred books and wrote extensively on simulation theory, AI, and philosophy.

## Relevance to UAP Discourse

Shkreli is not a UAP researcher and does not claim any firsthand UAP experience. His relevance is as an independent intellectual who — without apparent immersion in UFO literature — arrived at several positions that UFO researchers have reached via different routes:

### 1. Gnostic-Simulation-UFO Synthesis
Shkreli predicts that within 50–100 years most Americans will hold [[simulation-theory]] as a religion, and that UFO belief will similarly become formalized. He explicitly predicts these two narratives will converge into a neo-Gnostic worldview: the demiurge operates the simulation, and "arkonic emissaries" are the UAP intelligences — "somewhat demonic tricksters" who mediate between the simulation's substrate and its inhabitants. This is one of the more coherent articulations of how simulation theory and NHI could unify into a single explanatory framework.

### 2. UFO Encounters as Religious Conversion Experiences
Shkreli independently observes that UFO encounters pattern-match 1:1 with historical religious conversion experiences — he uses St. Francis of Assisi as the example, noting that Assisi's account could have "angel" replaced with "alien" without loss of coherence, including what sounds like radiation-burn stigmata. This is structurally identical to [[jacques-vallee]]'s "control system" hypothesis, reached without apparent knowledge of Vallée's work.

### 3. Turing and Parapsychology
Shkreli cites Alan Turing's "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" (1950) as containing, among candidate refutations of machine consciousness, the one Turing took most seriously: parapsychology — specifically that an ESP-capable human could influence random event generators in ways that would create uncontrollable experimenter effects, making the Turing test indeterminate. Shkreli endorses this: "I do think there is [a mind-over-matter effect]."

### 4. Bioelectric Field
Shkreli describes the tadpole morphology experiment: severing a limb stub and changing its bioelectric gradient causes head tissue to grow. His reading: the bioelectric field dictates morphology more fundamentally than DNA, and we do not understand the mechanism. This is adjacent to [[consciousness-non-local]] and the physiological effects on UAP witnesses.
