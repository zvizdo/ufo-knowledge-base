# James Copley

James Copley (transcribed as "James Cop" in the source) served as communications officer aboard the USS John F. Kennedy aircraft carrier. On July 2, 1971, he witnessed what is now regarded as one of the most dramatic USO/orb incidents in US naval history. He wrote about the incident around 2000–2001 and testified to the [[disclosure-project]] in 2000.

## The 1971 Account

The carrier had just completed a series of qualification tests. On the evening of July 2, 1971, at approximately 8:30 PM:

1. All inbound communications suddenly began producing gibberish
2. Over the intercom, Copley heard a sailor screaming: **"It's God. It's the end of the world."**
3. He went outside to see a glowing orange/yellow/reddish spherical object — approximately "the size of a beach ball at arm's length" — above or beside the ship
4. He watched for ~20 seconds; many other sailors also observed it; at least one sailor had to be sedated
5. The ship went to **general quarters** (battle stations), lasting ~20 minutes
6. Copley returned to his communications station; weapon systems were offline for the duration
7. He did not see the orb depart

**Captain's communication after the incident**: "As you all know, uh, certain things happen aboard a US naval vessel that have to remain on the vessel and not to be discussed anywhere else."

## EM Significance

This case is directly relevant to [[richard-dolan]]'s statistical finding that USO/orb cases involving entity or abduction encounters are 4× more likely to show EM interference. While no abduction occurred, the complete communications blackout and weapons system failure represent a dramatic expression of the EM effect pattern.
