# Brian Muraresku

Lawyer and author of *[[the-immortality-key]]* (2020), a historical investigation arguing that ancient Greek mystery religions (especially the Eleusinian Mysteries) and early Christianity employed psychedelic sacraments — and that these experiences were, at root, encounters with evidence for the persistence of consciousness beyond death.

Referenced in [[0qlppHc3-gg]] in the context of the broader thesis connecting [[consciousness-survives-death]] to multiple independent evidence streams: Muraresku's ancient-mysteries angle, [[ian-stevenson]]'s past-lives research, and [[diane-powell]]'s autism-telepathy data are all cited as converging on the same underlying reality.

## Background

Lawyer by training; no academic appointment. *The Immortality Key* was a New York Times bestseller. Interviewed extensively by academics including Classicists and theologians. Foreword by Graham Hancock.

## Notable Claims

- The Eleusinian Mysteries and early Christian rituals involved psychedelic substances (ergot-derived compounds).
- The experiences induced were not recreational but were specifically structured to demonstrate — to the initiate — that consciousness survives death.
- This ancient "technology" for accessing non-ordinary consciousness is a window into the same phenomena that modern researchers (Stevenson, Powell) document by different means.

## Eleusinian Mysteries and Gallimore Connection

In [[8XD1ZiuhXoY]], [[jesse-michaels]] cites Muraresku's work as context for the Gallimore episode: the Eleusinian Mysteries used ergot preparations, and [[plato-philosopher]] participated in them. Gallimore and Michaels use this to argue that DMT/psychedelic access to NHI is the deep historical thread running through Western civilization's foundational philosophy. [[new-nautics]]'s Eleusis Mind retreat center is explicitly named after the Eleusinian Mysteries, and Gallimore mentions the possibility of reconstructing the original kykeon preparation at the center.

## Near-Death Experience (Firsthand)

In [[I9VyyV4-3Js]], Muraresku describes his own near-death experience at age 5: hit in the head by a golf club, rushed to hospital bleeding, he felt **bliss and enormous comfort** — no pain — as his mother wept. During surgery, as anesthesia kicked in, he felt he "left his body" and had vivid visionary encounters. He woke with the imagery but no context for it. Only 10–20 years later, reading NDE and mystical literature, did he recognize it. He frames it now as a personal mystery ritual, consistent with the ancient "dying before dying" tradition he studies.

## Hagia Sofia DC (Setting)

The episode [[I9VyyV4-3Js]] was filmed at the **Hagia Sofia Greek Orthodox Cathedral** in Washington DC (beside the National Cathedral). Muraresku chose this setting deliberately: the cathedral embodies the mystery of his book — how Greek mystery religion fed into Christianity, and what Christianity would look like if the psychedelic sacrament were acknowledged.

## Leslie Kean Connection

Muraresku mentions in [[I9VyyV4-3Js]] that he is **close friends with [[leslie-kean]]**, the New York Times journalist who has broken major UAP stories. This places him at the intersection of the mystery-religion/consciousness cluster and the mainstream UFO disclosure cluster — an unusual bridging position.
