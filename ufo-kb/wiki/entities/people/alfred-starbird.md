## Background

General Alfred Starbird served as Commander of Joint Task Force 8 (JTF-8), the military command responsible for US nuclear testing operations in the Pacific. During Operation Dominic in 1962, he was designated SOPA — Senior Officer Present Afloat — making him the highest-ranking US military officer on scene for the Bluegill Triple Prime high-altitude nuclear test conducted over the Marshall Islands in October 1962.

Starbird's presence during Bluegill Triple Prime is documented in USS Summit County ship logs, providing contemporaneous official records that place him at the event.

## Notable Claims

- After the Bluegill Triple Prime test — during which a UAP "tagged along" and was reportedly downed — Starbird left the test site abruptly in a manner that was noted as unusual by those present and by subsequent researchers examining the operational record. This departure is consistent with a significant and sensitive event requiring immediate high-level attention.
- His documented presence via ship logs, combined with the abrupt departure, makes him a key figure in the chain of command for whatever UAP recovery operation followed Bluegill Triple Prime. Any recovery of [[bluegill-uap-debris]] from the Pacific would have passed through or around his command authority.
- His role as SOPA means he would have had direct situational awareness of the UAP event and the earliest command decisions about how to handle it.
