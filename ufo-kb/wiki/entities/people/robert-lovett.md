# Robert Lovett

Robert A. Lovett (1895–1986) was one of the archetypal postwar American establishment figures — a Wall Street banker (senior partner at [[brown-brothers-harriman]]) who rotated through the highest levels of government. He served as Under Secretary of State, Deputy Secretary of Defense, and ultimately Secretary of Defense under Truman (1951–53). He is also credited with writing the original memorandum that led to the CIA's founding.

## Background

Lovett's career exemplified the "Wise Men" generation — the small group of financiers, lawyers, and officials who built and administered the American national security state after World War II. His Brown Brothers Harriman connection placed him in the same institutional network as [[robert-anderson]] and [[john-j-mccloy]].

## Notable Claims / Role

According to sources cited in [[C4rSj5Aum7w]], [[henry-stimson]] brought Lovett, Anderson, and McCloy together to form the covert trust structure — the [[anderson-trust-gold]] — designed to manage the Yamashita gold recovered from the Philippines. The trust, capitalized by this looted treasure, is alleged to have become one of the funding sources for off-books intelligence and UAP-related programs. Lovett's authorship of the CIA founding document adds a layer of institutional significance: the man who helped create the CIA's legal architecture was also allegedly involved in creating the black-budget structure that may have operated alongside or outside it.
