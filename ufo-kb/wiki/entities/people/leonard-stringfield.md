# Leonard Stringfield

Leonard Stringfield was a UFO researcher who became the true pioneer of systematic crash-retrieval research in the late 1970s. While the broader UFO research community was focused on sightings and government documents via FOIA, Stringfield developed the methodology of interviewing military and intelligence insiders about alleged recovery events.

## Crash Retrieval Methodology

Stringfield published his findings in a series of self-published status reports called *UFO Crash/Retrievals*, beginning in 1978. These reports aggregated anonymous and named testimony from individuals who claimed first- or second-hand knowledge of US government recovery operations for crashed unknown craft.

## Jesse Marcel Sr. Interview

[[richard-dolan]] credits Stringfield as "one of the first people to interview and publish his interview with [[jesse-marcel]]" — the Roswell first sergeant whose public statement in the late 1970s re-ignited public attention on the 1947 incident. Stringfield's initiative helped transform Roswell from a nearly forgotten curiosity into the central case in UFO research.

## Dolan's Assessment

Dolan describes Stringfield as one of his primary inspirations: "He's one [of my heroes]. I never met the guy. Um he was amazing. Amazing." The posthumous tribute reflects how foundational Stringfield's systematic approach was for everyone who came after.
