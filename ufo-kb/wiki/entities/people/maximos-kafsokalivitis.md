# Maximos Kafsokalivitis

A historical Greek Orthodox monk who lived at [[mount-athos]] and was known for:

- **Burning his huts** (*kafsokalivitis* = "the burner of huts") — a practice of extreme ascetic detachment
- **Reported levitation / flying** — accounts of other monks witnessing him airborne
- His hermitage (*ski*) at Mount Athos is named **Kafsokalivion** after him

## Relevance to Ralph Larson

In [[ATJwqp5twAg]], [[ralph-moat-larson]] describes a 1991 time-travel dream in which he lived as a medieval monk at Mount Athos under the name "Odius Maximus." In the dream, he burned his hut and learned to fly.

In 2005, on a return visit to Mount Athos, Larson stumbled onto the ski Kafsokalivion by chance. A monk explained the history of Maximos Kafsokalivitis — the flying monk who burned his huts. Larson had no prior knowledge of this historical figure when he had the 1991 dream.

This is the most concrete external corroboration point in Larson's mystical narrative: two distinctive, unusual behaviors (burning huts, flying) appeared in his dream and map onto a real historical monk whose identity he did not know.

Jeffrey Kripal, a religious studies professor at Rice University (Houston), gave a lecture on "flying saints" at MIT. Carlos Eire (Yale) wrote *They Flew* — a scholarly account of flying saints including St. Joseph of Cupertino and Teresa of Ávila, concluding from eyewitness testimony that levitation reports meet an evidentiary threshold exceeding that required to establish historical battles.
