# George Ellery Hale

George Ellery Hale (1868–1938) was one of the most influential astronomers of the early 20th century. He built the **Mount Wilson Observatory** near Pasadena, California — the facility whose 100-inch Hooker Telescope Edwin Hubble used in 1923 to resolve individual stars inside Andromeda, proving it was a separate galaxy and shattering the assumption that the Milky Way was the whole universe.

Hale also co-transformed **Throop Polytechnic Institute** into **[[caltech]]**, making it a world magnet for figures including Hubble, Bohr, Oppenheimer, Heisenberg, and Einstein.

## Mystical / Esoteric Dimension

Hale saw astronomy as philosophy — a link back to ancient Greece, Mesopotamia, Egypt, and Babylon. The lodges where astronomers stayed at his observatories were reportedly decorated with Egyptian hieroglyphs.

More strikingly, Hale claimed he received **creative inspiration from a being** — which he variously described as an "elf" or "spirit." Some biographers attributed this to stress or eccentricity; others took it more seriously as a genuine phenomenological report.

His **Hale Solar Laboratory** in Pasadena includes a tribute to **Akhenaten** — the Egyptian pharaoh who attempted to replace the entire Egyptian pantheon with a single solar deity (Aten, the Sun Disk) — carved above the entrance arch. This was not incidental: Hale explicitly saw his solar research as joining a lineage stretching to ancient Egyptian sun worship.

When Hale died, his obituaries called him *"a priest of the sun"* and *"a modern Zoroaster."*
