# Jeremy Ree ("Alien Scientist")

Jeremy Ree (also known by the YouTube handle "Alien Scientist") is a UAP researcher and physics popularizer operating primarily through YouTube. He produces detailed technical content analyzing UAP propulsion hypotheses, experimental physics related to anti-gravity and field propulsion, and classified program claims. The "Alien Scientist" channel occupies a distinctive niche in the UAP research community as technically literate independent analysis rather than testimonial or disclosure-driven content.

**Edgar Fouché connection**: [[edgar-fouche]] was introduced to Jeremy Ree, who independently maintained a relationship with Fouché over time. Ree's independent research into the TR-3B and triangular UAP claims provided a complementary technical analysis alongside Fouché's insider-testimony account. The convergence of an independent physics-analytical researcher with Fouché's program-access claims is cited as one of the corroboration threads for the TR-3B hypothesis.

**Research approach**: Alien Scientist's content typically bridges mainstream physics concepts (electromagnetic field theory, plasma physics, general relativity) with classified program claims, attempting to assess technical plausibility of advanced propulsion hypotheses. This approach places the channel in the technical-analytical segment of the UAP research community rather than the testimonial/whistleblower segment. Ree engages with the physics of [[anti-gravity-manhattan-project]], [[biefeld-brown-effect]], and related propulsion anomaly research to assess whether claimed craft capabilities are physically achievable given what is known.

**Technical credibility function**: In the KB structure, Ree represents the "independent technical analysis" mode of UAP research — researchers who neither claim insider access nor focus on testimonial collection, but who instead evaluate the physical plausibility of what insiders and witnesses describe. This provides a different form of evidentiary contribution: not corroboration of claims by new claims, but assessment of whether the claimed capabilities are consistent with known physics or require genuinely new physics.

**Relationship to ARV research**: Ree's analysis of the [[alien-reproduction-vehicles]] claims and the triangular craft literature (including the [[xf-131]] and TR-3B) sits alongside [[mark-mccandish]]'s illustration work as another route by which the technical details of alleged classified programs enter public discourse.
