# Beatriz Villarroel

Associate professor of astrophysics at Stockholm University. Holds a PhD in astrophysics. Won the L'Oréal UNESCO Prize for Women in Science. Her research spans active galactic nuclei, transient phenomena, and the search for technosignatures / intelligent life. Runs the [[vasco]] (Vanishing and Appearing Sources during a Century of Observations) project.

## Background

Villarroel trained in mainstream astrophysics and built a conventional academic career before pivoting toward the search for anomalous transients in historical astronomical plate archives. The VASCO project was designed around the question: do any sources appear or vanish between historical survey plates and modern sky surveys? This methodology naturally captures any object that was briefly present decades ago but not catalogued.

## Notable Claims

### Pre-Sputnik Transients
In digitized photographic plates from the [[palomar-observatory]] (1950–1956), Villarroel's automated pipeline detected approximately 105,000 transients in the northern hemisphere. Assuming one-third are from the southern hemisphere, the total is ~70,000 globally. These observations predate the launch of Sputnik (October 4, 1957), meaning no human-made satellites were in orbit during the survey.

### Earth-Shadow Umbra Test
Her primary validation method: the Earth casts a cone-shaped shadow. At ~42,164 km (geosynchronous orbit), this shadow is calculable for any coordinates and time. Plate defects would be uniformly distributed regardless of shadow position. Solar reflections from real objects would disappear inside the shadow. Her results show a 30–35% deficit of transients inside the shadow — implying 30–35% of the transients are genuine solar reflections. The remaining ~65–70% may still be plate defects.

### Mirror-Like Geometry
The transients appear as short flashes rather than streaks — consistent with objects that are **extremely flat and extremely reflective**, like mirrors. This rules out rocks, ice, or spherical objects. The flash profile implies artificial geometry.

### Washington D.C. Flap Correlation
The two strongest multi-transient alignment events in her dataset occur on July 19, 1952 (3 very bright objects, found by colleague [[enrique-solano]]) and July 27, 1952 (5 objects on a narrow band). These match precisely the two weekends of the [[1952-washington-dc-flyover]] (July 19–20 and July 26–27) — a national event covered in newspapers and tracked by radar.

### Nuclear Test Correlation
Co-authored with [[steven-bruell]] on a paper (accepted at *Scientific Reports*) showing a statistically significant correlation between nuclear bomb tests and transient appearance. The day after a nuclear test, transient-observation days increase from 11% to ~19% — a 68% relative increase, p=0.008.

## Origin Story & Halton Arp Influence (lbGE3EC6StE)

Villarroel describes writing an undergraduate essay about "vanishing galaxies" inspired by [[halton-arp]]'s anomalous redshift findings. Arp had discovered pairs of interacting galaxies with incompatible redshifts, challenging the Big Bang's use of redshift as a universal distance measure. The mainstream response was to deny him telescope time — a pattern Villarroel sees replicated in her own experience. Her essay ended with a quasar vanishing, which sparked the question: *"Has anyone ever searched for galaxies or stars that vanish?"* — the seed of [[vasco]].

She also studied molecular biotechnology briefly before pivoting to astrophysics for her PhD, where she worked on active galactic nuclei and broad vs. narrow emission line spectra.

## ExoProbe (lbGE3EC6StE)

Villarroel's new project, [[exoprobe]], builds a telescope network in New Mexico to detect, verify, and physically retrieve a non-human artifact from Earth orbit. Unlike VASCO's historical archive approach, ExoProbe operates in real time and targets signals outside the atmosphere. Key challenge: removing the contamination of human space debris. A multi-telescope network allows 3D triangulation of object distance — eventually precise enough to attempt physical retrieval.

> "That's what I would like to do: to bring down an actual ET probe."

## European Crash Retrieval Initiative (lbGE3EC6StE)

A private, citizen-driven crash retrieval project Villarroel calls a "hobby project" — led by Thomas Pinder and Alex Tal in Sweden under the [[european-crash-retrieval-initiative]] umbrella. They maintain a database of European cold cases and hope to respond to a new hot case. As of April 2026, they have a cold case underway that she describes as "super cool, super scary" but will not detail publicly.

## The Jeff Marcy Cancellation (lbGE3EC6StE)

Villarroel collaborated with [[geoff-marcy]] on the VASCO nine-transients paper (finding nine objects appearing and vanishing in a small patch of sky simultaneously in 1952). The same week the paper was accepted by a journal, the National Academy of Sciences ejected Marcy — despite his cancellation having originated in 2015. She was advised not to issue a press release; she subsequently faced conference exclusions and social media attacks.

She continued the collaboration on principle: she "believes in human rights" including the right to rehabilitation and the principle that peer pressure should not override scientific judgment. The episode she describes as having ultimately **liberated** her from caring about peer approval — freeing her to work on UAP openly.

## Hypothesis-First Science Philosophy (lbGE3EC6StE)

Villarroel argues against the UAP "exclusion principle" — the approach of observing everything and filtering out the identifiable. Her alternative: start with a concrete prior hypothesis ("flying saucer") and design the experiment around the specific predicted signatures of that object. See [[hypothesis-first-uap-science]].

She is cautious about infrared UAP claims: humans have less experience classifying IR objects, so "more UAP" in infrared may reflect observer inexperience rather than genuine signal.

## Baltic Sea Anomaly Comments (lbGE3EC6StE)

Villarroel describes the [[2011-baltic-sea-anomaly]] as:
- Covered in **burned biological material** — anomalous given no local volcanic activity
- **Could not be sampled**: the object was so hard that divers/researchers could not extract a piece
- The "artificial structure" interpretation: the investigating team did not claim crashed UFO; they identified an unknown artificial structure
- There is a track suggesting **the object slid into the ocean**
- Describes it as "possibly the best candidate non-human artifact you can find"

## Kevin Knuth's Assessment (IWui5cBkwoE)

[[kevin-knuth]] endorses the VASCO methodology: "It's an excellent idea for a project." His specific concerns and observations:

- The 100,000-transient claim (the most recent paper covering 1949–1957, ending at Sputnik) is "a little shocking" — Knuth thinks the artifact-control challenge is correspondingly higher: "You really have to double your efforts to look to make sure there's not an artifact issue."
- Her umbra/shadow test (solar-illuminated side vs. Earth shadow side) he finds persuasive: "Plate defect rate should be completely proportional and constant" regardless of shadow position, so a drop-off inside the shadow is significant.
- He wants the raw plate data published (it's not yet available as of this interview) and replication by other observatories: "If you have multiple observatories, that would be ideal."
- He notes the hallmark of science is reproducibility: what's needed is other labs looking at the same plates and attempting to replicate the study.
- He separately mentions that NASA tracks what it calls "uncorrelated targets" all the time — per a Villarroel contact inside NASA — but Space Force, not NASA, systematically catalogs them.

Knuth does not dispute her findings; he flags the scale of the artifact challenge as the appropriate skeptical pressure point.
