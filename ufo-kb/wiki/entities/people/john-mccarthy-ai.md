# John McCarthy

American computer scientist (1927–2011). Co-founder of the field of artificial intelligence; coined the term "artificial intelligence" at the 1956 Dartmouth Conference. Creator of the LISP programming language. Ran the AI lab at Stanford University.

## Bureau of Simulation Thought Experiment

In the early days of AI (when there were only two or three AI labs worldwide), McCarthy told the following parable — cited by [[riz-virk]] in [[aKZ_MUbuk_Q]]:

> An insurance salesman follows a fully predictable routine — office, forms, payment, home, TV, repeat. One day he receives a letter from the Bureau of Simulation: "We have observed your daily behavior for six months and you have not done anything not predicted by the model of the world we run. If in the next six months you don't do something not predicted by the model, you will be replaced by a simulated human."
>
> The man quits his job, moves to the Himalayas, meditates, climbs mountains, transforms himself. Six months later, a new letter arrives: "In the last six months you still haven't done anything not predicted by the simulation model. You have been replaced." He is still sitting at his desk — but he doesn't exist anymore.

The parable is a sharp statement about **NPC mode**: a life so fully determined by environmental programming (social norms, material incentives, reflexive routine) that no genuine player-level agency is being exercised. From the outside, the person and the simulation are indistinguishable. This maps onto Virk's distinction between NPC and RPG modes of existence — and is McCarthy's contribution to simulation theory from within mainstream CS.
