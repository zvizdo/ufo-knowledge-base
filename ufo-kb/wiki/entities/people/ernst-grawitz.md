# Ernst Grawitz

Ernst-Robert Grawitz (1899–1945) was a senior SS medical officer who served as SS-Obergruppenführer, Reichsarzt-SS (chief physician of the SS), and president of the German Red Cross during the Third Reich. He was responsible for authorizing and overseeing SS medical experimentation on concentration camp prisoners, including experiments conducted by Josef Mengele, Sigmund Rascher, and others at Auschwitz, Dachau, and Ravensbrück.

**Medical experimentation authority**: Grawitz operated as the senior SS medical authority who approved human subjects research proposals submitted by SS doctors at the camps. He authorized cold-water survival experiments, altitude decompression experiments, infectious disease exposure, and other experiments that resulted in mass prisoner deaths. He was directly responsible for some of the most documented medical war crimes of the Nazi period.

**Die Glocke connection**: In Eric Wargo's analysis (citing Witkowski's sources), Grawitz is named as possibly the SS medical division head overseeing human subjects testing in the [[die-glocke]] test chamber — a chamber in which workers allegedly experienced severe physiological effects including rapid deterioration and death from exposure to the device's operation. If the Die Glocke device existed and operated as described, SS medical oversight of human subjects testing would have fallen under Grawitz's authority structure.

**Death**: Grawitz committed suicide on April 24, 1945 — the last days of the war — by triggering grenades in his family home, killing himself, his wife, and his children.

**Witkowski-tier claims**: The Die Glocke/Grawitz connection is Witkowski-source-tier — based on Polish intelligence material whose provenance is contested. The claim should not be taken as established; it is documented here as the source of the dangling reference.
