# Henry Kwanda

Romanian-born aeronautical engineer. In the [[RTEWLSTyUic]] account of [[thomas-townsend-brown]]'s career, Kwanda is identified as the figure who **convinced Brown to travel to France in 1956** to conduct the vacuum experiments that would confirm or refute the [[biefeld-brown-effect]] under ion-wind-free conditions. Without Kwanda's persuasion, the 1956 Paris vacuum test might not have happened.

**Lenticular aerodyne work**: Kwanda is described in [[RTEWLSTyUic]] as the inventor of **lenticular aerodyne** (disc-shaped aircraft) designs and as a consultant on **[[avrocar-project|Project Y (Silverbug)]]** — the Canadian-US classified disc aircraft program run by Avro Canada in the late 1950s. This places him at the intersection of the disc-aircraft engineering lineage and the electrogravitics research that Brown was pursuing.

**The 1956 Paris vacuum result**: The test Kwanda helped arrange at the Montgolfier facility in Paris was performed by [[jacque-corneau]] (French Air Force officer and Sud Aviation consultant) and produced a positive thrust result at vacuum conditions of at least 10⁻⁶ torr — eliminating the ion-wind hypothesis as an explanation for the observed effect. Corneau made a deathbed audio confession (2009) confirming the positive result, and a 12-page report also exists. If the result had been published and credited, mainstream physics would have been forced to engage with electrogravitics.

**KB significance**: Kwanda bridges three threads — Romanian exile engineering talent in postwar Europe, disc-aircraft development at the classified aeronautical frontier (Project Y), and the Brown vacuum confirmation that UAP reverse-engineering proponents cite as suppressed evidence. His persuasion of Brown to go to France in 1956 is therefore a hinge event in the electrogravitics suppression narrative.
