# Constantine Raudive

Latvian parapsychologist (1909–1974) best known for his systematic research into Electronic Voice Phenomena (EVP) — apparent voices captured on tape recordings in the absence of a live speaker. His 1971 book *Breakthrough* documented thousands of recorded voices he interpreted as communications from deceased individuals.

## Connection to Strieber's Implant

Two men visited Whitley Strieber in the mid-2000s at his home. One was known to him; one was not. They explained the function of the implant placed in his ear in May 1989:

- The implant was designed to guide the user toward needed information through synchronicity — drawing them to people, books, and conversations that answer questions they've posed.
- The inventor was identified as **Constantine Raudive** (Strieber initially misremembered the pronunciation as "Rodive").
- Strieber was struck by the apparent impossibility: Raudive died in the early 1970s, and the implant was placed in 1989 — suggesting either the technology was developed before Raudive's death, or his research was continued by others after his death.

A further coincidence: Strieber later discovered that a close friend who also has a similar "eye slit" implant (where words race through the slit too fast to read) turned out to be "one of the world's leading experts on Constantine Raudive." Strieber interprets this coincidence as evidence of a designed experiment — that the implant program selects and connects people across a network.

## EVP Technology and NHI Communication

Raudive's EVP research sits at the intersection of technology and mediumship: using electromagnetic recording devices to capture communications from non-physical entities. The implant connection, if accurate, suggests an extension of this principle — not recording voices externally, but embedding a device within a human body that mediates communication between the carrier and NHI or non-physical intelligences.
