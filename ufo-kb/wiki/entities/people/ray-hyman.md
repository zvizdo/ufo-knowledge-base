# Ray Hyman

Psychologist at the University of Oregon and prominent skeptic of parapsychological claims. Associated with [[james-randi]] (The Amazing Randi) in organized skepticism. Co-reviewed [[stargate-program]] data with [[jessica-utts]] in the 1995 CIA-commissioned American Institutes for Research (AIR) review.

## Stargate Data Review

Hyman found methodological issues in the Stargate experimental record that he argued invalidated the statistical conclusions Utts drew. He disputed whether proper controls and blinding were maintained consistently across the program's sessions.

The CIA cited Hyman's critique to justify terminating Stargate in 1995, despite Utts's conclusion that the effect was statistically robust. [[joe-mcmoneagle]] frames this as selective use of negative review to achieve a predetermined institutional outcome.

## James Randi / Million Dollar Challenge

McMoneagle refused to participate in James Randi's million-dollar challenge, criticizing the experimental design as fundamentally flawed. Hyman is listed as a colleague in organized skepticism around this community.

## Hyman's Concession (RofQnByLwOo)

In [[RofQnByLwOo]], Jesse Michaels specifically notes that Hyman — despite his skepticism and his previous critiques — ultimately agreed that:
- The psychic experiments **were free of obvious flaws**
- The data was **too large and consistent to be dismissed as statistical flukes**
- Something unexplained was happening **even in his skeptical opinion**

Hyman had previously written for the *Skeptical Inquirer* and produced a government-commissioned report highly critical of [[uri-geller]]. His concession that methodology was sound is therefore significant: he had both motive and prior record to find methodological problems. His inability to do so on the Stargate data strengthens [[jessica-utts]]'s statistical conclusions.
