# John Lear

Son of [[bill-lear]] (aviation pioneer, inventor of the Learjet, and a key figure in 1950s anti-gravity research). John Lear is the CIA pilot who introduced [[bob-lazar]] to journalist [[george-knapp]] in 1989 — an act that launched the most influential UFO whistleblower story of the modern era.

## Background

John Lear set multiple Aviation records in the 1970s, flew cargo planes for the CIA, and had a falling out with his father who cut him out of his will. Despite this, Lear appears to have been deeply aware of — and proud of — his father's exotic aviation work, including [[bill-lear]]'s proximity to [[thomas-townsend-brown]]'s [[guidance-technologies]] in Santa Monica.

Before the Lazar leak, Lear maintained an active UFO newsletter, spoke at UFO conferences in Las Vegas, became involved in [[mufon]], and visited [[paul-benowitz]] — the victim of [[richard-doty]]'s AFOSI disinformation psyop — reportedly leaving Benowitz more alarmed. According to [[greg-bishop]], Lear met with Benowitz multiple times until Benowitz became angry and didn't want to talk to him. Greg Bishop suspects Lear may have been tasked to follow up on Benowitz after the initial AFOSI operation — feeding him additional material. Crucially, Bishop reports that Lear **privately admitted** to an interlocutor before an early 1990s congressional UFO hearing that a key element he had propagated — the "Dulce firefight" (an alleged battle between humans and aliens at the alleged underground base) — was fabricated: "I made that up." (Greg Bishop, [[WBTqsbwJyqU]]). This admission is not widely known and significantly undercuts the Dulce firefight narrative. Lear also wrote anonymous pieces with Bill Cooper (*Behold a Pale Horse*) and introduced Lazar to the Billy Meier UFO tapes, which depicted a craft suspiciously similar to what Lazar later described as the "Sport Model."

## The CIA Leak Hypothesis

Jesse Michaels argues — and [[nick-cook]] endorses — that the Lazar story was deliberately seeded through John Lear by elements of the CIA as a **disinformation cover** for terrestrial exotic propulsion programs (the Aurora, B-2 electrogravitics) that were coming online in the late 1980s.

Evidence assembled for this hypothesis:
1. Lear claimed to have left CIA in 1983 but [[george-knapp]] himself acknowledges Lear may have flown CIA missions into the mid-1980s.
2. Area 51 security was well aware of John Lear — he and aviation photographer Jim Goodall camped outside the perimeter for years in the 1970s.
3. Despite Lear's years-long public UFO activity around Area 51, a secret CIA UAP program allegedly hired his close friend Lazar without apparent concern.
4. Lear introduced Lazar to the **Billy Meier tapes** — known hoaxes — just before Lazar went public describing a craft that looked exactly like Meier's "sports model."
5. The **F-117A leak** Lear gave Knapp in 1987 — information on a plane whose existence had already been partially compromised by a 1986 crash — established his credibility at the news station just before the Lazar story.
6. Documents given to Lazar (on human genetic manipulation) were nearly identical to those shown to Linda Moulton Howe by [[richard-doty]] at Kirtland AFB, suggesting coordinated disinformation across multiple channels.
7. Jacques Vallée's book documents Lazar claiming a "strange liquid" caused memory lapses — echoing MKULTRA-style amnesia induction consistent with an engineered witness.

## The Lear–Townsend Brown Connection

Bill Lear's Lear Corporation was located directly across the street from Brown's [[guidance-technologies]] in Santa Monica; [[linda-brown]] recalls the two men lunching daily. A video exists of Bill Lear visiting [[agnew-bahnson]]'s labs in North Carolina to witness Brown's gravitator experiments. When John Lear was shown footage of his father with Townsend Brown, he was reportedly very emotional. This suggests John Lear was fully aware of his father's exotic-propulsion research and thus understood the true nature of what was being protected by the Lazar disinformation campaign.

## Credibility Note

[[jeremy-corbell]] and [[george-knapp]] — the most serious journalists on the Lazar story — believe Lear was simply a credulous UFO enthusiast who was "duped by a lot of the people around him" rather than a deliberate CIA agent. Jesse Michaels respects their reporting but finds the circumstantial case for deliberate CIA involvement stronger.
