# Meister Eckhart

German Dominican friar and mystic theologian whose speculative mysticism articulated a theology of radical selflessness: the soul's union with the divine requires the complete dissolution of ego and self-will. His formulation — "looking down at God, not up" — inverts conventional piety, placing God and the soul on the same level through the soul's emptying of itself.

In the KB, Eckhart is cited specifically for his teaching that the more a person removes themselves from personal honor or glory in faith, the more responsibility God assigns them — because the channel only functions when self-interest is absent. This framework appears in the context of UAP-adjacent experiencers who report being "chosen" not because of status but because of a specific psychological posture of selflessness.

His influence runs through Christian mysticism (John of the Cross, Hildegard of Bingen), theosophy, and eventually into modern consciousness research. His heretical condemnation by Pope John XXII (1329, posthumously) makes him a canonical example of suppressed esoteric knowledge within a religious institution — a pattern the KB traces from ancient mystery schools through modern UFO secrecy.
