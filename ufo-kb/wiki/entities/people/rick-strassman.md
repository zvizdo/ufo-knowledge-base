# Rick Strassman

Psychiatrist whose 1990s DMT research program at the University of New Mexico (UNM) was the first modern controlled human study of DMT. His book *[[dmt-the-spirit-molecule]]* (2001) introduced the "spirit molecule" hypothesis — that the pineal gland releases DMT at birth and death, serving as a conduit for the soul. This hypothesis is now considered unconfirmed but not debunked; subsequent evidence (DMT spike at death in rodents, sigma-1 receptor binding, neuroprotection) gives it partial empirical support.

## 1990s DMT Studies

Strassman ran trials at University of New Mexico (Room 531) with approximately 60 volunteers receiving various doses of intravenous pharmaceutical-grade DMT. Key findings:

- A significant number of subjects spontaneously reported experiences structurally identical to alien abduction accounts — scanning by entities, hierarchical being structure (worker beings + a supervisory "top dog"), tours of technological environments, sense that beings were waiting for them — without any priming
- This overlap did not escape [[john-mack]]'s attention; Mack was asked about DMT because his abductees' reports resembled Strassman's subjects
- DMT shows **no tolerance build-up**: repeated injections at 30-minute intervals produced the same intensity of experience, a pharmacological anomaly

## DMTX Protocol (2015)

In 2015, Strassman collaborated with [[andrew-gallimore]] to write a paper proposing DMTX — using target-controlled IV infusion (an anesthesiology technique) with DMT to sustain stable breakthrough states. Strassman provided all his archived blood sampling data from the 1990s trials for the pharmacokinetic modeling. The paper established theoretical feasibility; [[chris-timmermann]] at Imperial College subsequently implemented it.

## Spirit Molecule Hypothesis

Strassman proposed that the pineal gland releases DMT at birth, death, and during deep sleep — functioning as the biological mechanism for the soul's transit and for dreaming. [[andrew-gallimore]] offers partial support (rodent DMT death-spike, sigma-1 neuroprotection) but questions the pineal gland specifically (too small to pump breakthrough quantities; other neurons more likely) and notes the dreaming connection lacks evidence.
