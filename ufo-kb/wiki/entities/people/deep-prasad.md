# Deep Prasad

## Background

Deep Prasad is a quantum computing entrepreneur who, at age 26, founded [[quantum-generative-materials]], raising $15 million to pursue an unusual objective: reverse-engineering UAP propulsion and material systems using quantum computers. He has a relationship with [[eric-weinstein]] (referred to as "my colleague"), who has articulated the thesis that UAP observables represent "new physics, not new engineering" — a framing Prasad adopts and operationalizes.

His interest in UAP and quantum computing emerged simultaneously: he initially aimed to build ultimate material simulation software (simulate any material's properties before synthesizing it in the lab), then recognized that the DoD's documented five observable properties of UAPs represented exactly the kind of physics that would be discoverable only through quantum simulation — classical computers lack the computational resources to solve the many-body Schrödinger equation at scales relevant to exotic materials.

He maintains a blog with essays including "The Evolution of Science and the Interactive Universe" (arguing the scientific method needs reform to handle non-repeatable anomalies) and "Curiosity as a Time Capsule" (10–20 year predictions for UAP research).

## The Macroscopic Quantum Thesis

Prasad's central claim for this KB:

**The DoD's five UAP observables — including instantaneous acceleration and hypersonic travel with no acoustic signature — cannot be explained by classical macroscopic physics. They are emergent properties of underlying quantum mechanics operating at macroscopic scale.**

The historical precedent: superconductivity (discovered 1911). Superconductors initially appeared to "break the laws of physics" (zero resistance). Forty years later, it was understood as quantum mechanics operating macroscopically — a quantum phenomenon producing a visible, large-scale effect. High-temperature superconductors still lack a full theoretical explanation today.

Prasad's reading: whenever a material or craft appears to violate classical physics, the correct inference is not "impossible" but "emergent quantum phenomenon not yet understood." The Tic-Tac's estimated 31× speed of sound with no sonic boom is his leading example: classical macroscopic fluid dynamics predicts shockwaves; their absence suggests the craft is not interacting with air the way macroscopic objects do.

## Quantum Computing Methodology

See [[quantum-computing-uap-reverseengineering]] for full concept page.

The many-body Schrödinger equation describes any material fully — its resonance frequency, speed of sound, heat capacity, strength. Solved, it would yield the complete property set of any combination of atoms. The bottleneck: classical computers can solve it for ~3 atoms. More atoms → exponential scaling of classical bits required. A caffeine molecule's 97 fully entangled electrons would require 2^97 classical bits to represent — more than the number of atoms on Earth's surface.

Quantum computers sidestep this via qubits in superposition. 10 qubits require 2^10 classical bits to represent their quantum state. Quantum systems can simulate other quantum systems because they share the same mode of information representation. Prasad's company maps material-science problems onto quantum algorithms, aiming to simulate metamaterials that exhibit the UAP five observables — even without having access to the original objects.

## Dark Ice Magnetometer (Hearsay)

Prasad describes Lockheed observing the robin bird's quantum magnetic navigation (cry4 cryptochrome radical-pair mechanism) and successfully replicating it as a technological sensor ~6 years before the episode. The resulting device — [[dark-ice-magnetometer]] — provides GPS-precision positioning anywhere on Earth with no satellite dependency, including underwater. Prasad frames this as a proof-of-concept that biological quantum sensing can be industrialized, and draws an analogy: an extraterrestrial visiting a new planet would similarly rely on magnetometer-based navigation rather than GPS.

## Scientific Method Reform

Prasad argues Sir Francis Bacon's repeatability assumption — the foundation of the modern scientific method — structurally excludes genuine non-repeatable anomalies from scientific study. The Tic-Tac sighting is his example: something that objectively occurred but is not reproducible on demand, and therefore falls outside the Baconian framework. His essay proposes a modified epistemology that can quantify and account for non-repeatable real events. He cites Thomas Kuhn's anomaly-driven paradigm shift model as a companion framework.

## The Cellular Automata UAP Theory

Prasad's self-described "lunatic theory": UAP sightings — individually seemingly random one-off events — are nodes in a cellular automata system orchestrated by a more intelligent entity. The goal of the automata is to push Earth civilization toward a complex, emergent societal state that does not yet exist. Individual sightings are "cells" whose rules produce an emergent phenomenon across civilizational timescales. See [[cellular-automata-uap-theory]].

He connects this to the apparent phenomenon of simultaneous independent discovery (same ideas arising in geographically isolated cultures) and the William James transmission theory of consciousness — ideas "zapped in" from outside rather than generated locally.

## AI and RSA Predictions

Prasad disagrees with the [[elon-musk]]/[[nick-bostrom]] near-term Terminator AGI framing. His alternative: AI is already dangerous but through social manipulation — TikTok and Facebook algorithms are artificial intelligences optimizing human attention with consequences comparable to the Facebook mood-manipulation experiments that allegedly correlated with increased suicides. "It's killing us with a whimper, not with a bang."

On quantum cryptography: RSA encryption (backbone of banking, messaging, online security) will be broken by quantum computers within ~15 years. This is not speculative to him — it is "not a matter of if but when." The consequence: all historical encrypted communications would become readable; cryptocurrencies relying on RSA-equivalent schemes would fail. He frames this as potentially forcing global cooperation, as every nation-state would lose its informational leverage simultaneously.
