# Charles Douglas Jackson

## Background

C.D. Jackson was a senior psychological warfare specialist who served as Special Assistant to President Eisenhower for psychological warfare. Outside government, he was publisher of TIME magazine — a position that gave him direct influence over one of the most widely read information channels of the Cold War era, creating an unusual dual role: government psyop architect and mass-media gatekeeper simultaneously.

## Psychological Strategy Board

Jackson was a key member of the **Psychological Strategy Board** alongside [[gordon-gray]] and [[philip-corso]]. His media background made him the communications-facing element of the PSB — the person positioned to coordinate how information would be shaped for public consumption.

Applied to UAP: Jackson's simultaneous control of government psychological warfare operations AND major media (TIME) represents the clearest documented case of the information-suppression architecture that researchers identify as responsible for mainstream media's decades-long dismissal of UAP. The Robertson Panel (1953), which recommended debunking UAP reports to reduce public interest, occurred during precisely this period and produced recommendations that TIME and other outlets would have been positioned to amplify.

This is the institutional node where government classification and media gatekeeping were most clearly integrated in the early UAP secrecy era.
