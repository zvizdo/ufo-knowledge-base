# Tim Gallaudet

Retired US Navy Rear Admiral. The most senior uniformed US military official to go public as an active UAP researcher focused on the underwater domain. His combination of credentials — Oceanographer of the Navy (Pentagon flag officer), NOAA Acting Administrator, US Naval Observatory Superintendent, Scripps-trained oceanographer — gives him unique standing at the intersection of ocean science and UAP research.

## Career Background

- Grew up in Southern California; joined the Navy specifically to attend Scripps Institution of Oceanography on a Naval Academy scholarship; married fellow Scripps oceanography graduate student Karen (a Navy diver who did salvage work and recovered aircraft from the Mediterranean floor)
- **Oceanographer of the Navy**: Pentagon flag officer (one-star) responsible for six oceanographic ships, ocean mapping, weather forecasting, and anti-submarine warfare support
- **Superintendent, US Naval Observatory** ([[us-naval-observatory]]): the institution that maps stars for celestial navigation reference frames and provides the accuracy underpinning GPS; credits time working with astrophysicists as a turning point in his openness to extraterrestrial life
- **Acting Administrator, [[noaa]]** and Acting Under Secretary of Commerce for Oceans and Atmosphere (Trump administration): led the country's primary ocean science agency; launched the National Ocean Mapping Strategy; declined to study underwater UAP when proposed internally
- Led Navy Meteorology and Oceanography Command (~3,000 personnel) at Stennis Space Center, Mississippi
- Post-government: runs Ocean STL Consulting LLC; hosts "American Blue Economy" podcast; publishes *Holding Fast in Heavy Seas: Leading America's Top Ocean Agency in Turbulent Times* (expected early 2025)

## The Go Fast Email (Firsthand)

As a flag officer commanding the Navy Meteorology and Oceanography Command, Gallaudet received the Go Fast video not as a leaked item but as an email on the **classified SIPRNet (Cypernet)** from US Fleet Forces Command's operations officer, titled: **"URGENT SAFETY OF FLIGHT ISSUE"** (all caps). The email stated: "If any of you know what these are, tell me ASAP. We are having numerous near-midair collisions, and if we don't stop it, we're going to have to cancel the exercise."

- Gallaudet recognized it immediately as non-human: "We don't have technology like that. We would never do that in a training range during carrier workups."
- The email was **wiped from his computer and his deputy's** the following day
- At the next monthly safety meeting, no one spoke up — "We all knew: someone just touched a SAP and they got rid of it. We could have had planes falling out of the sky, but we're not going to talk about it."
- He suspects DIA owned the SAP; the email wipe is attributed to whoever had authority over that program

## SAP Denial as Flag Officer (Firsthand)

As a one-star admiral, Gallaudet attempted to learn more about a Special Access Program that his own personnel were assigned to work under. The SAP control officer told him directly: **"No, sir. You don't have a need to know."** He describes this as directly analogous to what the [[wilson-davis-memo]] documents about Admiral Thomas Wilson.

## Underwater UAP Research

- Believes the ocean is the most underexplored UAP domain; the Navy's sonar technicians "see these [USO contacts] quite often"
- Has knowledge of classified satellite/overhead imagery of UAP/USO in the ocean that he cannot discuss on-air
- Identified an unexplained SoCal seafloor feature ("a section of underwater ridge carved out and moved horizontally 2 km") that geologist contacts cannot explain; wants to deploy an ROV on it
- Provided [[victor-vescovo]] with coordinates for this feature and for the [[2004-nimitz-tic-tac]] and [[2019-uss-omaha-uso]] encounter areas; Vescovo surveyed all of them with state-of-the-art multibeam sonar
- Noted that [[sycamore-knoll]] — an undersea feature featured in Ben Hansen's *UFO Witness* — was removed from Google Earth after Gallaudet began publicly discussing UAP-seabed interactions
- Proposed to the National Academies Ocean Studies Board that they survey government ocean data for anomalous activity; they suggested involving the Naval Studies Board for classified data
- Briefed [[gary-roughead]] (Naval Studies Board chair, former CNO); Roughead was supportive
- [[noaa]] declined his internal proposal to study underwater UAP
- Is a member of [[ocean-research-advisory-panel]] and working to have UAP added to the White House annual R&D priorities memo
