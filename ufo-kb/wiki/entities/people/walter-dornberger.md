# Walter Dornberger

Walter Robert Dornberger (1895–1980) was the head of the Nazi V2/A4 rocket program at Peenemünde. After the war he was brought to the United States under [[operation-paperclip]] and assigned to [[wright-patterson]] — the Air Force base that served as the primary foreign material exploitation center and where the Roswell crash debris was reportedly taken.

## Significance: Roswell Timing

[[peter-levenda]] highlights that Dornberger's posting to Wright Patterson and the arrival of Roswell debris were contemporaneous events. As the top Nazi rocket and aerospace engineer in US custody, Dornberger would have been the most logical person the military asked to examine unusual aerospace material. This creates a direct line from Nazi aerospace expertise to the analysis of whatever was recovered at Roswell.

This intersects with Levenda's broader thesis that Roswell may have been exotic human technology — a Soviet-operated [[horten-brothers]] flying disc built by repatriated Nazi engineers — which Dornberger, having worked with those engineers, would have been uniquely positioned to identify.

## Background

Dornberger was responsible for developing the V2 as a weapon system. His program employed forced labor from concentration camps. He was captured by British forces and later transferred to American custody. Unlike some Paperclip scientists who were integrated relatively smoothly, Dornberger carried more direct war crime exposure due to the use of concentration camp labor at Nordhausen.
