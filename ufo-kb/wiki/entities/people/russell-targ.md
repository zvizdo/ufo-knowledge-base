# Russell Targ

Laser physicist who, in partnership with [[hal-puthoff]], built the scientific foundation for government-sponsored remote viewing at **Stanford Research Institute (SRI)** beginning in 1972. Their work was funded initially by the CIA and later by the Defense Intelligence Agency as part of what eventually became the **Stargate Program** ([[stargate-program]]).

## SRI Remote Viewing Program

Targ and Puthoff published their results in *Nature* in 1974 — an extraordinary accomplishment given mainstream science's hostility to psi research. The paper reported statistically significant results from controlled experiments with multiple subjects, including [[ingo-swann]] and [[pat-price]]. The CIA's interest was explicitly intelligence-motivated: could humans psychically perceive locations, documents, or activities behind adversary security perimeters?

Pat Price (→ [[pat-price]]) became the most operationally productive of the SRI subjects — reportedly identifying Soviet facilities and technical data with enough accuracy that CIA handlers took the results seriously.

## Credibility and Controversy

Targ has spent decades defending the statistical validity of the SRI results against methodological critiques. His core claims:
1. The effect is real and replicable (he cites consistent effect sizes across hundreds of trials)
2. The 1995 government closure of Stargate was politically motivated, not evidence-based
3. Results are not dependent on belief — skeptics in the SRI program produced valid hits

Critics (including those in the 1995 AIR evaluation by Jessica Utts and Ray Hyman) split: Utts found evidence of an effect; Hyman found the protocols insufficiently rigorous.

## Stage Magician Origins

A detail surfaced by [[chris-ramsey]] in [[h3u8EkGI8OQ]]: before co-founding the Stargate program, Targ was a **stage magician**. In his younger career he experienced what he described as actual miracles during stage performances — events he could not explain through his knowledge of methods. This is the same phenomenon Ramsay independently found in surveying ~100 magicians: genuine anomalous outcomes cluster around professional magicians under specific conditions (flow state, no fallback plan, high rapport). The implication is that Targ's transition from stage magic to parapsychology research was not entirely coincidental — his firsthand experience of anomalous outcomes during performance may have predisposed him to take psi seriously.
