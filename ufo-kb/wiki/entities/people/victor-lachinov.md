# Victor Lachinov

## Background

Physicist affiliated with the Russian Academy of Sciences. Published an arXiv paper (three pages, mechanism on the third page) arguing that [[salvatore-pais]]'s proposed room-temperature superconductor mechanism — dubbed the [[pais-effect]] — is physically viable. The mechanism involves bipolarons rather than Cooper pairs as the charge carriers.

In Pais's account, Lachinov is described as a man of exceptional mathematical physics ability — "physical mathematics, where physics drives the mathematics, not vice versa" — and Pais considers Lachinov's endorsement the most meaningful external validation he has received.

## Bipolaron Mechanism

Lachinov's analysis focuses on the bipolaron hypothesis: at temperatures above those where conventional BCS superconductivity operates, electrons can pair not as direct Cooper pairs (facilitated by phonon exchange) but as **bipolarons** — composite quasi-particles made of two polarons. A polaron is an electron dressed by its lattice distortion field; a bipolaron is two such dressed electrons that form a bound state. Lachinov argues that the Pais effect — specifically, the driven oscillation of electrically charged material far from thermodynamic equilibrium — can stabilize bipolaron condensates at room temperature in materials like lead zirconate titanate (PZT), all three components of which superconduct individually at low temperatures.

## Relationship to Pais Patents

Lachinov's paper supports the one Pais patent that was **denied** — the room-temperature superconductor. The patent examiner rejected it as physically implausible despite:
1. Lachinov's published support
2. The logical dependency: the high-frequency gravitational wave generator patent (which was *granted*) explicitly requires a high-Q resonator, which requires the high-temperature superconductor to function

This inconsistency — granting patents that depend on a denied enabling patent — is noted by both Pais and Jack (the pseudonymous co-host) in the [[8TYMQOUDQBo]] interview as an anomaly suggesting the examiner either did not review the interdependencies or was instructed to deny specific claims.
