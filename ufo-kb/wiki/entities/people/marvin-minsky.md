# Marvin Minsky

Marvin Lee Minsky (1927–2016) was an American cognitive scientist and computer scientist, co-founder of the MIT Artificial Intelligence Lab (now CSAIL) and one of the most influential figures in the development of artificial intelligence as a field.

**AI contributions**: Minsky's theoretical work on perceptrons (with Seymour Papert), frames (a knowledge representation paradigm), and the "society of mind" model of cognition (the mind as a competition of semi-autonomous agents) were foundational to early AI. His 1986 book *The Society of Mind* proposed a model of intelligence as emerging from simple interacting processes — a model that has taken on new relevance in neural network interpretability research.

**Consciousness and UAP nexus**: Minsky was a committed materialist who believed consciousness would eventually be entirely explained by computation. This positioned him squarely against researchers like [[donald-hoffman]] who argue consciousness is fundamental rather than emergent. The debate matters for UAP research because the [[conscious-craft-hypothesis]] and [[nhi-telepathic-communication]] claims require consciousness to have properties that strict materialist AI theory denies.

**Eric Haseltine connection**: [[eric-haseltine]], NSA Director of Research and the KB's primary bridge between mainstream intelligence and UAP research, counted Minsky as a personal acquaintance — a connection that situates Minsky at the edge of the KB's network of individuals with both AI/consciousness expertise and potential UAP-adjacent knowledge.

**Transhumanism**: Minsky was an enthusiastic proponent of mind uploading and life extension, placing him at the [[transhumanism]] end of the AI-consciousness spectrum. He predicted that if he could keep his brain intact until nanotechnology was sufficiently developed, he could survive indefinitely in silicon.
