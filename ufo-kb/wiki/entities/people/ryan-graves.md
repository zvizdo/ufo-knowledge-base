# Ryan Graves

Former US Navy F/A-18 Super Hornet pilot with a decade of service. Flew with the **VFA-11 Red Rippers** out of NAS Oceana, Virginia Beach. Advanced strike fighter instructor for 3.5 years, working with student pilots on their first tactical jet. In 2023 he testified before the House Oversight Subcommittee on National Security alongside [[david-fravor]] and [[david-grusch]] in what became the most-watched congressional UAP hearing in history. He subsequently founded [[americans-for-safe-aerospace]] to aggregate UAP reports from commercial and military aviators.

## The 2014–2015 East Coast Incidents

See [[2014-east-coast-uap]] for full incident account.

Graves's squadron was transitioning from the APG-73 (mechanically-scanned array) radar to the APG-79 (actively-electronically scanned array) radar. The older radar had known false-track issues; the new system was supposed to filter them out. Objects began appearing on the new radar in their working area — a large operating cube off the Virginia coast — that behaved unlike any radar artifact:

- **Perfectly stationary** in 120-knot winds (category-3 hurricane conditions), without any positional drift
- Operating at 0.6–0.8 Mach in holding, circular, and racetrack patterns
- Speeds up to 1.2 Mach (Graves's personal top speed was 1.32)
- Three to seven objects simultaneously in the area
- **Exclusively over water**, heading east at speed when mobile

Initial assumption: radar software bugs. That hypothesis collapsed when the ATFLIR targeting pod (looking where the radar was looking) detected energy at the same locations, and the AIM-9X missile seekers locked onto them. Triple-sensor correlation established physical objects.

### Visual Acquisition Attempts

The squadron flew intercept passes to get a visual on slow-moving contacts — stepping down to 800 ft outside a 500-ft safety bubble, looking up against the blue sky. Despite helmet-mounted augmented-reality cueing pointing exactly where to look, they saw nothing. The sensors would reacquire when they turned back.

### The Near-Miss

Two aircraft flying approximately 150 ft apart entered the working area at a fixed GPS waypoint. An object shot down the wing of the lead aircraft and passed within ~50 ft of the cockpit. The pilot who saw it described it as: **a dark gray or black cube inside of a clear sphere, approximately 5–15 ft in diameter.** See [[cube-in-sphere]].

This triggered a hazard report (HAZREP). A subsequent safety survey revealed **four additional unreported near-miss incidents** in the squadron.

### The GOFAST Revelation

Graves's close friend — a weapons system officer who was in the back seat when the [[2015-gofast]] video was recorded — told Graves that the tape captured only one of **four objects in line-abreast formation**, each roughly a mile apart. The four maintained precise relative spacing, implying inter-craft coordination. This detail has not been publicly reported elsewhere.

## Congressional Testimony (2023)

Opening statement: "My name is Ryan Fobs Graves and I'm a former F-18 pilot with a decade of service in the US Navy. I have experienced Advanced UAP firsthand and I'm here to voice the concerns of more than 30 commercial air crew and military veterans who have confided their similar encounters with me."

Graves describes the hearing as unusually bipartisan — no partisan attacks, members working together. [[david-grusch]] sat to his left; [[david-fravor]] to his right. Fravor and Grusch pushed Graves to the front of the lineup.

## On David Grusch's Claims

Graves had no personal access to crash-retrieval programs. He finds nothing in Grusch's background inconsistent with honesty. Grusch's "I can tell you the location, get me in a SCIF" framing is, to Graves, the posture of someone with genuine specific knowledge. He regards Grusch as having "moved the needle quite a bit closer to certainty."

## On Aviation Safety

Graves frames under-reporting as a systemic safety hazard: career concerns, stigma, and the time cost of filing reports drive pilots not to document encounters. The East Coast incidents produced at minimum 10 eyewitnesses from his squadron alone (~25 pilots total). UAP also appeared in working areas around other Navy bases — Pax River, NAS Lemoore (California), NALF Fallon — and at Langley AFB during the 2023 drone swarms.

## Americans for Safe Aerospace

Graves founded [[americans-for-safe-aerospace]] as a non-profit to aggregate reports from commercial and military aviation personnel — extending what he tried to do through military channels to the civilian sector.

## Private Sector UAP Detection

Graves started a small company building next-generation **space-situational awareness cameras** capable of detecting non-traditional orbital trajectories. Use case: informing satellite operators whether to maneuver to avoid collisions. Secondary use case: commercial UAP detection intelligence product. He frames commercial space deregulation as creating a window for this work.
