# Richard Helms

Richard Helms (1913–2002) served as CIA Director from 1966 to 1973. His defining act in the MK-Ultra story: as Watergate unraveled and Nixon's departure looked inevitable, Helms calculated that congressional investigators would soon gain access to CIA files. He ordered the destruction of [[mk-ultra]] records before this could happen.

## The File Destruction

In 1973, with Watergate investigations expanding, Helms ordered all MK-Ultra files destroyed. The order was largely carried out. The program would have remained unknown but for [[john-marks]], a State Department official who found surviving financial records in 1974 — apparently overlooked in the destruction order — and brought them to Congress.

The destruction order stands as one of the most consequential acts of evidence suppression in U.S. government history: it successfully concealed most of what MK-Ultra had actually accomplished, allowing the CIA to adopt the "it didn't work" posture in subsequent congressional testimony.

## Significance

Helms's destruction order explains why the full scope of MK-Ultra's capabilities — including [[jolly-west]]'s 1955 claim of successful permanent memory implantation — is largely reconstructed from fragmentary surviving documents rather than complete operational records. Tom O'Neill's discovery of West's original (unredacted) 1955 report, which contains content censored from the Congressional version, suggests the destruction was targeted rather than total.
