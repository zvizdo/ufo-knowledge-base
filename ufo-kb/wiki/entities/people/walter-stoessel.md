# Walter Stoessel

US Ambassador stationed in Moscow who became one of the earliest documented victims of Soviet directed-energy harassment of US diplomatic personnel. Developed a rare blood cancer attributed to Soviet microwave beaming of the US Embassy that had been ongoing since at least the early 1970s. Died in 1986.

## Historical Significance

Stoessel's case is the foundational historical precedent for [[2016-havana-syndrome]]. The Soviet Union continuously beamed the US Embassy in Moscow with microwave radiation — a practice confirmed at the diplomatic level when Henry Kissinger confronted Soviet Ambassador Anatoly Dobrynin about it at the 1976 SALT talks. The Soviets' use of directed microwave energy against US personnel pre-dates the Havana incidents by decades, supporting the attribution to Russia.

The fact that a US Ambassador died of radiation-induced cancer and this was not treated as an act of war at the time is cited by [[eric-haseltine]] as the direct precedent for why Havana syndrome suppression occurs — acknowledging the attacks is politically catastrophic.
