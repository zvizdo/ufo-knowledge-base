# Matthew Fox

Matthew Fox (born 1940) is an American theologian and Episcopal priest, formerly a Dominican friar until his expulsion from the Dominican Order in 1993 by Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger (later Pope Benedict XVI) for his Creation Spirituality teachings, which Ratzinger deemed insufficiently orthodox. He subsequently became an Episcopalian.

**Creation Spirituality**: Fox developed "Creation Spirituality" as a theological framework emphasizing the positive role of creation (the universe, the Earth, human creativity) as the primary expression of the divine, rather than the fall-redemption narrative of traditional Western Christianity. He drew on Hildegard of Bingen, Meister Eckhart, and other medieval mystics, as well as eco-theology and feminist theology.

**The Physics of Angels**: Fox co-authored *The Physics of Angels: Exploring the Realm Where Science and Spirit Meet* (1996) with biologist [[rupert-sheldrake]]. The book engaged with medieval sources — Dionysius the Areopagite, Thomas Aquinas, Hildegard of Bingen — on the nature of angels, reading them through the lens of modern physics and panpsychism. The core question: if medieval cosmology treated angels as the intelligences guiding the celestial bodies, and if panpsychism (consciousness as fundamental) is now academically respectable, what is the difference between an "angel" and the consciousness of a star or planet?

**UAP relevance**: [[jesse-michaels]] notes that specific attributes assigned to angels in the medieval sources Fox and Sheldrake selected — irradiated light, electromagnetic-like lingering effects, capacity for instant translocation, interaction with human consciousness — map onto modern UAP phenomenology. The book provides a documented tradition for thinking about non-material intelligences that parallels contemporary NHI contact frameworks.
