# Marik von Rennenkampff

Former Pentagon/DoD official (Obama era) who writes about UAP for The Hill. His interest in UAP began in late 2019 when he watched [[david-fravor]]'s Joe Rogan interview — the testimony of a credible naval aviator (Commanding Officer of [[vfa-41-black-aces]]) convinced him the phenomenon warranted serious investigation. An extensive email exchange with [[mick-west]] deepened rather than resolved his conviction; he found West's alternative theories increasingly inadequate as he dug into the primary case files.

## Background & Entry Point

Previously skeptical — placed UAP "squarely in tinfoil hat territory." Missed the 2017 NYT story entirely. Fravor's Rogan interview was the turning point: "This guy's the real deal." Subsequently read raw Blue Book case files, studied [[james-mcdonald]]'s work, and began publishing UAP analysis for The Hill.

## Analytical Position

### Gimbal Video
Argues [[2015-gimbal]] represents a genuine anomaly. Cites air-to-air infrared experts who called the rotation "bizarre" and "never seen before," and who rejected the SR-71 comparison and the glare explanation (West's position). Contends the object's shape evolves as the sensor closes distance, consistent with perspective rather than morphing artifact. Points to the visual match with the [[2008-iraq-gimbal]] video as corroboration that the morphology is physical, not sensor-specific.

### Historical Case Record
Grounds his position in deep historical research: raw Blue Book case files (unanalyzed reports, not summaries), [[james-mcdonald]]'s *[[science-in-default]]* critique of the Condon Committee, and Rupelt's observation that ~70% of credible sightings cluster near nuclear sites. Merrick argues this multi-decade pattern establishes a baseline that videos must be interpreted against.

### Nuclear Nexus
Strongly endorses the [[ufo-nuclear-nexus]]: 167 Q-clearance nuclear-security personnel officially reported UAP encounters (argues this is a severe undercount due to stigma), Battelle's analysis found 33% of credible 1947–52 cases unexplained, Rupelt's nuclear proximity data.

### Institutional Acknowledgment
Tracks the escalating chain of official statements: Rubio's 2020 Senate provision, Ratcliffe's "technologies we cannot defend against" statement, AARO director Kosloski's public admission of "true anomalies" he cannot explain. Frames these as a coherent trajectory toward forced disclosure.

## Key Historical Framing

Endorses [[james-mcdonald]]'s conclusion: "The extraterrestrial hypothesis is the least unsatisfactory hypothesis for what hundreds and thousands of people have observed at close range over the last 80 years." Bases this on 30+ close-range, multi-witness cases involving police, pilots, and engineers — not just distant lights.
