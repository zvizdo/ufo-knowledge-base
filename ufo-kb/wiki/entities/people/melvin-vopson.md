# Melvin Vopson

Physicist at the University of Portsmouth. Developed the **mass-energy-information equivalence principle** — an extension of the Zuse → Landauer → Wheeler information-as-physics lineage. The key claim: **information has measurable mass**. Rolf Landauer established that erasing a bit of stored data releases heat (and therefore energy); Vopson extended this to argue that stored data therefore has mass (energy = mass via E=mc²).

**The data-mass calculation**: Vopson calculated that current world data, totaling trillions of gigabytes, equals approximately **1 kilogram of hidden dark mass** at the Landauer limit. At the current 25%/year data growth rate, Earth's data infrastructure will accumulate approximately **one moon's worth of mass** within ~340 years — a potential geophysical event without precedent in human timescales.

**UAP / simulation-theory application**: In [[jason-reza-jorjani]]'s discussion on [[jesse-michaels]]' podcast ([[EzPmG_7WhXc]]), Vopson's calculations are cited in the context of [[information-cosmology]] and [[simulation-theory]]: if the universe runs on information-as-substrate, nuclear detonations may be "matrix-busting" events that damage or disrupt the information substrate, explaining why UAP cluster around nuclear sites ([[ufo-nuclear-nexus]]). This extends the Riz Virk-style simulation framework with physical mass-consequences rather than pure metaphor.

**Status**: Vopson's mass-energy-information equivalence principle has not achieved mainstream physics consensus. The Landauer limit is real and experimentally confirmed; the extension to "data has mass" remains theoretically contested and awaits experimental validation. Whether the calculation could be empirically verified is an open question noted in the [[EzPmG_7WhXc]] transcript.
