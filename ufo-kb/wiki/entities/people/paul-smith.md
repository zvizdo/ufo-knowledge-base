# Paul Smith

Former CIA [[stargate-program]] remote viewer, currently based in Cedar City, Utah. A primary practitioner and vocal public advocate for remote viewing as a trainable, repeatable protocol. Author of *The Essential Guide to Remote Viewing*. Colleague of [[joe-mcmoneagle]].

## Background

Smith served in the Stargate Program under CIA/DIA management. He continues to teach remote viewing professionally and is one of the program's most accessible public voices. In [[eQIMantuasQ]], he is the primary interviewee on both operational history and the philosophy of consciousness.

## USS Stark Precognition (1987)

Smith's most striking personal firsthand claim, disclosed in [[eQIMantuasQ]]:

**Task**: "Describe whatever is most important for us to know about within the next few days."

**Session**: He described a vessel resembling an American destroyer. He perceived an aircraft circling at a distance, which then dropped **two small cylinders with stubby wings** making roaring sounds; the cylinders flew toward the vessel and struck it, leaving it "full of smoke and flames … leaning to one side, all bent and crumpled." He locked the session in his safe and left for the weekend.

**Outcome**: Monday morning a colleague called: "Paul, where's that session you did on Friday? Have you seen the papers?" The *Washington Post* headline: **USS Stark hit by Exocet missiles fired by an Iraqi jet**. Thirty-seven Americans were killed.

Smith notes there would have been a way to use this session to prevent the attack — but if it had been acted on and the attack didn't happen, future warnings would be disbelieved. He calls this "the perfect microcosm of an inherent conflict in parapsychology and making it work."

## Typhoon Submarine Corroboration

Smith independently corroborates [[joe-mcmoneagle]]'s 1979 Typhoon submarine session in [[RofQnByLwOo]]: McMoneagle's description of a massive nuclear sub with launch tubes **in front of the superstructure** was dismissed as impossible at the time. Eight months later the Typhoon floated out exactly as described.

## Remote Viewing Training and Protocol

In [[eQIMantuasQ]], Smith describes the cognitive discipline of remote viewing:

- **Signal vs. noise**: The RV signal is "very subtle" — coming from an unknown source into a physical brain. Most beginners don't recognize the signal at all.
- **Mental noise / analytical overlay**: The left brain immediately tries to "solve the problem" — if the target is the Eiffel Tower, the left brain gets an impression of crisscrossing metal girders, thinks "bridge," and provides the wrong label. The discipline is to report the impression without the analytical label.
- **Ego displacement**: Smith describes how [[joe-mcmoneagle]] "sends his ego off to do something else" during a session — a kind of practiced lateral dissociation. Smith confirms this is real but notes McMoneagle "makes it sound simpler than it is."
- **Zen component**: "It's all about process, not outcome. You have to learn to not care whether you succeed or fail." Smith notes these mental disciplines have direct real-world applicability beyond RV.

## Consciousness as Transmission

Smith articulates the **TV analogy** of consciousness in [[eQIMantuasQ]]:

> "That TV is not generating the program you're watching — that's coming over the airwaves. Pull out one of those plugs and the picture gets distorted. Pull out a different plug and it gets distorted in a different way. That's exactly what you might be doing with the brain when you're shutting pieces down and seeing it affect consciousness. That is evidence to show that physicalism isn't necessarily right."

He explicitly frames remote viewing's evidential value: if you can perceive things at a distance and verify those perceptions, the physicalist model (all experience is generated within the skull) cannot be correct. The **transmission/reception model** follows directly.

He also invokes the **binding problem of consciousness** — the unsolved question of how disparate sensory pathways (optic, auditory, motor coordination) are unified into a single experience — as additional evidence that something non-physical is doing the integrating work.

## Paradigm Suppression

Smith frames the resistance to parapsychology as structural rather than evidentiary:

- Skeptics "have the organs of publication" — they control the dominant paradigm
- The Copernicus parallel: when heliocentrism arrived, it wasn't rejected because of evidence; it was rejected because it threatened the received view
- Less than $30M has been spent globally on parapsychology in 100 years — less than a single particle accelerator
