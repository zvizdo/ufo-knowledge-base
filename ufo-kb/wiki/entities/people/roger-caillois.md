# Roger Caillois

French polymath and sociologist (1913–1978) associated with the Collège de Sociologie and later UNESCO. Best known for *Man, Play and Games* (1958) and his surrealist-adjacent research on mimicry and natural symbolism.

**Mantis / diabolos thesis**: Caillois wrote extensively about the praying mantis as an archetype of the **trickster/diabolos** — an agent of dialectical growth through apparent cruelty or opposition. "Diabolos" in Greek means *that which sets things into a state of dynamic tension* (diabolein = setting into dynamic opposition) — not evil in the Christian sense, but a productive creative force that generates growth through contradiction. The mantis's literal biology (the female devouring the male during mating) maps onto this archetype perfectly. Caillois was prompted to research mantis symbolism by surrealist poet Paul Eluard, who maintained an extensive insect collection.

**Application to NHI and mantid entities**: In the [[ABw7EsWh2Us]] transcript, [[jason-reza-jorjani]] draws on Caillois's mantis analysis to interpret [[mantid-entities]] — the distinctive praying-mantis-type non-human beings reported by experiencers including [[ted-owens]] and [[david-huggins]]. If the mantid being is culturally and philosophically the diabolos archetype, its role in contact experiences is neither demonic nor benevolent but dialectical: it forces growth through apparently predatory action. This reframes the soul-harvesting and traumatic-contact accounts as structurally productive rather than simply malevolent.

**Mimicry in biology and NHI**: Caillois's broader mimicry work — the observation that organisms adopt the appearance of their environment not merely for protection but as if compelled — extends to the NHI behavioral-mimicry hypothesis: entities that adopt human forms, emotional registers, and social roles may be doing so by an imperative structurally analogous to biological mimicry.
