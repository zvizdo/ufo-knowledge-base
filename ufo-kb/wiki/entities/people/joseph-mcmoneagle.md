# Joseph McMoneagle

US Army veteran and remote viewer; designated "Remote Viewer #001" in the government's classified psychic research programs. Participated in [[stargate-program]] and its predecessor programs (Gondola Wish, Grill Flame) from the program's early 1970s inception. His reported 200+ successful intelligence-collection operations via remote viewing led to his receiving the Legion of Merit — a formal military decoration that constitutes official government acknowledgment of remote viewing's intelligence value.

**Program context**: McMoneagle was one of the original operational remote viewers developed through [[hal-puthoff]]'s [[sri-international]] research program before that research was taken operational by the Army's Intelligence and Security Command. His career spans the entire developmental arc: from SRI research subject, to operational military viewer, to post-program civilian researcher. His experience grounds the argument that remote viewing was not a purely experimental failure but produced intelligence with enough operational value to earn formal military recognition.

**Significance for ongoing programs**: [[salvatore-pais]] cites McMoneagle as evidence that the government's remote viewing program was genuinely productive and was therefore never fully terminated — only declassified at the unclassified tier while presumably continuing in compartmentalized channels. This is a recurring KB pattern: official termination announcements (Stargate terminated 1995) followed by evidence suggesting the capability migrated to deeper classification levels.

**Monroe Institute connection**: After his military career, McMoneagle became affiliated with the [[monroe-institute]] — Robert Monroe's organization for researching altered states of consciousness, out-of-body experience, and related phenomena. This trajectory — from classified military remote-viewer to consciousness-research institute — is the same path taken by other KB figures at the intersection of official psi programs and wider consciousness research.

**Author and public figure**: McMoneagle published several books on remote viewing, including *Mind Trek* (1993) and *The Stargate Chronicles* (2002), making him one of the few official program participants to write substantively about his experiences under his real name.
