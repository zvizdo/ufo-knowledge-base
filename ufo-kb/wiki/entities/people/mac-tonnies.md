# Mac Tonnies

American author, blogger, and anomalist (1975–2009) who developed the "cryptoterrestrial hypothesis" — one of the primary modern variants of the [[ultraterrestrial-hypothesis]]. Tonnies argued that the entities associated with UAP phenomena are not extraterrestrial visitors but an ancient humanoid civilization indigenous to Earth: either a parallel evolutionary lineage that developed alongside *Homo sapiens*, or a precursor civilization that survived some catastrophe by retreating underground or into concealment.

**The cryptoterrestrial hypothesis**: Tonnies distinguished his position from simple "underground bases" theories by arguing for genuine biological intelligence — a co-evolutionary species with deep knowledge of human culture, language, and psychology precisely *because* it has always shared the planet. In this frame, "alien" behavior reflects deliberate concealment and manipulation rather than genuine alienness. The apparent lack of interstellar craft transit (one of Vallée's critiques of the ETH) is explained simply: they're already here.

**Posthumous publication**: Tonnies died unexpectedly in October 2009 at age 34. *The Cryptoterrestrials* was published posthumously that year, assembled from his manuscript and blog writings. His death before the book's formal reception gave the work an unusual position in the literature — widely cited but never publicly defended by its author.

**Legacy**: Tonnies's cryptoterrestrial frame is cited in the KB through [[ultraterrestrial-hypothesis]] as a distinct variant from Vallée's interdimensional hypothesis and [[john-keel]]'s ultraterrestrial coinage. His framing anticipates some elements of the "ancient civilization" hypothesis and the [[future-humans-hypothesis]].
