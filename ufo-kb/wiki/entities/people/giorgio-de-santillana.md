# Giorgio de Santillana

Professor of History of Science at MIT. Co-authored *[[hamlets-mill]]* (1969) with [[hertha-von-dechend]] of Frankfurt University. The book identifies very ancient, very early knowledge of precession embedded in myths from around the world, arguing that what appears to be mythological narrative is actually a mnemonic delivery system for precise astronomical data.

[[graham-hancock]] cites *Hamlet's Mill* as foundational to his understanding that ancient myths can preserve real astronomical knowledge without the storyteller needing to understand the encoded content. The key mechanism: a compelling story is so memorable that it gets repeated indefinitely, carrying embedded astronomical data forward even when the knowledge of that data has been lost.
