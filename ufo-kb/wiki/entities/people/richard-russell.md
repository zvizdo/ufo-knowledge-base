# Richard Russell

U.S. Senator from Georgia (1933–1971), widely regarded as one of the most powerful figures in mid-20th-century US government. Russell chaired the Senate Armed Services Committee during the height of the Cold War — giving him direct, classified access to US military intelligence. His firsthand observation of two Soviet disc aircraft on 4 October 1955 makes him the most senior US official on record to have directly witnessed a UAP.

## UAP Testimony

On 4 October 1955, Russell was traveling through the Trans-Caucasus (Azerbaijan, USSR) by train with Lt. Col. E.U. Hathaway (US Army, Senate Armed Services Committee) and Ruben Efron (Committee Consultant). At approximately 1910 hours, while the train was between ATJATY and ADZHIJABUL, Russell observed the first disc ascending from a Soviet launch area south of the rail line, then immediately called Hathaway and Efron to the window. All three clearly observed the second disc one minute later.

Russell's firsthand account:
- First disc: round/circular, outer surface revolving clockwise, two stationary interior lights near top, sparks/flame from bottom, no protrusions, wings, or fuselage
- Ascended vertically from area with searchlights pointing skyward to ~6000 ft, then accelerated northward at high speed
- Soviet trainmen immediately closed all curtains and refused access to windows after the sighting — an active information-control response

The report was cabled OPERATIONAL IMMEDIATE to HQ USAF by Lt. Col. Thomas S. Ryan (US Air Attaché, Prague), classified Top Secret NOFORN, and rated B-2 (Probably True) by USAIRA.

## Historical Significance

Russell's institutional standing is unmatched among UAP witnesses: as Chairman of the Senate Armed Services Committee, he received classified military briefings and held oversight authority over the programs most likely to have produced a domestic explanation. His own contemporaneous account — filed in an official government cable — leaves no gap in chain of custody. The Soviet trainmen's immediate curtain-closing and window-blocking adds an independent corroborating datum for the significance of the observation.
