# Brad Sorensen

Industrial designer and technical illustrator. Friend and professional associate of [[mark-mccandish]]. In 1988, Sorensen was admitted to a classified aerospace technology demonstration at [[norton-air-force-base]] (San Bernardino, California) alongside a high-level official. What he witnessed became the basis for McCandish's famous **Flux Liner / ARV line art** and constitutes one of the most operationally specific alleged firsthand ARV accounts in the UAP literature.

## The 1988 Norton AFB Event

Sorensen entered a classified hangar at Norton AFB that was divided by a black curtain into two sections.

### Public / Semi-Classified Section

- Advanced hovercraft technology
- A B2 bomber competitor airframe
- **The Pulsar** — a Lockheed-attributed craft with a claimed 120 nuclear warhead payload and scramjet propulsion

### The Curtained Section — "Mama, Baby, and Papa Bear"

Three saucer-shaped craft of different sizes (informally "mama, baby, and papa bear"):

**Shape and exterior:**
- Acorn / inverted jello-mold with a dome on top
- Visibly rough construction: rivets, machining slag and chips
- Appearance of something built by scientists, not aerospace engineers
- Sorensen's characterization: a *"poor man's interpretation"* of 1947 crash materials — reverse-engineered imperfectly from a recovered template

**Bottom section:**
- Central Tesla coil-like mechanism
- Copper plate arrays
- Rotating arm assembly

**Top section:**
- Removable crew cabin
- F-4 Phantom jump-seat flight controls (conventional cockpit retrofitted into an unconventional craft)

**On-site video demonstration:**
- Craft hopping across a dry lake bed
- Shooting vertically into the air with **zero dust disturbance** at lift-off

## Corroboration Chain

1. **1990 Aviation Week interview** — Aviation Week editor **Bill Scott** (then a senior editor) conducted a full interview with Sorensen — the first complete contemporaneous documented account. This predates McCandish's 2001 NPC presentation and provides a contemporaneous record not mediated through McCandish. Confirmed in [[nTiFs8LudUo]].
2. **[[mark-mccandish]]'s testimony** — McCandish produced detailed technical line art of the Flux Liner / ARV and presented it at the 2001 National Press Club Disclosure event. The same line art was later contracted by Chris Carter for the X-Files pilot's alien craft design, suggesting the design reached Hollywood via the same engineering community.
3. **Northrop/Lockheed engineers** — [[mark-mccandish]] received independent descriptions of the same triangular ARV (XF-131 Super Sentinel design) from engineers at Northrop and Lockheed who had grown frustrated with classification — corroborating that the program existed within those companies.
4. **[[uap-gerb]]** independently researched Sorensen's account and treats the Aviation Week 1990 interview as the strongest anchor.

## Eric Davis's Refutation

[[eric-davis]] was directly asked about Sorensen and McCandish in [[LnAiNChnuEQ]]:

> "I don't know where they're coming from and then other people kind of came out of the woodwork saying the same thing but no proof."

Davis received a classified briefing from program insiders stating that no ARVs have been built and no reverse-engineering progress was made after decades of effort. He places Sorensen/McCandish in the same category as [[bob-lazar]]'s claims — self-believed but not accurate.

> [!warning] Conflict
> Davis's classified briefing denies ARVs exist. Sorensen's 1990 Aviation Week account and McCandish's corroborating engineer descriptions pre-date Davis's briefing and are structurally independent of it. The conflict remains unresolved.

## Credibility Assessment

Sorensen is a private professional with no obvious financial motive for a fabricated account. His 1990 Aviation Week interview provides a contemporaneous record. Multiple independent lines (McCandish's separate engineer testimony; the X-Files design convergence) point toward the same event and hardware description. However, [[eric-davis]]'s classified briefing counter-claim is a significant evidentiary weight on the other side. See [[credibility-frameworks]].
