# Howard Morland

## Background

Howard Morland was a journalist working for *The Progressive* magazine who undertook an investigation to determine whether classified nuclear weapons information was genuinely secret or whether it could be reconstructed from publicly available sources. His conclusion, documented in an article the US government attempted to suppress, was that the Teller-Ulam thermonuclear bomb design could be meaningfully reconstructed by assembling fragments scattered across declassified government documents, academic publications, and publicly accessible materials.

## The Progressive / H-Bomb Case

In 1979, the US Department of Justice obtained a prior restraint injunction against *The Progressive* to prevent publication of Morland's article — the first successful prior restraint against a US publication since the Pentagon Papers case. The government argued that even though each individual piece of information was technically public, Morland's synthesis constituted a weapons specification.

The case was eventually dropped when essentially the same information appeared elsewhere in the public domain, mooting the injunction. Morland's article was subsequently published.

[[eric-weinstein]] invokes Morland in [[xnxasfyHtfo]] as a direct parallel to [[john-aristotle-phillips]]: in the same era, both demonstrated that the classification boundary for nuclear weapons physics is **inherently porous** — that motivated individuals assembling declassified fragments can reconstruct information the government treats as secret.

## Significance

The Morland case illustrates a core paradox of nuclear and advanced-physics classification:

- Classification works by preventing access to integrated specifications, not individual fragments
- Once fragments are scattered across the public record through declassification, academic publication, and policy documents, the integrated specification becomes recoverable by anyone willing to do the research
- This means the classification regime creates a **false sense of security** while the actual physics knowledge diffuses through the public domain on long timescales

Per Weinstein, this is why the most sensitive classified physics is never declassified even in fragments — and why programs like the crash retrieval effort described by [[eric-davis]] operate completely off the public record.
