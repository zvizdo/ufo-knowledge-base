# R.A. Schwaller de Lubicz

French occultist, philosopher, and heterodox Egyptologist (1887–1961) who spent years studying the temples at Luxor and developed an alternative framework for understanding ancient Egyptian sacred science. In the alternative archaeology context, Schwaller de Lubicz is best known as the originator of the observation that the erosion on the body of the [[great-sphinx]] appears to be caused by prolonged rainfall rather than wind and sand — an observation that implies the Sphinx was carved during a wetter climatic period, potentially pushing its construction date back thousands of years before conventional Egyptology accepts.

## Background

Schwaller de Lubicz was embedded in early 20th-century European esoteric and occult circles before turning his attention to Egypt. His major scholarly work, *The Temple of Man* (*Le Temple de l'Homme*, 1957), argued that the Temple of Luxor was a mathematical and symbolic encoding of human anatomy and cosmology. He was not a professional geologist, and his water erosion observation remained a footnote in alternative literature until it was taken up by [[john-anthony-west]].

## Notable Claims / Role

The water erosion hypothesis — later developed by West and geologist [[robert-schoch]] into a formal geological argument — originates with Schwaller de Lubicz's visual observation during his years in Egypt. This makes him the intellectual seed of the [[sphinx-water-erosion-theory]], even though the scientific case was built by others. In the broader alternative history framework, his work on Egyptian sacred science also informs discussions of encoded ancient knowledge and [[mystery-schools]].
