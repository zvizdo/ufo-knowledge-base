# Nikolai Fyodorov

Nikolai Fyodorov (1829–1903) was a Russian philosopher and librarian, considered the founding father of **Russian cosmism**. His central thesis: the true Christian task was not to await the resurrection as a passive eschatological event, but to *cause* it — using technology to literally resurrect every human who had ever lived. Once death was defeated, humanity's next duty was to leave Earth and colonize the cosmos.

This was not metaphor. Fyodorov meant it literally: technological mastery over death and space as the completion of Christian purpose.

## Influence

His most important student was [[konstantin-tsiolkovsky]], who took Fyodorov's cosmic destiny framework and derived the physics of spaceflight from it. Tsiolkovsky's 1903 rocket equation — the foundational formula of all rocketry — emerged from this cosmism worldview. The tradition that space exploration is a spiritually necessary task for humanity flows directly from Fyodorov through Tsiolkovsky to the entire Soviet and, indirectly, American space programs.

## Relevance to UAP/Occult-Aerospace Pattern

Fyodorov's cosmism predates the American occultist strand (Parsons, etc.) by decades and provides its Russian parallel. Both strands — American (Thelema/magic) and Russian (Christian cosmism) — treat the space program as fundamentally a transcendent, quasi-religious mission, not merely a technical or political one. This is the framework [[diana-pasulka]] calls [[cosmism]] in *[[american-cosmic]]*.
