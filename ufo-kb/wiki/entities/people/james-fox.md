# James Fox

American documentary filmmaker whose career has been devoted almost entirely to UAP/UFO subject matter. Fox is widely regarded within the UAP research community as the most successful filmmaker at bringing serious military and government testimony to mainstream audiences without sensationalizing the material.

## Background

Fox entered the UAP documentary space in the early 2000s after becoming convinced, through research and interviews, that the phenomenon warranted serious journalistic treatment. His approach — focusing on named military and government officials rather than anonymous sources, and emphasizing documentary evidence over anecdote — distinguished his work from the tabloid UFO programming dominant in the 1990s.

## Key Works

### "Out of the Blue" (2003)

Fox's first major feature documentary. It compiled testimony from military personnel, astronauts, and government officials from multiple countries. Interviewees included Gordon Cooper (astronaut), Fife Symington (former Governor of Arizona), Michio Kaku (theoretical physicist), and others. The film was notable for its restraint and sourcing standards and was screened at the United Nations.

### "I Know What I Saw" (2009)

A follow-up focusing on high-credibility military and government witnesses. Included testimony from more international officials, building on the post-"Out of the Blue" network Fox had developed.

### "The Phenomenon" (2020)

Fox's most significant and widely seen work. "The Phenomenon" covers the full arc of US government UAP involvement from Kenneth Arnold (1947) through the 2017 AATIP revelations. It features:

- John Podesta (former White House Chief of Staff) on record discussing the cover-up
- Fife Symington revisiting the Phoenix Lights
- Jimmy Carter discussing his own sighting
- Documentary evidence of government interest including previously classified communications
- The 1966 Westall mass sighting (Australia)

The film is broadly considered the best-documented mainstream UAP documentary to date and was endorsed by several of its high-credibility interviewees as accurately representing their statements.

## Collaborative Relationships

Fox has worked closely with [[steven-greer]] (Disclosure Project network) and [[leslie-kean]] (journalist, "UFOs: Generals, Pilots, and Government Officials Go on the Record"). His reliance on named officials with verifiable records places him in the more conservative wing of the disclosure media landscape relative to the more expansive claims typical of [[disclosure-narratives]].

## Relevance to McKinnon Interview

In [[2ttdlCa5ZCI]], Fox is referenced in the context of the broader disclosure community within which McKinnon's story circulates. The interview reflects a media ecosystem that includes Fox's documentaries as reference points for how UAP claims move from fringe to mainstream credibility.

### "Moment of Contact" (2022) and Sequel

Fox's most significant work on a specific incident. [[moment-of-contact]] documents the [[1996-varginha]] case in Brazil, which Fox investigated for ~15 years (three visits to Brazil per year at peak). With co-producer [[marco-leal]], he assembled the most complete witness chain for the case including: crash witness [[carlos-de-souza]], neurosurgeon [[italo-venturelli]] (face-to-face with the alien being), forensic physician [[armando-fortunato]] (autopsy of the officer who died), and senior pathologist [[jean-bachista-janini]] (bacteria findings). In 2025 Fox brought the three primary witnesses to Austin, Texas for the American Alchemy interview ([[UkKwa4jU0fc]]). He has offered $1M for the missing video that [[marcos-vinicius]] filmed of the being in 1996.

### "The Program" (forthcoming ~2026)

Fox's most explicitly bold documentary. He says: "I would go to jail to say things I've been exposed to in the program." Key subjects:
- [[hal-puthoff]] — "star of the documentary"; Fox had a screening with him the night before the [[bVhSPH2A5Vw]] interview
- [[jason-sanss]] — USAF Master Sergeant, 67th Intelligence Group, Nellis; face-to-face encounter with non-human-appearing entity at Nellis range, August 1994
- [[gary-mckinnon]] — found "non-terrestrial officers" list with ranks/names and "material transfer between ships" spreadsheet in US military databases
- [[david-grusch]] — Fox worked hard to portray him fairly; believes him completely

## Origin Story

Fox entered UFO research reluctantly in the early 1990s. A childhood friend, Renee Harris, was deep into Roswell. His boss at a San Francisco production facility, Richard Vanle, reframed it: "You haven't heard about Roswell?" Fox went from dismissal to conviction through research.

## Robertson Panel / Blue Book

Fox identifies the [[robertson-panel]]'s January 1953 policy of ridicule as the origin of the public dismissal reflex. The Robertson Panel was a CIA-originated committee that explicitly recommended systematic downplaying of UFOs through media, books, and movies. Fox calls it "the constitution for Blue Book." [[project-blue-book]] was a "dog and pony show"; a classified parallel program with far more funding and better scientists ran underground. [[jacques-vallee]] found a letter confirming this in Hynek's files — signed by H.C. Cross, Director of Exotic Metallurgy at [[battelle-memorial-institute]] — featured in [[the-phenomenon-fox]].

## Holloman AFB Investigation

Fox has spent years investigating the [[1964-holloman-afb-landing]] — an alleged 1960s landing of three disc-shaped craft at [[holloman-air-force-base]], New Mexico, where beings with Egyptian-style headgear met with military officers. His evidence chain includes: [[coral-lorenzen]]'s contemporaneous articles, [[bob-emenegger]] confirming the event happened, [[allan-sandler]] claiming to have seen the footage at [[norton-air-force-base]], and [[paul-charlton]]'s role processing the footage before it was confiscated. Fox conclusion: "I kind of believe it happened."

## Roger Leir and the Varginha Tapes

Around 2013, [[roger-leir]] contacted Fox urgently: "I know you're doing something on Varginha — I've got to talk to you." Fox kept postponing due to extreme workload. Leir gave Fox his Varginha-related tapes and then died before they could meet properly. Fox transferred the tapes, sent them back, and lost track of them. Around 2023–2024, while making the Varginha update, Fox recovered Leir's tapes and incorporated them into the new film.

## "Moment of Contact: New Revelations" (2025 updated cut)

A substantially revised and extended version of the 2022 documentary, structured to address mainstream skeptics. Key additions over the original:
- Condensed congressional hearings segment (Grusch, Bray, others) as framing — "this isn't the first time"
- Brief passes through Magenta (Italy), Roswell, Kecksburg, Kingman, Bentwaters
- **New witnesses in the Varginha section**: two forensic pathologists, chief of police, new crash witness ([[fabio-furtado]]), and [[italo-venturelli]] as the lead neurosurgeon
- All witnesses are on camera with name, face, and reputation — no anonymous testimony
- Flight path map showing cumulative sightings, drone footage of impact site and EZA approach route
- Venturelli's hospital walkthrough — physically standing in the room where the being was held

## Socorro Investigation

Fox is deeply invested in the [[1964-socorro]] case. He visited the National Archives with author Jay Sanford to retrieve original Blue Book documents including a military officer's footprint diagram of the beings. The case converted [[j-allen-hynek]] on CE3s and is geographically proximate to Holloman.

## Chuck Clark and the VHS Footage

During his first documentary, Fox worked with [[chuck-clark]], an Area 51 watchdog who showed Fox a VHS tape of an orange disc-shaped craft hovering and wobbling near Rachel, Nevada. Fox describes it as the best UAP footage he has ever seen in original state. After 26 years of chasing it, Clark cut Fox off permanently when Fox pushed one too many times. [[logan-paul]] attempted to buy the tape for ~$100K and was refused. A digital copy may exist in the Rachel, NV area per a local woman's hint to Fox.

## Buzz Aldrin / Larry King Live

Fox flew to the south of France to interview Aldrin for an early documentary; Aldrin stood him up at great personal cost to Fox. Years later, on Larry King Live with [[fife-symington]] discussing [[1997-phoenix-lights]], Fox watched Aldrin deny any UAP knowledge and describes it as "watching him lie on television." He tried to call him out but restrained himself at Symington's urging.

## Leslie Kean / Grusch Publishing Fight

Fox was present when the [[david-grusch]] story broke. [[leslie-kean]] attempted to publish it in the New York Times, where she is a staff writer. NYT journalist Julian Barnes, who has an anti-UAP bias, blocked it. The story ran in [[the-debrief]] instead, also nearly going to Politico.

## Disinformation Analysis

Fox suspects [[john-lear]] and [[bill-cooper]] were disinformation assets ("agent or useful idiot"). He frames the widely circulated Eisenhower-meets-aliens-at-Holloman story as deliberate disinfo adjacent to the real Holloman landing — "the best disinfo is slightly adjacent to the truth." The Rendlesham Forest book claiming alien contact is another example he cites.
