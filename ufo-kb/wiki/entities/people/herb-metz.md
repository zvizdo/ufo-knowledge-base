# Herb Metz

Parapsychologist who spent over a decade at the [[princeton-parapsychology-lab]] (PEAR), Princeton University, working under [[bob-jahn]] on random event generator (REG) experiments. Based in Princeton, New Jersey. Author of *The Selection Effect*. Interviewed by [[jesse-michaels]] in [[eQIMantuasQ]] alongside Stargate remote viewer [[paul-smith]].

## Random Event Generator Research

Metz's core experimental paradigm at PEAR:

**Setup**: An REG is tied to a provably quantum-random process — radioactive isotope decay, photon emission, or similar — that produces a binary output (1 or 0) at intervals. Over a large sample with no observer, the distribution of 1s and 0s converges to 50/50 within normal statistical deviation.

**Finding**: When an observer is present with a *set intention* to skew output toward 1s or toward 0s, the distribution shifts in the intended direction at **statistically significant rates**. PEAR's aggregate dataset, accumulated over 28 years, shows this effect is:
- Reproducible across operators, sessions, and REG device types
- Present even at physical distance from the device
- Directionally consistent: intend for 1s → get more 1s; intend for 0s → get more 0s; intend for even → get even

In [[eQIMantuasQ]], Metz summarizes the PEAR graph: "The final chart of the PEAR data is this beautiful graph that pretty definitively shows that the subjects were able to do what they were tasked to do."

## Experimenter Effect and Epistemological Implications

Metz raises a critical meta-issue: the observer effect extends to the experimenter. He cites [[gertrude-schmeidler]]'s "sheep and goats" finding: believers in psychic phenomena ("sheep") consistently produce better psi effects in controlled studies than skeptics ("goats"). This extends to experimenters: a skeptical experimenter (e.g., [[james-randi]] or Michael Shermer entering an REG study to debunk it) may themselves be suppressing the effect through their own intentional state.

This creates a fundamental epistemological problem: **a priori skepticism is self-refuting in psi research** because the skeptic's intentional state can become a confound. Metz frames this as: "We're living in a time where the system that regulated itself to keep its own integrity is harder and harder to maintain because it doesn't fit with a lot of what we're learning."

## Mind Lamp

Metz designed the **Mind Lamp** — a commercial REG device with an illuminated output that shifts through 8 colors probabilistically, the color being influenced by the underlying quantum-random generator. Psychologists purchased them to run in therapy offices during client sessions. In [[eQIMantuasQ]], Metz demonstrates with Jesse Michaels: after Michaels intends "green," the lamp changes to green within 30 seconds — a 2% base probability event (1/8 colors × likelihood of change within 30 seconds).

## TMS as Psi Amplifier

Metz hypothesizes that the brain maintains a **heavy self-regulatory process** that actively suppresses psi effects. He proposes that **transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS)** targeting specific inhibitory brain regions could reduce this suppression and create enhanced REG effects. This is an operationally testable prediction — one of the few concrete near-term experimental designs that could bridge neuroscience and parapsychology.

## Consciousness Theory

Metz endorses the non-local model of consciousness:

> "There are functions of mind that cannot be described purely by brain process — there is a causal activity that cannot be predicted from prior brain states."

He frames the PEAR data as "clear evidence that our brain-mind systems do things that are not in the realm of biology today."

He is optimistic about the trajectory of consciousness research: "Neuroscience is very brain-focused right now, but we will inevitably come to find that there are functions of mind that cannot be described purely by brain process."
