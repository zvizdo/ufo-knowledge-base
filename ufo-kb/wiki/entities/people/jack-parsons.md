# Jack Parsons

John "Jack" Parsons (1914–1952) is one of the foundational figures of American rocketry. He co-founded the **Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL)** alongside Frank Malina, Theodore von Kármán, and others, and made critical contributions to solid-fuel rocket propellant chemistry that underpins modern aerospace. He died in a laboratory explosion in 1952.

What makes Parsons remarkable — and relevant to the UFO/UAP knowledge graph — is his simultaneous deep involvement in Aleister Crowley's **Thelema** occult tradition. He led the **Agape Lodge** of the **Ordo Templi Orientis (OTO)** in Pasadena and conducted extensive magical workings, including the Babalon Working (1946), a ritual intended to summon a "Moonchild" or elemental entity. [[lue-elizondo]] notes that Parsons recited a Crowley hymn before rocket tests — a data point that Elizondo raises as part of a broader [[occult-aerospace-connection]] thesis.

## Background

Parsons grew up in Pasadena and was largely self-taught in rocketry. His Caltech-adjacent group (the "Suicide Squad") attracted serious scientists despite being outsiders, and their work directly seeded JPL. He corresponded with Crowley and was regarded as Crowley's most important American follower. His associate L. Ron Hubbard (later founder of Scientology) participated in some of his workings.

## Relevance to UAP Discourse

Elizondo and others raise Parsons as evidence that the founding layer of American aerospace development was not purely technocratic — that esoteric, non-mainstream epistemologies were present at the origin point of the US space program. Whether this constitutes a structural relationship or biographical coincidence is an open question captured in [[occult-aerospace-connection]].

His connection to [[wernher-von-braun]] is indirect: both worked in parallel tracks of US rocket development in the same postwar era, though von Braun came through [[operation-paperclip]] while Parsons was civilian/academic.

## Pasulka's Reading: Not Demonic, Working for the Common Task

[[diana-pasulka]] at Hereticon explicitly addresses a common misreading: "A lot of people say oh he was you know demonic but he didn't view what he was doing as demonic. He actually viewed what he was doing as good. He thought that he was working for the common task of humans of getting us into space."

This distinguishes Parsons from a Satanist in the popular sense — he understood himself as working *for* humanity's cosmic destiny, within a Greco-Roman/Thelemic framework that he treated as genuine cosmological contact, not performance.

This is the American equivalent of [[konstantin-tsiolkovsky]]'s Slavic-Christian cosmism: different theological framework, same foundational claim — that the space program is conducted in relationship with NHI, and that relationship gives it meaning. See [[cosmism]].

## The Golden Bough and "Science Is a Form of Magic"

[[chris-ramsey]] adds a significant new data point in [[h3u8EkGI8OQ]]: Parsons read **JG Frazer's *The Golden Bough*** (1890), the foundational 19th-century anthropological study of ritual magic, sympathetic magic, and homeopathic magic among indigenous tribes globally. Frazer observed that when those tribes encountered Enlightenment skepticism, their rituals **lost their power** — suggesting the efficacy of magic required a belief environment.

Parsons's response to the book: *"I believe that science is a form of magic. Magic is not a form of science."* This reverses the standard reductive relationship — rather than magic being pre-scientific superstition, science is the most modern instantiation of the impulse to control reality through systematic practice.

This framing connects to the **ritualistic pre-launch culture** observed at modern rocket companies:
- Crews at JPL, SpaceX, and NASA engage in small but consistent pre-launch rituals (specific clothes, positions, foods, lucky objects)
- **Gwen Shotwell** (SpaceX COO) reportedly wears special shoes for launches
- The precision required for rocket launches — any single component failure collapses the entire system — creates an environment where adding spiritual/ritual precision is intuitively appealing
- [[diana-pasulka]] has documented these ritualistic practices firsthand, noting that compartmentalized teams who cannot know each other's component roles nonetheless feel compelled to align their intentions as a system

The extension of the Parsons / Thelema occultism into modern rocket-company culture suggests this was not personal idiosyncrasy but the beginning of an institutional tradition in American aerospace.

## Additional Biographical Detail (zjpvfDFc4fg)

Parsons (born 1914 in Pasadena) was expelled from military school for blowing up a toilet block. As a teenager he and his friend **Ed Foreman** built homemade rockets from Foreman's father's workshop. His **JATO** (Jet-Assisted Take-Off) solid-fuel boosters had their first successful test in 1941; by 1942 the US military ordered 20,000 units/month. The team deliberately avoided the word "rocket" because it sounded too much like science fiction.

The **[[1946-babalon-working]]** was conducted with [[l-ron-hubbard]] as ritual partner. After the working, Hubbard ran off with Parsons' girlfriend and money. Parsons conducted a magical counterattack invoking **Bartabel** (spirit of Mars) in a Miami hotel room; Hubbard's yacht subsequently encountered a storm, and a court later forced repayment.

Per [[jacques-vallee]] (hearsay), there were rumors of a Mojave ritual where a **blonde woman appeared claiming to be from Venus** — potentially the first recorded Nordic-type NHI encounter, predating similar contactee stories by years.

Parsons was eventually **pushed out of JPL**. He died in a home laboratory explosion in 1952, officially ruled an accident with volatile chemicals, though some found circumstances suspicious. A **lunar crater now bears his name**. Some JPL insiders still call it "**Jack Parsons Laboratory**."
