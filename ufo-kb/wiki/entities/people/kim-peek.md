# Kim Peek

The real-life inspiration for the *Rain Man* character. Kim Peek had FG syndrome (not classic autism — his condition was later clarified as arising from hydrocephalus and an absent corpus callosum) and could recite word-for-word approximately 12,000 books, reading both pages simultaneously with each eye. He had a grossly enlarged head due to cerebrospinal fluid buildup and could barely hold it upright; his skull bones were slightly separated.

Cited in [[0qlppHc3-gg]] by [[diane-powell]] as the canonical case that standard neuroscience cannot explain: where does information of that density reside when the brain structure that conventionally houses it is absent? Powell uses this as a lever to introduce the [[consciousness-non-local]] "transmission" model — memory may reside outside the brain rather than being encoded in neural tissue.

## Neurological Profile

- No corpus callosum (the bundle of fibers connecting the two hemispheres)
- Hydrocephalus from birth: large cranium with significant cerebrospinal fluid pressure
- Read both pages of an open book simultaneously (left page with left eye, right page with right eye) and achieved near-perfect recall
- Could not perform basic activities of daily living

## Why This Matters for Non-Local Consciousness

Powell and [[ky-dickens]] frame Peek as evidence that the standard model of memory — information encoded in synaptic connections — is incomplete or wrong. If memory can exist without the usual neural substrate, the most coherent explanation is that the brain acts as a transducer or receiver (see [[consciousness-non-local]] transmission model), not as the storage medium.
