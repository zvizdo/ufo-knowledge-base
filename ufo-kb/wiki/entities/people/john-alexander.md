# John Alexander

Retired US Army Colonel who served in Special Forces, pursued graduate degrees in thanatology and human science, and developed the US Army's non-lethal weapons program at Los Alamos in the 1980s–90s. His UAP research work, conducted through the [[nids]] (National Institute for Discovery Science) and his own extensive network of cleared government contacts, produced a conclusion that runs against the dominant disclosure narrative in this KB.

## The "No Cabal" Conclusion

Alexander's central UAP finding, detailed in *UFOs: Myths, Conspiracies, and Realities* (2011): he systematically approached every senior government and military contact he could find with the explicit question "who runs the UAP program?" — and found no one. His conclusion is not that UAPs are not real (he believes they are), but that the widespread assumption of a tightly controlled cabal with full knowledge and recovered craft does not match his investigation.

This directly tensions with [[danny-sheehan]]'s S Force thesis and [[bob-maguire]]'s recovered-craft model. Alexander's explanation: the knowledge may be fragmented, the phenomenon may be beyond any single group's understanding, and the "cover-up" may be organizational dysfunction rather than coordination.

## NIDS Involvement

Alexander worked with [[robert-bigelow]]'s [[nids]] organization extensively — investigating [[skinwalker-ranch]] and participating in the broader NIDS research agenda. His military and cleared-community access gave him investigative reach that civilian researchers lack.

## NNSA Las Vegas / Wilson-Davis Facilitation (xnxasfyHtfo)

[[eric-davis]] clarifies in [[xnxasfyHtfo]] that the Wilson-Davis meeting chain ran through both Alexander and Davis's contacts at the **[[nnsa]] Las Vegas** office. Specifically, the NNSA Nevada site's Director of Intelligence and Director of Counter-Intelligence — both AFIO (Association of Former Intelligence Officers) members alongside Davis — were instrumental in facilitating the meeting. Alexander's NNSA Las Vegas connections complemented this facilitation chain.

This adds Alexander's role as a practical facilitator (not merely a conceptual figure) in the Wilson-Davis story, and places the NNSA Nevada office as a key node in that network.
