# Silas Newton

Wealthy Texas oil man, reportedly worth approximately $20 million at the time (~$200M in modern terms), and a semi-professional golfer. Primary whistleblower for the 1948 Aztec, New Mexico crash alongside Leo Gabbauer ("Dr. Gee"), as documented by journalist Frank Scully in *Behind the Flying Saucers* (1950).

Newton and Gabbauer were subsequently convicted of fraud over a claimed "doodlebug" magnetic oil-detection device. [[uap-gerb]], Scott and Suzanne Ramsey argue the conviction was a deliberate setup: a different device was substituted into the court case, and multiple witnesses testified to the doodlebug's genuine efficacy in finding oil deposits. Newton's wealth provides no obvious motivation for fraud.

Newton's personal diaries referenced Wilbur B. Smith (Canadian radio engineer, confirmed by Robert Sarbacher that UFOs were classified higher than the Manhattan Project) and confirm cross-connections in the post-WWII UFO retrieval network.
