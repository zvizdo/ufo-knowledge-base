# Bob Jahn

Princeton University's Dean of Engineering (plasma propulsion systems; several still used in space) who in 1979 founded the **Princeton Engineering Anomalies Research (PEAR)** lab — see [[princeton-parapsychology-lab]] — and co-authored *Margins of Reality* with Brenda Dunne. He ran the lab for 28 years.

Jahn came from the era of early quantum experimenters who believed reality was wide open to exploration; he expected that well-controlled experiments would be treated like any other science. He was wrong — the reception was "atrocious." Jesse Michaels has an anonymous source (now running a multi-billion dollar hedge fund) who helped run the PEAR lab and vouches personally for the data's integrity.

## Random Event Generator Experiments

PEAR's signature experiment: a random event generator (REG) tied to a quantum-mechanical process (radioactive decay, photon interference, electron tunneling) produces a perfectly even distribution of 1s and 0s over large samples. An observer with an *intention* to skew the output toward 1s or 0s can produce statistically significant deviations. The deviation is small but reproducible over thousands of trials.

Jahn's own model (from *Margins of Reality*): waves emanating from the mind help collapse the wave function. This is the parapsychological analog of [[roger-penrose]]'s Orch OR — both assign causal weight to consciousness in quantum measurement. [[rupert-sheldrake]] agrees with this framing and extends it to vision (the extramission model).

Jahn's summary of findings (quoted in episode): "The human mind and the information-processing machines seem to be entering into some sort of a dialogue which influences both the machine and the human mind."

## Relevance to Simulation Theory

Within [[riz-virk]]'s client-server consciousness model, PEAR REG results are direct evidence that individual minds (clients) can influence the shared rendering substrate (server). If reality is informational, then mind-matter interaction is not anomalous — it is the expected behavior of a conscious node interfacing with its own simulation layer.
