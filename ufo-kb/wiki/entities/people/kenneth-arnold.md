# Kenneth Arnold

Private pilot and businessman who made the most famous UFO sighting of the 20th century — the origin event of the term "flying saucer" and the beginning of the modern UFO era.

## The 1947 Mount Rainier Sighting

On June 24, 1947 — approximately two months before the [[1947-roswell]] crash — Arnold was flying a CallAir A-2 near Mount Rainier, Washington, searching for a missing Marine Corps C-46 transport aircraft. At approximately 3:00 PM, he observed nine bright, disc-like objects flying in a V-formation at an estimated speed of 1,200 mph (far exceeding any known aircraft of the era).

His description to a reporter was that they *"flew like a saucer would if you skipped it across water"* — describing their **movement**, not their **shape**. The reporter condensed this to "flying saucer." The objects were not saucer-shaped; they were crescent- or heel-shaped. Arnold later noted the irony that the most famous term in UFO history was a misquote.

## Significance

[[jesse-michaels]] cites Arnold in [[kRO5jOa06Qw]] to make a specific epistemological point: **the popular image of UFOs as round flying saucers did not originate in science fiction and then filter into witnesses' reports**. The causation ran the other way. Arnold's real observation — crescent-shaped objects skipping like a thrown saucer — was distorted by press into "flying saucers," which then entered popular culture, which then *shaped* how subsequent witnesses reported their own encounters (the analytical overlay thesis, also referenced in the Betty and Barney Hill context). The fiction reflected and amplified the real phenomenon, not vice versa.

This point is also made by [[jacques-vallee]] — that the same underlying phenomenon has been reported throughout history but described in the idiom of each era (angels, fairies, mystery airships, flying saucers, Tic-Tacs). Arnold's sighting is the pivot moment where the modern "analytical overlay" crystallized into public vocabulary.
