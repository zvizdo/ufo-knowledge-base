# Lewis Whitten

## Background

Lewis (Louis) Whitten was a physicist at the Martin Corporation's anti-gravity division in the late 1950s. Martin Corporation (now part of Lockheed Martin) maintained a formal anti-gravity research division during the 1950s — a largely forgotten chapter of aerospace history that the mainstream physics community has largely ignored.

Whitten was contracted by **Wright Air Field** (Wright-Patterson Air Force Base) to work on gravity research. This makes Wright-Patterson a direct funding source for anti-gravity work in the private aerospace sector — consistent with the broader picture that the most exotic physics was being developed under military contracts rather than in open academic settings.

## 1957 UNC Chapel Hill Conference

Whitten attended the historic 1957 **Gravity Research Conference** at the University of North Carolina, Chapel Hill — organized by Agnew Bahnson (a local industrialist who funded the conference along with Roger Field). This conference is significant in the history of suppressed physics:

- It brought together leading physicists working on quantum gravity
- [[herman-bondi]] asked the provocative question about removing the positivity conditions in Einstein's equations — which yields negative mass/energy and opens up warp-drive solution space
- After the conference, researchers in the anti-gravity/electrogravitics direction largely went silent

The conference occurred simultaneously with Bahnson separately funding [[thomas-townsend-brown]] — suggesting a deliberate parallel structure: mainstream theoretical physics (the conference) and experimental anti-gravity (Brown's work) being run in tandem.

## Bismuth and Townsend Brown

Whitten stated that Brown had "a weird isotope of bismuth that exhibited anti-gravity properties" in his capacitor experiments. Brown used bismuth as a high-K dielectric in high-voltage capacitor configurations. Whitten's report connects directly to the modern topological insulator hypothesis ([[bob-maguire]]) and [[garry-nolan]]'s bismuth samples.

The bismuth thread spans:
- Brown's 1950s experiments (high-K dielectric)
- Whitten's testimony (anti-gravity properties)
- Nolan's samples (magnesium-bismuth materials of unknown origin)
- Pais's patents (topological insulators as the material science substrate)

## Going Dark

After the 1957 conference, Whitten — like most anti-gravity researchers of that era — published nothing further on gravity. [[bob-maguire]]'s interpretation: researchers who were getting somewhere were stopped; the government funded string theory as an absorber of talent and misdirector of research; those who pushed in productive directions saw their work captured or their careers ended.
