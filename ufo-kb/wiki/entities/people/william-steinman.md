# William Steinman

## Background

William Steinman was a UFO researcher active in the 1980s who became interested in the mystery of **Dr. [[eric-wang]]** — head of Special Studies at [[wright-patterson]] Air Force Base — after Wang's name appeared alongside [[arthur-stansel]]'s in accounts of the [[1953-kingman-crash]].

## Investigation

Steinman spent months attempting to verify Wang's background, finding almost no trace: no employment records, no published research, no accessible biographical detail despite Wang's name being attached to a facility at the University of New Mexico. After following a trail through obscure archival records, he found a brief obituary in an old issue of a mechanical engineering magazine establishing that Wang had been born in Vienna in 1906 and graduated from the Vienna Technical Institute.

Despite reportedly being warned off the trail, Steinman took a different approach: he located and contacted **Maria Wang**, Eric Wang's widow. Her disclosures to Steinman constitute the most direct claimed link between [[henry-kissinger]] and active UAP program management:
- Eric worked on classified projects involving technology not of this world.
- He reported directly to Kissinger.
- Kissinger was deeply involved in the flying saucer program.
- Kissinger had visited their home on occasion.

## The Eric Walker Encounter

Steinman and a colleague (possibly Scott Crane) visited **[[eric-walker]] at Penn State** to ask about crash retrievals. Walker's response:
- Called them **"Don Quixote"** — dismissing the inquiry as chasing windmills
- Asked: *"What do you know about the sixth sense? What do you know about psi?"*
- Told them, in effect: until you know about that, you don't know anything about this

This was not outright denial — it was a redirection suggesting a **psi/parapsychology prerequisite** for insider UAP knowledge. Walker's pattern with Steinman, [[grant-cameron]], and [[kit-green]] (who was kicked out) is consistent: he had knowledge, refused to share it, and in Steinman's case indicated the barrier was conceptual rather than security-level.
