# Stanley Pons

American electrochemist at the University of Utah. With [[martin-fleischmann]], he announced in March 1989 that their palladium/deuterium electrolysis experiment had produced anomalous excess heat — more energy out than electrical energy in — suggesting a low-energy nuclear reaction (cold fusion) was occurring at room temperature.

## The 1989 Announcement

The press conference preceded peer review, generating enormous media coverage. The claim triggered an international replication rush; results were mixed and the mainstream physics community largely concluded the effect was not reproducible or was a measurement artifact. Pons and Fleischmann relocated to France (IMRA labs, funded by Toyota) to continue research.

## Role in Suppressed-Science Narrative

In the UFO/alternative-physics community, the cold fusion controversy is frequently cited as an example of [[free-energy-suppression]] — institutional suppression of anomalous energy findings with potential for disrupting established energy economics. [[giuliano-preparata]] and [[emilio-del-giudice]] provided theoretical frameworks attempting to explain the excess-heat phenomenon.
