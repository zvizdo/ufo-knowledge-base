# Buzz Aldrin

Apollo 11 lunar module pilot; second human to walk on the Moon on July 20, 1969. Decorated USAF fighter pilot and Korean War veteran. In UFO/UAP discourse he appears in two contexts: (1) his Freemason membership and the Masonic flag he carried to the Moon, and (2) the broader pattern — alongside [[edgar-mitchell]] and [[gordon-cooper]] — of Apollo-era astronauts being cited as having knowledge of or encounters with non-human intelligence.

## Background

Aldrin earned a Sc.D. from MIT and is a **33rd-degree Scottish Rite Mason**. He carried a **Masonic silk flag** (hand-stitched with the Scottish Rite symbol, now on display at the Masonic Temple in Washington DC) to the Moon on Apollo 11. The Apollo 11 mission even established an appendant body of the Masonic Lodge on the Moon according to the Masonic Grand Lodge of Ohio.

More significantly: just before the first moonwalk, Aldrin performed a **private communion ceremony** on the lunar surface using a small silver chalice, wine, and bread he had brought specifically for this purpose. He recalled "the wine curling slowly and gracefully up the side of the cup." He has noted that the first liquid ever poured on the Moon was communion wine.

The 33rd-degree rank is symbolically tied to the 33 vertebrae of the spine and the crown chakra as a gateway to divine insight. He carried a Masonic flag to the Moon; this has been referenced by researchers connecting the early space program to esoteric traditions, intersecting with the [[occult-aerospace-connection]] thread.

## Larry King Live Incident

[[james-fox]] provides a firsthand account: Fox had previously arranged to interview Aldrin in the south of France for an early documentary, at significant personal and financial effort. Aldrin stood him up. Years later, Fox was booked on Larry King Live alongside Aldrin and [[fife-symington]] to discuss the [[1997-phoenix-lights]]. Fox photographed Aldrin in the green room getting makeup applied (over his handler's objections — "No pictures, sir" — Fox took the photo anyway). On air, Fox watched Aldrin deny any UAP connection: "I watched him lie on television and it was really frustrating." Fox tried to call him out; [[fife-symington]] urged restraint afterwards.

> ⚠ Conflict: Aldrin's public denial of UAP knowledge on Larry King stands against the pattern documented elsewhere of Apollo-era astronauts having significant UAP exposure. Fox's firsthand characterization ("lie on television") is one witness's interpretation, not verified independently.
