# Kelly Johnson

Founder and long-time director of [[lockheed-skunk-works]], the classified advanced development division of Lockheed. Johnson is credited with designing some of America's most significant classified aircraft, including the U-2 spy plane and the SR-71 Blackbird — the latter remaining the fastest jet-powered aircraft ever built, even after its retirement in 1990.

## Anti-Stealth Position

According to [[nick-cook]]'s account (corroborated by [[ben-rich]]'s own statements), Johnson was anti-stealth toward the end of his career, while Rich was strongly pro-stealth. Rich describes how the Skunk Works was divided on whether to pursue stealth radar-baffling technology at all — at one point even contemplating "burying" the stealth concept entirely (similar to the way nuclear physics discussion was suppressed after the Manhattan Project), rather than letting it be known that such a capability was being developed.

Rich ultimately prevailed, and the F-117 stealth aircraft was the result.

## Historical Significance

Johnson's Skunk Works management philosophy ("build a little, test a little, learn a lot") and his personal engineering genius set the template for how America's most sensitive classified aircraft programs were run. His successors — including Rich, [[jack-gordon]], and [[robert-weiss]] — operated within the institutional culture he created.
