# Bill Sweetman

Aviation journalist at [[janes-defence-weekly]], close collaborator of [[nick-cook]] in tracking classified (black-world) aircraft programs in the 1980s and 1990s. Cook describes Sweetman as "a kind of Sherlock Holmes" who would piece together clues that the black world left — or had not intended to leave — in public documents, procurement records, and engineering data.

## Aurora Investigation

Sweetman's most significant contribution was building the evidentiary case for the [[aurora-aircraft-program]] — the alleged classified Mach-5 replacement for the SR-71 Blackbird. His methodology involved:
- Engineering analysis of performance data from what the NASP (National Aerospace Plane / X-30) program was channeling into the public domain
- Procurement and budget anomalies (a ~$2 billion line item in a late-1980s defense budget attributed to an unspecified program)
- Cross-referencing the North Sea sighting by [[chris-gibson]] (Royal Observer Corps) with the engineering profile of a hypersonic aircraft being tanked mid-air by a Boeing KC-135
- The conviction that [[nasp-program]] (Reagan's "Orient Express") was a white-world technology-development cover for Aurora's scramjet, materials, and propulsion work

Sweetman ran a major story on Aurora in Jane's Defence Weekly alongside Cook.

## NASP-as-Cover Thesis

Sweetman argued that the National Aerospace Plane program, officially aimed at 45-minute Washington-to-Tokyo commercial flight by the X-30, functioned primarily as a classified-to-public technology funnel for Aurora. When NASP was cancelled publicly, Sweetman believed its real outputs had already been absorbed into the classified Aurora program.
