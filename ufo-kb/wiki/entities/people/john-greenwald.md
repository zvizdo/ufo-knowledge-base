# John Greenwald Jr.

Founder of [[black-vault]], the largest private FOIA (Freedom of Information Act) archive of government documents. Greenwald has submitted tens of thousands of FOIA requests over decades, making him the most systematic civilian documentarian of US government UAP secrecy. His work is methodologically distinct from whistleblower-based journalism: he relies on official document release pathways, and his findings are therefore less susceptible to the "circular reporting" problem.

## Background

Greenwald occupies an unusual position in the UAP discourse: he is neither an NHI advocate nor a conventional skeptic. He acknowledges that some UAP cases have no prosaic explanation while refusing to endorse the NHI hypothesis. [[michael-shellenberger]] describes him as "neither fish nor fowl" — a figure underappreciated in both camps. Shellenberger highlights Greenwald's work as foundational because it demonstrates institutional stonewalling through official channels, not just whistleblower testimony.

## Key Findings

### Secrecy Is Growing
Greenwald's most significant meta-finding: based on FOIA response patterns, classification levels, and document redaction scope, UAP secrecy is **not declining** toward disclosure — it is **increasing**. This directly challenges the common disclosure-movement narrative that the government is gradually releasing information. See [[disclosure-narratives]].

### Pentagon Inconsistency
The Pentagon stated after initial video releases that only three UAP videos existed (FLIR1, Gimbal, GoFast). When Greenwald pressed further, a different Pentagon branch acknowledged additional videos exist — but refused to release them under national security grounds. Shellenberger summarizes: "They'll say one thing on Monday and say something else on Friday."

### Law Enforcement Exemption
At least one FOIA denial cited a **law enforcement exemption** rather than the expected national security exemption. This is a legally significant anomaly: it implies there is a classified law enforcement investigation into UAP-related activity — the existence and nature of which is itself a secret. Congressional members should at minimum be informed of such investigations.

### Fast Walkers FOIA Denied
Greenwald's FOIA request for documents on [[fast-walkers]] (objects transiting Earth's outer defense detection layer, tracked by the NORAD Defense Support Program) was denied by NORAD on grounds of extreme sensitivity. The same term appears in a Wikileaks email between aerospace contractor [[bob-fish]] and [[john-podesta]], suggesting the topic bridges public-facing and classified domains.

### Redaction Scope
Greenwald has documented the government redacting basic administrative facts about UAP programs — how long they've been collecting data, how many reports they hold, what UAPs might be — as though the government doesn't want to legitimize specific hypotheses by acknowledging it has considered them.
