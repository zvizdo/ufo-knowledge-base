# Julian Jaynes

## Background

Princeton/Yale psychologist, author of *The Origin of Consciousness in the Breakdown of the Bicameral Mind* (1976). His central thesis — that ancient humans operated with a bicameral (two-chambered) mind in which the right hemisphere generated commands experienced as gods' voices, while the left hemisphere obeyed — was widely ridiculed but has seen a revival of serious academic interest.

## Notable Claims

Pre-1000 BC, humans did not have introspective self-consciousness as modern people experience it. What they described as hearing gods or divine beings was a literal neurological phenomenon — auditory hallucinations generated by the right hemisphere. Stress-triggers, oracular states, and ritual intoxicants could induce these voices. The Bronze Age Collapse (~1200 BC) forced the transition to modern unified consciousness, leaving echoes in religious structures, oracular traditions (Delphi), and the Hebrew Bible's arc from direct divine speech to prophets to silence.
