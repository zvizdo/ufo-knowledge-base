# Michael Rolando Richards

African-American sculptor based in New York. His entire body of work was obsessed with themes of **flight and martyrdom**. His most famous sculpture, *Tar Baby vs. St. Sebastian*, depicts Richards himself standing vertically and being impaled by airplanes — a self-portrait as a martyr pierced by aircraft, deliberately echoing the Medieval saint Sebastian traditionally depicted transfixed by arrows.

## The Sculpture and Its Context

*Tar Baby vs. St. Sebastian* drew on the iconography of St. Sebastian — a 3rd-century Roman soldier martyred by arrows — and transposed it onto the body of a Black American aviator figure (the Tuskegee Airmen tradition). The sculpture was recognized as major work; on the strength of it (and his broader portfolio) Richards was awarded **studio space in the Twin Towers** by the **Lower Manhattan Cultural Council**, which ran a program granting studio residencies to cohorts of ~15 artists for six-month periods.

## September 10–11, 2001

Of the cohort of artists sharing Twin Tower studio space at the time, Richards alone chose to **stay in the studio overnight on September 10, 2001**. He was working through the night. He was killed on the morning of September 11.

## Significance as a Precognition Case

Richards's case is cited in [[RofQnByLwOo]] as one of the most eerie modern precognition examples:

- The sculpture (depicting himself impaled by airplanes as a martyr) preceded his death by impalement in a building struck by airplanes
- The self-referential precision — not just "a person" but himself, not just "weapons" but planes specifically — goes beyond thematic coincidence
- He was the only artist among a full cohort to remain in the building that night

The case is offered not as proof of deliberate precognition but as an instance of the unconscious [[quantum-brain-precognition]] signal pattern Wargo argues the brain emits: the future knowledge state leaking backward into creative output without the artist consciously recognizing its prophetic content.

## Additional Works (hXYdkcv5TtY)

In [[hXYdkcv5TtY]], Wargo adds further detail about Richards' other works in progress at the time of his death, which visitors to his studio witnessed:

- A sculpture of Richards **riding a burning meteor**
- *Fallen Angel* — Richards' **torso with wings**, the imagery of a fallen or martyred aviator-angel

The accumulation of flight, martyrdom, and crash imagery across his body of work — not just the famous *Tar Baby vs. St. Sebastian* — reinforces the precognitive interpretation. It was not a single unusual choice but a sustained creative preoccupation.
