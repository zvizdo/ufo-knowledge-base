# Monica Resza

Materials scientist and co-inventor of the **[[mandeloy-alloy]]** — a family of nickel-based superalloys that solved one of the most persistent problems in advanced rocket propulsion. She disappeared June 22, 2025 on a hiking trail in the San Gabriel Mountains and has not been found.

## Background

Resza worked in one of the most demanding corners of propulsion science: high-pressure, oxygen-rich rocket engines. The core problem was metallurgical: alloys strong enough to hold engine components together in extreme environments would combust; alloys that didn't combust were structurally inadequate. This forced the US to rely on Russia's RD-180 engine for sensitive national security launches during and after the Cold War.

Working with [[dallas-hardwick]] at the Rockwell Science Center in the 1990s, Resza co-engineered a nickel-based alloy — **Mandaloy** (named by fusing the first three letters of each co-inventor's name) — that survived the crushing pressure of oxygen-rich combustion without fracturing or burning.

Key milestones:
- **1990s** — Mandeloy developed at Rockwell Science Center
- **1999** — [[afrl]] begins co-funding their work; the same AFRL later commanded by [[neil-mccasland]] (2011–2013)
- **Mandeloy 100 and 200** — scaled into a family of alloys, each calibrated for different temperature/pressure conditions
- **Mandeloy → AR1 engine** — used in the kerosene-fueled AR1 engine (Rocket Dine / Aerojet Rocketdyne), the US-built replacement for Russia's RD-180; national security critical

The development partnership ran between AFRL and Resza's team at Pratt & Whitney Rocketdyne (later Aerojet Rocketdyne). Dallas Hardwick was embedded at Wright-Patterson's materials directorate until her retirement in 2012 — the same period McCasland was preparing to take command.

## Disappearance

**Date:** June 22, 2025  
**Location:** Near Mount Waterman, Angeles National Forest, Los Angeles County, California

Resza was hiking with friends. She was 30 feet behind them on the trail — and then she was gone. Search and rescue teams scoured the area for 8 days by land and air; the only item recovered was her beanie (tucked into her pack's hip belt in photographs from that morning), found ~400 yards off the trail.

After 8 days, per standard protocol, the synthetic aperture radar mission concluded and the case was handed to the homicide bureau / missing person's unit. Volunteer groups (including expert mountain rescue teams) continued searching for months. The volunteer Facebook group posted its last entry November 18, 2025 — 150 days after her disappearance — still planning a recovery expedition.

No mountain lion den or bear den was found in the area. Civilian searchers who descended the ravine nearest her last known location described the terrain as steep but not fatally so.

**What she had:** backpack, hiking boots, hiking pants, water; unclear whether she had her phone.

No remains, no scent trail, no confirmed sightings.

## The Mandeloy-McCasland Connection

The coincidence structurally noted in the source:
- Resza co-invented Mandeloy under AFRL contracts
- McCasland commanded AFRL at Wright-Patterson when Mandeloy 200 was being co-developed
- Dallas Hardwick (Resza's co-inventor) was embedded at Wright-Patterson's materials directorate through 2012 — when McCasland arrived
- Both Resza and McCasland have now disappeared within months of each other

Whether this connection is causal, coincidental, or simply reflects the small world of classified propulsion research is unresolved.

## Exotic Metallurgy Context

Some researchers connect Mandeloy to the broader lineage of **exotic nickel alloys** with roots in Wright-Patterson's Batelle Memorial Institute contract of 1949 (studying nickel-titanium alloys just two years after the Roswell crash). [[nitinol]] followed in 1959 at the Naval Ordnance Laboratory. Mandeloy has only ~1–4% titanium vs. Nitinol's ~45%, placing it in a different metallurgical neighborhood — but the clustering of nickel-based exotic alloys emerging from the same research ecosystem raises questions in UAP discourse that the source declines to definitively answer.

Resza's own patents cite Hastelloy X-214 and Monel alloy K500 as antecedents — not overtly exotic lineages. But the broader pattern of her research occurring within the same institutional ecosystem as McCasland and Roswell debris remains notable.
