# Hyman Rickover

## Background

Hyman G. Rickover (1900–1986) was the "Father of the Nuclear Navy" — the admiral who drove the development of nuclear-powered submarines and surface ships in the United States. He is one of the most consequential figures in 20th-century US military history.

## The Townsend Brown Exchange

[[bob-maguire]] (citing an unspecified source but presenting as established knowledge in the UAP research community) describes an exchange between [[thomas-townsend-brown]] and Rickover:

> "Townsend Brown went to Hyman Rickover, who was head of the Nuclear Navy in the late '50s, early '60s, and he was like 'look, I'm working on these — I'm getting all these crazy gravitational effects.' And Rickover goes, 'Oh, don't waste your time — we're already on it.'"

This exchange is significant:
1. Brown was showing Rickover his anti-gravity / electrogravitic research results — not fringe speculation but demonstrable experimental effects.
2. Rickover's response — "we're already on it" — implies the **US Navy had a classified anti-gravity program** already underway in the late 1950s or early 1960s.
3. This connects directly to why the Navy is the institutional home for [[salvatore-pais]]'s patents decades later — the Navy's anti-gravity interest is long-standing, not a recent development.

## Implications

If the Navy knew about and was working on anti-gravity in the late 1950s — the same period as the 1957 UNC Chapel Hill conference, [[lewis-whitten]]'s work at Martin Corporation, and the broader electrogravitics research boom — then the apparent collapse of public anti-gravity research after that period was not a failure of the science but a capture of it.

The Rickover exchange also places the Navy's UAP interest in a different light: the Navy didn't discover UAP propulsion physics via the 2004 Nimitz encounter. They had been working on it for at least 50 years.
