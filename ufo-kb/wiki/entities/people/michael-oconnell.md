# Michael O'Connell

A Harvard-trained psychiatrist who trained directly under [[john-mack]] — the head of Harvard's psychiatry department who became the most credentialed mainstream researcher to take alien abduction reports seriously before his 2004 death. O'Connell continued Mack's practical clinical work with experiencers, carrying the torch in the South Carolina–North Carolina region before his own death (confirmed deceased by the time of this interview, 2026).

## Work with Chris Bledsoe

O'Connell worked with [[chris-bledsoe]] for years to gradually recover memories of Bledsoe's foundational 4-hour missing-time event on January 8, 2007. His core clinical insight:

> *"They pack a lot of information in your mind and it's locked away. If you remembered it all at once, it would probably drive you crazy. We're going to program you so it comes out a little at a time."*

Before treatment, Bledsoe experienced severe headaches and would **pass out** every time he tried to think past the missing-time threshold. Within days of O'Connell beginning work with him, Bledsoe began having nightmares, speaking in his sleep, and narrating his experiences while family filmed him. The memories emerged as a *"virtual tour"* — beginning with an overhead vision of Egypt: pyramids, the Sphinx, Hathor's temple, animals and birds. Bledsoe then spent years studying Egyptian symbolism with scholars including [[robert-gilbert]].

## Significance

O'Connell represents the continuation of Mack's clinical lineage into the active generation of experiencer researchers. His proximity to South Carolina placed him geographically close to Bledsoe's North Carolina location; they visited frequently. His death closes off one of the few therapeutic pathways specifically adapted to the missing-time/abduction experiencer population that Mack had trained.
