# Glenn Gaffney

Glenn Gaffney served as **Director of Science and Technology at the CIA** — the agency's apex position for technical collection and advanced research. He is currently on the board of [[aaro]] (All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office), the DoD's UAP research body.

## Eric Davis's Firsthand Account (LnAiNChnuEQ)

[[eric-davis]] provides a **firsthand, classified-setting account** of Gaffney's intervention in [[LnAiNChnuEQ]] — the most specific insider account of this event in the KB:

During the AAWSAP period, BASS/EarthTech (operating under DIA auspices) attempted to get a defense contractor (identified in context as [[lockheed-martin]]) to share crash retrieval materials they had held since before the 1989 program shutdown. The company's senior VP and executive VP had direct interactions with Gaffney over this.

Davis describes Gaffney's reaction:

1. **Surprised**: "He was surprised that that company still retained materials that when they were supposed to give it up and return it back in '89."
2. **Antagonistic**: Gaffney was "very antagonistic" about the company releasing materials to BASS/EarthTech under DIA.
3. **Decisive refusal**: "His answer… absolutely not we're not going to do that."

The reason given: BASS/EarthTech held TSSI clearance but not SAP-level access, and the material was SAP-classified. [[harry-reid]] was simultaneously attempting to get SAP elevation for AAWSAP through the wrong channel (via [[william-lynn]]), which would have been needed to authorize the transfer.

> ⚠ This is Davis's firsthand account of working in the AAWSAP ecosystem. It upgrades the existing Gaffney entry from "alleged under oath in congressional testimony" (secondhand via Jesse Michaels) to a first-person insider account.

## Key Allegation (Congressional Testimony)

During [[ATJwqp5twAg]], [[jesse-michaels]] cites testimony given under oath to Congress: Gaffney **blocked the transfer of UAP material from Lockheed Martin to Bigelow Aerospace**. This is a specific operational claim — not a general statement about UAP awareness — and was reportedly made under oath.

[[ralph-moat-larson]] says he did not know about this. He notes that the CIA's Science and Technology Directorate operated independently of the intelligence side he managed — "most of what they do, I had no reason to be brought into." This is consistent with the compartmentalization model in which the Director of Intelligence and the Director of Science and Technology have parallel but separate access to sensitive programs.

If accurate, the allegation implies:
1. UAP material was held or managed by Lockheed Martin at some point
2. Bigelow Aerospace (Robert Bigelow's company, which held the AATIP contract via BAASS) was in a position to receive it
3. A CIA official at the S&T apex exercised authority to stop the transfer

> ⚠ Conflict: This claim is secondhand in the interview (Jesse citing congressional testimony); primary source not verified in this KB.
