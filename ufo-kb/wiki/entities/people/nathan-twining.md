# Nathan Twining

US Air Force General who commanded Air Materiel Command in 1947 and later served as Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff (1957–1960). He is most significant in the UAP record as the author of the [[twining-memo]], a September 1947 internal assessment that formally concluded UAP were real, not imaginary or misidentified aircraft, and listed their observed characteristics.

## Notable Claims

### The Twining Memo (1947)
In response to a request from Brigadier General George Schulgen, Twining's memo described UAP as exhibiting:
- Metallic or light-reflecting construction
- Absence of trail except in certain conditions
- Circular or elliptical shape, flat on bottom, domed on top
- High maneuverability and ability to form groups
- **Evasiveness when approached** — specifically noted as a consistent behavioral characteristic

The evasiveness point is cited by [[marik-von-rennenkampff]] in [[1iaH1a3A4Lk]] as historical documentation supporting the claim that UAP have consistently avoided closer examination — predating by 77 years Mick West's "crappy video problem" argument.

## Historical Significance

As both Air Materiel Command chief and later Chairman of the Joint Chiefs, Twining occupied positions with maximum access to UAP-related intelligence. His 1947 assessment carries institutional weight precisely because it concludes the phenomenon is real while coming from the highest Air Force command level. [[project-blue-book]] and the [[condon-commission]] came later; the Twining Memo preceded them and was more direct.
