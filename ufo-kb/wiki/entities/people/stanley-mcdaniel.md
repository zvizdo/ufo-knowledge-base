# Stanley McDaniel

Philosopher and author of *The McDaniel Report* (1993), a 200-page analysis of NASA's handling of alleged anomalous structures in the Cydonia region of Mars — the "Face on Mars" mesa, the Cydonia pyramids, and related landforms first photographed by Viking 1 in 1976. McDaniel's background in philosophy (with emphasis on critical thinking and scientific method) informed a methodological critique rather than a direct claim that the structures are artificial.

**The McDaniel Report**: McDaniel's argument was that NASA failed to conduct a proper scientific investigation, not necessarily that the structures were artificial. His specific criticisms: (1) NASA's 1976 dismissal was based on a single low-resolution image insufficient for the conclusion drawn; (2) subsequent higher-resolution imaging was not prioritized; (3) public communications used dismissive rhetoric inconsistent with the evidence; (4) the institutional process for evaluating anomalies was systematically biased toward null results. The report attracted attention from scientists who felt NASA's handling had been scientifically inadequate.

**McMoneagle citation**: [[joseph-mcmoneagle]] cites McDaniel in the context of Mars anomaly research. McMoneagle himself claimed to have remote-viewed the Martian surface during the [[stargate-program]] — a set of sessions that, if accurate, would constitute independent anomalous corroboration for structures in the Cydonian region.
