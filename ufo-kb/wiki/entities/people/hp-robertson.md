# H.P. Robertson

Caltech physicist and CIA science advisor. The non-coincidental continuity of his career: **he was the U.S. scientist assigned to investigate [[1942-foo-fighters]] during WWII**, and then 7 years later was specifically chosen to chair the CIA's [[robertson-panel]] in January 1953 — the classified review that created the mandate for [[project-blue-book]] to explain away and minimize UFO sightings.

[[kirk-mcconnell]] and [[jesse-michaels]] explicitly note this is "not a coincidence": the man who classified what Foo Fighters were (or weren't) was then appointed to create the institutional architecture for UAP debunking.

## WWII Foo Fighter Investigation

Robertson was **assigned by the US intelligence services** to investigate the Foo Fighter phenomenon — the anomalous luminous objects repeatedly observed by Allied fighter pilots over Germany and the Pacific (1942–1945). The Allied command was deeply concerned these might represent German secret weapons technology.

[[luis-alvarez]] was also part of this investigation alongside Robertson. Both men subsequently appeared on the 1953 Robertson Panel — the continuity spanning the WWII investigation to peacetime suppression.

What Robertson's wartime investigation actually found — and how much it informed the 1953 Panel's mandate — remains classified.

## Atomic Program Connections

Robertson had deep ties to American nuclear weapons programs that rarely appear in accounts of the Robertson Panel:

- Served on the National Defense Research Council (NDRC) under FDR alongside [[vannevar-bush]]
- Was embedded in the same atomic weapons oversight infrastructure that overlapped with early UAP classification
- The Robertson Panel preceded the formation of the full [[project-blue-book]] bureaucracy by months and set its public-facing mandate

This creates a structural pattern noted by [[jesse-michaels]]: the people who marginalized UAP as "quacky" were themselves among the atomic scientists and officials who had the most reason to suppress it. See the parallel case of [[edward-condon]].

## The Robertson Panel Mandate

The panel's classified memo (1952–53) established that:
1. UFO reports posed no direct military threat
2. Public interest in UFOs was a security concern (mass attention could clog military communication channels)
3. The Air Force should actively debunk reports and discourage serious investigation

This mandate — effectively instructing Blue Book to explain away rather than investigate — is the institutional foundation of the "physics-lockdown" applied to UAP research through the 1960s.
