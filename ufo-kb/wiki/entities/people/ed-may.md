# Ed May

Parapsychologist; took over leadership of the [[stargate-program]]'s research arm from [[hal-puthoff]], directing it through approximately its final decade until official declassification in 1995. May does not believe the program actually ended — he suspects classified psychic research continued.

## Decision Augmentation Theory

May's primary theoretical contribution, cited by [[eric-wargo]] in [[hXYdkcv5TtY]]:

**The problem**: Decades of experiments show subjects can reportedly influence random number generators (RNGs) with their minds — *micro-PK* (micro psychokinesis). This seems to imply consciousness directly affects physical outcomes.

**May's alternative**: Researcher **precognition** — not subject PK — drives the results. The researcher unconsciously perceives which trial start-times will generate random outcomes in the desired direction, and begins the trial at those moments. No mind-over-matter occurs; instead, the experimenter is retrocausally navigating to favorable start-points.

May's term for this mechanism: **decision augmentation** (also rendered as "decision augmentation theory" or DAT).

### Implications

Wargo considers decision augmentation theory potentially "hugely important across science":

- If researcher precognition can bias trial start-times in RNG experiments, it could introduce systematic bias in *any* experiment with a human decision about when to begin recording
- This could be a hidden factor in the **replication crisis** in psychology and health sciences — though the hypothesis requires accepting precognition first, a barrier that blocks most mainstream scientists from even considering it
- Decision augmentation cannot be ruled out by standard double-blind protocols (the researcher still decides when to start the trial)

## Relationship to the Stargate Program

May took over from Hal Puthoff at the SRI/Stargate program and led the research into the early 1990s. He is a committed skeptic of macro-PK and levitation claims but takes precognition seriously as a demonstrated effect requiring explanation. His skepticism of the PK framing while endorsing the precognition framing puts him in an unusual position in the parapsychology community.
