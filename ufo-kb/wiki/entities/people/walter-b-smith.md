# Walter Bedell Smith

## Background

General Walter Bedell Smith served as Director of Central Intelligence from 1950 to 1953, succeeding [[roscoe-hillenkoetter]]. A decorated WWII officer who served as Eisenhower's chief of staff, Smith is known in UAP research primarily for a declassified CIA memorandum from his directorship.

## UFO Memo

During his tenure, Smith authored a memo proposing that UFO phenomena could be **operationalized as a psychological warfare instrument against the Soviets**. The strategic logic: the Soviet Union was also experiencing UAP sightings and was likely uncertain about their origin. The US could exploit this uncertainty — feeding disinformation, amplifying confusion, and using the UFO discourse as a destabilizing psychological tool in the Cold War competition.

This memo is significant for two reasons:
1. It confirms that at the highest CIA levels, UFO reality (or at least the strategic utility of the mystery) was taken seriously in 1952–53 — the same window as the Washington DC flyover incidents and the Robertson Panel.
2. It establishes the explicit weaponization of UFO knowledge as a **psychological warfare asset** — meaning the CIA had institutional incentive to both classify UAP truth AND exploit UAP confusion. This dual motive explains the persistent mixture of suppression and theatrical disinformation that characterizes the subsequent decades.

This connects directly to the [[psychological-strategy-board]] (comprising [[gordon-gray]], [[charles-douglas-jackson]], and [[philip-corso]]) that developed related operational frameworks under Eisenhower.
