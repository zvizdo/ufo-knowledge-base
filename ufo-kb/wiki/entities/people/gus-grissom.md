# Gus Grissom

One of the original Mercury Seven astronauts; commanded Gemini 3 (first crewed Gemini mission) and was slated to command Apollo 1, positioning him to be the first human on the Moon. Died in the Apollo 1 capsule fire on January 27, 1967 — along with [[ed-white]] and Roger Chaffee — during a launch pad test.

## Lemon Protest

Grissom was vocally and persistently dissatisfied with the Apollo 1 capsule's safety and communication defects. After repeated complaints up the chain of command went unaddressed, he took extraordinary action: he invited press to the top of the rocket, affixed a lemon the size of a grapefruit to a coat hanger outside the capsule, and held an unauthorized press conference declaring the vehicle "a lemon, a piece of junk." The story made the evening news.

The higher-ups' failure to respond to his complaints is interpreted by [[bart-sibrel]] and [[joe-rogan]] as consistent with superiors who knew the moon mission as planned was not going to happen — the real schedule hadn't been revealed to the astronauts yet, so defects were not being fixed because the vehicle wasn't actually supposed to fly as represented.

## CIA on the Launch Pad

Per [[joe-rogan]]'s account (attributed to Grissom's wife's testimony), the night before the fire — January 26, 1967 — Grissom came home from work and told his wife:

> "For some strange reason, the CIA is all over the launch pad today. I wonder why they're here inspecting the equipment. I've never seen them here before."

He was dead the following day from what was officially classified as faulty equipment in the capsule.

## Significance in Moon-Landing Debate

Grissom's death is cited in [[moon-landing-hoax]] discourse as the clearest evidence of deliberate elimination of a potential whistleblower:
1. He was publicly critical of the program's integrity
2. CIA presence on the launch pad the day before his death has no obvious innocent explanation
3. The capsule used pure oxygen atmosphere at high pressure — a fire risk that experienced engineers knew was dangerous; Grissom had complained about it
4. His death removed the most likely first-moon-walker and the most vocal internal critic simultaneously
