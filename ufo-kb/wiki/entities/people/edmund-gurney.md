# Edmund Gurney

British psychical researcher (1847–1888); one of the co-founders of the Society for Psychical Research (SPR) in 1882, alongside [[frederic-myers]] and [[henry-sidgwick]]. Gurney was a philosopher and musician who brought rigorous statistical methodology to the investigation of anomalous experiences.

**Phantasms of the Living**: Gurney co-authored *Phantasms of the Living* (1886) with Frederic Myers and Frank Podmore — the first large-scale systematic statistical study of apparitions, crisis apparitions, and telepathy. The work collected and analyzed over 700 cases of people reportedly perceiving a distant person's death or distress simultaneously with the event, attempting to apply statistical analysis to telepathic phenomena.

**SPR methodology**: Gurney and the early SPR established the methodological standards that defined academic parapsychology: collecting cases through standardized questionnaires, requiring independent corroboration, calculating the probability of chance coincidence, and distinguishing between fraud, self-deception, and genuine anomaly.

**Death**: Gurney died in June 1888 at age 41 under ambiguous circumstances — apparently an accidental overdose of chloroform he was using as a sleep aid, though some historians have speculated about suicide or other causes.

**KB relevance**: In the [[spectral-revolution]] concept, Gurney represents the 19th century's attempt by credentialed elite academics to bring scientific rigor to anomalous experiences — the same institutional impulse that later produced government-funded [[remote-viewing]] programs and formal UAP studies.
