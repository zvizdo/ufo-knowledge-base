# Solon

Athenian statesman and lawgiver (c. 638–558 BC). During a visit to Egypt, he spoke with priests at the Temple of Neith at Sais in the Nile Delta. The priests read from hieroglyphs on the temple walls, translating records of a great civilization that was "destroyed and submerged beneath the sea." When Solon asked when this happened, the priests responded matter-of-factly: "9,000 years ago." Since this was approximately 600 BC, that places the event at 9,600 BC — which [[graham-hancock]] identifies as meltwater pulse 1B, one of the major sea-level-rise events at the end of the last Ice Age.

Solon did not write the account himself but passed it down through family lineage. It reached Plato, who recorded it in the dialogues *Timaeus* and *Critias* — the only surviving literary source for the Atlantis tradition.

The specificity of the date — not merely "long ago" but a precise figure derivable from the priests' records — is central to Hancock's argument that the Atlantis tradition encodes real historical memory rather than philosophical fiction.
