# Clyde Tombaugh

American astronomer who discovered Pluto in 1930. His ashes are aboard NASA's New Horizons spacecraft, now speeding toward interstellar space — the most expensive urn ever built. Joined [[lincoln-lapaz]] at [[white-sands-missile-range]] in 1953–1954 on the Near-Earth Satellite Project, a military-funded initiative to track small natural satellites.

## Background

Tombaugh's track record as a planet-finder and precision observer made him ideal for a project requiring careful sky surveillance. He remained, until his death, publicly convinced that some UFOs represent extraterrestrial spacecraft.

## Near-Earth Satellite Project

Co-led with [[lincoln-lapaz]]. The project was tasked to find natural Earth mini-moons (captured asteroids). Its final report concluded there are **no natural satellites of Earth** — yet LaPaz had separately claimed in 1954 that two unknown objects were found and identified as natural asteroids. The contradiction between these two statements is unresolved. Tombaugh's involvement in the project, alongside the green-fireball investigator LaPaz, lends the effort the character of a genuine search for anomalous orbital objects rather than a purely scientific curiosity about natural mini-moons.
