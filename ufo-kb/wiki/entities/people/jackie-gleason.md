# Jackie Gleason

American television comedian best known for *The Honeymooners*. Reportedly a passionate UAP enthusiast. In the most significant UAP-adjacent claim associated with Gleason, President [[richard-nixon]] allegedly took him — a personal friend — to an Air Force base "in the South" where crashed UAP wreckage and dead alien bodies were stored in a warehouse.

## The Nixon Story

- Gleason was reportedly a close friend of Nixon who shared an interest in UAP
- Nixon allegedly invited Gleason to a southern Air Force base (name unspecified) and showed him a warehouse containing crashed UAP hardware and alien bodies
- Gleason was so profoundly disturbed that his wife later said "he didn't eat for a week" and "his whole world view had been changed"
- Gleason's wife reportedly confirmed this story as "100% legit" to [[mark-croa]], a Florida golf club janitor who worked at Gleason's club as a youth and later had a phone conversation with Gleason's widow

Gallaudet (Tim Gallaudet, firsthand secondhand via Mark Croa) treats this as corroborated-secondhand: "Gleason was a big UAP enthusiast... and what Mark Croa told me, hearing from Gleason's wife, that it was 100% legit — that it happened."

## Credibility Assessment

This is multi-layer hearsay: Gleason → Gleason's wife → Mark Croa (fisherman) → Gallaudet (on-air). The primary firsthand witness (Gleason) is deceased. The story has circulated in UAP circles since at least the 1980s and is also documented in Beverly McKittrick's account. Gallaudet presents it as one of several items that raised his confidence in the crash retrieval program thesis.
