# Eric Olson

Son of [[frank-olson]], the CIA biochemist working on biological warfare at [[fort-detrick]] who was dosed with LSD by [[sidney-gottlieb]] at a Deep Creek Lake conference in 1953 and then fell to his death from a 10th-floor New York hotel window.

**The murder investigation**: Eric Olson spent decades pursuing evidence that his father's death was murder rather than the suicide the CIA originally characterized it as. His most significant finding: forensic and physical analysis of the hotel room window established that it was physically too small for a 6-foot man to have jumped through in the manner the official account required. He also commissioned forensic exhumation of his father's body, which found pre-mortem cranial trauma inconsistent with a fall and more consistent with a blow before the window.

**White House acknowledgment**: In 1975, the Church Committee's investigation of MK-ULTRA brought the Frank Olson case to light. President Ford personally met with the Olson family and expressed regret; the family subsequently received a $750,000 congressional compensation (often cited as $700,000). The compensation was widely interpreted as an acknowledgment of government culpability without a formal admission of murder.

**Significance**: Eric Olson's case is the most thoroughly documented instance of the CIA allegedly murdering a US government employee to prevent disclosure. It is cited in the KB in the context of the pattern of witness deaths connected to classified programs, and as evidence of how far the CIA/DoD secrecy apparatus was willing to go to maintain program security.
