# Arthur Stansel

## Background

Arthur Stansel was a decorated WWII veteran (three Purple Hearts, one Bronze Star) who built a post-war career in physics and engineering at [[wright-patterson]] Air Force Base. He was working as a consultant on blast effects of atomic bombs during [[operation-upshot-knothole|Operation Upshot-Knothole]] — the 1953 nuclear test series at the Nevada Test Site — when he was suddenly reassigned to a classified mission.

His involvement in Upshot-Knothole is confirmed by declassified documents from the test series.

## The Kingman Mission (May 1953)

Shortly after the 19 May 1953 detonation of "Harry" (32 kilotons, the largest blast of the series), Stansel and approximately 15 other specialists were:
1. Ordered to leave all personal belongings behind.
2. Flown to Phoenix.
3. Transported by bus with blacked-out windows to a classified location near [[kingman-arizona]].
4. Told they would assess a "secret test vehicle" that had crashed.

His assigned task was to determine impact velocity.

Under floodlights at the site, Stansel observed:
- A disc-shaped craft roughly **30 feet in diameter**, made of dull brushed metal, shaped like two deep saucers fused together.
- The craft was buried approximately **20 inches in the sand**, with no landing gear, no visible damage, and a small open hatch.
- Inside a nearby tent: **a number of 4-foot-tall humanoid figures** laid out on tables.

Stansel stated on record: *"I accepted it was probably some United States government vehicle, a highly classified vehicle. In fact, we were told that's what it was."* He noted the statement as the cover story he was given, not necessarily his own belief.

## Credibility

Stansel's military record (three Purple Hearts, Bronze Star) and confirmed presence at Upshot-Knothole through declassified documents place him as a credible insider witness by the standards of the [[credibility-frameworks]] concept. Former intelligence official [[chris-mellon]] has independently stated that the Kingman craft was recovered and studied over subsequent decades, lending some corroboration to the claim's general outline.

His superior in the Wright-Patterson special studies chain was Dr. [[eric-wang]].

## Open Questions

- Whether the floodlit site was specifically linked to the "Harry" detonation or a separate event occurring around the same time.
- Whether the 15 specialists who accompanied Stansel have been identified or have corroborated the account independently.
