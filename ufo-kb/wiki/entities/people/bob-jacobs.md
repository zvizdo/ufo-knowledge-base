# Bob Jacobs

Robert (Bob) Jacobs served as a First Lieutenant in the US Air Force at Vandenberg AFB, California, where he commanded the 4756th Test Squadron's optical instrumentation team. His team's mission was to film Air Force missile tests using high-powered telescopic cameras to provide post-flight analysis data.

In the autumn of 1964, during a test flight of an Atlas-F missile carrying a dummy re-entry vehicle, Jacobs's Big Sur telescope camera tracked the missile from launch through re-entry. When the film was developed and screened the next day, it showed a disc-shaped craft approaching the warhead, circling it, and firing four bright pulses or beams at it — after which the warhead tumbled out of its programmed trajectory. Jacobs was summoned to a meeting with his superior, Major Florenze Mansmann, where two men he believed to be CIA officers viewed the film, took the reel, and instructed both men never to speak of it. Jacobs signed a security oath and remained silent until 1982, when he broke the story publicly.

The incident is a central exhibit in the [[ufo-nuclear-nexus]] literature compiled by [[robert-hastings]], who interviewed Jacobs at length. Major Mansmann separately confirmed the basic account before his own death. The filmed event is documented as the [[1964-vandenberg-bob-jacobs]] incident.

Hastings also introduced Jacobs to [[richard-barth]], another Vandenberg witness whose CE3 encounter occurred in the **same month** — September 1964 — as Jacobs's filmed event. Barth independently observed UAP "tagalong" behavior (tracking an Atlas missile launch) as early as May 1964 and experienced the CE3 in September. The two men spoke directly; Barth speculates the craft he was aboard and the craft Jacobs filmed could be the same vehicle.

## Johnston Island / Operation Fishbowl (V00WcEiKRAY)

[[ross-coulthart]] notes that Jacobs was stationed at Johnston Island (Marshall Islands) in November 1963 during Operation Fishbowl — a year before the Vandenberg filming. The scuttlebutt among all island personnel (engineers, technicians, airmen) was that the Soviets had launched a nuclear weapon into orbit; JFK ordered it shot down; and something **fell toward the island from the explosion in the sky and was recovered**. Jacobs: *"I was never told what it was."*

This corroborates the widespread recovery rumors Coulthart has been investigating in the [[1962-bluegill-uap-shootdown]] / [[bluegill-triple-prime]] context — personnel at Johnston Island throughout the Fishbowl period believed a recovery had occurred.

## Firsthand Account (fzvwBBSmWYA)

In his American Alchemy interview with [[jesse-michaels]], Jacobs provided his most detailed public account:

- A **giant 18-wheel truck** brought a Kodak optic telescope with 2,000-inch focal length to Big Sur (vs. standard 2-inch 35mm) — "you can imagine how big the image would be"
- An Atlas missile "popped up through the ground fog" at 0600 as planned
- At the film screening the next day, "an object flew in chasing the warhead at the same speed, going between 6,000 and 8,000 mph" — fired a beam, circled to fire again from a new angle, repeated four times, then departed the way it came; the warhead "did this [tumbling motion] and fell out of the frame"
- Major [[florenz-mansman]] analyzed the film **frame by frame with a loop magnifier** and later wrote private letters to Jacobs (mid-1980s) describing "a disc-shaped object with a dome" — the disc **pivoted before each of the four flashes** of light
- Two CIA men in gray suits stood behind Mansman; Mansman confiscated the film; Jacobs was told "it never happened"
- Jacobs's records were deleted from Vandenberg; the government initially denied he ever served there, then denied he was ever in charge of the 1369th Photographic Unit Division
- His **mailbox was blown up**: he received a phone message — "Your mailbox all light. What a beautiful sight. You're going down [expletive]" — and the mailbox exploded
- [[lue-elizondo]] told [[kirk-mcconnell]] that he had seen the Big Sur film in **a Pentagon safe drawer**; Kirk Patrick (AARO) then allegedly looked and claimed it was not there
- Jacobs: "They never realized there are backups in a whole lot of places"; radar data confirming the anomalous aerial object pacing the warhead exists per [[david-grusch]]'s task-force colleague
