# Harry Rositzki

Retired CIA officer identified by [[greg-bishop]] as "Falcon" — the senior figure who recruited and handled [[bill-moore]] during the counterintelligence operation of which the [[paul-benowitz]] disinformation campaign was one minor element. Rositzki has since died.

## Background

Rositzki came out of the OSS in World War II and spent his career as one of the CIA's primary Soviet specialists. His documented activities included:
- Working with Reinhard Gehlen and other former Nazi intelligence officers to establish post-WWII intelligence networks in Europe targeting the Soviet Union
- Operations in Afghanistan monitoring Soviet radio transmissions
- Extensive Soviet counter-intelligence work

In the late 1970s / early 1980s, he was pulled out of retirement to run an unofficial counterintelligence operation targeting Soviet and Chinese intelligence assets operating in or around the UFO research community. (Greg Bishop, via [[WBTqsbwJyqU]])

## Role in the Benowitz Affair

Rositzki apparently hand-picked all the personnel involved in the operation, including [[rick-doty]] (the AFOSI operative) and [[bill-moore]] (the UFO researcher recruited as an asset). He was the first person Moore met when recruited — along with Doty — in what Moore initially thought was a prank call on his book tour for *The Roswell Incident*.

Moore described Rositzki's framing to Bishop: "Think of it as a giant wheel connected to a bunch of other giant wheels that you would never notice. The only reason [the Benowitz operation] is noticed is because it had to do with UFOs." Rositzki's interest in UFOs was purely instrumental — it was one more tool for vetting adversary assets.

## Significance

Rositzki's profile — a serious, high-level Cold War counterintelligence professional with no particular interest in UFOs — is important context for understanding why the Benowitz operation was so effective and so cynical. The people running it were not believers or fanatics; they were competent professional intelligence operators who saw the UFO subject as a useful substrate for their actual work. (Greg Bishop, firsthand research, via [[WBTqsbwJyqU]])
