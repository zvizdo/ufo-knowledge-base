# Konstantin Meyl

German physicist and professor who argues that Oliver Heaviside's 19th-century simplification of James Clerk Maxwell's original electromagnetic equations — reducing 21 equations to four vector equations — eliminated a physically real scalar-wave component from mainstream electromagnetic theory. Meyl has worked to restore and extend that framework, deriving **helicoidal longitudinal scalar waves** as a physical prediction.

Jesse Michaels references him as calling himself the "modern Tesla" — situating Meyl within the extended electrodynamics tradition that includes [[nikola-tesla]], [[thomas-townsend-brown]], and [[andrija-puharich]].

## Key Technical Claims

- Maxwell's full original equations include terms that, when solved faithfully, predict scalar waves — wave types without conventional transverse EM character
- Heaviside reduced the equations for tractability; this simplification discarded physics that may be real and measurable
- **Helicoidal waves** are one predicted wave type; these structurally match the double-helix geometry of DNA, raising the possibility that DNA is a scalar wave receiver/transmitter — a hypothesis Meyl has reportedly tested in botany experiments (making flowers grow in distinctive patterns using scalar longitudinal waves)
- The Biefeld–Brown effect and Aharonov–Bohm effect may be manifestations of the same underlying scalar physics

## Significance for Consciousness Research

If scalar waves are real and biologically coupled via DNA, they represent a candidate physical mechanism for non-local information transfer — including the telepathic phenomena documented by [[diane-powell]] and [[ky-dickens]]. [[diane-powell]] in [[0qlppHc3-gg]] explicitly identifies scalar waves as a "very interesting idea" when discussing transmission mechanism hypotheses.
