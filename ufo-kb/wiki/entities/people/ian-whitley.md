# Ian Whitley

Space psychologist who works on **high-performance readiness** — psychological and cognitive preparedness for astronauts and fighter pilots in extreme environments. Separately, or as an extension of this core work, she is researching the **retention of synesthesia in infants**.

## The Synesthesia-Infancy Discovery

Whitley's key finding (as reported by [[chris-ramsey]]):
- **All babies are born with synesthesia** — the coupling of senses (sound triggers color experience; smell triggers sound, etc.)
- At approximately **6 months of age**, this synesthetic coupling is lost in most individuals
- Whitley is developing modalities to help infants and children **retain synesthesia longer**

The significance: if the original [[stargate-program]] data showing all six high-talent remote viewers had synesthesia is robust, then synesthesia may be a neurological prerequisite for psi-type perception rather than merely a correlate. Early loss of synesthesia may mean early loss of a latent perceptual channel. See [[synesthesia-remote-viewing]].

## Adjacent Finding: Autism and Synesthesia

Ramsay connects Whitley's research to the non-verbal autistic children described in *The Telepathy Tapes* (Diane Powell), many of whom are also synesthetes. The common thread — synesthesia + partial dissociation from embodied sensory filtering — suggests these individuals may access what Ramsay calls "primordial" perceptual modes: less ported into the physical body's sensory frame, more connected to a non-local information field.
