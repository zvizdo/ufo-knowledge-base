# Henry Stimson

Henry Lewis Stimson (1867–1950) was one of the most consequential American statesmen of the 20th century. He served as Secretary of War under both William Howard Taft and Franklin D. Roosevelt/Harry Truman (1940–1945), and as Secretary of State under Herbert Hoover. His tenure as wartime Secretary of War made him the civilian architect of both the Manhattan Project's secrecy apparatus and the strategic bombing campaigns against Germany and Japan.

## Background

Stimson was a Wall Street lawyer (partner at the firm that became Winthrop, Stimson, Putnam & Roberts) and a lifelong Republican who crossed party lines to serve Democratic presidents during national emergencies. His moral seriousness and institutional authority made him the elder statesman among the postwar "wise men" generation. His decision to use the atomic bomb against Japan — which he later expressed ambivalence about — is one of the most consequential individual decisions of the 20th century.

## Notable Claims / Role

In accounts of the Yamashita gold discovery chain, Stimson is the figure who received the matter from President Truman (via [[clark-clifford]]) and took responsibility for organizing a covert management structure. He assembled three fellow establishment figures — [[robert-lovett]], [[robert-anderson]], and [[john-j-mccloy]] — to form the trust that would manage the recovered treasure. This group became the [[anderson-trust-gold]]. Stimson's earlier work building the secrecy architecture around the Manhattan Project gave him both the experience and the institutional credibility to construct a similarly compartmentalized financial structure.
