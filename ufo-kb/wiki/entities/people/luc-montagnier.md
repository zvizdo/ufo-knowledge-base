# Luc Montagnier

French virologist (1932–2022); Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine 2008 (shared with Françoise Barré-Sinoussi) for the discovery of the human immunodeficiency virus (HIV). Professor at the Institut Pasteur and later director of the World Foundation for AIDS Research and Prevention. One of the most distinguished virologists of the 20th century.

**HIV discovery context**: Montagnier's Nobel Prize work identified HIV as the causative agent of AIDS in 1983, a finding disputed at the time by Robert Gallo (NIH) who claimed co-discovery. The priority dispute was eventually partially resolved with the shared Nobel; the controversy is background to Montagnier's later willingness to publish heterodox findings, suggesting a pattern of independent thinking regardless of institutional pressure.

**DNA water teleportation experiments**: In his later career, Montagnier published controversial research (2009–2011) claiming that highly diluted DNA solutions emit low-frequency electromagnetic signals, and that these signals can be used to reconstitute the DNA sequence in a separate, distant water sample — effectively "teleporting" genetic information via electromagnetic radiation through water. The claimed mechanism: DNA sequences produce specific low-frequency electromagnetic "signatures" that can be captured, transmitted, and used to guide the self-assembly of DNA from precursor materials in a distant water medium.

**Scientific reception**: The DNA teleportation claims were not reproduced by independent labs and were widely criticized as methodologically flawed, including comparisons to Jacques Benveniste's "water memory" claims and homeopathy. Barré-Sinoussi (Montagnier's Nobel co-laureate) publicly distanced herself from the claims. Scientific consensus is that the teleportation interpretation reflects experimental artifacts (contamination, confirmation bias, instrumental noise).

**KB relevance and calibration**: The [[dna-intelligence-encoding]] concept cites Montagnier's experiments alongside the [[dna-phantom-effect]] (Russian experiment) as evidence for the thesis that biological information may have electromagnetic substrates beyond material chemistry. The KB documents this as a heterodox claim from a credentialed source, not as an established finding. Montagnier's Nobel status is explicitly what makes the claim KB-worthy — the same experiments from an unknown researcher would not merit inclusion.
