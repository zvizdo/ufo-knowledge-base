# Elena Hardy

Bioinformatics expert who analyzed the publicly available NCBI genetic database entries for the [[nazca-tridactyl-mummies]]. Her work focused on attempting to *falsify* the hypothesis that a known genetic mutation could account for the tridactyl digit phenotype.

## Background

Approached the question scientifically: she searched the literature for genes involved in digit development (identifying ~50 candidate genes in the Sonic Hedgehog pathway), then looked for mutations in those genes in the mummy genetic data. She found a GLI3 mutation not widely known even in mainstream genetics.

## Notable Claims

- GLI3 mutation found in mummy NCBI data; this mutation is associated in the literature with polydactyly (fusion or extra digits).
- Not a super-common mutation — unusual enough to make her pause.
- A Chinese family study shows GLI3 mutation causing digit developmental disorders with only one copy (heterozygous) — inherited from one parent.
- The mutation is "definitely not known by the huaquero gravediggers who dug up the bodies," making its presence in the data hard to explain as fabrication.
- Jesse: if this finding can be replicated, it would be a major smoking gun.

## Credibility Assessment

Appears to be a genuine bioinformatics analyst approaching the question with falsification intent (attempting to debunk, not confirm). Her finding is preliminary and needs independent replication. The GLI3/digit-number connection is real in the scientific literature, though tridactyly (three-fingeredness) is not identical to polydactyly. See [[credibility-frameworks]].
