# Clark Clifford

Clark McAdams Clifford (1906–1998) was one of the most influential figures in postwar American political life — a Washington attorney and presidential adviser who served as Special Counsel to President Truman and, decades later, as Secretary of Defense under President Lyndon Johnson (1968–69). He is a textbook example of the "wise man" archetype: the private lawyer who maintains extraordinary influence across successive administrations.

## Background

Clifford entered Truman's orbit as a naval aide and quickly became one of the president's most trusted advisers. He is credited with drafting significant portions of the National Security Act of 1947, which created the CIA, NSC, and unified Department of Defense. After leaving government, he built a Washington law firm (Clifford & Warnke) that became a hub of establishment influence.

## Notable Claims / Role

In accounts of the Yamashita gold discovery, Clifford is identified as the conduit between the field briefing brought by [[charles-willoughby]] and Edward Lansdale and President Truman himself. Clifford received the briefing from Willoughby and Lansdale, then brought the matter to Truman. This set in motion [[henry-stimson]]'s assembly of the [[anderson-trust-gold]] group. Clifford's position at the nexus of military intelligence, the White House, and the legal-financial establishment made him the natural relay point for a discovery of this sensitivity.
