# Mark McCandish

Technical illustrator and researcher who produced detailed schematic illustrations of the **Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)**, also called the **Flux Liner**, based on eyewitness descriptions of a craft reportedly seen in a hangar at a 1988 air show at Norton Air Force Base. McCandish's drawings remain among the most specific public visualizations of an alleged reverse-engineered electrogravitic craft.

## The ARV / Flux Liner

McCandish's illustrations depict a three-tiered disc-shaped craft with a central column and ring-shaped capacitor arrays — consistent with electrogravitic / Biefield-Brown-effect propulsion architecture. The craft was reportedly seen by witnesses alongside a standard F-117 at the air show, suggesting a black-program operational vehicle.

McCandish presented this testimony at the **2001 National Press Club Disclosure event** organized by [[steven-greer]], alongside witnesses like [[donna-hare]] (NASA photo-scrubbing).

## Documentary and Alleged Assassination

[[james-allen]] produced a documentary about McCandish and the Flux Liner testimony. Allen subsequently died of an aggressive cancer within approximately three weeks of diagnosis; autopsy reportedly showed heavy metal poisoning. [[michael-herrera]] and [[uap-gerb]] suggest Allen was killed via a scalar frequency weapon — the weaponized form of technology also used for healing (see [[scalar-frequency-healing]]).

## Eric Davis's Explicit Refutation (LnAiNChnuEQ)

[[eric-davis]] explicitly dismisses McCandlish's ARV claims in [[LnAiNChnuEQ]]:

> "I don't know where they're coming from and then other people kind of came out of the woodwork saying the same thing but no proof."

Davis's position is based on a classified briefing from program insiders that **no ARVs were built and no reverse-engineering progress was achieved after decades of effort**. He places McCandlish in the same category as [[bob-lazar]]-type mythology — sincerely held accounts that were not grounded in actual program knowledge.

[[bruce-maccabee]], a Navy physicist and UAP photo analyst, assessed ARV photographs circulating in the UAP community as "hoaxes."

> ⚠ **Conflict**: Davis's "no ARVs" classified briefing conflicts directly with McCandlish's ARV witness accounts and illustrations. Both cannot simultaneously be accurate. This conflict is not resolved in the KB.

## Norton AFB 1988 — The Origin Event (nTiFs8LudUo)

The [[nTiFs8LudUo]] episode provides the most detailed account to date of how McCandish's ARV illustrations were produced. [[brad-sorensen]] (McCandish's friend and professional associate) was personally admitted to the 1988 Norton AFB classified aerospace display and described the three saucer craft ("mama, baby, papa bear") to McCandish in technical detail. See [[1988-norton-afb]] for the full incident account.

Key additions from [[nTiFs8LudUo]]:
- A **1990 Aviation Week interview** conducted by editor Bill Scott is confirmed as a contemporaneous corroborating record of Sorensen's account, pre-dating McCandish's 2001 NPC presentation
- **X-Files pilot connection**: Chris Carter contracted McCandish to design alien craft for the X-Files pilot episode; McCandish used the **XF-131 Super Sentinel** (triangular ARV) design — based on descriptions from Northrop and Lockheed engineers who had independently told him about the same program
- **Death (2020)**: McCandish died from an apparently self-inflicted gunshot wound; circumstances remain unclear and the documentary editor who handled related interview materials received Men-in-Black-type threats
