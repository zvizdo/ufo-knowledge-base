# James Forrestal

## Background

James Vincent Forrestal served as the first United States Secretary of Defense from 1947 to 1949, having previously served as Secretary of the Navy from 1944 to 1947. His tenure coincided precisely with the pivotal early years of the UAP phenomenon in the US: the 1947 Roswell incident, the formation of the CIA (1947), the establishment of the Air Force as a separate service, and the earliest formal government investigations into flying discs.

Forrestal was known for his intense anti-communism, his bureaucratic battles over military unification, and — by the end of his tenure — increasingly erratic behavior that led to his forced resignation in March 1949. He was subsequently hospitalized at Bethesda Naval Hospital, where he died on May 22, 1949, officially by falling from a window. The circumstances of his death have been disputed for decades.

## Notable Claims

### Briefing JFK on UFOs

According to [[harold-malmgren]]'s account (as relayed in the source), Forrestal "fully briefed" JFK on UFOs at some point before Forrestal's death — which occurred when Kennedy was still a young congressman. This claim, if accurate, would mean Kennedy had direct UAP knowledge long before he became president, providing context for Malmgren's later assertion that JFK's intent to share this knowledge with the Soviets was central to [[jfk-assassination-ufo]]. Forrestal's access to the most sensitive early recovery programs — given his dual role overseeing both the Navy and the newly unified Defense Department — makes him a plausible vector for such a briefing.

### Suspicious Death at Bethesda Naval Hospital

Malmgren and the source interview's host describe Forrestal's death as suspicious. Forrestal's brother reportedly stated that James was of sound mind the day before his death — directly contradicting the narrative of a suicidal breakdown. The source places Forrestal's death in the broader context of the [[ufo-secrecy-cabal]]'s pattern of eliminating individuals whose UAP knowledge made them liabilities, connecting it structurally (if not evidentially) to the same forces discussed in [[jfk-assassination-ufo]].

The Bethesda Naval Hospital setting is significant: it is a military medical facility where access and information could be controlled by intelligence-adjacent personnel. The same hospital would later be the site of JFK's official autopsy — a convergence that the source treats as meaningful.

### Forrestal's Position in the Custodian Structure

As the first SecDef and former SecNav, Forrestal would have had supervisory authority over both the Navy's recovery operations (relevant to the Bluegill Triple Prime account described by [[richard-bissell]]) and the earliest Air Force UFO investigation programs. His position at the junction of military services and the new national security apparatus made him one of the most informed officials of the founding UAP period. His removal — whether by resignation, breakdown, or foul play — created a gap in civilian oversight of the programs he had helped initiate.

## Richard Dolan's Reconstruction (GQlWf54K_7Y)

[[richard-dolan]] provides the most detailed public reconstruction of Forrestal's death, going beyond Malmgren's account:

**Forrestal's Unraveling**: People noticed Forrestal developing "weird nervous ticks." He said he was "being followed by strange foreign looking men." He started obsessively dipping fingers into glasses of water and wetting his lips. The journalist [[drew-pearson]] was writing stories about his deterioration — possibly participating in a public-discrediting operation to set up the eventual "suicide" narrative.

**The Limo Ride**: After his farewell ceremony, [[stuart-symington]] (Secretary of the Air Force, one of Forrestal's primary enemies) was waiting in the limousine. No one else knows what was said. When Forrestal got out of that limo, he went into his office, closed the door, stared at a blank wall for hours, and repeated only: "You are a loyal fellow."

**Bethesda Sequestration**: Placed on the 16th floor of [[bethesda-naval-hospital]] by national security order — against medical advice. His regular corpsman (who had befriended Forrestal and been promised a job as Forrestal's assistant when released) goes AWOL without explanation on the final night. Replacement corpsman takes the shift.

**The Final Night (May 21–22, 1949)**: Henry Forrestal (brother) calls the hospital threatening to "raise holy hell" if James is not released by the next day. That night, between 1:30–1:45 AM, the replacement corpsman checks on Forrestal: calm, in bed, apparently transcribing Sophocles' *Ajax*, declines a sedative. Shortly after — he's gone.

**Suicide Method Analysis**: The official story holds that Forrestal got up, walked to a small hallway window behind a radiator, tied his bathrobe cord *tightly* around his neck, tied the other end *loosely* around the radiator, and tried to hang himself out the 16th-floor window. The cord "slipped." Dolan: "Couple of big tough Navy guys grab him and throw him the hell out the window. I'm absolutely convinced that's what happened."

**The Will Cutts Review**: The duty corpsman was interviewed for approximately 15 minutes. No critical examination. Dolan: "Like, thank you for your testimony, young man. You can go."

**Denied Spiritual Adviser**: Throughout his hospitalization, Forrestal was denied access to his priest, Father Sheehy — an extraordinary restriction on a hospital patient.

**Key Visitors**: Truman; Lewis Johnson (Forrestal's successor); Lyndon Baines Johnson (senator); John Fitzgerald Kennedy (congressman).
