# Kevin Knuth

Physicist at the [[university-at-albany]] (SUNY Albany). Grew up in Wisconsin; did his PhD at Montana State University in Bozeman starting 1988, where a professor first told him about UFO incursions at [[malmstrom-air-force-base]] (a joke he laughed off for a year). Worked at NASA Ames in the early 2000s in the intelligent systems division on machine learning / Hubble image analysis. Left before "full cost accounting" curtailed blue-sky research under the Bush administration.

In ~2015, while preparing an astronomy lecture, Knuth stumbled onto Robert Hastings's 2010 press conference and realized the Malmstrom story was real — and that it had been ongoing for 20 years before he first heard it. He then began actively researching UAPs, gave a 3-hour departmental talk (standing room only), and went public just before the 2017 New York Times article.

## Background

- PhD in physics, Montana State University (started 1988)
- NASA Ames Research Center, Intelligent Systems Division, early 2000s
- Professor of Physics, University at Albany (SUNY)
- Co-founder (with Matthew Shostakus) of [[u-albany-project-x]] (formerly UAPx)
- Intentionally declined classified clearance at NASA to preserve open-science approach

## Notable Claims

### Nimitz Tic-Tac Performance Analysis
Knuth and colleagues calculated the Tic-Tac's performance in the [[2004-nimitz-tic-tac]] encounter: dropping from 28,000 ft to sea level in 0.78 seconds yields ~**5,000 G** minimum acceleration (F-35 wings shear at 13 G; missile frames survive to ~60 G). Power requirement: ~**1,100 gigawatts** — more than the entire US nuclear power output — in a craft the size of an F-18. At 1% inefficiency, the waste heat alone would melt any known material, implying either near-perfect efficiency or a fundamentally different mechanism (inertia/gravity manipulation, warp).

### JAL 1628 (1986) — Knuth's Highest-Conviction Case
[[kenju-terauchi]], captain of Japan Airlines Flight 1628, reported a walnut-shaped object roughly four 747s in length (~aircraft carrier size) following the plane for 45 minutes over Alaska. Military height-finding radar tracked the object; [[john-callahan]] (FAA chief of accidents) secretly copied the radar data before handing it to Reagan's scientific team and the CIA. [[daniel-koumbi]] at the Niels Bohr Institute later analyzed this radar data and found accelerations of **9,000–11,000 Gs** and a top speed of ~**250,000 mph** (moon transit in 54 minutes). Reagan's scientific team's reaction: "We've never had 45 minutes of radar data from one of these things before."

### US Reverse-Engineering Program
"I think that's probably true. I've heard this from enough people who've done that kind of work for years." He has met individuals who claim to have worked on recovered craft or seen them at bases — found them entirely credible. Their most believable admission: "We don't know what anything is or how anything works."

### Underwater UFO Bases
Knuth argues that water worlds are the ideal habitat: stable temperature (0–100°C bounded), pressure controllable by depth, high heat capacity damping variation, shielding from radiation and micrometeorites, electromagnetic opacity (making sonar the only detection modality), and access to seawater as a mineral resource. His best bet: NHI bases in Earth's oceans. He references the HMNZS Southland encounter (800 ft USO, closed 2 km in 25 seconds underwater without creating detectable waves, yet returned sonar).

### Anti-Gravity Researcher Intimidation
Mentions "handfuls of people doing weird things" in government labs and at NASA (doing remote viewing, asymmetric capacitor research, studying anti-gravity). Friends working on anti-gravity have received threats. He doesn't know who is behind this but notes Occam's Razor points to a group already possessing the knowledge.

### Kecksburg / Donald Hornig
Kevin's father's cousin [[donald-hornig]] was the chief scientific advisor to President Johnson (and earlier to Eisenhower and Kennedy) and designed the electronic trigger for the plutonium bomb. Knuth found an official government document about the [[1965-kecksburg]] crash listing Hornig as present on the scene — as one would expect from a sitting presidential science advisor. Hornig's wife [[lilli-hornig]] (who appeared in the *Oppenheimer* film) knew nothing about it when Knuth asked.

### Malmstrom — Personal Testimony Chain
1988: Montana State professor tells him UFOs are shutting down Malmstrom nuclear missiles (running joke). 2015: Salas 2010 press conference — realizes it's real and has been happening for 20+ years. Louisiana Mardi Gras, ~3 years before interview: meets his wife's Uncle Matt who was stationed at Malmstrom 1971–74 — confirms it "happened all the time, these things would come in and shut down the nuclear weapons," completely matter-of-fact.

### NASA ML Attempt on Ramey Memo
At NASA, Knuth and a colleague in the intelligent systems division tried to apply image-processing ML algorithms to clean up the memo General Ramey is holding in the famous Roswell newspaper photograph (clearly stamped TOP SECRET). Abandoned due to insufficient compute (300 MHz machines). The goal was to publish methodology as a NASA publication with Roswell as one example — "our sneaky plan." Knuth notes similar informal investigations likely occur across government labs.

## UAP Research Program (U Albany)

Knuth co-leads [[u-albany-project-x]] with [[matthew-shostakus]] and Professor Cecilia Levy, endowed by a gift from local businessman Tony Gorman. Formerly UAPx (disbanded and moved to U Albany). One of the few university-supported UAP data collection and analysis programs. Published a scientific review paper on UAP study history (mentioning Bud Clem's Hanford sighting).

*A Tear in the Sky* (2023): five-day rooftop observation in Laguna Beach + Catalina Island team. Learned "what not to do." The bright light that excited the team turned out to be the ISS (daylight savings time not accounted for by their satellite tracking app); Shostakus reconstructed the ISS's size to within one foot from pixel analysis. Knuth personally saw something drop into the sea in infrared through night-vision goggles at the same time the flare camera was triggering — but the flare camera's "things falling" turned out to be a known malfunction mode.
