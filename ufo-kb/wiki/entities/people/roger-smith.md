# Roger Smith

Roger Smith was Charles Manson's parole officer during the critical 1967–1969 period in San Francisco and Los Angeles. His dual role — parole officer and academic drug researcher — is one of the most anomalous details [[tom-oneill]] documents in his investigation.

## The Coincidence

Smith's academic specialty at Berkeley was: **adolescent kids who join gangs that become violent while using LSD and amphetamines**. This is the precise behavioral transformation he was simultaneously overseeing in his parolee Charles Manson. Manson reportedly called Smith "Jubal" — the protective lawyer figure in Robert Heinlein's *Stranger in a Strange Land*, suggesting an almost familial relationship.

## Implications

If Manson was an MK-Ultra behavioral-modification subject, Smith's role as parole officer would provide the institutional mechanism for regular access and monitoring. Parole check-ins would create documented, supervised contact. Smith's LSD and drug research expertise would make him capable of administering and evaluating whatever conditioning protocols were being applied.

[[tom-oneill]] presents Smith as part of the "Cornucopia of characters" — a half-dozen individuals with CIA or government research connections who clustered around Manson during his free years, several of whom have no accessible records and whose FOIA requests returned Glomar responses.

## Connection to Haight-Ashbury Clinic

Smith was also associated with the [[haight-ashbury-free-clinic]] — the same venue where [[jolly-west]] was conducting CIA-connected LSD research on hippies. This creates a three-way institutional overlap: West (CIA researcher), Smith (parole officer/drug researcher), and Manson (parolee/alleged subject) all operating in the same small San Francisco milieu.
