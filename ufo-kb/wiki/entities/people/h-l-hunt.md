# H.L. Hunt

Haroldson Lafayette Hunt (1889–1974) was a Texas oil magnate who, at his peak, was frequently cited as the wealthiest American. He built his fortune through oil speculation in East Texas (the massive East Texas Oil Field, discovered 1930) and managed Hunt Oil Company. His wealth was later estimated at roughly $8–10 billion in mid-century dollars.

**Political operations**: Hunt was openly far-right in his politics and used his wealth to fund political advocacy. His most visible project was *Life Line*, a syndicated radio program distributed to hundreds of stations in the 1960s promoting anti-communist and anti-New Deal positions. He also wrote *Alpaca* (1960), a novel advocating a constitution where voting rights were proportional to taxes paid.

**JFK assassination accounts**: Hunt is named in multiple Kennedy assassination accounts as part of the Texas oil establishment that allegedly had motivation to remove Kennedy. The most specific account is [[madeleine-brown]]'s claim that Hunt was present at the [[clint-murchison-jr]] mansion on November 21, 1963, the night before the assassination. Kennedy's policies — including oil depletion allowance reviews and nuclear test ban discussions — threatened Texas oil interests financially.

**Sons and network**: Hunt's sons, Lamar and Bunker Hunt, continued the family's financial and political operations. Lamar Hunt founded the Kansas City Chiefs and helped create the AFL; Bunker Hunt famously attempted to corner the silver market in 1980.

**KB significance**: H.L. Hunt represents the intersection of oil-industry financial power with the political infrastructure that multiple JFK-assassination frameworks implicate. His inclusion in the Murchison mansion account, if accurate, would represent oil-money complicity in the assassination.
