# Earl Warren

14th Chief Justice of the United States (1953–1969). Prior to his Supreme Court appointment, Warren served three terms as Governor of California and was the 1948 Republican vice-presidential nominee. His tenure as Chief Justice produced landmark decisions including *Brown v. Board of Education*, *Miranda v. Arizona*, and *Engel v. Vitale* — making him one of the most consequential judicial figures of the 20th century.

**Warren Commission**: Warren chaired the **President's Commission on the Assassination of President Kennedy** (1963–64) — the body whose final report (the "Warren Report") concluded that Lee Harvey Oswald acted alone, using a Mannlicher-Carcano rifle, and that Jack Ruby acted alone in killing Oswald. The commission was assembled within days of the assassination by President Johnson, who reportedly told Warren that national security required a fast, authoritative resolution of the "communist plot" question.

**Structural critique**: Critics across the political spectrum — including [[danny-sheehan]], [[oliver-stone]], and [[james-douglass]] — argue that the Warren Commission's conclusions were reached with classified material withheld. The CIA did not provide the commission with information about the anti-Castro operations involving [[e-howard-hunt]], [[frank-sturgis]], [[bernard-barker]], and [[david-morales]] — all of whom are named in JFK-conspiracy literature. The commission also did not receive full access to the Mafia-CIA joint assassination plots targeting Castro, which were ongoing at the time of Kennedy's death.

**Allen Dulles conflict**: [[allen-dulles]], fired by Kennedy after the Bay of Pigs, was a member of the Warren Commission — creating a structural conflict of interest: the man Kennedy fired was placed on the investigative body reviewing whether Kennedy was killed by CIA-connected operatives. Warren accepted this composition without apparent objection.

**KB significance**: Earl Warren is not himself accused of participation in any conspiracy. His role in the KB is structural: as the front-of-house authority who lent institutional legitimacy to the lone-gunman conclusion while operating with restricted access to the most relevant classified files. The pattern — a prestigious official producing authoritative findings from incomplete evidence — recurs in the KB's analysis of official UAP investigation (Blue Book, AARO) under [[credibility-frameworks]].
