# Thane Heins

Canadian inventor and free-energy researcher based in Ontario. Heins claims to have discovered a motor topology he calls the "Paramagnetic Bi-Toroid Transformer" (PBT), in which electromagnetic induction — which conventionally opposes and thus brakes a rotating motor (Lenz's Law) — instead appears to accelerate the motor under certain configurations. If genuine, this would represent a profound departure from foundational electromagnetic theory.

## Background

Heins operates through a company called Potential Difference Inc. He has given public demonstrations of his motor setup, and the effect has received attention both from mainstream skeptics (who attribute the apparent acceleration to measurement error, load-decoupling, or regenerative effects) and from free-energy researchers who view it as evidence of suppressed electromagnetic physics. Heins has stated that engineers from major industrial corporations, including Siemens and Philips, have visited and contracted interest — though neither company has made public statements confirming or validating his claims.

His work sits within a long tradition of claimed over-unity motor effects (John Bedini, Edwin Gray, etc.) that circulate in the suppressed-physics community. None have achieved reproducible validation in peer-reviewed physics.

## Relevance to UAP/Suppressed-Physics Discourse

[[gary-mckinnon]] mentions Heins in the context of free-energy research that exists outside mainstream physics, specifically citing the Siemens/Philips connection as evidence that major industrial players are quietly investigating these claims. This places Heins in the same discursive cluster as the [[biefeld-brown-effect]] (electrogravitic thrust), [[thomas-townsend-brown]] (suppressed electrogravitic research), and the broader claim that non-conventional propulsion physics is actively suppressed or quietly studied by institutions that publicly dismiss it.

The key analytical point: McKinnon is not claiming Heins has solved free energy — he is using Heins as an example of the pattern in which anomalous claims attract serious institutional interest (Siemens, Philips) without public acknowledgment, mirroring the pattern he alleges in aerospace and UAP propulsion.

## Mainstream Assessment

Physics reviewers who have examined Heins's demonstrations typically conclude that the apparent acceleration is an artifact of the test setup — specifically, that removing electromagnetic load from the generator side causes the motor to speed up not by gaining energy but by losing its braking resistance. This is consistent with standard Lenz's Law behavior. No peer-reviewed publication confirms an over-unity or anti-Lenz effect.
