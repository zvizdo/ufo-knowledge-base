# Max Born

German-British theoretical physicist (1882–1970), Nobel Prize in Physics 1954, and one of the founders of quantum mechanics. Born directed the Institute of Theoretical Physics at the University of Göttingen, which in the 1920s was the most important center for quantum physics in the world.

## Göttingen Network

In the 1920s, Born taught and mentored a cohort that would go on to shape both wartime physics and post-war UFO secrecy:

- **[[j-robert-oppenheimer]]** — studied under Born at Göttingen; later directed the Manhattan Project
- **[[edward-condon]]** — also studied under Born at Göttingen; later directed the NCAR and authored the [[condon-report]] that dismissed UFOs

The connection is significant because Condon's dual identity — student of Born, co-author of the [[mcmahon-secrecy-act]], and eventual UFO debunker — suggests he was embedded in the nuclear physics network from the start. The man who wrote the law governing UAP secrecy and later ran the official "nothing to see here" study trained alongside Oppenheimer under the same mentor.
