# René Girard

French Catholic philosopher and literary theorist (1923–2015). Taught at Stanford University; his colloquiums in the Bay Area drew scholars from UC Berkeley and elsewhere. A devout Catholic who attended Latin Mass.

## Mimetic Theory

Girard argued that **all human desire is mimetic** — we desire what others desire, not things for their intrinsic value. This produces rivalry, envy, and ultimately violence. The social resolution of mimetic violence is the **scapegoat mechanism**: a community channels its accumulated violence onto a sacrificial victim (the scapegoat), producing temporary peace. This mechanism, Girard argued, is the structural engine of most human cultural and religious institutions.

Key work: *Violence and the Sacred* (1972) — described by [[diana-pasulka]] as a "theory of everything for human violence" that destabilized multiple disciplines because of its ambition. Pasulka notes that the Jesuits at Berkeley who taught her were reluctant to embrace Girard because he had "no transcendent evil" — his account of evil is anthropological, not metaphysical.

## The Heresy

Girard's model was rejected by:
- **Religious scholars**: because his Christology was too anthropological — the crucifixion as the exposure of the scapegoat mechanism, not a metaphysically transcendent redemption event.
- **Evolutionary biologists**: because his grand theory of violence and culture competed directly with Darwinian social models without being reducible to them.

[[diana-pasulka]] compares this double rejection to Plato's similar status as too comprehensive to fit any single disciplinary box.

## Connection to Pasulka and NHI Context

Pasulka studied under Girard's influence as a graduate student, attending his colloquiums. [[peter-thiel]] also studied under Girard at Stanford — creating a Girard-Thiel-Pasulka intellectual lineage that surfaces in their shared analysis of:
- The **Antichrist as the ultimate scapegoat operator**: presenting as world-savior ("peace and safety") while doing the most harm (the Thessalonians formulation Thiel cites)
- **Effective altruism as a form of evil**: outward-looking "we'll rearrange the world for good" thinking that never looks inward ("am I doing harm?") matches the Antichrist structure
- **Looking inward** as the core practice of genuine community (dialectic, Buddhist sangha, early Christian ecclesia) — the opposite of the scapegoat/Antichrist outward gaze

## Girard Applied to the Eucharist (tS_64sTN5AU)

In [[tS_64sTN5AU]], [[diana-pasulka]] extends Girard's framework to the **Catholic Mass** directly — a reading she describes as emerging from her religious studies background:

- The Mass is a ritualized re-enactment of the sacrifice of a **perfect innocent victim** (Jesus).
- Crucially, in Girard's reading, the Gospels are the first mythology in history to **take the side of the victim** rather than the mob — exposing the scapegoating mechanism for what it is.
- But when the ritual is repeated again and again, across generations, participants **no longer consciously know what they are doing**: they enact a killing without awareness that it is a killing. The ritual becomes automated.
- For Catholics and Orthodox Christians, transubstantiation means the bread and wine literally *become* the body and blood — not symbolically (that is the Protestant reduction). So the congregation is literally eating a sacrificed person, Pasulka points out to her students' shock.
- This automated unconsciousness is what Girard called the **defense mechanism** — religion as a structure that controls human violence by routinizing and ritualizing it until it is invisible.
- The **UFO as forcing function**: Pasulka applies Girard here — UAP phenomena may function precisely to **break through this programmatic behavior**, forcing a moment of genuine self-examination outside the ritual's scripted frame.

She also notes Girard's double-rejection parallels her own: too large a theory of everything for any single discipline, doubly exiled as a result.
