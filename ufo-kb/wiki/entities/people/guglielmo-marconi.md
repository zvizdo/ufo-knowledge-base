# Guglielmo Marconi

Italian inventor credited with developing radio communication. In UFO lore, allegedly appointed by Mussolini to head RS-33 (a secret Italian government committee, equivalent to Majestic-12) to investigate and study the craft from the [[1933-magenta-crash]]. His grandson confirmed his grandfather's involvement in this committee in a video interview with Ogo magazine (an Italian celebrity magazine), stating Marconi participated in a select group with Luce Mussolini to study the matter.

In a separate interview with [[jesse-michaels]] (referenced in [[zrmG2jBckgk]]), Marconi's grandson also confirmed that in **1953**, an Italian government committee (RS-33) was set up with Marconi as president and **Enrico Fermi** as vice president, following UFO apparitions in Varese, northern Italy. The precise dates and events differ somewhat from earlier accounts (1933 Magenta crash vs. 1953 Varese sightings), suggesting either RS-33 was reactivated in 1953 or multiple Italian UFO committees existed.

## The Transatlantic Experiment Parable

In [[zrmG2jBckgk]], [[filippo-biondi]] uses Marconi's famous 1901 transatlantic radio experiment as a parable about open-minded science. The entire academic establishment (including Poincaré) said it was impossible — because Maxwell's equations require line-of-sight propagation and the Earth is a sphere. Marconi proceeded anyway. The signal got through because of the ionosphere — a "third solution" that neither the academics nor Marconi himself knew existed. Marconi believed electromagnetic waves followed a curved path (wrong), but was right about the outcome for an unknown reason until he discovered the ionosphere.

Biondi's point: Marconi flunked out of school; his professors didn't take him seriously; he won the Nobel Prize. Open-mindedness ("heretical thinking") drives scientific progress.
