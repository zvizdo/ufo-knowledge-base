# Nick Cook

## Background

Aviation and defense journalist, former editor at Jane's Defence Weekly. Authored *The Hunt for Zero Point* (2001), one of the first mainstream books to seriously investigate the classified anti-gravity research conducted by aerospace companies in the 1950s–60s and the possibility that the US government continued this work in black programs.

## The Podkletnov–Schauberger Connection

During research for *The Hunt for Zero Point*, Cook interviewed [[eugene-podkletnov]], the Russian physicist famous for rotating superconductor / gravity-shielding experiments. In that conversation, Podkletnov mentioned that his **father had been a Soviet intelligence agent** tasked with retrieving [[victor-schauberger]]'s engineering files at the end of World War II. Cook had the instinct to ask about Schauberger unprompted, and Podkletnov's response confirmed that there was a Soviet intelligence operation specifically targeting Schauberger's work.

This is cited in the [[8TYMQOUDQBo]] interview as evidence of an intelligence-level interest in implosion-based propulsion dating to at least the late 1940s, and as a data point in the [[occult-aerospace-connection]] / rotating-propulsion lineage.

## Hunt for Zero Point

- Published 2001; the book brought terms like "electrogravitics" and "Townsend Brown effect" into mainstream discourse
- Named after the zero-point energy concept central to classified propulsion research
- Cook conducted interviews at major aerospace contractors and found a conspicuous pattern: engineers who had worked on anti-gravity research in the 1950s were uncomfortable on the record and often deflected

## The G-Engines Article and George Trimble

Cook's investigation began when an anonymous package appeared on his Jane's desk containing a 1956 magazine article — "The G Engines Are Coming" — listing the top aerospace companies of the day (Martin, Lear, Bell, Convair) as actively pursuing anti-gravity within years. Martin VP [[george-trimble]] predicted the timeline would resemble the Manhattan Project. Cook filed it away until he tracked down Trimble for an interview — only to receive a message back that Trimble was "really quite spooked" and refused to speak, "now or ever." Cook calls this the pivotal moment: "If I wasn't interested before, I am now."

## Nazi Programs and Kammler-Stab

Cook traced the anti-gravity lineage back through Nazi Germany — specifically to the [[kammler-stab]] program run by SS General [[hans-kammler]] in Czechoslovakia and Poland. Cook visited the Schauberger family archive, where they showed him [[victor-schauberger]]'s diary from 1942 (while Schauberger was in Jonastal/Yonet, Czechoslovakia): "What I am doing is secret" — and all locations Schauberger noted in the diary map directly onto the documented Kammler-Stab network of facilities.

Cook also visited the Wenceslaus mine site in Poland with [[igor-witkowski]] — seeing the hexagonal concrete test rig and extensive cabling infrastructure. Cook's own conclusion: the facility was most likely a German nuclear research site (beyond the Heisenberg program) rather than the time-dilation Bell device Witkowski claimed.

## Ben Rich and Skunk Works

Cook interviewed [[ben-rich]] (Skunk Works director) multiple times. Key moments:
- Rich described how Skunk Works had "contemplated burying" the stealth program entirely — locking it in a metaphorical warehouse "like Raiders of the Lost Ark" — before proceeding
- When Cook asked Rich how the technology to "take ET home" would work, Rich replied: "How does ESP work? All points in space and time are connected. That's how it works." — then walked away. Cook: "I wasn't expecting a question. I was expecting him to just say something."
- Rich's deathbed statement (to John Andrews, Testors): "We already have the means to travel among the stars, but these technologies are locked up in black projects, and it would take an act of God to ever get them out to benefit humanity."

Cook's assessment of Rich: "He talked in parables. He talked in analogies."

## The ASTRA Incident

During a mid-1990s visit to Lockheed's Palmdale headquarters to interview Skunk Works head [[jack-gordon]], Cook was escorted through the classified headquarters building. On leaving, he noticed the designation **ASTRA** on an org chart above all currently acknowledged aircraft. When he asked his escort about it, he was immediately removed. The visit date was April 1st — leading Cook to later wonder whether the whole thing was a deliberate disinformation breadcrumb.

A subsequent Skunk Works director confirmed: the incident was "very well known" internally; ASTRA "hasn't flown yet" and "will make an appearance sometime soon." As of the G7Ns4Aq1tVc recording (2024), it has not appeared publicly. See [[astra-program]].

## The Aurora Investigation

Cook and colleague [[bill-sweetman]] ran one of the most comprehensive public investigations of the classified [[aurora-aircraft-program]] — a supposed Mach-5 SR-71 replacement tested at Area 51. Their evidence included USGS sonic booms tracking toward Area 51, [[chris-gibson]]'s North Sea sighting, British MOD documents, and General [[george-muellner]]'s retirement-day gap puzzle.

## Ning Lee

Cook personally interviewed [[ning-lee]] at a 2003 Washington DC conference on high-frequency gravitational waves. He recalls she had "a view that this was science and technology that should be for the people." Within about a year of their meeting, Lee "just disappeared and no one knew what happened to her" — shortly after AC Gravity LLC received a DOD contract and security clearance. See [[ac-gravity-llc]].
