# Bob Wood

Dr. Bob Wood was an aerospace engineer and UFO document researcher best known for co-curating — with his son [[ryan-wood]] — the **Majestic Documents** collection: the most extensive privately held set of alleged [[majestic-12]]-era papers. He died before the GQlWf54K_7Y interview; Dolan refers to him as "Ryan Wood's late father."

## Majestic Documents Work

Bob and Ryan Wood assembled and subjected to forensic analysis hundreds of alleged government documents spanning the 1940s–1960s, related to UFO crash retrievals and the alleged MJ-12 oversight structure. Crucially, the Wood collection is physically distinct from the MJ12 documents that surfaced in 1984 on photographic negatives (sent to filmmaker [[jamie-shandera]] from near [[kirtland-afb]]): the Majestic Documents are original paper documents, printed on period paper, studied through forensic means (paper aging, typeface dating, ink analysis, period-appropriate formatting conventions).

[[richard-dolan]]'s assessment:
> "If this is a hoax, who created this? They printed on actual paper. Ryan Wood's collection — his late father Dr. Bob Wood and he — it's about an inch and a half thick of documents printed on actual paper of that era. Who created all this? The level of detail, the sophistication, the period accuracy across multiple decades of documents... it rules out a lone actor."

The collection includes an alleged **FDR letter** referencing extraterrestrials, and an **Einstein-Oppenheimer letter** discussing the UFO phenomenon — both debated but considered by Dolan to contain "truth in them."

It also includes the **"burned memo"** — a document referencing the code name "Lancer" (Kennedy's Secret Service designation) and language about "wet work" if the subject became "too dangerous." This document predates Kennedy's assassination and is central to the [[jfk-assassination-ufo]] evidence chain.

## Credibility and Caveats

Dolan does not assert full authenticity for the entire Wood collection; he notes the MJ12 document question remains "genuinely uncertain." His position: the sophistication and scope of the material makes lone-actor hoaxing implausible, and the forensic work the Woods conducted (as opposed to relying only on photographic negatives) raises the evidentiary bar significantly.
