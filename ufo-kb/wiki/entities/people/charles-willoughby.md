# Charles Willoughby

Major General Charles A. Willoughby served as General Douglas MacArthur's G2 — chief of military intelligence — throughout World War II and the occupation of Japan. In accounts of the Yamashita gold discovery and the formation of the [[anderson-trust-gold]], Willoughby is the figure who flew with Edward Lansdale to Washington to brief President Truman on the recovered treasure found in the Philippines.

## Background

Willoughby was one of MacArthur's most trusted subordinates, nicknamed "my little fascist" by MacArthur due to his conservative political views. After the war he remained involved in intelligence and anticommunist networks. His role in the Yamashita gold chain places him at the precise moment when the discovery was elevated from a military field matter to a covert presidential-level policy question.

## Notable Claims / Role

The briefing Willoughby and Lansdale brought to Truman — relayed through [[clark-clifford]] — reportedly set in motion the decision by [[henry-stimson]] to assemble the small group of establishment figures ([[robert-lovett]], [[robert-anderson]], [[john-j-mccloy]]) who would manage the covert trust built around the recovered gold. Willoughby is thus the critical link between the field discovery and the creation of the black-budget infrastructure allegedly funded by it.
