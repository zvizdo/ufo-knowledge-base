# Barry Goldwater

US Senator from Arizona (1953–65, 1969–87) and 1964 Republican presidential candidate. Also served as a Major General in the Air Force Reserve, giving him higher institutional access than most members of Congress. His pursuit of UAP information at Wright-Patterson represents one of the most credible institutional-access attempts on record.

## Notable Claims

In a 1994 Larry King interview, Goldwater stated on the record that he believed the US government possessed knowledge of UFOs and that this information could be found at [[wright-patterson]]:

> "I think the government does know. I can't back that up but I think that at Wright-Patterson field if you could get into certain places you'd find out what the Air Force and the government knows about UFOs. Reportedly a spaceship landed, it was all hushed up, quieted."

He also described his direct attempt to access the restricted areas: he called [[curtis-lemay]] — then sitting Chief of Staff of the Air Force — and asked to enter a room at Wright-Patterson where secret technology was allegedly stored. LeMay's reaction was extraordinary:

> "He got mad and hell at me, cussed me out — said 'Don't ever ask me that question again.'"

The vehemence of LeMay's reaction, from one of the most powerful military figures in America to a sitting Senator and presidential candidate, is cited as significant evidence that something real was being protected.

## Credibility Assessment

Goldwater held USAF Reserve commission at Major General rank, had the political standing of a presidential nominee, and had direct access to LeMay. His account of LeMay's reaction is firsthand. The exchange is cited in [[5udx_SDdL3Y]] as evidence that [[wright-patterson]]'s restricted areas contained material serious enough that the Air Force Chief of Staff would personally and angrily deflect a Senator's request.
