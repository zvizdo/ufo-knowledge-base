# Vasily Smyslov

7th World Chess Champion (1957–1958). Soviet-era grandmaster. His identity as "Vasilei Smith" in the Ilyumzhinov transcript is uncertain — the transcript is auto-generated and "Smith" is likely a phonetic distortion of "Smyslov." The description fits: very tall (~2m), Soviet-era world chess champion, played matches in Germany against Hübner (Robert Hübner is a German grandmaster whose career spans the 1970s–1990s), and was active at world championship ceremonies in Moscow under Ilyumzhinov's FIDE presidency.

Other candidates include Victor Korchnoi (who also played Hübner in Germany). This should be verified if Ilyumzhinov is ever asked to clarify. The incident itself is secondhand regardless.

## The Alien Encounter (Hearsay — Ilyumzhinov)

At the opening ceremony of a world chess championship in the Kremlin, this champion approached [[kirsan-ilyumzhinov]] at the banquet, pulled him aside, and asked privately: "Kersonen, tell me. Did you actually meet them? Just answer yes or no." Ilyumzhinov said yes. The champion turned to his wife and said "Can we publish the recordings now?"

He then disclosed: in the 1970s, before a match in Germany against Hübner, while alone in his hotel room analyzing the game and drinking hot tea to treat a cold, a translucent entity appeared before him — "not a physical being but more like an image, a translucent entity." It started moving chess pieces on the board, demonstrating the entire match. The next day, the game played out move-for-move identically to what the entity had shown him. He documented it in his records. His wife "strictly advised him not to speak of it to anyone" — he was world chess champion at the time and feared psychiatric institutionalization.

When Ilyumzhinov asked why he hadn't spoken in the 50 years since, the champion replied: "If I had said anything, they would have taken me to a nuthouse." Then: "But I did speak up." Reply: "Well, you're the president. They probably wouldn't risk taking you there just yet."
