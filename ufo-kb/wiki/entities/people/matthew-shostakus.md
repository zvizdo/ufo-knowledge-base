# Matthew Shostakus

Physicist/researcher at the [[university-at-albany]], co-founder of [[u-albany-project-x]] (formerly UAPx) with [[kevin-knuth]]. Primary scientific collaborator and instrumental analysis specialist.

## Background

Knuth describes him as working "furiously" on neutron-activation analysis for the last couple months before the interview. He is also first author of the scientific paper on the 2021 Catalina Island field mission (*A Tear in the Sky* mission data, published 2024 — Knuth says "just this summer").

## Notable Technical Work

### Neutron-Activation Isotope Analysis
Shostakus developed a novel non-destructive technique for analyzing isotope ratios in UAP debris:
- Irradiate a small portion of the material with a neutron source
- The neutrons create heavier isotopes (by adding a neutron to existing atoms)
- Watch those heavier isotopes decay back to their original state
- From the decay pattern, infer the original isotope composition

**Advantage over mass spectrometry**: Non-destructive (the sample returns to its prior state); does not require dissolving material in acid. Works on thousands of atoms at a time rather than requiring bulk dissolution.

**Goal**: Find isotope ratios that don't occur naturally on Earth and don't match asteroid compositions — a potential "smoking gun" that a material is off-world or artificially processed.

### Materials Being Studied
- **[[arts-parts]]**: Art Bell's magnesium-bismuth nanolayer samples, allegedly from the Roswell crash
- Material from **Ukraine**
- Material from **Israel**
- Has had contact with government representatives about this work (specifics not shared)

### Catalina Island Mission (2021)
First author of the published scientific paper on the data collected during the five-day Laguna Beach / Catalina Island UAP observation mission. Mission identified the ISS as one observed object (correctly, through Shostakus's pixel analysis showing the ISS's size to within one foot). Discovered the flare camera's "objects falling into sea" was a known malfunction mode.

[[garry-nolan]] does comparable material analysis at Stanford using mass spectrometry.
