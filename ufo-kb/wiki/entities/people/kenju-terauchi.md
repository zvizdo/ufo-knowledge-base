# Kenju Terauchi

Captain of Japan Airlines. Piloted JAL Flight 1628, a 747 cargo freighter transporting Beaujolais Nouveau wine from Paris to Anchorage via the northern Pacific route, on November 17, 1986. The encounter he reported became one of the most rigorously documented UAP events in the public record due to the survival of military radar data.

## Background

A senior JAL captain at the time of the incident. After filing his official report, he was effectively fired and had his pilot's license revoked. He was later reinstated, several years after the original incident.

## Notable Claims

- Initial encounter: two rectangular craft appeared in front of the 747 with lights scanning vertically into the cockpit. Crew could feel heat from the illumination.
- Main encounter: an enormous walnut-shaped object, roughly four 747s in length (by his estimation, ~aircraft carrier scale), appeared and paced the plane for ~45 minutes at a distance of ~7 km, moving from one side of the plane to the other and back.
- When the object was positioned directly in front of the aircraft, it was so large that it blocked his entire field of view.
- He reported the encounter to ATC in real time and drew a detailed sketch of the object for Japanese television.

## Credibility Context

His account was broadcast on NBC Nightly News (Tom Brokaw/Connie Chung) after the incident. Military height-finding radar independently tracked the object. [[john-callahan]] (FAA chief of accidents) confirmed the radar return. [[daniel-koumbi]] later analyzed the radar data and found accelerations of 9,000–11,000 Gs. [[kevin-knuth]] calls JAL 1628 his "highest-conviction" UAP case.
