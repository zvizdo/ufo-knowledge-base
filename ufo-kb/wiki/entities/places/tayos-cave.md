# Tayos Cave

## Description

A cave system in the Ecuadorian Amazon known in local indigenous tradition. Gained international attention through claims (associated with Erich von Däniken's *The Gold of the Gods*, 1972) that it contains metallic artifacts of apparently pre-technological or non-human manufacture — a "library" of metal plates with unknown inscriptions.

[[luis-felipe-san-salvador]] reports that local people in the region widely know this as a real place containing real artifacts.

## Neil Armstrong's Expedition

After returning from the Moon (Apollo 11, 1969), [[neil-armstrong]] participated in a BBC-accompanied expedition to Ecuador to search for the Tayos Cave. He failed to find it. According to [[luis-felipe-san-salvador]], Armstrong was deliberately sent in the wrong direction by San Salvador's father, who held the real coordinates.

The fact that the first man on the Moon made searching for Tayos his next significant expedition is treated as a meaningful signal by the alternative archaeology community — suggesting Armstrong had serious reasons to believe the cave contained something important.

## San Salvador's Claim

San Salvador is an Ecuadorian filmmaker who claims to descend from 16th-century conquistadors. He says his family has inherited maps pointing to both El Dorado and the Tayos Cave, going back to the era of Atahuallpa's ransom. He conducts annual Amazon expeditions to find it. Jesse Michaels proposed a joint expedition with [[luke-caverns]] and San Salvador.
