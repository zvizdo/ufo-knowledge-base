# Roswell Army Air Field

Roswell Army Air Field (RAAF) was a US Army Air Forces installation in Roswell, New Mexico, operational from 1941 to 1947 (when the Air Force became independent) and continuing as Roswell Air Force Base and later [[walker-air-force-base]] until its closure in 1967.

**509th Bomb Group home**: RAAF was the postwar home of the [[509th-bomb-group]] — the unit that dropped the atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki and remained the only nuclear-capable bomber unit in the world in 1947. This made Roswell Army Air Field, despite its relatively modest size, the most strategically significant air installation in the United States at the time of the Roswell incidents.

**Roswell Incident**: On July 8, 1947, RAAF public information officer Lt. Walter Haut issued a press release (authorized by base commander Col. William Blanchard) stating that the base had recovered a "flying disc." This press release — retracted within hours — is the starting point of the [[1947-roswell]] story. The base's facilities, personnel, and security apparatus were responsible for whatever was actually recovered, transported, and classified.

**Security culture**: The 509th's nuclear weapons culture meant extreme compartmentalization and secrecy were normal operational parameters at RAAF — making a cover-up of unusual recovered materials structurally straightforward within the base's existing security culture. Whether that cover-up involved a weather/Mogul balloon, a classified aircraft, or something more exotic, RAAF had both the motivation and the institutional capacity to manage it.

**Later history**: Following Air Force independence (1947), RAAF became Roswell Air Force Base and was later renamed Walker AFB. It closed as an active military installation in 1967 and is now the Roswell International Air Center (civilian airport).
