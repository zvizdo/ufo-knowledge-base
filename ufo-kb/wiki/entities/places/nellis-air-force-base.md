# Nellis Air Force Base

Major USAF installation near Las Vegas, Nevada, administratively responsible for a vast network of gunnery and test ranges in the Nevada desert numbered Areas 1 through 60. In [[charles-hall]]'s account, Nellis is his official posting (March 1965 – May 1967) and the command authority that administered the [[indian-springs]] Gunnery Ranges — including Areas 53 and 54 ("Dreamland"), where the [[tall-whites]] maintained their base.

## Significance in Hall's Account

Hall draws a geography that contradicts popular usage:
- [[area-51]] = Groom Lake = merely the entry point/administrative designation for the numbered range system
- "The Areas" runs from 1 to 60
- **Dreamland** = Areas 53 and 54 specifically — the northern Indian Springs Valley where the Tall White base was located

The Nellis base commander's authority nominally ran over all of these ranges. However, Hall's actual interactions and duties were governed by an unusual compartmentalized arrangement — Air Police could not approach within a mile of Hall's weather stations, and his commanders had to check in before and after any contact with him.

The Nellis weather station bathrooms were used by the Tall Whites on a regular basis — they selected them because they were the cleanest facilities available near their base.

## 1994 Nellis Range UAP / Jason Sanss Encounter

In August 1994, [[jason-sanss]] (USAF Master Sergeant, 67th Intelligence Group) was stationed at Nellis when a UAP flew past the "schoolhouse" training area and descended into the valley. This is the basis for the publicly available "Nellis Range UFO" video, in which air traffic control confirms it cannot identify the aircraft type. Sanss's private account — reported by [[james-fox]] in [[the-program-fox]] — goes further: a face-to-face encounter with a figure that "did not appear to be human" wearing military-style dress. Fox draws a speculative connection between this and [[gary-mckinnon]]'s "non-terrestrial officers" list.
