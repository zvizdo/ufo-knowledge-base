# Holmesburg Prison

Philadelphia prison (operated 1896–1995) that became infamous as the site of Dr. Albert Kligman's University of Pennsylvania dermatology experiments on inmates from 1951 through 1974. Kligman conducted experiments on prisoners sponsored by the US Army, Dow Chemical, Johnson & Johnson, and — per declassified records — the CIA's MK-Ultra-adjacent program apparatus. Experiments included the application of dioxin (TCDD), radioactive isotopes, and experimental pharmaceuticals to prisoners who were not given meaningful informed consent.

**Albert Kligman**: Kligman was a University of Pennsylvania dermatologist who treated Holmesburg as a research convenience — a captive population with no ability to refuse. He is also the inventor of Retin-A (tretinoin), one of the most profitable dermatological compounds in pharmaceutical history, developed from his prison experiments. His career illustrates how the same researcher can produce both genuinely useful pharmaceutical innovations and deeply unethical experimentation, often using the same institutional platform.

**CIA connection**: CIA-funded experiments at Holmesburg under the MK-Ultra umbrella included testing mind-altering drugs and psychoactive compounds on prisoners without their knowledge or consent. These experiments were part of the same classified program as the Subproject 68 (Ewen Cameron at McGill) and other MK-Ultra nodes documented in declassified Senate records.

**KB significance**: Holmesburg is cited alongside [[mk-ultra]], [[tuskegee-experiment]], and [[operation-northwoods]] as a documented instance of US government-affiliated human experimentation on US citizens — building the evidentiary base for the prior-probability argument about UAP-adjacent experimentation. The Holmesburg case specifically establishes that medical researchers at major research universities conducted CIA-funded experimentation on institutionalized populations, under the cover of legitimate pharmaceutical research.

**Lawsuit and legacy**: Holmesburg prison survivors sued Kligman and the University of Pennsylvania in 2000. The case was settled without admission of wrongdoing. Kligman died in 2010; the University of Pennsylvania posthumously stripped him of an endowed lectureship in 2022.
