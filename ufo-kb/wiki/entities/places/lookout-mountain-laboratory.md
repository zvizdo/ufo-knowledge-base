# Lookout Mountain Laboratory

A classified installation in **Laurel Canyon**, Los Angeles, a few blocks from [[thomas-townsend-brown]]'s residence on Wonderland Drive during 1942–1945. Officially described at the time as a "World War II radar command," subsequent declassification has revealed it was actually the **primary film laboratory for nuclear test footage** from the Manhattan Project.

## Nuclear Film Function

All high-speed and classified film from US nuclear weapons tests — including atmospheric detonations — was processed and analyzed at Lookout Mountain. The lab worked under heavy camouflage ("a canopy of camouflage" per Jesse Michaels's visit) and operated under deep secrecy. When a nuclear device detonates, no electronics function in the first few seconds — meaning **film** (not electronic sensors) was the only way to capture what appeared in the immediate aftermath. This made the Lookout Mountain film analysis function uniquely important for observing UAP/anomalous phenomena that appeared during nuclear tests.

## Brown's Proximity

Brown's documented residence on Wonderland Drive, Laurel Canyon during 1942–1945 places him immediately adjacent to the lab at the precise moment when Navy radar expertise (Brown's confirmed specialty per the 1943 FBI file) would have been invaluable. The FBI file showing Brown living at this address during this period possibly corroborates [[paul-schatzkin]]'s contention that Brown went from the Navy directly to Martin Vega Corporation in LA — rather than staying in Norfolk for the Philadelphia Experiment as alternative historians assert.

Jesse Michaels and Paul Schatzkin visited the site together while making the [[RTEWLSTyUic]] documentary.

## UAP Connection

If UAP/anomalous light phenomena appeared in nuclear test films — as [[robert-hastings]] and others have documented via nuclear-site witness accounts — then Lookout Mountain was the facility where those films were analyzed. Brown's proximity and radar expertise create an obvious operational reason for his presence: he may have been reviewing such film data and attempting to connect anomalous signatures to his electrogravitics research.
