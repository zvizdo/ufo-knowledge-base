# Osireion

## Description

Located at [[abydos]] in Upper Egypt, behind and 30 feet lower into the ground than Seti I's 19th-dynasty mortuary temple (~1290 BC). The structural contrast is dramatic:

- **Seti I's temple** (above): modestly-sized limestone blocks, decorated with hieroglyphics in classic dynastic style
- **The Osireion** (below): enormous red Aswan granite blocks — some of the largest in all of Egypt — no hieroglyphics, completely different construction style

The mainstream Egyptological explanation is that Seti I built both structures. [[luke-caverns]] finds this implausible and argues the Osireion predates Seti I by thousands of years, belonging to the same unknown pre-dynastic builder responsible for the **Valley Temple** adjacent to the [[great-sphinx]] at Giza.

## The Pre-Dynastic Argument

Three lines of evidence:

1. **Material and scale**: The granite blocks are far larger than anything else in the same temple complex — only two blocks of this size could fit in a large room. This matches the Valley Temple next to the Sphinx, also built with enormous red granite megaliths.
2. **No hieroglyphics**: Like the 4th-dynasty pyramid interiors, the Osireion has no inscriptions — yet it is supposedly from the 19th dynasty (~1290 BC), by which time elaborate temple decoration was universal.
3. **Depth**: The Osireion sits ~30 feet lower than the dynastic-era temple — possibly because it was already ancient and partially buried when Seti I built his temple above it.

The "popular belief that it's 12,000 years old" is not based on evidence; Luke's claim is simply that it predates Seti I significantly and shares a common architect with the Valley Temple — a more conservative but still revisionary position.

## Connection to the Sphinx Complex

The Valley Temple adjacent to the [[great-sphinx]] at [[giza-plateau]] is also constructed from enormous red granite blocks in the same megalithic style, with no hieroglyphics. Luke argues these two structures — separated by ~500 km — were built by the same civilization or school, likely the same pre-dynastic people who first shaped the Sphinx.
