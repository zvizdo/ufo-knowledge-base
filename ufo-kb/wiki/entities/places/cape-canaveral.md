# Cape Canaveral

Cape Canaveral Air Force Station (CCAFS) is the US Air Force's primary eastern space launch range on Florida's Space Coast, collocated with Kennedy Space Center. It is administered under the [[45th-space-wing]] and has been the launch site for the Space Shuttle, Atlas, Titan, Falcon, and many other rockets since the 1950s. The facility's space-program identity also provides effective cover for classified programs operating on the base.

## History

Cape Canaveral began as the **Banana River Naval Air Station** during World War II. Notable events:
- **Flight 19** (1945): The ill-fated Navy training flight that became the defining Bermuda Triangle story departed from what was then Banana River NAS; a PBY rescue aircraft that also disappeared took off from the same flight ops area. See [[1945-flight-19]].
- **Joint Long Range Proving Ground** (1949): post-war designation; later renamed the **Eastern Launch Range** and home of the **Eastern Space and Missile Center**.
- Post-war transition: converted to Air Force control; became the primary test range for early missile programs. EG&G's prime-contractor relationship dates to the [[atomic-energy-commission]] era.
- 1960s–onward: home to Mercury, Gemini, Apollo, Atlas, Titan, Saturn V, Space Shuttle, and now Falcon programs

## EG&G as Prime Contractor

[[egng]] served as prime contractor at Cape Canaveral, responsible for all ground maintenance, building management, roads, traffic infrastructure, and healthcare clinics. Every other contractor operating on the base — Boeing, Raytheon, Martin Marietta (pre-1995), Lockheed — leased facilities and support services from EG&G.

The arrangement gave EG&G an unmatched panoramic view of all operations and the natural ability to compartmentalize contractors from one another. [[greg-rogers]] notes that to enter Cape Canaveral Air Force Station, even a three-star general on vacation needed a current clearance and need-to-know. The space program identity allows activities unrelated to the space program to operate under its security umbrella — "If you put Project X on Cape Canaveral, anyone who asks just assumes it's space program related."

## The 1992 Saucer Sighting

During an EG&G-escorted building tour in spring 1992, [[greg-rogers]] was briefly left alone in a corridor when his escort departed ahead of him. In that window, a USAF major showed Rogers a **closed-circuit TV feed of a pearly-white saucer craft** levitating, rotating, and maneuvering inside a hangar. The craft bore US Air Force markings. The major's explanation for the unconventional design: **"We got it from them."**

Rogers speculates the CCTV feed could have been sourcing from Lockheed Skunk Works, [[area-51]], [[wright-patterson]], or another undisclosed location on the cape itself. See [[1992-cape-canaveral-saucer]].

## Connection to Bermuda Triangle Anomalies

[[greg-rogers]] notes that pilots and navigators operating out of Cape Canaveral were trained to be aware of compass unreliability over certain areas of the Atlantic due to magnetic deviations — these areas were marked on official maps. Rogers' personal hypothesis: ancient metallic meteorites on the ocean floor create localized magnetic fields. The same anomalies are frequently cited in Bermuda Triangle disappearance accounts. One unexplained event Rogers experienced personally: a search-and-rescue flight that arrived 10 minutes later than expected with no navigational explanation.
