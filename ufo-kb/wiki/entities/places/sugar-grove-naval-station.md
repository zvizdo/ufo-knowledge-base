# Sugar Grove Naval Station

A classified US Navy signals intelligence (SIGINT) installation at Sugar Grove, West Virginia (designated NAVCAMS EASTLANT), used during the Cold War to monitor Soviet satellites. It became the paradigmatic demonstration that [[remote-viewing]] can overshoot its assigned target and lock onto something of intelligence significance nearby.

## The Sugar Grove Incident

[[kit-green]], a CIA officer overseeing the [[stanford-research-institute]] remote viewing research contract, designed a validation test to convince CIA skeptics of the program's legitimacy. He gave a colleague's grid coordinates for a private mountain cabin to [[pat-price]] as a blind test target.

[[pat-price]] did not describe the cabin. Instead, he described the **Sugar Grove Naval Station** — approximately 100 yards down the road — in significant operational detail:
- The installation's physical layout and function (monitoring Soviet satellites)
- **Top-secret codewords** from Sugar Grove's classified programs: **Q-ball, nine-ball, eight-ball** (billiard-themed code names) — none of which Price could have had prior exposure to

The consequence was severe: the test intended to validate the program instead triggered a security investigation against [[kit-green]]'s own CIA office. "Every law enforcement person in California showed up at SRI" (attributed to Puthoff) because the Navy was furious about classified installation details appearing in civilian research transcripts.

[[skip-atwater]] describes this event as an "Awakening" for the SRI scientists — the realization that something was operating beyond their controlled protocol. The viewer had spontaneously "drifted" from the assigned target to an adjacent classified facility of higher strategic significance.

## Significance for Remote Viewing Research

The Sugar Grove incident established several operational principles:
- **Non-locality and target drift**: remote viewers may be attracted to targets of intelligence significance even when those targets are not the assigned coordinates
- **Signal-to-noise problem**: the "noise" (cabin) was overwhelmed by the "signal" (classified SIGINT station) in Price's perception
- **Institutional risk**: the program's validity was demonstrated in a way that created security exposure — a paradox that followed the research throughout its existence

[[skip-atwater]] later applied the lesson from the Sugar Grove drift in designing Project 8200: if viewers can spontaneously find more significant targets, what happens if they are explicitly pointed at alleged UAP-related locations (the four mountains identified by [[pat-price]])?

## Current Status

Sugar Grove Naval Station was officially closed in 2015. Its Cold War history as a classified listening post has since been partially declassified.
