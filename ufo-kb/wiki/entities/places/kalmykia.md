# Republic of Kalmykia

Federal subject of the Russian Federation located in the South of European Russia, on the western shore of the Caspian Sea. The only European Buddhist republic in Russia and the only Mongolic-speaking entity in Europe. Capital: Elista.

## History and Identity

The Kalmyk people are descendants of the Oirat Mongols — the western branch of Genghis Khan's empire. They are Tangrians by origin: Tangri (also Tengri) is the supreme deity in the sky religion of Mongolian and Turkic peoples. Kalmyk word: "Tangri" = sky. [[kirsan-ilyumzhinov]]: "We Kalmiks believe in the great sky. That is why I love space."

The Jangar epic — a foundational Kalmyk oral poem passed down over millennia without writing, through songs — describes the underworld in detail: how creatures live there, how they communicate, how they sometimes come to the surface. Ilyumzhinov cites this as ancient evidence for the [[subterranean-civilizations]] hypothesis.

Genghis Khan's Kalmyk cavalry rode into Paris in 1814 on camels after defeating Napoleon, witnessed by Balzac. Alexander Dumas spent 3 months in the Kalmyk steppes and wrote extensively about the Kalmyk people.

## Under Ilyumzhinov

[[kirsan-ilyumzhinov]] was elected president in 1993 at age 30, becoming the youngest president in post-Soviet space. He governed until 2010. Key innovations:
- **Chess in schools:** Introduced chess as a compulsory subject for all children from the earliest grades
- **Ethnoplanetary thinking curriculum:** Schools gave lectures on "ethnoplanetary thinking" — expanding children's consciousness beyond national borders to planetary responsibility
- **New constitution (1993):** Article 10 declared all citizens of Kalmykia responsible for all global problems — ozone holes, African hunger, asteroids, armed conflicts — not just issues within Kalmykia's borders. "We must pass this planet on in the same condition in which we received it."

## Tangrianism and the UFO/Space Connection

Kalmyk spiritual tradition (Tangrianism) is sky-oriented: looking upward, drawing energy from space and stars. Genghis Khan invoked the great sky (Tangri) before every campaign. This cosmological orientation, Ilyumzhinov argues, predisposes Kalmyks toward belief in space civilizations and openness to contact — unlike cultures that locate the divine within or below.
