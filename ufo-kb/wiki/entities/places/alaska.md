# Alaska

Alaska is the largest US state by area, located in the extreme northwest of North America, bordered by Canada to the east and south, the Pacific to the south, and the Arctic Ocean to the north. Its proximity to Russia across the Bering Strait made it a primary Cold War strategic monitoring location, with extensive radar infrastructure, military installations, and surveillance assets.

**UAP hotspot status**: Alaska has a documented concentration of UAP incidents above what would be expected from its low population density:

- **JAL Flight 1628 (1986)**: The [[1986-jal-1628]] encounter — the most radar-documented UAP event in the public record — occurred over Alaska. Japan Airlines 747 captain [[kenju-terauchi]] observed walnut-shaped and rectangular craft for 45 minutes, tracked by military radar; [[john-callahan]] preserved the data.

- **Nathan Twining connection**: General [[nathan-twining]], whose 1947 letter to Air Force leadership described UAP as real and extraordinary, reportedly ran aerial observation programs over Alaska — suggesting high-level military attention to UAP activity in Alaskan airspace predating the modern disclosure era.

**Strategic context**: Alaska's position as the closest US territory to Russia made it the primary early warning zone during the Cold War. NORAD and military radar infrastructure was dense; any anomalous radar returns in Alaskan airspace would receive high-quality attention from trained personnel. This makes Alaskan UAP reports disproportionately credible in terms of instrumented verification.

**Indigenous context**: Alaska's indigenous communities (Yupik, Inuit, Athabascan, etc.) have traditional accounts of anomalous phenomena — lights, craft, and encounters — that predate the modern UAP research era.
