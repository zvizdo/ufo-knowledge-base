# Nassau, Bahamas

A recurring location in the Townsend Brown story and a possible hub for private intelligence operations tied to the [[caroline-group]]. Nassau serves as the nexus point between Brown's classified work, [[robert-sarbacher]]'s UAP secrecy role, MI6 spy [[william-stevenson]]'s Caribbean operations, and the alleged Nassau Group time-travel program.

## Caroline Group Hub

The yacht *Caroline* (owned by Eldridge Reeves Johnson of Victor Talking Machine Company / RCA Records) made Nassau a regular port. When the Caroline docked here, [[william-stevenson]] would come aboard for meetings. The Caroline Group's intelligence network used Nassau as a coordination point outside the visibility of J. Edgar Hoover's FBI.

Brown and Sarbacher's frequent Nassau meetings — documented in Schatzkin's biography through Linda Brown's account — fit this pattern. The 1933 Smithsonian expedition aboard the Caroline also stopped in Nassau before proceeding to its "official" scientific mission, suggesting the stop was functionally a meeting point rather than a waypoint.

## The Nassau Time-Travel Group

[[tim-taylor]] (American Cosmic's "Tyler D") reportedly told his family's friend Chris Bledsoe's son that he was in **an elite group called the Nassau Group** — headed by [[thomas-townsend-brown]] — with access to time-travel technology. This claim connects Nassau to Brown's lifelong obsession with time travel and the gravity-time coupling in general relativity.

Linda Brown confirms her father traveled frequently to Nassau and that the meetings there involved "shadowy people that we don't really know who they are."

## Nassau Group — Bledsoe Firsthand Confirmation

[[chris-bledsoe]] confirmed in [[bM18PJY6_Zc]] that [[tim-taylor]] mentioned the Nassau Group to him directly early in their relationship (~2008), suggesting Bledsoe might need to "go hide out there" for protection when the UAP story was at peak security classification. This is Bledsoe's firsthand account of the Nassau Group claim — corroborating but distinct from Ryan Bledsoe's account that Taylor told him and his father about the time-travel group's existence and Brown's role as president.

Tim Taylor's biography *Launch Fever* also describes him flying cargo jets between Florida and Nassau and describes time as moving differently there — interpreted by Jesse Michaels as possible coded reference to the time-travel group.
