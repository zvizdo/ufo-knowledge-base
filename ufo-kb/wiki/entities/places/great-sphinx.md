# Great Sphinx

## Description

A monolithic limestone statue — human head on a lion's body — carved from a large natural limestone outcropping at the eastern edge of the [[giza-plateau]]. The monument faces east, toward the sunrise. Its enclosure walls and the body itself show distinctive erosion patterns that are the central evidence in the debate over its age.

## The Pre-Dynastic Argument

[[robert-schoch]] and [[john-anthony-west]] demonstrated that the erosion on the Sphinx's *body* (vertical runnels, rounded profiles on the enclosure walls) is consistent with heavy precipitation over long periods — not the wind/sand erosion that dominates in the current desert climate. For this water erosion to have formed, the Sphinx must have been exposed during a significantly wetter climate epoch in Egypt, which ended well before the conventional 4th-dynasty date (~2500 BC). Schoch's geological estimates push initial construction to at least 7000 BC, possibly earlier.

[[luke-caverns]] calls this "probably the strongest argument in all of Egypt of an exceptionally old artifact or monument." Even [[graham-hancock]] has converged on the idea that while the pyramids are 4th dynasty, the Sphinx (and possibly the [[osireion]] and Valley Temple) are older.

## The Limestone Outcropping Origin

The Sphinx was carved from a pre-existing massive limestone outcropping that would have been visible to Nile traffic long before dynastic Egypt. The height of the Sphinx's head (representing the original top of the outcropping) is so prominent that it is implausible this feature was "accidentally discovered" during Khafre's pyramid complex construction — the mainstream story. It was almost certainly a venerated landmark for a very long time before being shaped into its current form.

## Tunnels and Underground

[[zahi-hawass]] has officially acknowledged three independent tunnels associated with the Sphinx. [[edgar-cayce]]'s 1940s claim that a Hall of Records lies beneath the Sphinx prompted 1990s drilling — quickly abandoned with minimal methodology.

[[filippo-biondi]]'s [[beyond-protocol]] SAR Doppler tomography ([[zrmG2jBckgk]]) detected a **single cube structure descending beneath the Sphinx**, connected to the same underground network as the tubes beneath [[khafre-pyramid]] and [[menkaure-pyramid]]. [[trevor-grassi]] independently identified shafts between the Sphinx and Khafre as potential secondary entrances to the underground network.
