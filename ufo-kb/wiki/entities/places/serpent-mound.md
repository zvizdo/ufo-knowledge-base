# Serpent Mound

## Description

Serpent Mound is a 1,348-foot-long prehistoric effigy mound located on a plateau in Adams County, Ohio. Constructed in the form of a serpent with an open mouth appearing to swallow or cradle an oval form, it is the largest known serpent effigy in the world. Its construction is attributed to either the Adena culture (c. 300 BCE – 100 CE) or the Fort Ancient culture (c. 1000–1650 CE); scholarly consensus has shifted between these attributions.

The site is a National Historic Landmark and sits atop a crypto-explosion structure — possibly a meteorite impact crater — which has drawn attention from researchers interested in the intersection of astronomical events and sacred site placement.

## Archaeoastronomical Significance

Serpent Mound's alignment has been analyzed in relation to solstice and equinox events. The head of the serpent aligns with the summer solstice sunset; the curves of the body have been interpreted as encoding lunar standstill cycles. This positions it within a class of ancient structures — alongside Stonehenge, the Nazca Lines, and Egyptian monuments — where sophisticated astronomical knowledge appears embedded in large-scale earthwork construction.
