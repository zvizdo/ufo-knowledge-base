# Gran Sasso Laboratory

Italy's national underground physics laboratory, located 1.4 km beneath the Gran Sasso massif in the Apennines, accessible through a tunnel. The lab is "alive" — continuously ventilated and electrically serviced. Its principal physics experiment is the search for the **Majorana particle** (a particle that is its own antiparticle), which would demonstrate the existence of matter particles that are their own antiparticles — crucial for understanding why matter dominates over antimatter.

## Relevance to Giza Research

In 2024, [[filippo-biondi]] used the Gran Sasso laboratory as the highest-quality proof-of-concept scan for his [[beyond-protocol]] SAR Doppler tomography technique. Using a superior noise-averaging processing method (2 months of compute time), he successfully produced images of the lab's internal structures from satellite data. These images are significantly cleaner and more convincing than the original Giza pyramid scans from the 2022 paper.

Jesse Michaels describes the result as "transparent like a crystal" — the clearest validation that Biondi's technology can image known underground structures using satellite data alone. This was presented at the Malta 2024 [[beyond-protocol]] conference.

The quality gap between this scan and the original Giza pyramid scans is a central point of contention: Biondi acknowledges the difference is due to superior processing and computational resources, not a fundamental methodology issue.
