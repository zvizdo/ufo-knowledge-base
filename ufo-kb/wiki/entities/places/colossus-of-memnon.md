# Colossus of Memnon

Two massive ancient Egyptian stone statues (18th Dynasty, ~1350 BC traditionally) on the west bank of the Nile near Luxor, representing Pharaoh Amenhotep III. They stand ~18 meters tall and are among the largest surviving ancient stone monuments.

## Plasma Discharge Evidence

[[matt-lroy]] draws attention to burn marks on the statues that are present **only on the northeast-facing sides**. This directionality is significant:

- Random fire or ambient environmental weathering would produce omnidirectional marks.
- A directional burn pattern — northeast-only — suggests the heat source came from a specific direction.
- Lroy interprets this as evidence of a plasma discharge event associated with a CME, generating directed electromagnetic/thermal energy from the northeast.

This connects to [[robert-schoch]]'s [[plasma-discharge-theory]]: massive solar coronal mass ejections create atmospheric plasma that can heat or burn surfaces directionally.
