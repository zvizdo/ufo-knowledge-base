# Mount Nemrut

There are two peaks named Nemrut in Turkey:

1. **Nemrut Dağı (southeastern Turkey)**: Famous for massive ancient stone statues of gods/kings on its summit (~1st century BC, Commagene kingdom). A well-known tourist site.

2. **Nemrut southwest of Lake Van**: The site relevant to [[matt-lroy]]'s research. Features a massive crater from a violent volcanic explosion that Lroy dates to the Older or Younger Dryas period (~12,000–14,500 years ago).

## Significance in Lroy's Model

The Lake Van Nemrut's volcanic explosion is a candidate local cause for the regional [[vitrification-evidence|vitrification]] observed at [[kepkesi]] and other sites. A massive eruption could:
- Generate extreme atmospheric heat capable of melting exposed stone surfaces
- Produce an ash column blocking sunlight and cosmic rays (analogizing Mount St. Helens — a volcano whose 1980 eruption killed observers miles away)
- Align with the broader [[older-dryas]] / [[younger-dryas-impact|Younger Dryas]] catastrophe timeline

Lroy does not fully resolve whether the vitrification was caused by volcanic heat from Nemrut or by a broader [[plasma-discharge-theory|plasma discharge]] CME event — he treats both as potentially contributing.
