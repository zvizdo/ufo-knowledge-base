# Göbekli Tepe

Megalithic site in southeastern Turkey, currently dated to approximately 11,600 years ago (the oldest enclosures). The site features T-shaped limestone pillars up to 6 meters tall arranged in circles, with elaborate carved relief decoration.

## Pillar 43, Enclosure D — Astronomical Diagram of 12,500 BC

[[graham-hancock]]'s analysis of Pillar 43 in Enclosure D: the carvings — a scorpion (= Scorpius), a vulture with outstretched arms (= Sagittarius), and a circle on the wing of the vulture (= the sun) — form a coherent astronomical diagram of the summer solstice sky ~12,500–12,800 years ago.

Key details:
- The scorpion = the constellation Scorpius
- The vulture figure = the constellation Sagittarius (or a related figure; different cultures name it differently)
- The circle on the vulture's wing = the sun on the arm of that constellation at the summer solstice
- Using precession, this configuration resolves to ~12,500–12,800 years ago

This is significant because Enclosure D is dated to ~11,600 years ago — but its imagery memorializes a sky more than 1,000 years earlier. The builders of Göbekli Tepe regarded that earlier epoch (c. 12,500–12,800 years ago) as astronomically important — the same "First Time" (*Zep Tepi*) that Hancock argues is encoded at Giza through the [[orion-correlation-theory]] and [[great-sphinx]] orientation.

## Connected to Giza

Two independent civilizations (or one civilization with multiple sites) chose the same ~12,500 BC epoch as the reference point to encode in their most important monuments. This cross-site convergence is central to Hancock's argument for a [[lost-civilization-hypothesis]] — a common ancestral source distributing astronomical knowledge globally.
