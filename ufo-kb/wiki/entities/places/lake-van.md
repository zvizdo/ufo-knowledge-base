# Lake Van

A large lake in eastern Turkey (modern Van Province) surrounded by a cluster of megalithic archaeological sites that [[matt-lroy]] identifies as remnants of the pre-Flood advanced civilization. The region is distinct from the better-known Göbekli Tepe cluster (southeastern Turkey) and represents Lroy's most original fieldwork contribution.

## Key Sites in the Region

### [[kepkesi]]
Giant square basalt blocks (Mohs hardness ~7) with machine-cut surfaces and vitrified faces — stone surfaces melted to glass-like state. [[robert-schoch]] confirmed the vitrification during a site visit with Lroy. Basalt's extreme hardness means melting requires temperatures exceeding 2,000°C — ruling out ordinary fire. Lroy interprets this as plasma discharge evidence from a CME event.

### [[mount-nemrut]] (southwest of Lake Van)
Volcanic explosion during the Older/Younger Dryas period created a massive crater. Lroy argues the eruption produced extreme atmospheric heat events capable of causing regional vitrification, and its ash column could have blocked cosmic rays.

## Significance

The Lake Van region provides Lroy's physical evidence for [[vitrification-evidence|vitrification]] as the smoking gun of ancient CME events. Combined with [[robert-schoch]]'s [[plasma-discharge-theory]], the melted basalt surfaces suggest something beyond any known natural process short of a nuclear detonation or massive solar plasma event.

Mud-brick structures built atop buried basalt megaliths indicate later cultures built on top of — and deliberately concealed — earlier pre-Flood structures.
