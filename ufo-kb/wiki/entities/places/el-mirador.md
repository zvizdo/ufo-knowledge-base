# El Mirador

## Description

A pre-Classic Maya city in the remote Guatemalan jungle, ~2 hours by helicopter from the nearest access point. Contains some of the largest pyramids ever built, including La Danta and El Tigre. The scale is difficult to grasp — [[luke-caverns]] described walking down one pyramid, crossing a flat platform, and walking for *five minutes* before reaching the next slope: "For the last 5 minutes I was walking on the pyramid straight. That's how big it was."

The pyramid mass is comparable to the Great Pyramid of Giza in total volume.

## La Danta and La Iglesia (Ligra)

[[luke-caverns]] identifies La Danta and what he calls "Ligra" (possibly La Iglesia or another structure) as the two oldest pyramids in the Maya world — also the largest stones ever used in Maya construction. Crucially, they were built using the largest stones first — the *opposite* of how Maya construction typically evolved, where blocks got smaller over time. This mirrors the pattern at [[giza-plateau]] where the oldest structures used the largest blocks.

The stone interlocks are so massive that tree roots grow *flat against the surface* rather than penetrating between blocks — unlike most Maya pyramids where roots split the structure apart.

## Pattern: Oldest = Largest

Both at El Mirador and in Egypt, the oldest known monuments use the most massive construction. Luke finds this fascinating and puzzling — it suggests the earliest builders possessed more, not less, capability than their successors.
