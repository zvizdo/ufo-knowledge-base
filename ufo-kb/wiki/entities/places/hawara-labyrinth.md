# Hawara Labyrinth

An ancient underground complex at Hawara in the Faiyum region of Egypt, attributed to pharaoh Amenemhat III (12th dynasty, ~1850 BC). It was described in antiquity by multiple classical authors as one of the most extraordinary structures in the ancient world — and remains largely unexcavated.

## Classical Descriptions

- **Herodotus** (5th century BC): Described the labyrinth as surpassing in complexity even the pyramids; containing 3,000 rooms — 1,500 above ground and 1,500 below (the underground portion he was forbidden to enter). He called it "beyond all description."
- **Pliny the Elder**: Described it as a vast, incomprehensible structure with roofed corridors, courts, and inner rooms.
- **Strabo** and **Diodorus Siculus**: Both wrote accounts confirming its scale.

These accounts predate any known systematic archaeological excavation, yet describe a structure of extraordinary complexity.

## Current State

The surface area of the labyrinth has been located at Hawara. Ground-penetrating radar surveys have confirmed the presence of large underground structures. However, excavation has been stalled — blocked by Egyptian Ministry of Culture restrictions and by the presence of a modern village over part of the site.
