# Osiris Shaft

## Description

Located on the Khafre causeway at [[giza-plateau]], inconspicuous from the surface — a visitor would not know where it is without a guide. [[luke-caverns]] visited it with "Land of Kim" (a guide).

The shaft descends in three levels:

1. **Initial chamber**: Contains seven extending side-chambers of unknown purpose
2. **Second descending level**: Large ladder descent
3. **Third level (lowest)**: A chamber filled (partially) with water. In the center stands an elevated platform supporting a massive sarcophagus, raised above the water level. Today the rising water table has submerged the sarcophagus, but in antiquity it would have been visible: a sarcophagus on an island, surrounded by water, underground.

## The Herodotus Match

In the 5th century BC, the Greek historian Herodotus was told (but apparently didn't personally descend) of a great shaft beneath the pyramids containing "the tomb of a great king on an island surrounded by a lake of water." The Osiris Shaft — when discovered/re-excavated in the 1980s–90s — matches this description precisely.

[[zahi-hawass]] published a paper claiming sole credit for the discovery.

## Open Questions

- What was the shaft originally built for? The seven side-chambers at the first level have no identified function.
- How old is it? The shaft is associated with later-period use (Saite period, 26th dynasty) but its original construction date is unknown.
- Was the water deliberate — a ritual lake for Osirian resurrection rites?
