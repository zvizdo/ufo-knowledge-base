# Dulce, New Mexico

Small town on the Jicarilla Apache reservation in Rio Arriba County, northwestern New Mexico. Dulce became one of the most mythologized locations in UFO lore, allegedly housing a joint human-alien underground base beneath Archeleta Mesa on the reservation boundary.

## Origin of the Mythology

The Dulce underground base mythology originated substantially from two intertwined sources:

1. **Myrna Hansen's hypnotic regression (early 1980s)**: [[myrna-hansen]], an abductee whose case was investigated by [[paul-benowitz]], described being taken to an underground facility under hypnotic regression sessions conducted by [[leo-sprinkle]]. Benowitz interpreted this as confirming the existence of an alien base near Dulce/Archeleta Mesa. (Greg Bishop, firsthand research, [[WBTqsbwJyqU]])

2. **AFOSI amplification**: The Air Force Office of Special Investigations ([[afosi]]), running a disinformation campaign against Benowitz via [[rick-doty]], actively directed Benowitz's attention toward the Archeleta Mesa / Dulce area — away from [[kirtland-afb]] where his original observations had been made. This included taking Benowitz in a helicopter to the area and staging props (jeeps, water tanks) there to give him something to photograph and focus on.

3. **John Lear's fabrications**: [[john-lear]], who communicated with Benowitz after the initial AFOSI operation, propagated the "Dulce firefight" story — an alleged battle between humans and aliens at the underground base. Lear privately admitted to an interlocutor before an early 1990s congressional UFO hearing: "I made that up." (Greg Bishop, [[WBTqsbwJyqU]])

## Archeleta Mesa

The specific location most associated with the alleged base is Archeleta Mesa, which straddles the Colorado-New Mexico border on the Jicarilla Apache reservation. [[gabe-valdez]] — a New Mexico state trooper who investigated cattle mutilations in the area — found evidence of a real crash at or near the mesa during this period: burned brush, cleared debris, and a pen marked "US Government Property." Whether this was related to a stealth drone, classified aircraft, or something anomalous is unknown.

## Assessment

Greg Bishop's view: the underground base mythology is largely a product of genuine abductee testimony (Hansen), AFOSI-directed attention redirection, and deliberate fabrication (Lear). The real cattle mutilation activity in the area, investigated by Valdez, is a separate but related phenomenon that contributed to the broader mythology. [[greg-bishop]] does not dismiss the possibility that something genuine underlies the accounts but cannot separate signal from manufactured noise. (Greg Bishop, [[WBTqsbwJyqU]])
