# Karst Cave System (Giza Plateau)

Natural cave and tunnel network beneath the [[giza-plateau]], formed by gradual dissolution of the plateau's limestone bedrock by slightly acidic groundwater over geological timescales. Multiple researchers and engineers working below the plateau have encountered the system; its existence is not disputed, though its full extent is unknown.

## Known Evidence

- Hydrogen sulfide (H2S) gas has been documented emanating from the subsurface, encountered as an occupational hazard by Egyptian engineering crews working below the plateau.
- The [[osiris-shaft]] contains brackish water from an independent aquifer not connected to the Nile, consistent with a perched water system in karst voids.
- Transverse horizontal tunnels were revealed when the Osiris Shaft was pumped dry, suggesting interconnected passages rather than isolated pockets.
- The Egyptian government operates permanent pumping stations around the Giza plateau to manage subsurface hydrology — consistent with managing an active karst water system.

## Role in Jeffrey Drum's Chemical Manufacturing Hypothesis

[[jeffrey-drum|Jeffrey Drum]] identifies the karst cave system as the primary feedstock supply for the pyramid chemical complex. H2S rising naturally from the karst zone would, if directed through pyramid shaft systems, serve as the initial reactant for the proposed H2S → H2SO4 conversion chemistry. The pyramid shafts connecting down to the karst zone would function as passive intake conduits requiring no pumping infrastructure.

## Open Questions
