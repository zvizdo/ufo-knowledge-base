# Lalibela

A complex of eleven medieval rock-hewn monolithic churches in the Amhara region of Ethiopia, carved directly from volcanic rock in the 12th–13th centuries. Named after the Ethiopian king Lalibela who is traditionally credited with their construction.

## The Templar Connection (Hancock's Speculation)

Graham Hancock in [[sign-and-the-seal]] and [[dfPfPB601hw]] presents this speculative chain:

1. An Ethiopian prince named **Lalibela** was in exile in Jerusalem during the Crusades — at the time when the Knights Templar held the Temple Mount, using the Al-Aqsa Mosque as their palace.
2. Hancock speculates that Prince Lalibela disclosed to the Templars that the **Ark of the Covenant** was in Ethiopia.
3. The Templars followed this intelligence, helped install Lalibela on the Ethiopian throne, and were involved in constructing his rock-hewn churches.
4. **Evidence**: One of the Lalibela churches has a **Templar cross painted on its ceiling**. Another has a fresco of **Al-Aqsa Mosque with a cross on top** — the visual representation of Al-Aqsa when the Templars were using it as their headquarters. This fresco would only have been commissioned by someone who had personally seen the Templars' cross on the mosque.
5. A "strange force of people who sound like foreigners, not Ethiopians" accompanied Lalibela's return to power.

This is Hancock's speculation — not established historical fact — but the Templar cross and Al-Aqsa fresco details are specific enough to take seriously as a hypothesis.

## Significance

Lalibela is one of the most extraordinary examples of rock-cut architecture in the world — and one of the least visited by mainstream Western archaeology. The churches are UNESCO World Heritage sites.
