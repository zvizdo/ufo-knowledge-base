# Wright-Patterson Air Force Base

Wright-Patterson Air Force Base (WPAFB) near Dayton, Ohio is the headquarters of the Air Force Materiel Command and home to the Air Force Research Laboratory (AFRL), making it the primary center for US Air Force aerospace research, development, test, and evaluation. It is also the most frequently cited location in UAP crash retrieval claims.

**Historical significance**: Wright-Patterson is built on the site of the Wright brothers' original aviation experiments. It was the home of the Air Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC), which operated [[project-blue-book]] and its predecessor UFO investigation programs. The Foreign Technology Division at Wright-Patterson (later the National Air and Space Intelligence Center, NASIC) is responsible for analyzing captured foreign aircraft and technology — making it the natural institution to receive and analyze recovered UAP hardware, if any exists.

**Hangar 18**: The most durable UAP claim associated with Wright-Patterson is the existence of "Hangar 18" — a secure facility where recovered UAP hardware and biological material from the [[1947-roswell]] and other incidents were allegedly stored. The claim has been attributed to multiple sources over decades. Former Senator Barry Goldwater claimed he was denied access to a restricted area at Wright-Patterson when he asked his friend General Curtis LeMay about recovered UAP material.

**Blue Book connection**: [[project-blue-book]] was operated from Wright-Patterson under the Air Technical Intelligence Center. The site thus connects official USAF UFO investigation history directly to the alleged storage and suppression of UAP evidence — the same institution nominally investigating public UAP reports was potentially sitting on recovered hardware.

**Aztec connection**: The [[1947-aztec]] crash retrieval claim involves material allegedly transported to Wright-Patterson for analysis by the Air Technical Intelligence Center.
