# Palomar Observatory

Major astronomical observatory located on Palomar Mountain in San Diego County, Southern California. In the 1950s it was one of the most prominent observatories in use in the world. Home to the 200-inch Hale Telescope, then the world's largest. Astronomer Fritz Zwicky conducted notable work there, as did many other leading astronomers of the era.

## Photographic Plate Archive

In the 1950s, astronomers captured sky images on large, heavy glass photographic plates coated with a light-sensitive emulsion. The Palomar Observatory Survey produced extensive coverage of the northern hemisphere sky. These plates have since been digitized, making them available for modern digital analysis.

The digitized plates are used by contemporary astronomers for a range of studies — not just UAP-related research. This is relevant because systematic plate defects of the kind Villarroel has been accused of "seeing" would affect all studies using this data, not just hers — providing an incentive for the broader astronomical community to acknowledge defects or correct them.

## VASCO Analysis (1950–1956)

[[beatriz-villarroel]]'s [[vasco]] project analyzed the Palomar plates from approximately 1950–1956 — representing roughly 780 hours of exposure time, covering approximately 1.1 transient events per square degree per hour. The northern hemisphere sample yields ~105,000 transients, of which 30–35% appear to be genuine solar reflections based on the earth-shadow umbra test.

## Historical Context

The Palomar plates represent a unique, irreplaceable archive from the period before any human-made satellites were in orbit. No contamination by Starlink, GPS, or other human space infrastructure. What [[donald-menzel]] did to the Harvard plates cannot undo the Palomar archive — which survived outside his control.
