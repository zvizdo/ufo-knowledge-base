# Redstone Arsenal

Redstone Arsenal is a US Army installation in Huntsville, Alabama. It has been the center of US Army missile and rocket development since World War II and is one of the most significant classified research locations in the US defense apparatus.

**German rocket team nexus**: Redstone Arsenal was the postwar home of [[werner-von-braun]] and the German rocket engineers brought to the US through [[operation-paperclip]]. The team developed the Redstone ballistic missile (first deployed 1956), the Jupiter-C rocket that launched the first US satellite (Explorer 1, 1958), and the Saturn V rocket that carried astronauts to the Moon. The Redstone Arsenal thus represents the geographic and institutional center of the US space program's origins in captured Nazi rocket expertise.

**Ning Lee classified gravity research**: Physicist [[ning-lee]] and UAH physics chair Larry Smalley relocated to Redstone Arsenal after winning a DoD contract for their gravity research. Ning Lee had been conducting experiments at the University of Alabama Huntsville showing measurable weight reduction in test masses above spinning superconductors — research that attracted classified DoD attention. The move to Redstone was documented by researcher [[nick-cook]] when he interviewed Lee in 2003, after which Lee's public profile disappeared and she went dark until her death in 2021.

**NASA Marshall Space Flight Center**: Redstone Arsenal is co-located with NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center (MSFC), which was also built around the von Braun team. The dual military-NASA character of the Huntsville complex makes it a unique intersection of classified military and nominally civilian space research.

**Physics-lockdown pattern**: Ning Lee's disappearance into classified research at Redstone exemplifies the [[physics-lockdown]] pattern — promising anti-gravity or gravity-modification research that reaches a threshold of DoD interest, after which the researchers and their results are absorbed into classified programs.
