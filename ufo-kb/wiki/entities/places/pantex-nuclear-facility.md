# Pantex Nuclear Facility

The Pantex Plant, located near Amarillo, Texas, is the United States' primary facility for the assembly, disassembly, and maintenance of nuclear warheads — the most sensitive nuclear weapons handling facility in the US inventory.

[[george-knapp]] received from a military source a detailed account of a UAP incursion at Pantex. The object was described as **jellyfish-shaped** — an atypical morphology distinct from the sphere and disc shapes most commonly associated with UAP encounters; this unusual shape has appeared in several other nuclear-facility-adjacent accounts.

The UAP conducted a **controlled pass between warhead holding facilities 3 and 4** — threading through the most sensitive section of the installation. The trajectory was deliberate: it moved between the two most restricted areas of the facility before departing at a 45-degree angle. The encounter involved several anomalous technical features:

- **Infrared invisible**: the object was invisible to standard military IR detection systems
- **Thermal visible**: detectable in thermal imaging, suggesting selective spectral signature suppression — active countermeasure behavior rather than passive cloaking
- **Weapons jamming**: ground personnel's weapons could not achieve a targeting lock; the object could not be engaged despite being visually observable

The combination of selective IR invisibility and active weapon system jamming is cited by Knapp as evidence of real-time awareness of human detection and response systems — consistent with NHI craft demonstrated capability across other nuclear-facility encounters.

The Pantex incursion fits within the broader pattern documented in the [[ufo-nuclear-nexus]] literature: UAPs demonstrating specific operational interest in nuclear weapons storage, assembly, and delivery facilities.
