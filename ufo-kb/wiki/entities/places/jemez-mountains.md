# Jemez Mountains

> *Stub. Expand with additional UAP-relevant context and incidents as they enter the KB.*

Mountain range in north-central New Mexico immediately west and northwest of Los Alamos townsite and [[los-alamos-national-laboratory|LANL]]. The range surrounds the Valles Caldera, a Pleistocene supervolcano caldera. Range peaks include Redondo Peak (~3,432 m / 11,254 ft); typical 1948-era observer vantages from Los Alamos townsite would have placed UAP at the higher elevations of the Jemez range silhouette against the western evening sky.

## UAP Relevance

The Jemez Mountains are the foundational green-fireballs / Project Twinkle terrain. Per DOE-UAP-D002 ([[doe-uap-d2-james-tuck-correspondence-1970-1976]], handwritten letter to [[james-l-tuck]] dated 11-23-70), during 1948–1951:

- Several sightings of green lights were made at Los Alamos
- Usually occurred during the early part of the night, 9:00–11:00 pm
- "Usually in the Jemez Mountains"
- "Green lights weaving in and out of mountain peaks"
- All reported to the Los Alamos Protective Force Headquarters and "should be a matter of record on their logs"

The Jemez Mountains terrain context distinguishes this corpus from "fireball" reports elsewhere — the lights' apparent "weaving" through peaks places them in low atmosphere, not high meteor altitudes, and rules out conventional meteoric explanations a priori.
