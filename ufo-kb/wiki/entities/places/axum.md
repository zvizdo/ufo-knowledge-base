# Axum

Ancient capital of the Aksumite Empire, located in the Tigray region of northern Ethiopia. Ethiopia's most important religious city; the center of Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity.

## The Ark of the Covenant

Ethiopia is the only country in the world with a living national tradition claiming to hold the actual [[ark-of-the-covenant]]. The Ark is said to be kept in the **Chapel of the Tablet** — a sanctuary chapel adjacent to the Cathedral of St. Mary of Zion.

[[graham-hancock]] conducted fieldwork here in the 1980s–90s, interviewing successive guardians. His findings:
- Each guardian universally develops **cataracts** over their eyes — consistent with chronic radiation exposure
- Each guardian expects to die within approximately **3 years** of appointment
- When Hancock asked why, one guardian explained: *"because it is a thing of fire"*
- The guardians believe the Ark causes this

Hancock was permitted through the outer gates of the sanctuary compound but not inside the Chapel. He deliberately did not attempt to force entry — both out of respect and because the Axumites are "well prepared to defend it."

## Italian Occupation Period

During the Italian occupation of Ethiopia (1935–1941), the Ethiopians reportedly moved the Ark from Axum to an island in Lake Ziway, south of Addis Ababa, for safekeeping. This was to protect it from possible seizure — Hancock suspects Himmler's agents may have attempted to locate it.

## Ethiopian Jewish (Beta Israel) Tradition

The national epic (*Kebra Nagast* / "Glory of Kings") credits the Ark's arrival to Menelik I, son of Solomon and the Queen of Sheba. However, [[graham-hancock]] considers the **Beta Israel** (Ethiopian Jewish / Falasha) tradition more historically coherent: they describe their ancestors fleeing Israel with the Ark, keeping it on a Nile island for centuries, then following the Blue Nile south to Lake Tana before eventually reaching Axum.

Most Ethiopian Jews were airlifted to Israel by Operation Solomon (1991); a large community remains.
