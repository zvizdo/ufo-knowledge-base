# Chaco Canyon

Chaco Canyon is a shallow canyon in northwestern New Mexico that was the hub of the Ancestral Puebloan civilization at its height (~850–1150 CE). The canyon contains the largest pre-Columbian structures in North America, including Pueblo Bonito (with ~600 rooms) and at least 13 other "great houses" — massive planned multi-story structures with sophisticated masonry.

**Astronomical alignments**: Chaco construction is oriented with remarkable astronomical precision. The great houses and kiva structures incorporate alignments with solar solstices and equinoxes, lunar standstills (the 18.6-year cycle), and specific star risings. The "Sun Dagger" petroglyph site atop Fajada Butte marks the solstices and equinoxes through intersecting spiral petroglyphs.

**Regional network hub**: Chaco was connected to outlier communities across the Four Corners region via a network of engineered roads — extremely straight roads that in many cases continue past the visible destination, as if oriented toward astronomical or cosmological rather than purely practical endpoints. [[chimney-rock-colorado]] is one of the most significant outliers, specifically positioned to observe the 18.6-year major lunar standstill.

**Construction anomalies**: The timber used to build Chaco's great houses was transported hundreds of miles — there are no trees capable of producing the sized beams within the local landscape. The engineering implied — planning horizon, labor coordination, resource logistics — is consistent with a highly organized society but leaves the organizational mechanism unclear.

**Ancient mysteries context**: Chaco appears in UAP-adjacent research through the [[ancient-astronaut-hypothesis]] tradition, which interprets extreme astronomical precision and unexplained engineering achievements as evidence of non-human assistance. Mainstream archaeology attributes Chaco to indigenous innovation; the scale and precision remain subjects of active research.
