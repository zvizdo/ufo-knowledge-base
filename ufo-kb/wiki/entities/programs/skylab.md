# Skylab

Skylab was NASA's first space station, launched May 14, 1973, and operational through 1974 (with a final atmospheric reentry in 1979). Three crews occupied it over three missions: Skylab 2 (28 days), Skylab 3 (59 days, commanded by [[alan-bean]]), and Skylab 4 (84 days). The station was designed around a converted Saturn V third stage and hosted solar observation, Earth resources experiments, and medical research.

**Alan Bean's UAP account**: Commander [[alan-bean]], who told [[kevin-knuth]] the story directly, reported that during Skylab operations (~1977 in Knuth's account, though Bean commanded Skylab 3 in 1973), the crew photographed a **red flashing light** that followed their spacecraft for part of an orbit before disappearing. Bean stated the photographs were taken and are publicly available in NASA archives.

**Context for astronaut UAP reports**: Skylab was in low Earth orbit for extended periods, giving crews extensive time to observe the Earth and space environment. Multiple Skylab crew members made observations that were documented in flight logs. The prolonged duration missions (particularly Skylab 4 at 84 days) provided unprecedented opportunity for anomalous observation.

**NASA [[apollo-program]] connection**: Skylab was a direct successor to the Apollo program, using Saturn V hardware repurposed as an orbital laboratory. [[alan-bean]] was already an Apollo 12 veteran when he commanded Skylab 3 — giving him two space flight experiences to compare.

**[[soviet-space-program]] parallel**: Soviet cosmonauts aboard Salyut stations reported similar experiences of craft or lights pacing their spacecraft — a pattern consistent with the NHI nuclear monitoring and space monitoring hypotheses.
