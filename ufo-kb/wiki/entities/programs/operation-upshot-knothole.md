# Operation Upshot-Knothole

## Mandate

Operation Upshot-Knothole was a series of eleven nuclear weapons tests conducted at the Nevada Test Site between March 17 and June 4, 1953, jointly administered by the US Army and the Atomic Energy Commission. The tests evaluated weapons designs and studied the effects of nuclear detonations on military equipment and personnel, including large-scale military exercise components (Operation Desert Rock V).

## Personnel

- Atomic Energy Commission administrators and Los Alamos/Livermore weapons designers
- [[arthur-stansel]] — a witness to the subsequent alleged crash retrieval near Kingman, Arizona; reportedly on site in the Nevada test region during this period

## Outputs / Findings

Eleven detonations of varying yields. Several shots tested thermonuclear boosted fission designs. The tests were among the most publicized of the 1950s atomic testing program, with significant press coverage and civilian observers invited to witness some shots. The military exercise component involved tens of thousands of troops.

**UAP Connection**: Shot "Harry" (May 19, 1953) — colloquially known as "Dirty Harry" due to its unexpectedly high fallout — was conducted days before the alleged [[1953-kingman-crash]] (reported as May 19 or 20, 1953, near Kingman, Arizona). The proximity in time and geography between a nuclear detonation and a reported UAP crash is cited by [[ufo-nuclear-nexus]] researchers as consistent with a pattern of UAP interest in nuclear detonations. [[arthur-stansel]], an engineer who claimed firsthand knowledge of the Kingman crash retrieval, was reportedly in the Nevada-Arizona region during the test series.
