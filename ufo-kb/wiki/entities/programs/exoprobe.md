# ExoProbe

Active research project led by [[beatriz-villarroel]], designed to go beyond VASCO's historical archive approach by deploying a real-time telescope network to find, verify, characterize, and ultimately physically retrieve a non-human artifact from Earth orbit.

## Mandate

Unlike [[vasco]] (which compares historical Palomar plates to modern sky surveys), ExoProbe targets the **present-day space environment**. The operating assumption, drawn from VASCO's pre-Sputnik findings, is that non-human manufactured objects may still be present in Earth orbit — but are now obscured by the vast proliferation of human-made space debris.

## Key Technical Elements

### Space Debris Removal
The core challenge is separating anomalous signals from the ~27,000 catalogued human-made objects and uncatalogued debris. Villarroel mentions a specific debris-removal method she cannot publicly disclose.

### Telescope Network (New Mexico)
ExoProbe is building a network of telescopes with at least one already operational in New Mexico. The network approach enables:
- **Triangulation**: Two or more separated telescopes can measure parallax to determine distance to an object in three dimensions.
- **Confirmation**: A signal seen in multiple independent telescopes simultaneously eliminates most artifact causes.
- **Localization**: Precise enough localization could eventually enable physical approach and retrieval.

Villarroel's stated goal: *"to bring down an actual ET probe."*

### Observation Focus
Looking outside the Earth's atmosphere for signatures of non-human artifacts, including:
- Solar reflections (short flashes or streaks)
- Emissions from artificial objects
- Optical signatures consistent with mirror-like geometry (per VASCO findings)
- Potentially infrared surveys (though Villarroel is cautious: humans have less experience classifying IR objects, making false positives more likely)

## Hypothesis-First Design

ExoProbe operationalizes Villarroel's [[hypothesis-first-uap-science]] philosophy: rather than looking for "anything anomalous," it targets specific predicted signatures of a **flying saucer** or **metallic orb** — enabling a defined experiment with testable failure modes.

> "If we're thinking of metallic orbs, you design a project designed for looking for metallic orbs." — Villarroel

## Support Needed

Villarroel is actively seeking:
- Funding for additional telescopes and running costs
- Additional telescope sites to improve triangulation precision
