# Robertson Panel

A five-day scientific advisory panel convened by the [[cia]] in January 1953, officially to assess the national security implications of UFO reports. Chaired by physicist H.P. Robertson of Caltech. The panel reviewed 75 UFO cases over five days and concluded that the phenomenon posed no direct threat, but that the volume of reports could clog military communication channels.

## Mandate and Findings

The panel's stated purpose was scientific review. Its actual recommendations went further:

1. **Debunking campaign**: The panel recommended that the Air Force and other agencies actively reduce public interest in UFOs by "training" the public to view the phenomenon skeptically. Recommended involvement of major media and advertising.
2. **Surveillance**: Recommended monitoring civilian UFO groups (such as NICAP) for potential "subversive elements."
3. **Strip Special Status**: Recommended that [[project-blue-book]] be stripped of its "special status" and redirected toward debunking rather than investigation.

## Significance

The Robertson Panel is the smoking gun for the thesis that official UFO investigations were designed to produce null results. Its recommendations are consistent with the behavioral pattern described in [[ufo-secrecy-cabal]] — institutional investigation as whitewash. [[jacques-vallee]] and [[james-mcdonald]] both cited the panel as evidence of deliberate suppression over genuine inquiry.

The [[pentacle-memo]] (authored by [[howard-cross]] of Battelle Memorial Institute) predates the panel and appears to have influenced its conclusions — [[howard-cross]] was on the panel's staff.
