# Project Ozma

The first organized search for interstellar radio signals from intelligent extraterrestrial civilizations. Launched by astronomer [[frank-drake]] in 1961 at the National Radio Astronomy Observatory (NRAO) in Green Bank, West Virginia. Drake assembled an antenna and dish using scrapped WWII radar parts and pointed the apparatus at two nearby Sun-like stars (Tau Ceti and Epsilon Eridani).

## Historical Position

Project Ozma initiated what became the 70+ year SETI (Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence) program. Outside of the anomalous [[1977-wow-signal]] (discovered by Jerry Ehman at Ohio State, never repeated), no confirmed alien radio signal has been detected.

## Limitation Identified by Bracewell

[[ronald-bracewell]] identified a fundamental problem with radio-based SETI: interstellar distances make real-time conversation impossible. A message to Epsilon Boötis (203 light-years away) takes 406 years round trip. His proposed alternative — physical autonomous probes parked locally — would enable real-time contact. If his model is correct, SETI has been searching in the wrong medium while evidence of probes exists in Earth orbit.

## The Volume Problem

One study calculated that the galaxy volume SETI has searched so far is equivalent to comparing a hot tub of water to all of Earth's oceans when asking "where are all the fish."
