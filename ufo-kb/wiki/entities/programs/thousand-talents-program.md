# Thousand Talents Program

**The Thousand Talents Program** (*Qianren Jihua*) is a Chinese government initiative launched in 2008 to recruit overseas scientists, researchers, and entrepreneurs — primarily from the United States — to transfer advanced technology and knowledge back to China. Participants often simultaneously held US research positions and secret China contracts, constituting intellectual property theft and violations of US federal disclosure requirements.

## Key Prosecutions

The most prominent case is **Charles Lieber** — chair of Harvard's Department of Chemistry and a pioneer in nanotechnology. He was convicted of six felonies in December 2021 for concealing his participation. The [[wuhan-institute-of-technology]] paid Lieber $50,000/month, $150,000+ in living expenses, and $1.5M to establish a laboratory in China — for years before the COVID-19 pandemic emerged. Jesse Michaels notes the geography (Wuhan) has never been widely discussed in connection with Lieber's prosecution timeline.

Multiple other US academics and researchers have faced charges for undisclosed participation. The FBI has described this as a systemic problem affecting major research universities in physics, chemistry, and biology.

## UAP Relevance

The program represents a systematic CCP strategy to acquire US advanced technology — including potentially classified or dual-use aerospace and materials research — through academic channels. In the context of [[david-grusch]]'s testimony about a US-China-Russia cold war over NHI technology, the Thousand Talents Program is the documented infrastructure for how the CCP would accelerate such acquisition.
