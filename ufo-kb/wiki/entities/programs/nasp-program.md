# National Aerospace Plane (NASP / X-30)

Official US government program, announced by President Reagan, aimed at developing a single-stage-to-orbit hypersonic vehicle capable of flying from Washington to Tokyo (or Singapore/Hong Kong) in 45 minutes — hence Reagan's nickname "the Orient Express." The program centered on the X-30 research aircraft and was officially cancelled in 1994 without producing a functional vehicle.

## Sweetman's Cover-Program Thesis

[[bill-sweetman]], Jane's Defence Weekly journalist, argued that NASP's primary function was as a **white-world technology development funnel** for the classified [[aurora-aircraft-program]]. NASP was allegedly used to:
- Channel scramjet (supersonic combustion ramjet) research into public-domain academic and industry papers
- Develop new high-temperature materials (pyroceramic and similar) needed for hypersonic airframes
- Sustain the engineering talent base and industrial supply chains for hypersonic propulsion

When NASP was "cancelled," the real outputs — the classified spin-offs — were already absorbed into Aurora. The public cancellation served to close the visible technology pipeline without exposing what it had fed.

[[nick-cook]] largely agrees with Sweetman's thesis, noting that NASP's cancellation produced suspiciously few public post-mortems for a program that had consumed substantial funding.
