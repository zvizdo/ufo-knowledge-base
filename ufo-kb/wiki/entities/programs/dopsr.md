# DOPSR — Defense Office of Pre-publication and Security Review

## Mandate

DOPSR (the Defense Office of Pre-publication and Security Review) is the Pentagon's clearing house for review of unclassified material authored by current or former DoD personnel before public release. Its statutory function is to flag and redact information whose disclosure could damage US national security — covering memoirs, novels, op-eds, technical articles, and other public-facing material from those who have held DoD clearances.

## Outputs / Findings

- In [[OOr98r51Sj0]], DOPSR is described as having taken **six months to process just 20 pages** of [[jake-barber]]'s novel [[sentinels-of-ether]] (a 10% chunk of the 120,000-word manuscript), escalating the review to subject-matter experts inside classified Special Access Programs, and returning **seven pages of redactions**.
- Barber inverted DOPSR's preventive function and used it as a *discovery tool*: by submitting material he suspected was classified and watching which lines came back redacted, he treated DOPSR as a real-time classification map of the actual legacy program.
- The redaction trail becomes its own authentication signal — "the manuscript becomes a Trojan horse. The Pentagon's own bureaucracy becomes the delivery mechanism."
