# Operation Dominic

Operation Dominic was a 1962 US nuclear test series comprising 31 detonations conducted in the Pacific Proving Grounds, primarily near [[johnston-island]] and Christmas Island. It was the largest US nuclear test series by number of detonations and was launched in response to the Soviet resumption of atmospheric testing in 1961.

## Mandate

Dominic had two primary objectives: weapons development validation following the 1958–1961 test moratorium, and exploration of nuclear effects at altitude. The high-altitude sub-series, [[bluegill-triple-prime|Operation Fishbowl]], was specifically designed to study nuclear weapon effects in the exoatmosphere — including X-ray pulse propagation, EMP generation, and the behavior of re-entry vehicles under nuclear conditions.

The Fishbowl tests included:
- **Starfish Prime** (July 9, 1962) — the most powerful high-altitude detonation (1.4 Mt at 400 km); created an artificial radiation belt, generated a geomagnetic storm, and produced an EMP that destroyed satellites and caused power disruptions in Hawaii.
- **Bluegill Triple Prime** (October 26, 1962) — the final successful Fishbowl test; filmed by two KC-135 aircraft under the "Kettle" program. Its footage remains the most restricted of all Fishbowl records. See [[bluegill-triple-prime]].
- Checkmate, Kingfish, Tightrope — additional Fishbowl tests at varying altitudes.

A key scientific finding from Dominic, particularly from Starfish Prime, was that the X-ray pulse from a high-altitude nuclear detonation could destroy or disable ICBM re-entry vehicles. This insight became the technical foundation for Edward Teller's Project Excalibur X-ray laser concept and, ultimately, Reagan's [[strategic-defense-initiative]].

## Personnel

- **General Alfred Starbird** — Senior Officer Present Afloat (SOPA) and commander of Joint Task Force 8 (JTF-8); oversaw the full Dominic test series.
- **William Ogle** — Los Alamos National Laboratory, J Division; weapons design lead.
- **Lawrence Geist** — AEC Albuquerque Division; logistics and post-test material handling. Geist later directed Harald Malmgren to handle recovered UAP material at Los Alamos following the [[1962-bluegill-uap-shootdown]].

## Outputs / Findings

- Confirmation that high-altitude nuclear detonations produce X-ray pulses capable of disabling re-entry vehicles — the basis for both offensive ICBM countermeasure research and the later [[strategic-defense-initiative]].
- Starfish Prime's artificial radiation belt damaged or destroyed multiple US and UK satellites, demonstrating the scale of exoatmospheric nuclear effects.
- The Bluegill Triple Prime Kettle 2 film was reported "unable to locate" by the Department of Energy in response to a 2023 mandatory declassification review request by Jeffrey Kruchshank, making it the only Fishbowl test with partially unaccounted footage.
- Per Harald Malmgren (as relayed in [[09KP8XVf5nY]]), UAP "tag-alongs" at missile and nuclear tests by 1962 were sufficiently routine that military personnel had developed casual slang terminology for them — indicating institutional familiarity with the phenomenon at the operational level.
