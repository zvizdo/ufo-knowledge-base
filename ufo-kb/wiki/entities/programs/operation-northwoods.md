# Operation Northwoods

Declassified 1962 proposal by the US Joint Chiefs of Staff to orchestrate false-flag attacks against US citizens and infrastructure, blame Cuba, and use the manufactured incidents to justify military invasion. The proposal — formally titled "Justification for US Military Intervention in Cuba" — was signed by all members of the Joint Chiefs and sent to Secretary of Defense Robert McNamara. President Kennedy rejected it; Kennedy's rejection is sometimes cited in JFK-assassination discourse as a moment that deepened JCS hostility toward his presidency.

**Specific proposals**: The Northwoods document contained remarkably specific operational concepts. These included: developing a "terror campaign" in Miami and Washington DC attributed to Cuban operatives; sinking a US ship in Guantanamo Bay and blaming Cuba; staging fake Cuban attacks on civilian aircraft (suggesting an aircraft could be replaced by a drone and a real aircraft secretly flown to an alternate location); shooting down a civilian airliner and blaming Cuban MiGs; staging the defection of a fake Cuban pilot in a Russian MiG; and creating a "remember the Maine" incident at Guantanamo. The document was approved at the highest military level before Kennedy's rejection.

**Declassification**: The documents were declassified in the late 1990s through the Assassination Records Review Board — the same body created by the JFK Records Act that [[oliver-stone]]'s film forced through Congress. Their public release constituted direct documentary proof that the Joint Chiefs of Staff had proposed murdering Americans and staging a war, signed the documents, and sent them to the civilian chain of command.

**UAP discourse significance**: Northwoods is the most frequently cited documentary precedent for the argument that the US government is institutionally capable of large-scale deception of its own citizens on national security matters. The objection "the government can't keep a secret" is dismantled by the Northwoods documents: the plan was kept secret for 35 years and was approved at the highest military levels. A 75-year UAP cover-up is not extraordinary compared to what Northwoods documents was the institutional baseline.

**Broader false-flag pattern**: Northwoods sits alongside [[gulf-of-tonkin]] (staged or misrepresented naval incident used to justify Vietnam escalation), [[1898-uss-maine]] (contested explosion used to justify Spanish-American War), and [[operation-gladio]] (NATO false-flag terror network in Europe) as a cluster of documented or highly plausible government-orchestrated or government-exploited deceptive incidents.
