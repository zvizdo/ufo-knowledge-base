# Project A-119

## Mandate

Project A-119 was a classified US Air Force study examining whether detonating a nuclear bomb on the Moon would be feasible and what its psychological impact on the Soviet Union (and the global public) would be. The project was motivated by the Cold War space race context — the Soviets had just launched Sputnik (1957), and American planners were considering dramatic demonstrations of technological capability.

The project was ultimately abandoned, partly on scientific grounds and partly due to concerns about contaminating the Moon and the PR risks if it went wrong.

## Personnel

[[carl-sagan]] was a scientific consultant on the project — specifically to analyze the potential visibility of the nuclear explosion from Earth. His involvement required classified access.

## Significance for Carl Sagan's Trajectory

[[bob-maguire]]'s account places Project A-119 as one step in Carl Sagan's trajectory into classified national security work:
1. Project A-119 consultation — gave Sagan clearances in the late 1950s.
2. Sagan's documented private UFO curiosity in personal diaries (debating mentors about UFO credibility).
3. At some point, Sagan "bumped up against the program" — the classified UAP crash retrieval or related programs.
4. Bob witnessed Sagan with a contractor green badge and top-secret clearance at an NSA meeting.
5. Sagan subsequently pivoted to public UFO skepticism.

The A-119 consultation is the documented beginning of a clearance relationship with the Air Force that may have expanded into broader access.
