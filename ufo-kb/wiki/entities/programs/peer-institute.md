# PEER Institute

The Program for Extraordinary Experience Research (PEER) was founded by [[john-mack]] as an institutional home for the systematic scientific study of anomalous experiences — primarily alien abduction accounts, but also broader phenomena of consciousness, post-mortem contact, and related experiences. Mack established PEER to give legitimate academic infrastructure to research that Harvard itself would not formally sponsor.

## History

Mack founded PEER in the early 1990s as his anomalous-phenomena work intensified. The institute allowed him to conduct interviews, maintain a case archive, and publish — at arms length from but in proximity to Harvard's credibility. After Mack's death in a traffic accident in London in 2004, the institute required new leadership.

At his memorial, Mack's colleagues and collaborators urged [[diane-powell]] — herself a Harvard-trained neuroscientist on a parallel trajectory — to take on PEER and continue his work. In a detail [[ky-dickens]] describes as synchronistic, Powell subsequently found and purchased a house in Oregon that happened to share its construction date with John Mack's birthday.

## Significance

PEER represents institutional continuity across two suppression events:

1. Mack faced a formal Harvard investigation and censure for his abduction research; PEER was how he maintained a research identity outside direct Harvard control
2. Powell had her medical license revoked following publication of *[[esp-enigma]]*; her subsequent research on [[autistic-savant-telepathy]] continued this tradition

The handoff from Mack to Powell is itself evidence of a networked community of credentialed researchers who have faced institutional repercussions and continue anyway.
