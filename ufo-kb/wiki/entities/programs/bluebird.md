# Project Bluebird

Project Bluebird was the first formal CIA mind-control program, signed into operation by the CIA Director in **April 1952** — three months before the Korean War began in June. The program transitioned into [[artichoke]] and eventually became [[mk-ultra]].

## Key Facts (per [[colin-ross]])

- **Predated the Korean War**: The CIA's public disinformation narrative was that Bluebird was a defensive reaction to Chinese/North Korean brainwashing of American prisoners of war. This is false — the program was operational *before* the war started.
- **Experiments on secretaries and others**: Documents describe experiments using "secretaries and whoever they could get their hands on" — hypnotizing them, getting them to complete tasks and assignments, and inducing amnesia for those tasks afterward.
- **Verbal trigger / amnesia compartmentalization**: Consistent with the technique published by [[gh-esterbrooks]] and later incorporated into [[mk-ultra]]: create a triggered alternate identity that can receive and execute instructions, then return the subject to their normal state with no memory.

## The Cover Story Problem

The Korean War disinformation was intended to frame Bluebird as a purely defensive, reactive program. The documented timeline — April 1952 start, June 1952 Korean War — demolishes this framing. The CIA was developing offensive mind-control capabilities independently, not in reaction to perceived foreign threat.
