# AATIP

The **Advanced Aerial Threat Identification Program** is the Pentagon UAP research office whose existence was revealed in the December 2017 *New York Times* story "Glowing Auras and 'Black Money': The Pentagon's Mysterious UFO Program." The disclosure launched the modern UAP mainstreaming cycle.

## Mandate

AATIP's stated mandate was to investigate anomalous aerial phenomena that posed potential threats to US airspace and national security. In practice, [[lue-elizondo]] (its director) understood its mission as determining the nature and origin of UAPs — including whether they were non-human in origin. The program formally terminated in 2012 according to the official record, but Elizondo has indicated that informal work continued.

## Personnel

- [[lue-elizondo]] — director; resigned in October 2017 citing obstruction
- [[chris-mellon]] — former Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense for Intelligence; co-architect of the 2017 NYT disclosure
- [[harry-reid]] — the Senate appropriator who championed AATIP (and [[aawsap]]), working with Ted Stevens and Dan Inouye to secure the black-budget line item
- [[robert-bigelow]] — much early AATIP investigative activity occurred at Bigelow facilities; the [[aawsap]] contract was awarded to Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies

## Outputs / Findings

The program's most tangible public output was the release of three declassified UAP videos: FLIR1 (Tic-Tac), GIMBAL, and GOFAST — which the Pentagon later officially acknowledged. These videos are the most significant government-acknowledged UAP footage in the public record and form the empirical anchor of the modern disclosure argument. See [[tic-tac]] for the FLIR1 encounter specifically.

## FOIA Exemption via Bigelow Aerospace

All data gathered during the 2007–2012 AATIP investigation period is both **classified** and **FOIA-exempt**. The mechanism: the program ran "under the auspices of [[bigelow-aerospace]]" — a private company. Private company data is shielded from FOIA through proprietary protections even when the research was government-contracted and government-funded. [[brandon-fugel]] (current [[skinwalker-ranch]] owner) confirmed in [[7W7iOt57fOo]] that this structure was set up deliberately. The primary public window into AATIP/AAWSAP findings at the ranch is the partially declassified book [[skinwalkers-at-the-pentagon]] (2021). See [[uap-private-sector-foia-exemption]] for the broader pattern.

## Relationship to AAWSAP

AATIP and [[aawsap]] are often conflated but were related, partially overlapping programs. AAWSAP was the broader program with the larger mandate (including paranormal phenomena); AATIP was the narrower aerial-threat-focused successor. Elizondo moved between both.

## Budget Context (dzTZbSNsKV8)

[[garry-nolan]] and [[jesse-michaels]] note in [[dzTZbSNsKV8]] that AATIP's $22 million budget is absurdly small compared to comparable aerospace programs. The F-35 program budget exceeds $100 billion — making AATIP's funding less than 1% of that single fighter jet program. Nolan frames this as a structural explanation for why UAP science hasn't advanced: funding is the bottleneck, and the feedback loop that drives NCI cancer research (money → papers → more money) doesn't exist for UAP research.
