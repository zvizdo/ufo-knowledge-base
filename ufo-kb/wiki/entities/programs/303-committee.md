# 303 Committee

## Overview

The 303 Committee (and its predecessors/successors under various names — the **5412 Group**, the **Special Group**, the **40 Committee**) was the secret inter-agency body that approved all CIA covert operations. Nominally, its function was to shield the president from direct involvement in sensitive operations by inserting a layer of deniable oversight. In practice, this created a power center that could at times authorize operations without explicit presidential sanction.

Membership typically included the **National Security Adviser**, senior CIA officials, and representatives from the State and Defense Departments.

## Kissinger's Role

[[henry-kissinger]], as National Security Adviser under Nixon (1969–75), was central to the 303 Committee's operations. His philosophy of compartmentalized governance — "exclude from the making of decisions all those theoretically charged with carrying them out" — aligned naturally with the committee's design for plausible deniability.

UAP researchers have suggested that the 303 Committee (and the 5412/40 Committee equivalents across administrations) may have been the locus of UAP program oversight — giving it another name that overlaps with or is interchangeable with [[majestic-12]] or [[pi-40]] in certain contexts.

## UAP Research Significance

The committee's structure — small, inter-agency, compartmentalized from presidential knowledge, with legal authority over covert operations — is precisely the kind of body that could oversee a UAP program with minimal documentation and maximum deniability. The evolution of names across administrations (5412 → Special Group → 303 → 40 Committee) mirrors what UAP researchers argue happened to MJ12 itself: an evolving institutional structure retaining continuity of function while changing formal designation.
