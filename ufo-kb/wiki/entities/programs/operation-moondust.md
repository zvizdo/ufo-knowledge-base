# Operation Moondust

## Mandate

Operation Moondust was established by the US Air Force to recover and analyze **foreign space debris** — principally Soviet satellites and re-entry vehicles — as part of Cold War technical intelligence collection. Under its nominal mandate, recovery teams would locate, secure, and analyze any foreign-origin space hardware that came down on or near US-accessible territory.

## Extended Remit: UAP Material

Declassified documents obtained through FOIA reveal that Moondust's operational footprint extended beyond conventional space hardware to include **unidentified objects**, including:
- **Spherical objects** recovered on New Zealand farmland.
- A **fireball and subsequent sightings of silent aircraft** near a crash site in **Bolivia**.
- A **1957 UAP event in Afghanistan** — described in documents as rivaling the scale of the Roswell incident — with materials sent to [[wright-patterson]] Air Force Base.

These documents describe recovery procedures and reporting chains strikingly similar to those described in MJ12 documents and in modern testimony about legacy UAP programs.

## Kissinger Telegram (January 1977)

In January 1977, days before leaving office, [[henry-kissinger]] sent a declassified telegram to the US Embassy in Ottawa requesting a report on "possible returned space objects." The subject line was: **MOON DUST**. This confirms Kissinger's active awareness of and interest in the Moondust program as recently as his final days in government.

## Significance

The Moondust framework provides a plausible institutional vehicle through which UAP recoveries could have been processed under a "space debris" classification cover — providing legal and bureaucratic infrastructure for handling non-human materials without creating a UAP-specific paper trail visible to standard oversight mechanisms.
