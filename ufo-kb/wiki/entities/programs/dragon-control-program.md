# Dragon Control

A classified NASA mission-control station at [[cape-canaveral|Cape Canaveral]] allegedly run by [[tim-taylor]]. Disclosed firsthand by [[chris-bledsoe]] in [[bM18PJY6_Zc]]. The station oversees a layered surveillance chain that tracks every rocket launched from the cape — explicitly to monitor non-human-intelligence ("sponsor") interaction with the vehicle.

## The Surveillance Chain

Per Bledsoe's firsthand description, every Cape Canaveral launch is observed through a four-stage continuous-coverage chain:

1. **Ground-based articulating cameras** — optical coverage from launch through ~50,000 ft
2. **WB-57 high-altitude aircraft** ([[nro]]-affiliated; 7 onboard cameras) — intercepts at 50,000 ft; coverage to ~100,000 ft
3. **F-104 Starfighter** (7–8 cameras) — intercepts beyond the WB-57 ceiling; coverage to optical limit
4. **[[nro]] satellites** — take over from optical-limit altitude through orbit

At orbit insertion, Taylor enters a **private room** with access to all satellite feeds. Per Bledsoe: **only Taylor has eyes on what approaches the rocket** at that altitude. The [[nro]] (not CIA or Air Force) is identified by Bledsoe as the institution holding the classification keys for UAP data captured in this surveillance chain.

## Access Protocol

To enter the Dragon Control facility, Bledsoe was reportedly required to obtain a **dragon tattoo** — a personal-marker access requirement consistent with classified-program tradecraft. The tattoo requirement implies a formal access protocol, not casual visitor admission.

## Taylor's Statement

Per Taylor (to Bledsoe):

> *"For your rocket company to succeed, you have to know what they want."*

The "they" — referenced as **"sponsors"** in adjacent disclosures — is the non-human-intelligence layer that Taylor's surveillance chain is allegedly designed to track. The implication: commercial space companies that succeed (or fail) at orbit insertion may be doing so based on sponsor-tolerance criteria observable from Dragon Control's vantage.

## Institutional Context

- Taylor also worked at [[aerospace-corporation]], an FFRDC closely tied to [[nro]] satellite acquisition.
- Taylor's name reportedly came up in connection with [[elon-musk]] — who runs Dragon capsule launches from the same Cape Canaveral facility (the program shares the "Dragon" name, though whether that is coincidence or signaling is unclear).
- The combination of NASA + NRO + Aerospace Corporation + commercial-launch oversight places Dragon Control at the intersection of civilian space, intelligence-community satellite collection, and emerging commercial space — exactly the locus where UAP custody would be operationally consolidated.

## Significance

If Bledsoe's description is accurate, Dragon Control represents the most concrete contemporary disclosure of an **operational UAP monitoring program** integrated into routine launch operations. The surveillance is not opportunistic but built into every cape launch as a structural function.
