# Operation Paperclip

Operation Paperclip was a US government intelligence operation (1945–1959) in which the Joint Intelligence Objectives Agency (JIOA) recruited over 1,600 German scientists, engineers, and technicians from Nazi Germany and Austria for employment by the United States military, NASA, and defense contractors. The operation's name refers to the paperclips used to attach favorable JIOA security summaries to the dossiers of scientists whose Nazi Party membership would otherwise have disqualified them for US entry.

**Key figures**: The most prominent Paperclip recruits included:
- **Wernher von Braun** — lead engineer of the V-2 rocket program; became the principal architect of NASA's Saturn V rocket and Apollo program
- **Arthur Rudolph** — V-2 production manager at Mittelwerk (which used slave labor); later managed the Saturn V program; US citizenship revoked in 1984 after exposure of war crimes
- **Kurt Debus** — first director of Kennedy Space Center
- **Hubertus Strughold** — aerospace medicine pioneer; later accused of participation in Nazi human experiments

**Deception of oversight**: The Paperclip program operated in direct defiance of State Department and presidential policy barring employment of Nazi war criminals. The JIOA systematically falsified security dossiers to conceal SS membership, Nazi Party membership, and involvement in war crimes. This practice — using classification and bureaucratic manipulation to circumvent civilian oversight — is cited in UAP discourse as a precedent for how classified programs can systematically circumvent accountability.
