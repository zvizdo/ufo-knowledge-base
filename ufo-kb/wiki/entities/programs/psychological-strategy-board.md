# Psychological Strategy Board (PSB)

U.S. interagency body created by President Truman in 1951 to coordinate psychological warfare strategy during the Cold War. Disbanded in 1953 and replaced by the Operations Coordinating Board (OCB) under Eisenhower. Headed by **Gordon Gray** (later Eisenhower's National Security Advisor) and staffed by **Charles Douglas Jackson** (Time magazine; psychological warfare specialist) and [[philip-corso]].

## UAP Relevance

[[matthew-pines]] identifies the PSB as the formal Eisenhower-era architecture for integrating UAP psyop strategy — the institutional node where the decision to use UAP phenomena as potential psychological warfare tools (both against the Soviet Union and to shape domestic perceptions) was formalized. The [[walter-b-smith]] CIA memo proposing to weaponize UFO phenomena connects directly to PSB-era thinking.

## Kissinger's Role

[[henry-kissinger]] served as a direct consultant to the PSB and its successor, the **Operations Coordinating Board**, in 1951, 1952, and again in 1955 — making him one of an elite group deemed capable of staffing the psychological warfare oversight body. He consulted directly with Gordon Gray, the PSB director. A declassified CIA document on the PSB's mission describes winning hearts and minds through strategic influence and identifies only select outsiders as suitable consultants — Kissinger was among them.

This placed Kissinger, while still a graduate student at Harvard, in regular contact with the core institutional apparatus that was simultaneously managing UFO phenomena as a psychological warfare asset.
