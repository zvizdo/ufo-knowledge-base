# Uranverein

The Uranverein (German: "uranium club") was Germany's nuclear weapons research program during World War II, formally organized under the Reich Research Council (Reichsforschungsrat) and sponsored by the Wehrmacht and SS. It ran from 1939 to Germany's defeat in 1945 and ultimately failed to produce a working nuclear weapon.

**Key figures**: The program's scientific leadership included Werner Heisenberg (theoretical physics), [[walter-gerlach]] (administrative coordinator for nuclear physics), Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker, Kurt Diebner, and others. Gerlach served as the Reich's plenipotentiary for nuclear physics, coordinating research across multiple institutions.

**Failure reasons**: The program's failure has been attributed to:
- A fundamental error in Heisenberg's critical mass calculation (off by orders of magnitude)
- Inadequate supplies of heavy water and uranium-235
- Allied bombing of key facilities including the Norsk Hydro plant in Norway
- Resource competition with other weapons programs
- Possibly deliberate slowdown by some scientists who did not wish to give the Nazis a bomb

**Farm Hall**: After Germany's defeat, ten Uranverein scientists (including Heisenberg, Gerlach, von Weizsäcker, and others) were interned at Farm Hall in England by the British. Recordings of their private conversations captured genuine surprise at the American atomic bomb's existence — interpreted by historians as confirming they had not been close to success, but also as evidence of how they were managing their public narrative.

**Operation Paperclip nexus**: German nuclear scientists who were not interned at Farm Hall were targets of [[operation-paperclip]] assessment. The nuclear physics track of German expertise was evaluated alongside the rocket program (von Braun) and biological/chemical weapons expertise.

**Die Glocke connection**: In UAP discourse, the Uranverein is invoked in relation to [[die-glocke]] — the alleged Nazi anti-gravity device supposedly developed in parallel to the nuclear program. [[walter-gerlach]] is named in Witkowski-source accounts as a possible consultant. The connection is unverified.
