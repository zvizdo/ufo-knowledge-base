# Soviet Space Program

The Soviet space program ran from the launch of Sputnik 1 in October 1957 through the dissolution of the USSR in 1991. It achieved numerous firsts — first satellite, first human in space (Yuri Gagarin, 1961), first spacewalk (Aleksei Leonov, 1965), first space station (Salyut 1, 1971). The program operated in parallel to NASA during the Space Race and achieved its own extensive UAP incident record.

**Cosmonaut UAP encounters**: Multiple Soviet cosmonauts reported UAP encounters during space missions. The most documented categories include:
- Luminous objects observed near spacecraft during orbital missions
- Objects that paced spacecraft for portions of orbits before departing
- Photographic evidence that was confiscated by Soviet military/KGB before it could be analyzed

**Akinashev encounter**: An unnamed cosmonaut (possibly "Akinashev," per [[akinashev-cosmonaut]]) reported that during an early space station mission, a craft pulled alongside his spacecraft and paced it in orbit. The craft had visible windows. The crew photographed it; the photographs were confiscated by Soviet authorities. The cosmonaut drew a picture from memory that became public.

**Suppression pattern**: Soviet UAP incident suppression was systematic and parallel to US suppression: witnesses were debriefed and silenced, photographic evidence was classified, and official denials were issued. The KGB's UAP files — partially declassified after 1991 — confirmed active Soviet UAP monitoring from the 1950s onward.

**Nuclear facility incidents**: Consistent with the [[nhi-monitoring-nuclear-civilizations]] pattern, Soviet ICBM and nuclear weapons facilities experienced UAP incidents parallel to the Malmstrom AFB pattern in the US.

**Post-Soviet disclosures**: After Soviet dissolution, some former cosmonauts and military officers disclosed UAP encounters publicly. The pattern of reports is consistent with the US military witness pattern: credentialed observers, reluctant public disclosure, evidence of prior institutional knowledge.
