# Stargate Program

The Stargate Program was a US government initiative to develop and operationalize psychic intelligence collection, primarily through remote viewing — the practice of perceiving distant or hidden locations and objects through non-sensory means. It was founded by physicists **Hal Puthoff** and **Russell Targ** at the **Stanford Research Institute (SRI)** in the early 1970s and ran under various code names (SCANATE, Gondola Wish, Grill Flame, Center Lane, Sun Streak) before being consolidated under the Stargate designation.

## Mandate

The program's official mandate was to assess whether remote viewing could provide actionable intelligence — particularly in scenarios where conventional collection methods (satellite imagery, human intelligence, signals intelligence) were unavailable or insufficient. It was funded and overseen at various stages by the CIA and the Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA), reflecting institutional competition over control of the program.

The deeper mandate — never officially stated — was to determine whether human consciousness could operate non-locally, potentially bridging the gap between conventional intelligence methods and the anomalous perceptual phenomena reported by individuals in contact with UAP-related material or events.

## Outputs / Findings

**Operational validation:** The Rosemary Smith / Soviet TU-22 location is the most publicly confirmed operational success of the program, validated by the sitting US president. It demonstrated that remote viewing could yield actionable intelligence under controlled conditions.

**TK-089 Soviet Submarine** (c.1979): [[joe-mcmoneagle]] and [[harley-trent]] independently described a prototype submarine with 20 slanted launch tubes and RS-27 missiles (5 warheads × ~50 Mt each) before overhead imagery existed. Robert Gates (CIA Russia desk) dismissed the report as "total fantasy"; overhead photography 114 days later confirmed the design exactly. McMoneagle states the 4-day intelligence haul exceeded all prior Soviet submarine intelligence in history.

**MX Missile Program**: McMoneagle remote viewed the Air Force's 23-shelter railroad system. A white paper went to the Situation Room; tested for one year with 100% accuracy. The program was cancelled. Senator William Cohen sent McMoneagle a letter crediting him with saving ~$100 billion.

**Statistical validation**: [[jessica-utts]] (American Statistical Association president) analyzed Stargate data for the 1995 AIR review and concluded the remote viewing effect was real, with P-values "off the chart" compared to psychology research. Co-reviewer [[ray-hyman]] disputed the methodology; CIA cited Hyman's critique to close the program despite Utts's positive conclusion.

**Institutional acknowledgment of non-local consciousness:** The program's multi-decade continuation — despite periodic skeptical reviews — reflects an institutional conclusion that the phenomenon was real enough to warrant sustained investment. The 1995 American Institutes for Research (AIR) review concluded that remote viewing demonstrated a statistically significant effect, but recommended termination on the grounds that the intelligence yield was insufficient to justify the program. This framing implicitly concedes the phenomenon's reality.

**Connection to UAP material handling:** Harald Malmgren's account of hearing words telepathically while handling recovered UAP debris at Los Alamos (following the [[1962-bluegill-uap-shootdown]]) is directly connected to Stargate in [[09KP8XVf5nY]]. The implication is that the US government, having encountered telepathic or non-local communication as a byproduct of UAP contact, launched or expanded Stargate partly to formalize and understand this phenomenon — and potentially to train personnel to communicate with non-human intelligences (NHI) through the same modality.

The Stargate research also connects to the question of NHI communication modalities more broadly: if UAP-associated entities communicate through consciousness rather than physical signals, then remote viewing research is not merely a peripheral curiosity but a central intelligence priority.

## Scientology Penetration

[[danny-sheehan]]'s account adds a significant complication: both **[[ingo-swann]]** and **[[pat-price]]** were **high-level clears within the Church of Scientology**, and Swann was actively filing intelligence reports on the program to Scientology's UK Guardian's Office. This means:
1. The program's most capable practitioners were simultaneously loyal to a non-government organization with its own institutional intelligence apparatus.
2. Scientology may have had access to Stargate's most sensitive operational details in real time.
3. The Church of Scientology — with its interest in consciousness technology — was monitoring government efforts to operationalize the same capacities its own training programs developed.

Sheehan saw these reports directly inside Scientology's Florida HQ files while reviewing materials as a condition of potentially representing L. Ron Hubbard.
