# Operation CHAOS

CIA domestic counterintelligence program begun in 1967 in San Francisco, running parallel to the FBI's COINTELPRO. Both programs were designed to infiltrate, disrupt, and neutralize the left-wing counterculture movement and the Black militant movement — particularly the Black Panthers. Both were exposed by the Church Committee and other Congressional investigations in 1975–76.

## Origin

President Johnson and FBI Director J. Edgar Hoover assessed by 1967 that the Black Panthers' growing power and the anti-war movement represented the greatest internal threat to the United States since the Civil War. The CIA — which by law cannot operate domestically — nonetheless launched CHAOS in 1967 out of San Francisco. The program:
- Planted agents inside counterculture and left-wing organizations
- Provoked organizations to commit crimes that could result in members being killed (e.g., setting up gun thefts or bank robberies where agents were waiting)
- Fomented internal rivalries, particularly within and between Black militant factions
- Documented results: FBI admitted (after a 1970s whistleblower) that two US Organization members killed at UCLA in 1968 were effectively orchestrated by FBI agents on Washington's orders

## The Tate Connection

Hoover's November 1968 memo to the LA field office stated explicitly: white supporters of the Panthers must be made to fear that when the revolution comes they will be "lined up against the wall and slaughtered." The liberal Hollywood establishment was financially supporting the Panthers through formal groups; celebrities including Jane Fonda, Jean Seberg, Warren Beatty, Marlon Brando, and Donald Sutherland attended fundraisers.

August 8–9, 1969: five white people were slaughtered in the house that was a symbol of that support — the Tate property.

Whether the murders were orchestrated, enabled, or were simply a coincidental match to CHAOS's goals is the central open question of [[tom-oneill]]'s investigation.

## Documented Effect

Within weeks of the Manson arrests:
- Public fear of hippies spread nationally
- Hitchhiking of hippies stopped
- Abbie Hoffman and other movement leaders noted the immediate marginalization of the peaceful protest wing
- Author Don Watt wrote that August 8, 1969 was "the day the '60s ended"

These outcomes precisely matched CHAOS/COINTELPRO's stated objectives.
