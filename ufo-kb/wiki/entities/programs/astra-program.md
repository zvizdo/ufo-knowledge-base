# ASTRA Program

Classified Skunk Works aircraft designation observed by [[nick-cook]] during a mid-1990s visit to Lockheed's Palmdale headquarters. Cook noticed it on an organizational chart depicting Skunk Works aircraft from the 1940s to the present day — ASTRA appeared at the **top of the chart, above all currently acknowledged programs**.

## The Incident

Cook was visiting Skunk Works head [[jack-gordon]] in the unusual setting of the actual classified headquarters building (rather than the sanitized "airlock" area kept for visitors). On his way out, he passed an org chart and spotted ASTRA above what should have been the most advanced acknowledged aircraft (either the YF-22 prototype or the Darkstar drone).

When Cook asked his escort "What's Astra?", he was **immediately bundled out of the building**. A subsequent Skunk Works head confirmed to Cook that this incident was "very well known internally" and said "we're all aware of that incident."

## April Fool's Ambiguity

Cook later noticed the visit date was **April 1st**. In retrospect, he wondered whether the org chart placement was intentional — a deliberate breadcrumb planted for the journalist to find, classic disinformation practice. In Cook's world: "I knew I was playing a game with them." The fact that he was unusually invited into the classified headquarters building adds to this suspicion.

However: "If there was nothing there, why was it memorable?" — suggests even the Skunk Works insiders attached real significance to the incident beyond a prank.

## Later Confirmation

A subsequent Skunk Works director told Cook:
- ASTRA exists (as a known internal program designation)
- "It hasn't flown yet" (speaking approximately a decade after the initial sighting)
- "It'll make an appearance sometime soon"
- "Keep watching the skies"

As of the [[G7Ns4Aq1tVc]] recording (2024), ASTRA has not appeared publicly.
