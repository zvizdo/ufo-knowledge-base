# Project Blue Book

The USAF's official UFO investigation program, 1952–1969. Successor to [[project-sign]] (1947–1949) and Project Grudge (1949–1952). First director: [[edward-ruppelt]].

## Mandate

Investigate UFO reports; maintain public confidence that the USAF took the issue seriously. De facto mandate (per Salas): serve as public-facing PR operation while any genuine findings remained classified.

## Personnel

- [[edward-ruppelt]] — first director; read and confirmed the [[estimate-of-the-situation]] document
- J. Allen Hynek ([[j-allen-hynek]]) — scientific consultant
- [[jacques-vallee]] — worked with Hynek; found the [[pentacle-memo]] in Blue Book files

## Outputs / Findings

- 12,618 reported UFO sightings investigated; 701 classified "unidentified"
- The [[estimate-of-the-situation]] (Project Sign, 1948) concluded ET origin; USAF Chief of Staff [[horace-vandenberg]] rejected it and suppressed it, transforming the program's orientation
- Terminated in 1969 following the [[condon-commission]] null-result report
- Colonel [[robert-hiper]] wrote to Condon explicitly requesting the Commission show the program was a "waste of time and money"
- **Battelle Memorial Institute analysis** (cited in [[1iaH1a3A4Lk]]): Battelle — already managing nuclear materials programs — conducted a statistical analysis of 1947–52 sightings; found **33% of the most credible, sufficiently-documented cases were classified as unknown**. [[edward-ruppelt]]'s separate analysis found ~70% of credible cases in proximity to nuclear sites, which is why Battelle was involved in the first place.

## The Sheehan Photo Viewing — January 1977

After Blue Book's official termination in 1969, its records were transferred to the National Archives. [[danny-sheehan]], working as special counsel to the Congressional Research Service UAP study commissioned by President [[jimmy-carter]], gained access to the **classified section** of Blue Book's microfiche files in the basement of the Madison Building, National Archives, in January 1977.

In those classified files — not included in the publicly released Blue Book materials — Sheehan found photos of a **~40-ft saucer crashed in a snow-covered embankment** surrounded by U.S. Air Force personnel, with unknown symbols around the dome base. He traced the symbols on the back of a legal pad.

This suggests that:
1. The publicly released Blue Book records do not represent the full archive.
2. A classified section survived intact into at least 1977.
3. The crash-retrieval evidence was documented in government files within the Blue Book administrative structure.
