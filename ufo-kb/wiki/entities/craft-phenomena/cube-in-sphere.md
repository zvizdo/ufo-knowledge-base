# Cube-in-Sphere

UAP morphology first described publicly by [[ryan-graves]] on behalf of a pilot in his squadron (VFA-11 Red Rippers) who came within approximately 50 ft of the object during a near-miss incident off the Virginia coast in 2014–2015 (see [[2014-east-coast-uap]]).

## Description

- **Outer layer**: a clear (transparent or translucent) sphere
- **Inner object**: a dark gray or black cube, suspended inside the sphere
- **Estimated diameter**: approximately 5–15 ft (best estimate given brief encounter duration)
- **No visible propulsion**, exhaust, or external control surfaces

## Reported Encounters

### VFA-11 Near-Miss (~2014–2015)
A pilot in Graves's squadron nearly collided with this object as two aircraft crossed the entry point to their working area. The object shot between the two jets, passing within ~50 ft of the lead cockpit. The pilot's description was consistent and specific enough that when Graves relayed it to a room of fellow Navy pilots after the 2017 NYT story, roughly half immediately raised their hands in recognition: "Oh yeah, the cubes and spheres, you know, we saw those."

### Distribution
Graves reports this morphology has been observed at **every major US Navy base** he is aware of: Virginia Beach/NorFolk area, NAS Lemoore (California), NALF Fallon (Nevada), and NAS Patuxent River (Maryland). The geographic spread suggests a phenomenon that is not location-specific.

## Anomalous Properties (Inferred)

- **Non-propulsive**: No exhaust plume, propeller, or visible thrust mechanism
- **IR masking**: ATFLIR detected anomalous energy but could not resolve shape detail (see [[phenomenology-categories]])
- **Stationary in extreme wind**: Objects from this morphology group held position in 120-knot winds without drift
- **Size**: At 5–15 ft, significantly smaller than any known tactical fixed-wing aircraft

## Significance

This morphology is geometrically distinct from the established UAP shapes in the historical literature (discs, cigars, triangles, Tic-Tac). Its widespread recognition within the Navy pilot community — without any prompting — suggests a shared observational record that predates public reporting.

> ⚠ Conflict flag: The cube-in-sphere shape does not match the Tic-Tac (ovoid, white, ~40 ft) described by [[david-fravor]] in 2004. Whether these represent different craft types, different NHI groups, or the same phenomenon in different configurations is unresolved.
