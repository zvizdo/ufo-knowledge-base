# Skinny Bob

"Skinny Bob" refers to a series of videos posted to YouTube in 2011 under the channel "Ivan 0135," purportedly showing footage of a grey-type alien walking, making facial expressions, and interacting with humans in a clinical setting. The footage became one of the most widely circulated pieces of alleged alien video evidence on the internet.

## Description

The videos depict:
- A slender, large-headed entity with large dark eyes, thin limbs, and no visible ears
- Fluid movement with subtle gestures and apparent emotional expression
- What appears to be a government or military clinical environment
- Multiple camera angles suggesting a documentary/archival style

The production quality is notably high relative to other alleged UAP footage of the era, which both fuels belief (it "looks real") and suspicion (it looks like a high-budget CGI production).

## Community Assessment

The UAP research community is largely dismissive. [[jesse-michaels]] addresses the video directly in [[JE1oM89dpBc]]:

> "It's clearly like a [—] dude."

This represents the mainstream UAP researcher position — Skinny Bob is treated as a CGI character, not genuine footage. The high production value is cited as evidence of fabrication rather than authenticity (genuine government footage of an alien would be expected to be lower-quality archival material, not cinematic video).

No forensic analysis has conclusively proven CGI, but the community consensus — including among figures who believe strongly in NHI reality — is that this specific video is manufactured.

## Significance

Skinny Bob exemplifies a recurring challenge in UAP discourse: high-quality media that superficially supports NHI claims can actually undermine the field by:
1. Distracting community attention from credible evidence
2. Providing debunkers with easy targets
3. Making genuine footage harder to evaluate against a backdrop of known hoaxes

It also illustrates the "grey alien" morphology's deep cultural embeddedness — any hoaxer fabricating alien footage defaults to the large-head/dark-eyes template, making it impossible to determine whether the template originates in genuine encounters or pop culture.
