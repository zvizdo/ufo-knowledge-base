# Frontiers of Propulsion Science

## Overview

Published by the American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics (AIAA) in 2009. Edited by [[eric-davis]] and [[mark-millis]], this 739-page volume is the most comprehensive peer-reviewed academic anthology of exotic propulsion concepts ever assembled. Topics covered include:

- Warp drives and metric engineering (Alcubierre and modifications)
- Zero-point vacuum energy extraction
- Laser and beam propulsion
- Nuclear propulsion variants
- Antigravity and inertial modification
- Experimental verification strategies

## Significance

In [[8TYMQOUDQBo]], [[salvatore-pais]] brought his personal copy to the interview and said: "My hat's off to Eric Davis just for that book alone." He praises both Davis and [[mark-millis]] as "amazing." Pais notes that while Davis is the "real forte behind the physics," Millis's organizational contribution in assembling the BPP program's legacy into a single academic resource is equally significant.

The book serves as a bridge between classified physics programs (AAWSAP DIRDs, black program propulsion research) and open academic discourse — using the AIAA's peer-review credibility to bring metric engineering into legitimate scientific conversation.

## Townsend Brown / Bifield-Brown Coverage

The book includes discussion of the [[biefeld-brown-effect]] — the electrostatic asymmetric capacitor thrust phenomenon attributed to [[thomas-townsend-brown]]. [[eric-davis]]'s entry takes it "at face value" as possibly coronal/ionic wind, but notes that to truly connect electromagnetism and gravity via the Brown effect would require **a fifth-dimensional space** — an observation Pais finds "interesting" and connects to his own terahertz / fifth-dimension coupling thesis.
