# The Road to Eleusis

1978 book co-authored by ethnomycologist **R. Gordon Wasson**, LSD synthesizer **Albert Hofmann**, and Boston University classicist **[[carl-ruck]]**. Argued that the *kykeon* — the sacred drink consumed by initiates at the [[eleusis|Eleusinian Mysteries]] — contained an **ergot-based psychoactive compound**, effectively arguing that the foundational mystery rite of Western civilization was a psychedelic experience.

## The Core Argument

| Author | Contribution |
|--------|-------------|
| R. Gordon Wasson | Ethnomycological precedent — documented ritual psilocybin use in Mexico; established that ancient cultures regularly incorporated psychoactive fungi into religious ritual |
| Albert Hofmann | Chemical analysis — ergot (*Claviceps purpurea*) grows on grain, contains alkaloids from which LSD is derived; the kykeon preparation is chemically plausible as a psychedelic compound |
| Carl Ruck | Classical philology — Greek literary sources describe kykeon as producing effects far beyond ordinary intoxication; the transformation reported by Eleusinian initiates is inconsistent with wine alone |

## Significance in the KB

*The Road to Eleusis* was the text that sent [[brian-muraresku]] "down the rabbit hole" that resulted in *[[the-immortality-key]]* 42 years later. Muraresku describes it as the academic foundation for the modern inquiry into psychedelics and ancient religion.

The book also raised the question that [[carl-ruck]] formulated and Muraresku carried forward: **Was [[dionysus]] the god of wine, or the god of psychedelics?**
