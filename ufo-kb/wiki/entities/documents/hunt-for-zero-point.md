# The Hunt for Zero Point

## Overview

Published 2001 by [[nick-cook]], former Jane's Defence Weekly editor. Cook spent several years investigating whether the 1950s anti-gravity research boom — undertaken by Bell Aircraft, Lockheed, Martin, Lear, and others — was real and whether it continued in classified programs. The book concludes that it was real and that the black-budget continuation is highly probable.

## Key Revelations

### Soviet Intelligence and Schauberger Files
During his research, Cook interviewed [[eugene-podkletnov]], the Russian physicist known for rotating superconductor / gravity-shielding experiments. Podkletnov disclosed that his **father had been a Soviet intelligence agent tasked specifically with retrieving [[victor-schauberger]]'s engineering files** at the end of WWII. This is the most concrete intelligence-community evidence for the importance of Schauberger's implosion/rotation work that has entered public discourse.

### 1950s Aerospace Anti-Gravity Programs
Cook documents the wave of public anti-gravity claims by major aerospace contractors in 1956–57 (Bell, Lear, Martin, Sikorsky) following the funding and conference ecosystem around the work of [[thomas-townsend-brown]] and researchers like [[lewis-whitten]]. The sudden silence after 1957 — which [[agnew-bahnson]]'s 1957 Chapel Hill gravity conference represents the peak of — is a key piece of evidence for physics lockdown.

### Electrogravitics
Cook popularized the term "electrogravitics" — the study of whether electrical charge asymmetry can produce thrust or gravitational effects. This connects directly to [[biefeld-brown-effect]] and to Townsend Brown's gravitator experiments.

## Significance

The book brought terms like "electrogravitics" and "Townsend Brown effect" into mainstream UFO/UAP discourse. It was the first widely-read mainstream work to systematically argue that classified anti-gravity research is real, ongoing, and connected to the B-2 stealth bomber program (which Cook argues may use electrostatic thrust augmentation).

Cited in [[8TYMQOUDQBo]] by Pais (re: Podkletnov connection) and cross-referenced throughout the KB as the primary secondary source for the 1950s anti-gravity boom.
