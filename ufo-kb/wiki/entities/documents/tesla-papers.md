# Tesla Papers

## Summary

Nikola Tesla's private research papers were seized by the FBI immediately after his death in January 1943 at the Hotel New Yorker in New York. [[john-trump]] — MIT physicist and uncle of Donald Trump — was dispatched to review them and officially concluded they contained "nothing of consequence to the war effort."

## Key Questions

In [[09KP8XVf5nY]], [[harold-malmgren]] explicitly raises the Tesla papers as a suspicious case: unlike the JFK files, there has been no sustained public demand for their release, despite Tesla's documented claims of having received communications from extraterrestrial intelligences and his work on energy transmission and advanced propulsion concepts that remain unexplained.

The question is whether John Trump's "nothing of consequence" assessment was accurate or whether it was a cover for the classification and retention of the most sensitive material — consistent with the [[physics-lockdown]] pattern of entire theoretical domains being sealed during the nuclear era.

[[thomas-townsend-brown]], who claimed similar "space brother" contact experiences and worked on electrogravitics, represents a parallel case — suggesting a coherent (if classified) tradition of researchers working on non-mainstream propulsion with claimed non-human contact.

## Provenance

The papers are held in part by the Nikola Tesla Museum in Belgrade (donated by Tesla's nephew), but the full set of what was seized by the FBI in 1943 and what was ultimately retained by US intelligence has never been publicly accounted for.
