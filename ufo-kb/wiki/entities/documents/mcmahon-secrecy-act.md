# McMahon Secrecy Act (Atomic Energy Act of 1946)

The Atomic Energy Act of 1946 — informally called the McMahon Act after Senate sponsor Brian McMahon — established civilian control of atomic energy through the [[atomic-energy-commission]] (AEC). It also created the classification architecture for nuclear technology that has persisted, via successive amendments and the Department of Energy, to the present.

## The Condon Irony

[[edward-condon]], who later directed the [[condon-report]] (the 1969 NCAR study that officially dismissed UFOs as not worthy of further scientific study), **co-authored this law**. The man who became the face of official UFO dismissal wrote the very legal framework that governs their secrecy. This is the central irony documented in the [[debunkers-as-atomic-insiders]] concept page.

## UAP Classification Mechanism

[[david-grusch]] and [[bob-maguire]] independently identify the Atomic Energy Act (and its successor [[atomic-energy-act-classification]] provisions now administered by DOE) as the statute that *automatically classifies* all recovered non-human technology:

> If recovered UAP hardware involves nuclear or energy-related technology — or is classified under DOE's special access programs — it is placed beyond normal congressional oversight without requiring an explicit presidential classification order.

This "automatic" classification mechanism is why UAP programs could theoretically persist for decades without being disclosed through normal oversight channels.

## Key Provisions

- Established the AEC as civilian custodian of all nuclear weapons development
- Created Restricted Data (RD) classification category — information that is *born classified* and does not require a stamp to be protected
- Made disclosure of RD punishable as espionage
