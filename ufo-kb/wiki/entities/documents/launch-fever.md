# Launch Fever

Tim Taylor's autobiography covering his career as a NASA mission controller at Cape Canaveral. Cited by multiple researchers ([[chris-bledsoe]], [[diana-pasulka]], [[jesse-michaels]]) as containing **coded references** to activities that, in context, imply involvement in classified UAP-adjacent work.

## Key Passages (Reported)

- **Nassau references**: Taylor describes flying cargo jets between Florida and Nassau, apparently unaware that his co-pilot was smuggling drugs. He mentions that *"time moved differently in Nassau"* — interpreted by Jesse Michaels as coded language for the [[nassau-group]] time travel program, given Taylor's separate claims to Bledsoe and Ryan Bledsoe about membership in that group.
- **Access levels**: The autobiography documents Taylor's deep access to NASA launch facilities and his unique monitoring role.
- **Consciousness protocols**: Per [[diana-pasulka]]'s *American Cosmic* account, Taylor advised that consciousness-based protocols (sun gazing, etc.) are relevant to successful launches — an extraordinary statement from a NASA mission controller.

## Significance

Linda Brown (daughter of [[thomas-townsend-brown]]) left an Amazon review of this book in the early 2010s — before any public awareness of Tim Taylor. This independently confirmed the connection between Taylor and the Townsend Brown family, and preceded Jesse Michaels's documentary investigations by years.
