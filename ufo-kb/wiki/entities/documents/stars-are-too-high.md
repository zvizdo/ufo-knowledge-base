# The Stars Are Too High

Science fiction novel written by [[agnew-bahnson]] — the industrialist who funded the 1957 UNC Chapel Hill gravity conference and the [[institute-for-field-physics]] at the University of North Carolina. Bahnson died in a plane crash before or shortly after publication.

## Plot

The novel involves:
- A team of **rogue Nazi scientists** who were captured and brought to America after WWII
- These scientists collaborate with American counterparts to **build man-made flying saucers** using **anti-gravity technology**
- The makers of these saucers then **hatch a plan to fake an alien invasion** in order to quell tensions between the United States and the Soviet Union — a manufactured UAP event as a geopolitical de-escalation tool

## Significance

The novel is remarkable for several reasons:

1. **The author's real activities**: Bahnson was simultaneously funding real anti-gravity research and convening the world's top physicists to work on gravity. His fiction premise — Nazi scientists + American collaboration + man-made saucers + staged alien invasion — maps suspiciously closely onto what some researchers believe actually occurred in classified programs.

2. **The Nazi → US pipeline thread**: The novel's Nazi-scientists-to-America plot mirrors the documented [[operation-paperclip]] program and the [[kammler-stab]] technology transfer.

3. **The false-flag UAP hypothesis**: The staged alien invasion as a Cold War geopolitical tool is a direct fictional statement of the [[catastrophic-disclosure-fear]] and [[controlled-disclosure]] dynamics — the idea that UAP phenomena might be at least partly staged for political effect.

4. **Bahnson's plane crash**: The "timely death in a plane crash" of the person who ran the most serious public anti-gravity research program of the 1950s — and who wrote a novel that could be read as a barely-fictionalized account of what was actually happening — fits a familiar pattern in UFO discourse.
