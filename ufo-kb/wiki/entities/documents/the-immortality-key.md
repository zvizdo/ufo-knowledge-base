# The Immortality Key

2020 book by [[brian-muraresku]] arguing that the Eleusinian Mysteries of ancient Greece and early Christian eucharistic rituals both employed psychedelic sacraments — specifically ergot-derived compounds — and that the experiences they induced were explicitly designed to give participants direct experiential evidence of post-death consciousness continuity. Foreword by Graham Hancock. *New York Times* bestseller.

Referenced in [[0qlppHc3-gg]] as one of several converging evidence streams pointing toward [[consciousness-survives-death]] — grouped alongside [[ian-stevenson]]'s past-lives research and [[diane-powell]]'s autism-telepathy data.

## Key Claims

- The Eleusinian Mysteries (celebrated at [[eleusis]] near Athens for ~2,000 years) were organized around a psychedelic drink (*kykeon*) containing ergot-derived compounds.
- Early Christian communion rites in some communities may have similarly involved psychoactive substances.
- The purpose of both traditions was to produce a death-like experience followed by a profound sense of continuity — essentially a controlled near-death experience demonstrating that consciousness survives the body.
- This "immortality technology" was not metaphorical but pharmacological and experiential.
- [[carl-ruck]] (BU) asked 42 years before the book's publication: **was Dionysus the god of wine or the god of psychedelics?** — Muraresku's book is the extended answer.
- Jesus used the Greek word **"musterion"** (a religious secret confided only to the initiated, per the Greek lexicon) when asked why he spoke in parables — Christianity was born with secrets by its founder's own declaration.
- John 6 employs the Greek *hotrogo*: "whoever eats my flesh and drinks my blood **has** eternal life" (present tense immediacy, not deferred afterlife) — structurally parallel to Dionysian mystery-rite immortality grants.
- In 364 AD, the Roman hierophant **Praetextatus** told Emperor Valentinian that shutting down the Mysteries would be existentially catastrophic: *"Life would be a biatos [unlivable]."*
- The book is supported by new **organic chemistry data from ancient chalices** — for the first time, material evidence for what was in the cups.
