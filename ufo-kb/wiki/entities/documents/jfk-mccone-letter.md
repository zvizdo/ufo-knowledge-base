# JFK to John McCone Letter

A memorandum allegedly authored by President [[john-f-kennedy]] and addressed to acting CIA Director **John McCone**, asking for data on "unknowns" — the language used in mid-twentieth-century classified contexts for what the public called UFOs. The premise of the request is that Soviet airspace observers might misinterpret unknown objects as US incursions, and the US needed to coordinate with the USSR on shared protocols to prevent unintended escalation.

## Summary

- **Recipient**: John McCone (acting CIA Director after JFK's dismissal of [[allen-dulles]])
- **Subject**: Request for UFO/unknowns data
- **Stated motivation**: prevent US-Soviet misperception of unknowns as adversarial military activity
- **Released**: under FOIA in 2005 (per Jesse Michaels in [[lbGE3EC6StE]])
- **Investigator**: [[john-greenwald]] (blackvault.com) has reviewed the document; "verdict's still out" on authenticity

## Key Claims

- The President of the United States is requesting CIA's UFO data
- The request frames UFOs as a US-Soviet coordination problem, not a domestic-secrecy problem
- The request post-dates JFK's firing of Dulles, suggesting JFK was bypassing rather than relying on the existing CIA UFO custodianship structure

## Provenance

The document surfaced in a 2005 FOIA release. Its authenticity has not been conclusively established — common doubts include period-typography matches, signature verification, and the chain of custody between the Kennedy White House and the modern release. [[john-greenwald]] has investigated and remained agnostic.

The document's framing — coordination with the USSR around mutual unknowns observation — **does dovetail with the on-record [[1971-salt-talks-unknowns]] discussions** in which Henry Kissinger and the Russian Ambassador discussed microwave-beam phenomena affecting US embassy staff in Moscow. The structural precedent for such US-Soviet coordination on unknowns predates any disclosure of this letter, which has been used both for and against authenticity.
