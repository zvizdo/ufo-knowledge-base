# A Tear in the Sky

*A Tear in the Sky* (2023) is both a documentary film and the basis for a peer-reviewed scientific paper authored by [[matthew-shostakus]] and colleagues at the [[u-albany-project-x]] / [[scientific-coalition-for-uap-studies]] team. It chronicles a five-day rooftop observation mission at Laguna Beach and [[catalina-island]], led by [[kevin-knuth]].

## The Mission

The team deployed multi-spectral sensors (infrared, visual, flare cameras) on rooftops for five consecutive nights. Key results:

- **ISS misidentification**: The bright light that initially excited the team turned out to be the International Space Station — the satellite tracking app hadn't accounted for daylight savings time. Shostakus later reconstructed the ISS's physical size to within one foot from pixel analysis, validating the sensor rig's calibration.
- **Infrared sea-drop event**: Knuth personally observed through night-vision goggles something drop into the sea in infrared at the same time the flare camera triggered — but the flare camera's "things falling" turned out to be a known sensor malfunction mode.
- Knuth's assessment: they learned "what not to do." The mission is valued as a **methodology-building exercise** more than an anomaly detection success.

## The Shostakus Paper

[[matthew-shostakus]] authored a scientific paper based on the Catalina mission's data and methodology, published in 2024. It is one of the rare peer-reviewed publications from a civilian UAP field observation effort — significant because peer review provides an independent standard that government-only data lacks.
