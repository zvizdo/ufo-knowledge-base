# VASCO Nuclear Transients Paper

Peer-reviewed study by [[steven-bruell]] and [[beatriz-villarroel]], accepted at *Scientific Reports* (Nature Publishing Group) at the time of the [[1zRWi_r3HRM]] interview.

## Summary

Compiled a 2,700-day database (spanning the Palomar plate survey period) recording:
- Whether a nuclear bomb test occurred on each day (134 tests total)
- Whether a [[vasco]] transient was observed that day (~300 days with transients)
- Whether a UAP sighting appeared in the CUFOS database that day

## Key Claims

- **Nuclear-transient correlation**: Transient-day frequency increases from 11% to ~19% the day after a nuclear test — a **68% relative increase**, p = 0.008 (8 in 1,000 chance of error)
- **Nuclear-UAP-transient triad**: All three pairwise correlations are statistically significant
- **1956 cessation**: The last nuclear-test-correlated transient was March 17, 1956; 38 additional nuclear tests across the following year yielded zero correlated transients

## Significance

This is the first peer-reviewed paper to statistically quantify the nuclear-UAP correlation using instrumental (plate transient) data rather than witness testimony. The triad finding is critical: UAP sightings (witness reports) and transients (plate data) both correlate with nuclear tests, and with each other. This cannot be explained by plate defects, cosmic rays, or observatory-local effects — a cosmic ray artifact would not correlate with civilian UAP sightings from around the country.

## Provenance

Data: VASCO transient catalog from [[palomar-observatory]] digitized plates; CUFOS UAP database; publicly available nuclear test records.
