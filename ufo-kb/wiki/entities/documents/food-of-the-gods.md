# Food of the Gods

Terence McKenna's 1992 book proposing what became known as the "stoned ape" hypothesis: the consumption of psilocybin mushrooms was the catalytic event responsible for the rapid expansion of human consciousness and cultural complexity in prehistoric times.

## Core Argument

McKenna argues that early hominids following cattle herds in Africa would have consumed psilocybin-containing mushrooms growing in the dung. At low doses, these mushrooms enhance visual acuity (hunting advantage); at higher doses, they produce the radical consciousness expansion that McKenna argues drove the "Quantum Leap Forward" in human behavior — abstract thought, art, language, ritual.

## Connection to Hancock

[[graham-hancock]] in [[dfPfPB601hw]] recommends *Food of the Gods* as one of the first books to suggest the quantum leap in human consciousness was driven entirely by psychedelic experimentation:

> "McKenna thinks that this Quantum Leap Forward in human behavior and in human consciousness was entirely the result of experimenting with psychedelics."

Hancock credits McKenna as "a key thinker in this field" and notes that McKenna described a mushroom trip where the mushrooms said: "within my memory exist the blueprints for hyper-light drives."

The thesis connects directly to [[brian-muraresku]]'s [[the-immortality-key]] (ergotized barley at Eleusis) and to Hancock's own *[[supernatural-visionary]]* (psychedelic access to NHI entities in Paleolithic cave art).
