# Passport to Magonia

Jacques Vallee's 1969 book — widely considered the foundational text for the folkloric/interdimensional hypothesis of UAP. Vallee catalogued medieval European accounts of encounters with fairies and elves and demonstrated that their phenomenology is identical to modern alien abduction reports.

## Core Finding

The fairy and elf encounters documented in medieval folklore — across multiple independent traditions — share structural features with modern alien abduction accounts:
- Surgery performed on humans
- Abduction into another realm
- Sexual encounters with the entities
- Hybrid offspring
- Missing time
- Re-entry into the human world

Vallee's conclusion: we are not dealing with two separate phenomena — one folkloric, one extraterrestrial — but with **a single phenomenon that has been culturally reframed across time**.

## Significance

This argues against the nuts-and-bolts extraterrestrial interpretation: if the same contact experience was occurring 800 years before modern aircraft, the explanation cannot be spacecraft from other planets. The phenomenon predates the technological frame we now impose on it.

## Graham Hancock's Use

Hancock in [[dfPfPB601hw]] calls Vallee's *Passport to Magonia* a "breakthrough book" and says "anybody who's interested in the alien UFO mystery needs to go read Passport to Magonia." He quotes from it extensively in *[[supernatural-visionary]]*:

> "[Vallee] was the first to recognize that there are incredible phenomenological parallels between the entities that were called fairies and elves in the Middle Ages and the entities that we call aliens today."

Hancock extends the analysis even further back: shamanic cave art (30,000+ years old) depicts the same entity types. Magonia adds the medieval layer between cave art and the modern UFO era.
