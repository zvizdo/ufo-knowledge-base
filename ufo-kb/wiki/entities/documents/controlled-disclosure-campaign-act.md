# Controlled Disclosure Campaign Act

The Controlled Disclosure Campaign Act (CDCA) is a 64-page comprehensive UAP disclosure statute drafted and advocated by [[danny-sheehan]]'s [[new-paradigm-institute]]. It is the most structurally ambitious UAP legislation attempted in the U.S. Congress.

## Legislative History

- **Senate Intelligence Committee**: passed **17–0** (unanimous)
- **Full Senate vote**: passed **94–6** (near-unanimous bipartisan support)
- **House status**: blocked by [[michael-turner]] (House Intelligence Committee chair, Ohio 10th District / [[wright-patterson-air-force-base]]); Turner talked Speaker [[mike-johnson]] out of allowing a floor vote
- **Partial enactment**: 23 of 64 pages were enacted into law (embedded in NDAA or similar vehicle)
- **Turner's replacement**: Brad Wenstrup replaced Turner as House Intel chair in January 2025; expected to be more tractable
- **Primary Senate sponsors**: [[chuck-schumer]] (original majority leader sponsor), [[mike-rounds]] (committed to re-introduction), [[marco-rubio]] (co-sponsor; now Secretary of State)

## Key Provisions — Enacted (23 pages)

- **National Archives mandate**: all military and intelligence community UAP documents must be transferred to the National Archives by **September 2025**
- This creates a legally enforceable deadline that operates regardless of whether the remaining 41 pages pass

## Key Provisions — Pending (41 pages)

- **Nine-person independent review panel**: Presidential appointment with nominations from designated organizations including [[new-paradigm-institute]]
- **Scope**: all documents and physical evidence related to UAPs since **January 1, 1945**
- **Declassification default**: documents older than 25 years made publicly available unless a specific security exception is successfully argued to the panel
- **Judicial subpoena authority**: enforceable by courts — extends to all three branches of government and private contractors
- **Physical evidence transfer**: recovered craft, biological samples, and tech artifacts transferred to National Archives control
- **Funding**: $22 million
- **Private contractor coverage**: the most novel provision — targets Waived Unacknowledged Special Access Programs (WUSAPs) operated by aerospace companies outside normal congressional oversight

## Strategic Context

The CDCA is modeled on the **JFK Assassination Records Collection Act** and the subsequent Records Review Board — a precedent Sheehan knows intimately. The ARRB released millions of pages; Sheehan argues the same mechanism works for UAP with adequate subpoena power.

The judicial subpoena mechanism is the critical innovation. Congressional subpoenas can be challenged, delayed, and politically neutralized; judicial subpoenas backed by contempt-of-court sanctions cannot be easily evaded.

The physical evidence clause is the most contested provision. Sheehan believes aerospace contractors and certain military commands will fight this hardest because it would expose not just documents but the physical reality of recovered craft and materials — removing the ability to deny that physical programs exist.

## Citizens for Disclosure

[[new-paradigm-institute]] is building the **Citizens for Disclosure** grassroots network in all 435 congressional districts and all 50 states to generate constituent pressure for full CDCA passage. 1,500 volunteers are currently involved.
