# Kettle Footage

## Summary

The Kettle footage refers to film shot from the "Kettle 2" camera position during the [[bluegill-triple-prime]] high-altitude nuclear test on October 26, 1962, in the [[marshall-islands]]. Multiple camera positions were used to document the detonation, and the Kettle series was positioned to capture objects in proximity to the test zone.

## Significance

If the [[1962-bluegill-uap-shootdown]] account — relayed from [[richard-bissell]] through [[harold-malmgren]] — is accurate, the Kettle 2 camera would have been positioned to capture the UAP "tag-along" that was reportedly downed by the nuclear X-ray pulse. The footage's existence has been referenced by [[jeffrey-kruchshank]] as documentary evidence that should exist in the government's film archive.

## Provenance Question

In 2023, Kruchshank contacted [[byron-ristvet]] at the Department of Energy seeking this footage. Ristvet responded that the DOE was "unable to locate" it. This is the specific open question flagged by [[harold-malmgren]]'s daughter: *"Was the Kettle 2 footage deliberately destroyed, or genuinely lost?"*

The targeted unavailability of precisely the footage that would document the alleged shootdown — while other test films from the same series remain accessible — is consistent with the [[ufo-secrecy-cabal]] pattern of removing or reclassifying anomalous evidence.
