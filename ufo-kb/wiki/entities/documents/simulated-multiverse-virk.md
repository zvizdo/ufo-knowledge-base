# The Simulated Multiverse

Second book by [[riz-virk]]. Extends the simulation hypothesis from a single-simulation model to a multi-timeline model grounded in [[hugh-everett]]'s Many-Worlds Interpretation. The simulated multiverse is Virk's name for the complete set of branching timelines that the simulator runs, evaluates, and prunes.

## Key Arguments

- **Many Worlds = save-state runs**: the branching universes of Everett are not physically independent cosmoses but information-state forks that the simulator runs in parallel.
- **Timeline pruning**: the simulator saves game states, runs branches forward to evaluate outcomes, prunes sub-optimal branches, and lets the best timeline proceed. This explains Mandela Effect (variable changed in a pruned branch leaves memory traces), deja vu (timeline rerun), and Philip K. Dick's sense that simulators chose the Allied-victory timeline over his German-victory novel.
- **Optimization function**: what are the simulators optimizing toward? Virk leaves this open — possibly maximum consciousness, maximum love, maximum survival, or all three.
