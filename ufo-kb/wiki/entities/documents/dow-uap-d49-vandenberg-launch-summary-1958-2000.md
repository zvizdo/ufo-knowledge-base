# USAF — Vandenberg AFB Launch Summary, 1958–2000

Official registry of all major launch operations from [[vandenberg-afb|Vandenberg Air Force Base]], California, prepared by the 30th Space Wing History Office (30th SW/HO) as of 3 February 2000. Signed by Jeffrey Geiger, Office of History. Distributed to HQ USAF/XOFS, HQ AFSPC/HO, [[45th-space-wing|45th SW/HO]], NASA HO, [[kennedy-space-center|Kennedy Space Center]], and contractor organizations ([[aerospace-corporation|Aerospace Corp]], Boeing, TRW, Orbital Sciences Corp). Released in PURSUE Release 01 as the companion chronological index to [[dow-uap-d48-modeling-space-booster-failures-1996-09]].

## Document Structure

The summary is organized in four sections:
- **Glossary**: 80+ acronyms covering vehicle types, program categories, and facility designations
- **Launch Facility Guide**: Histories of all active and decommissioned pads, including acceptance dates, first-launch dates, original purpose, and current status — with specific milestones noted
- **Annual Launch Summary by Command**: Cross-tabulated tables 1958–2000 (Chart I 1958–78, Chart II 1979–98, Chart III 1999–2000) showing launches per year broken out by command: AFSC, SAC, NAVY, NASA, ADC, RAF, AEC, CRL, ACC, AFSPC, AFMC, and commercial operators (LM, Orbital Sciences, Boeing)
- **Chronological Launch Log** (the primary dataset): Entry 1 to 1,790, listing launch sequence number, date (Vandenberg local time), nickname, order number, facility designation, vehicle type, program category, cumulative count by booster type, command, and cumulative count by command

## Key Milestones from the Launch Facility Guide

- **First Vandenberg launch**: 16 Dec 1958, TUNE UP, Thor IRBM (AFSC), SLC-2E (fmr 75-1-1)
- **First Vandenberg space launch**: 28 Feb 1959, SLC-1W — "world's first polar-orbiting satellite" per the facility guide; this Thor/Agena A mission marked the beginning of Vandenberg's role as the primary polar-orbit launch site
- **First ICBM launch**: 9 Sep 1959, Atlas D, BMRS A-2, DESERT HEAT
- **First in-silo launch**: Titan I from prototype Titan II silo at SLTF (3 May 1961) — used to characterize in-silo launch environment effects on an emplaced missile
- **OSTF destroyed**: 3 Dec 1960, in-silo explosion during simulated Titan I launch at OSTF — "a scarred hole in the ground remains" — no actual launches had been conducted from the facility
- **First weapon-system training**: RAF Thor IRBM IWST launches from LE-8 (fmr 75-2-8), beginning 16 Apr 1959; program concluded 18 Jun 1962 with 21 total RAF launches
- **First Vandenberg Titan II**: 16 Feb 1963, 395-C silo
- **SLC-6** (Space Shuttle pad, originally [[manned-orbiting-laboratory|Manned Orbiting Laboratory]]): included in facility guide but no shuttle launches conducted at Vandenberg

## UAP Attribution Role

This document is the Western Range counterpart to [[dow-uap-d48-modeling-space-booster-failures-1996-09]]'s failure-mode probability database. Together, the two documents enable date-specific UAP attribution analysis near [[vandenberg-afb]]:

1. A UAP report citing a date near Vandenberg can be cross-checked against the chronological launch log — if no launch is on record for that date (or several days around it, accounting for memory errors), the launch explanation is ruled out
2. If a launch did occur, d48's Mode-5 probability data (only ~8% of all failures produce off-nominal trajectories visible far from the flight line) provides the statistical benchmark for assessing whether the vehicle could account for the observation

**Notable attribution implications**:
- The AEC-sponsored space probe launches 1960–1963 (from PLC-A and PALC-B, constructed for AEC) are not commonly known; observers near Vandenberg in that era may not have known AEC was conducting launches from there
- RAF Thor IRBM IWST launches 1959–1962 involved British crews operating US missiles — 21 launches during the peak UAP reporting period of 1959–1961; these are unlabeled in public memory
- Peak launch density of 100–120/year in 1963–1967 means roughly one launch every 3 days on average — dense enough to explain many California coastal UAP reports if Mode-5 failure trajectories are included
- The SAC Minuteman/Titan ICBM operational test program (633 total SAC launches over 40 years) was the most operationally significant hidden source of aerial anomalies: test launches often had classified nicknames and were not announced publicly in advance

## Cross-Cutting Significance

- **Completeness**: 1,790 entries with date, facility, vehicle, program, and command — the most complete open-source record of Western Range launches as of 2000
- **RAF participation**: 21 British IWST launches 1959–1962 represent the only foreign military use of Vandenberg that appears in this record
- **AEC use**: 16 AEC launches 1960–1963 using Nike/Viper I, Deacon Arrow II, Kiva/Hopi, and Astrobee 1500 sounding rockets — nuclear-weapons agency conducting space probe operations from US military ranges
- **Transition era**: Captures the full Cold War-to-post-Cold War shift: SAC dominance 1960–92 → AFSPC takeover for satellite operations 1990–2000; Boeing and OSC commercial launches 1995–2000 reflecting the dawn of commercial spaceflight from a military base
