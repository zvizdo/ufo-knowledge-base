# DMT: The Spirit Molecule

Book by [[rick-strassman]] (2001), based on his clinical DMT research at the University of New Mexico (1990–1995) — the first FDA-approved human psychedelic study in decades. Strassman administered intravenous DMT to 60 volunteers under controlled conditions and systematically documented their experiences.

## Key Claims

1. A significant subset of subjects encountered autonomous beings that appeared to exist independently of the subject's mind — beings with their own agendas, environments, and knowledge.
2. Entities had consistent typology: insectoid, machine-like, humanoid, or divine.
3. Some entities performed what felt like medical procedures on subjects.
4. The subjective reality of the encounter felt more real than ordinary waking life.

Strassman was unable to classify these encounters as hallucinations under the standard framework, and the book ends with his growing uncertainty about the ontological status of DMT entities — opening the door to the [[dmt-nhi-hypothesis]].
