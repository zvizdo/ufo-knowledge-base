# Encounters (Pasulka)

2023 book by [[diana-pasulka]], the follow-up to *[[american-cosmic]]*. Where *American Cosmic* focused on Pasulka's own entry into UAP research and the experiences of "Tyler" ([[tim-taylor]]), *Encounters* broadens to document a wider network of experiencers and researchers — including alleged members of secret space programs and Silicon Valley AI communities.

## The "Simulacrum" Account

One of the most striking details in *Encounters* (cited in [[tS_64sTN5AU]]): Pasulka interviews a woman whose father was allegedly part of a **secret American space program**. The woman describes a recurring joke that her father and his colleagues would exchange at lunch:

> *"A joke my father and his friends often exchange consisted of one word: simulacrum."*

The use of Baudrillard's term — a copy without an original, a virtual replacement for reality — as an insider joke in the secret-space-program world is flagged by [[jesse-michaels]] as "giving me goosebumps." It corroborates the [[simulation-theory]] cluster in the KB: the phenomenon may involve a layer of reality that undercuts consensus physical reality.

This account is hearsay (daughter reporting what her father said), but it is a named source in a published academic book.

## The Fourth Research Tradition

Pasulka argues there are **four UAP research traditions**:

1. **Intelligence/classified tradition** — Alan Hynek and successors; research ongoing but not public; probably good reasons for opacity.
2. **Public hobbyist ufology** — public-facing civilian scholarship.
3. **Academic scholarship** — [[john-mack]], Pasulka herself originally.
4. **Emergent fourth tradition** — accidental collaborations between insiders (Tyler / [[tim-taylor]]) and civilian scholars (Pasulka), producing transparency never seen before. This is characterized as *emergent* — neither party planned it; something bigger than its parts appeared.

## Simone Chapter

A chapter is devoted to "Simone" — a figure in Pasulka's network who believes AI will achieve what the Gutenberg Press did: **take power from a small entrenched group and distribute it widely**. Simone frames the current AI transition as hopeful because of this democratizing potential, while acknowledging it could go the other way (CCP surveillance model). Pasulka partially disagrees: she believes personal salvation matters more than collective structural outcomes.

## Disclosure as Colonial Concept

*Encounters* explicitly frames the current "disclosure" moment as **colonial hubris**: the assumption that the US government discovering/acknowledging UAP constitutes a first-time revelation erases millennia of Indigenous, Aboriginal Australian, Haitian Vodou, and other non-Western traditions that have maintained continuous contact frameworks. This is one of Pasulka's sharpest critiques of the mainstream UAP discourse.

## Puppet Masters: Plato's Cave Withheld

Pasulka writes that she has refrained from teaching her students **one specific thing** about Plato's Cave allegory: the existence of the **puppet masters** — the figures casting the shadows. The book engages with the question of who is actually controlling the narrative and dropping the monolith.
