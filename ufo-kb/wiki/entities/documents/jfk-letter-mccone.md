# JFK Letter to McCone

## Summary

This document — a letter or memo from President John F. Kennedy to CIA Director [[john-mccone]] — is referenced in [[09KP8XVf5nY]] as part of the documentary evidence that JFK was actively trying to gain access to CIA/intelligence UAP files in the period leading up to his assassination in November 1963.

## Provenance

The letter's authenticity is contested. It circulates in UAP research circles as part of a broader set of documents suggesting Kennedy's intent to share UAP information with the Soviets and his awareness that he was being denied access to compartmentalized programs. The CIA's institutional posture under Dulles/Bissell/McCone involved creating classification layers (AEC, Eyes Only) that could exclude even the President.

[[harold-malmgren]]'s account in [[09KP8XVf5nY]] — his firsthand opinion that JFK's assassination was primarily motivated by his UAP disclosure intent — contextualizes the letter as part of a dangerous push against the secrecy apparatus. [[douglas-caddy]]'s relay of [[howard-hunt]]'s deathbed statement ("it was about the alien presence") provides independent corroboration from the assassination's institutional vicinity.

## Key Claims

- Kennedy was pushing CIA to share UAP materials/briefings with the Soviets as a unifying device
- The letter indicates Kennedy had awareness of classified UAP programs but was being kept below the full picture
- The CIA interpreted this push as a threat to the secrecy infrastructure
- The letter is dated **November 12, 1963** — exactly 10 days before JFK's assassination on November 22 ([[TH10mD9hQas]])

## E. Howard Hunt Corroboration

CIA operative [[e-howard-hunt]] — who was himself connected to the assassination's institutional orbit — reportedly claimed before his death that JFK's assassination was partially motivated by his inquiries into UFOs. Jesse Michaels cites this in [[TH10mD9hQas]] as independent corroboration from someone embedded in the relevant networks. Hunt's statement is hearsay (relayed posthumously) but carries weight given his position.
