# Death by Astonishment

Forthcoming book by [[andrew-gallimore]]. A history of science's encounter with DMT — the trajectory from [[stephen-szara]]'s 1956 self-injection through [[rick-strassman]]'s 1990s trials to the contemporary DMTX program. The title is an homage to [[terence-mckenna]] and [[william-burroughs]] (Gallimore notes Jesse Michaels attributes it to McKenna or Burroughs).

Foreword written by [[graham-hancock]].

## Described Scope

Gallimore in [[8XD1ZiuhXoY]]: "If you're really interested in what DMT is about and why it's such an enigmatic and mysterious molecule, read *Death by Astonishment*." It covers:
- Why mainstream scientific explanations for DMT (hallucination, dream imagery, archetypal symbols) fail to account for the vast majority of the experience
- The systematic process by which Gallimore eliminated standard explanations before concluding something genuinely inexplicable is occurring
- The shift from early biochemistry/pharmacology to the consciousness/NHI framework

The title reflects the fundamental phenomenological quality of the DMT experience: it astonishes completely and immediately.
