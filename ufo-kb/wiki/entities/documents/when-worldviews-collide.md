# When Worldviews Collide

Essay/talk by Harvard psychiatrist **[[john-mack]]** exploring the epistemic gap between the abduction-experiencer worldview and the materialist-academic worldview that frames reception of his clinical research. The piece is referenced in the [[C4rSj5Aum7w]] transcript and in adjacent discussions of Mack's lineage.

## Summary

Mack's argument: the dispute over the reality-status of abduction experiences is downstream of a deeper dispute over what counts as evidence and what counts as a real category of experience. Worldviews that admit only sensory-corroborated material claims will never be moved by abduction-experiencer testimony; worldviews that take subjective reports as data points see something different from the same testimony. The work is part of Mack's larger argument that academic medicine has structurally narrowed the categories it can recognize.

## Key Claims

- Abduction-experience reception is gated by background metaphysical commitments
- Materialist filtering produces systematic miscategorization of experiencer reports
- Anomalous experience requires non-reductive frames
