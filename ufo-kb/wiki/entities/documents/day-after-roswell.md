# The Day After Roswell

## Summary

*The Day After Roswell* was written by Colonel Philip Corso, a decorated Army officer who served on the National Security Council staff under Eisenhower. Corso claims that after Roswell, he was placed in charge of distributing recovered alien technology to US defense contractors for reverse engineering. He specifically names Bell Labs as a recipient — claiming the transistor was derived from alien technology — and lists night-vision goggles, fiber optics, and Kevlar as other reverse-engineered outputs.

## Key Claims

- Corso personally oversaw the "seeding" of Roswell-derived technologies to US industry via the Army's R&D office
- Bell Labs received alien semiconductor materials that accelerated the transistor
- The Roswell craft's guidance system informed inertial navigation development
- The Army, not the CIA or Air Force, was the primary custodian of the crashed materials he worked with

## Provenance

The book is a direct-testimony claim with significant controversy. It has been disputed by researchers who note that Corso was known to embellish and that the technological histories he cites have alternative documented explanations. However, [[philip-corso]]'s claim is referenced approvingly in [[09KP8XVf5nY]] — particularly through [[richard-bissell]]'s private confirmation to [[harold-malmgren]] that Roswell materials were distributed for reverse engineering, providing an independent corroboration of the general thesis if not Corso's specific details.

[[pippa-malmgren]]'s heuristic — "look for technologies where there's no onramp" — directly engages with Corso's framework.
