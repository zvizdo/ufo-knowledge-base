# Flatland: A Romance of Many Dimensions

Satirical mathematical novella published in 1884 by English schoolmaster and theologian **Edwin Abbott Abbott** (1838–1926). Depicts a two-dimensional world ("Flatland") inhabited by geometric figures, narrated by a Square who is visited by a three-dimensional Sphere.

## Core Geometric Argument

The Sphere's visit to Flatland is incomprehensible to the Square and other Flatlanders because:
- A 3D object intersecting a 2D plane appears only as a cross-sectional slice
- The Sphere enters their plane as a point, grows to a circle, then shrinks back to a point as it passes through
- The *inside* of the Sphere is invisible to Flatlanders — they see only the intersection boundary
- The Sphere can appear and disappear without entering or exiting through any door

## Application to UAP Hyperdimensional Hypothesis

In [[fyX8V1XXmQM]], [[jacques-vallee]] uses Flatland explicitly to explain the "bigger inside than outside" witness case:

> A woman in San Jose observed a disc the same diameter as her house. Inside, a staircase ascended to a circular room "like a movie house" — far larger than the object's exterior cross-section.

Vallée's explanation using the Flatland analogy:
- If the craft exists in 4D (or higher) space, what the 3D witness perceives is only the **cross-section** of the craft intersecting our 3D plane
- The full interior of the craft extends into the fourth dimension, invisible to 3D observers
- This is geometrically equivalent to Flatlanders seeing only the 2D slice of the Sphere while its 3D volume remains inaccessible

[[carl-sagan]] developed the same analogy publicly with the **tesseract** (4D hypercube) in *Cosmos* — a 4D object whose 3D shadow has all the properties Vallée describes.
