# The ESP Enigma

Book by [[diane-powell]] presenting a scientific examination of ESP phenomena. Its publication led to the revocation of Powell's medical license in Oregon, triggering a psychological evaluation she had to pass to have it reinstated. The book was ultimately deemed scientifically sound — she was cleared in both Oregon and California — but the ordeal caused lasting professional and personal trauma.

Cited in [[0qlppHc3-gg]] by [[ky-dickens]] as a canonical example of institutional retaliation against a credentialed researcher for asking anomalous questions. The revocation and reinstatement process mirrors [[john-mack]]'s Harvard trial.

## Key Claims (as described in source)

- Presents the scientific case for ESP as a real, investigable phenomenon.
- Reviewers who eventually cleared Powell reportedly found the science "extremely sound."
- The very act of publishing the book — not any fraudulent claim or patient harm — triggered the license revocation.
