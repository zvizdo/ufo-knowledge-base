# Fingerprints of the Gods

Graham Hancock's 1995 book and the work that established his reputation. It argues that a technically sophisticated civilization existed before the last Ice Age, was destroyed in a global catastrophe, and left its fingerprints in the form of megalithic monuments, astronomical alignments, and mythological flood traditions worldwide.

## Core Arguments

- The Giza pyramids and Sphinx encode astronomical alignments pointing to ~10,500 BC — the epoch of the "First Time" in Egyptian tradition
- Göbekli Tepe and other anomalous sites predate the standard "cradle of civilization" by millennia
- The approximately 200 global flood myths are not independent — they preserve memory of the same catastrophic meltwater events at the end of the last Ice Age
- Antarctica hypothesis (since revised): Hancock originally speculated the lost civilization may have been on Antarctica during a period of crustal displacement; this has since been de-emphasized in favor of submerged continental shelves

## Legacy

*Fingerprints* is widely cited as a "cult classic" that introduced millions to alternative archaeology. Even critics acknowledge it synthesized the evidence for a global catastrophe in an unusually accessible way.

Jesse Michaels in [[dfPfPB601hw]] describes it as Hancock's "90s cult classic" and credits it as establishing the intellectual foundation for his ongoing work.
