# NanoSIMS

Secondary ion mass spectrometer located in Stanford University's engineering department. Used by [[garry-nolan]] to perform isotope-ratio analysis on alleged UAP debris samples, including the [[1957-ubatuba-orb]] magnesium fragments and related materials.

## Capabilities

- Measures **elemental composition** by atomic mass
- Measures **isotope ratios** within each element at high resolution — the key capability for UAP materials analysis
- The isotopic signature of naturally occurring elements is fixed by planetary geology; deviations signal either isotope separation (industrial processing) or an off-world origin

## Use in UAP Materials Analysis

Nolan ran samples through NanoSIMS as part of his work analyzing materials forwarded by [[jacques-vallee]]. The instrument revealed:

1. **Ubatuba magnesium** — isotope ratios "not even close to natural"; would require centrifuge isotope separation to produce
2. **General principle** — natural abundance of isotopes is consistent across Earth; any significant deviation is either industrial artifact or anomalous origin

The NanoSIMS analysis provides a falsifiable, quantitative test that moves UAP materials analysis beyond visual/morphological description into physical chemistry.
