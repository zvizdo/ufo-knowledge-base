# Pioneer 10/11 Data

## Background

NASA's Pioneer 10 and 11 were the first human-made spacecraft to traverse the outer solar system beyond Jupiter and Saturn (1970s). Pioneer 10 crossed the asteroid belt in 1972, flew by Jupiter in 1973, and became the first spacecraft to reach escape velocity from the solar system. Pioneer 11 followed a similar path and flew by Saturn in 1979.

## The Binary Star Finding

In the mid-1980s, analysis of Pioneer telemetry data by [[thomas-van-flandern]] and [[robert-harrington]] (U.S. Naval Observatory) identified **gravitational anomalies** in the probes' trajectories inconsistent with the known masses of the solar system's planets. Their interpretation: a massive unseen object — a dead stellar companion ("black sun") — approximately 50 billion miles from the inner solar system was exerting gravitational influence on the probes.

This finding was published in a 1987 science encyclopedia. According to [[matt-lroy]], a diagram on approximately page 2,488 of that encyclopedia shows the orbital relationship between this dead star and our sun.

## Official Response

Astronomer **Miles Standish** subsequently published a reanalysis claiming Van Flandern and Harrington's work was a "mistake." Their binary star interpretation was not accepted by the mainstream astronomical community.

The **Pioneer Anomaly** (a separate but related anomalous acceleration in the probes) was ultimately resolved by mainstream physics in ~2012 through thermal radiation pressure modeling — heat from the probes' own nuclear power sources created a small but measurable thrust. This resolution does not address the gravitational binary-companion interpretation specifically.

## Alleged Suppression

[[matt-lroy]] claims:
- The 1987 encyclopedia data was subsequently removed from internet archives and databases
- The 1987 print encyclopedia is now the only surviving documented source for the binary star diagram
- [[thomas-van-flandern]] and [[robert-harrington]] both died of throat cancer (both non-smokers) shortly after pursuing this research
- [[nasa]] had institutional motivation to suppress findings that could cause public panic about a civilizational-reset binary companion

> ⚠ Note: The "Pioneer Anomaly" has a mainstream physics resolution (thermal recoil). Van Flandern's binary star interpretation was a separate and non-mainstream hypothesis. The mainstream community attributes the data suppression claim to his interpretation being wrong, not to institutional cover-up.
