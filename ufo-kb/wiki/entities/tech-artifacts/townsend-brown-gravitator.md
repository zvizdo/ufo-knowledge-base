# Townsend Brown Gravitator

[[thomas-townsend-brown]]'s primary experimental apparatus: an **asymmetric capacitor** consisting of a small positively charged electrode and a larger negatively charged electrode. When driven with megavolt-range electricity, the negative electrode appeared to "chase" the positive electrode — even when the positive electrode was oriented upward (against gravity). The effect seemed to produce genuine thrust, as if the electromagnetism was creating a synthetic gravitational field.

## How It Works (Proposed Mechanism)

The "infinite chasing effect" observed: the negative plate pursues the positive plate regardless of orientation relative to gravity. If the positive plate faces upward, the system appears to partially levitate. This was interpreted as a link between electromagnetism and gravity — precisely the unification Einstein spent the latter half of his career pursuing.

The modern physics interpretation ([[biefeld-brown-effect]]) notes that Brown's high-K dielectric materials (bismuth) may be proto-topological-insulator physics — the same domain that [[salvatore-pais]]'s Navy patents exploit.

## Witnessed Reactions

The experiments attracted some of the most credentialed witnesses in mid-20th-century science and aviation:

- **[[edward-teller]]** (inventor of the H-bomb): "I don't know how this works." His wife reportedly turned to Brown's daughter Linda: "You don't know how nice it is to hear him say that."
- **[[curtis-lemay]]** (Air Force Chief of Staff): Followed Brown down the stairs pursuing him after a demonstration. Brown's daughter Linda recalled LeMay "chasing my dad down the steps."
- **[[bill-lear]]** (aviation pioneer, adjacent Santa Monica offices): Close collaborator and witness.
- **[[victor-brandus]]** (Wright Field technical representative): "Believe it or not, I think I just saw a flying saucer and it frightened me."
- **[[agnew-bahnson]]** (industrialist): Funded both the experiments and the 1957 UNC Chapel Hill theoretical physics conference while simultaneously watching Brown work.

## B2 Stealth Bomber Connection

British aerospace journalist Bill Gunston claimed to be "very sure" that Brown's electrogravitics work was incorporated into the B2 stealth bomber, with the leading edge charged to millions of volts positive and the trailing edge to millions of volts negative — precisely the negative-to-positive thrust of Brown's experiments. A 1968 Northrop paper on "electro-aerodynamics" was cited as supporting evidence before it "mysteriously disappeared." See [[townsend-brown-b2-connection]].
