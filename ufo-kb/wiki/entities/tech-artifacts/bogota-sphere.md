# Bogotá Sphere (Bogga Sphere)

An anomalous metallic sphere found in Colombia by a prospector who does mining and underground metal detection work. A multi-minute video exists of the object flying in the air before the man picks it up. The prospector gave it to [[jaime-maussan]] in Mexico City, who calls it the "Bugga Sphere" (after the Colombian location).

## Physical Characteristics

- Aluminum alloy reportedly **three times the hardness** of standard aerospace aluminum
- Symbols are present around the top of the sphere
- Top surface resembles a semiconductor chip or circuit board in appearance
- A seam runs along the side

## Provenance Gap

There is a short gap in the video footage between when the sphere is airborne and when the man picks it up on the ground. This gap is acknowledged as a legitimate provenance question by [[jesse-michaels]] and [[ross-coulthart]]. Both note they are neither endorsing nor debunking the object.

## Symbol Recognition

[[ross-coulthart]] showed photographs of the symbols to:
- **[[mario-woods]]** — USAF Security Police; 1977 Ellsworth AFB CE3 abduction witness; saw symbols inside the craft → **Woods "freaked out"; recognized the symbols as matching those he saw inside the craft**
- **[[randy-anderson]]** — Crane Naval Surface Warfare Center witness; was shown off-world technology underground at NSWC Crane → **Anderson "freaked out"; recognized the symbols as matching those he saw**

Two independent witnesses with incompatible geographic and institutional backgrounds (USAF South Dakota 1977; US Navy Indiana undated) recognizing the same symbol set is the most significant evidentiary datum.

## AI Analysis

Coulthart ran a photograph through Grok (AI) and asked it to decipher the symbols. The AI returned the phrase **"the circuit."** Coulthart initially thought this suggested a hoax (the top does look like a semiconductor), but acknowledges AI symbology analysis is frequently wrong.

## Skeptical Interpretation

A visual match was identified between the sphere and a specific 9-liter fuel tank shape with cosmetic alterations. Ross and Jesse treat this as a legitimate alternative hypothesis without resolving it.
