# B2 Spirit Stealth Bomber

Advanced stealth bomber developed by Northrop (now Northrop Grumman) and operated by the US Air Force. First revealed to the public in 1988/1989, with first flight 1989 and operational deployment 1997. In the UFO/UAP research community, the B2 is a focal point for the [[townsend-brown-b2-connection]] claims-thesis — the argument that its performance incorporates [[thomas-townsend-brown]]'s electrogravitics work via the [[biefeld-brown-effect]].

## The Performance Anomaly

[[bill-gunston]] (Jane's Defence Weekly doyen) publicly stated that **the B2's performance data does not add up given the power of its engines** — implying an undisclosed lift/thrust mechanism. He then described the suspected mechanism: "leading edges of millions of volts positive, followed by trailing edges of millions of volts negative" — precisely the capacitor configuration of Brown's electrogravitic thrust research.

Aviation Weekly reported that the B2 "surfs its own electrostatic wave: the negative cloud chasing the positive wing." This description of the Byfield-Brown effect appeared in what should be a conventional technical publication.

## The Odlum–Northrop–Brown Chain

The most compelling institutional evidence for the connection:
1. [[floyd-odlum]] was both the **sole investor in Brown's [[guidance-technologies]]** and the **majority owner of Northrop** (before its merger with Grumman)
2. Brown shut Guidance Technologies in fall 1967; Northrop published a paper on electrogravitics just three months later
3. After Brown's death, Odlum paid $2,500/month to Brown's wife for the rest of her life
4. **Some believe B2 is named after Byfield Brown** — two B's

## The Bill Gunston Self-Censorship

After stating his conclusions, Gunston explicitly said he would not pursue the story further: "I don't want to reside in the Tower of London" — acknowledging he believed this subject was classified and reporting further would have serious consequences.

[[nick-cook]] signs off on Gunston's account, noting that Gunston was not a UFO advocate but a rigorous aerospace data journalist.
