# TD-100 (Transdermal 100)

The Transdermal 100 (TD-100) was a thought-transmission device developed by [[andrija-puharich]] at his company [[intellect]] (operating in Elmsford, New York) during the 1960s. The TD-100 transmitted radio frequencies tuned to be received directly by the brain's audio-processing centers, creating the perception of heard sounds or voices without any auditory signal entering through the ears.

**Mechanism**: Early versions of the TD-100 required a physical tooth implant — a small radio receiver embedded in a tooth — which picked up the transmitted signal and conducted it to the jaw and skull, where it was perceived by the auditory cortex as sound. Puharich subsequently worked to eliminate the implant requirement and beam the frequency directly to the skull's bone-conduction pathway.

**Pentagon demonstration**: Puharich demonstrated the TD-100 at the Pentagon: he fitted a general with the tooth device, positioned a broadcaster in the hallway, and the general successfully received the transmission — reportedly saying *"God damn it, I hear it, the bastard did it."* This demonstration established the device's functionality to military authorities.

**Filmed experiments**: Footage exists of TD-100 experiments at the Intellect facility showing a subject in a separate room receiving transmissions — early documented video evidence of successful thought-transmission technology.

**CIA context**: [[intellect]] is described as a probable CIA front organization. The TD-100's development in a classified-adjacent context suggests the device's capabilities were of direct intelligence interest for applications including covert communication and psychological manipulation.
