# XF-131 Super Sentinel

The XF-131 "Super Sentinel" is the designation used by illustrator and researcher [[mark-mccandish]] for a triangular Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV) design he documented based on engineering descriptions provided by frustrated Northrop and Lockheed engineers who had worked on the classified program.

**McCandish's documentation**: McCandish produced detailed technical illustrations of the XF-131 based on firsthand accounts from engineers at Northrop and Lockheed who were willing to describe the vehicle's basic design despite classification constraints. The illustrations became the most widely reproduced technical schematic of a claimed reverse-engineered triangular craft.

**X-Files connection**: The X-Files pilot episode, contracted to McCandish by creator Chris Carter, used the XF-131 design — suggesting the same hardware description entered both classified contractor channels and mainstream entertainment production from the same engineering source community. This parallel dissemination (classified program → entertainment → public awareness) is part of the soft-disclosure hypothesis.

**TR-3B lineage question**: [[edgar-fouche]]'s open question — "Does the TR3B represent the same lineage as the [[xf-131]] design McCandish documented?" — frames the XF-131 as potentially the same platform known through different intelligence channels. Fouché's TR-3B account (nuclear plasma propulsion, "Magnetic Field Disruptor") and McCandish's XF-131 (triangular ARV, gravitational field propulsion) describe what may be the same or closely related classified vehicle.
