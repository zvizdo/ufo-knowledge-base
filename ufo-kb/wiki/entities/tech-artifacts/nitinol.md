# Nitinol

**Type**: Shape-memory alloy (nickel-titanium)  
**Developed**: Naval Ordnance Laboratory, 1959 (William Buehler; name = **Ni**ckel **Ti**tanium **N**aval **O**rdnance **L**aboratory)

## Relevance to UAP Discourse

Nitinol appears in UAP discourse as a candidate **[[memory-metal]]** — material that returns to a pre-set shape after deformation — which multiple witnesses describe as a property of recovered UAP debris:

- **[[jesse-marcel]]** described Roswell debris as impossibly thin material that "crinkled up and then uncrinkled itself" — consistent with shape-memory behavior.
- **[[1947-roswell]] debris**: Multiple witnesses describe foil-like material with memory properties. Nitinol was developed 12 years later; reverse-engineering hypothesis suggests the 1947 material was NHI-derived and informed its development.
- **[[william-buehler]]** (NRL scientist who developed Nitinol) gave ambiguous answers when pressed on whether anomalous materials inspired the research.
- **[[jacques-vallee]]** has cited Nitinol and related shape-memory alloys as candidates for analysis in his materials collection network with [[garry-nolan]].

## The Reverse-Engineering Hypothesis

The timing (recovered 1947; analogous material developed 1959) fits the pattern of **[[reverse-engineered-craft]]** claims: that NHI technology was recovered and fed into classified R&D programs with public output emerging years later. This is circumstantial; no primary-source documentation confirms a Roswell → Nitinol pipeline.

## The Mandeloy Extension

[[wlsQIbpEKSM]] adds a downstream data point: the **[[mandeloy-alloy]]**, a nickel-based superalloy co-invented by [[monica-resza]] and [[dallas-hardwick]] at the Rockwell Science Center in the 1990s under [[afrl]] contracts. Mandeloy sits in a different metallurgical neighborhood from Nitinol — only ~1–4% titanium vs. Nitinol's ~45% — but the lineage clustering is notable:

- 1947: Roswell crash; Jesse Marcel describes burn-resistant foil
- 1949: Wright-Patterson's Battelle Memorial Institute studies nickel-titanium alloys
- 1959: Nitinol developed at Naval Ordnance Laboratory
- 1990s: Mandeloy developed under AFRL contracts at the same Wright-Patterson institutional ecosystem
- Both Mandeloy's co-inventor (Resza, missing 2025) and the AFRL commander who oversaw its development (McCasland, missing 2026) have disappeared

The source declines to call this a causal chain, but acknowledges the "optics are bizarre."
