# F-117 Nighthawk

The Lockheed F-117 Nighthawk was the world's first operational stealth aircraft, a ground-attack platform designed to evade radar through its angular, faceted design. Developed by Lockheed's [[skunk-works]] division, it became operational in 1983 and served the USAF until 2008.

**Extreme secrecy**: The F-117 program was among the most tightly compartmentalized classified programs in US aviation history. The aircraft was designed, tested, and deployed for years before any public acknowledgment. It was based primarily at [[tonopah-test-range]] in Nevada during 1982–1992, flying only at night. Publicly, the Air Force denied it existed.

**UAP reports**: The F-117's nighttime flights generated thousands of civilian UFO reports across Nevada and surrounding states during the 1980s. Unusual lights at night, silent angular shapes, and aircraft behavior inconsistent with known civilian aircraft all fed into the UFO report stream. This is the canonical example of a classified aircraft program generating genuine UAP reports that were in fact classified US technology.

**The "misidentification" argument**: The F-117 case is frequently invoked to argue that a significant fraction of historical UAP reports represent classified US (or Soviet) aircraft rather than non-human phenomena. The counter-argument is that F-117 can explain some reports but not the majority — particularly reports predating 1983, reports of instantaneous acceleration, and reports with physically anomalous characteristics.

**[[skunk-works]] lineage**: The F-117 was the successor to the U-2 and SR-71 in Lockheed Skunk Works' classified aircraft development tradition. Its development demonstrated that radical aerodynamic innovation was possible within the black program architecture.

**Retirement and UAP disclosure**: The F-117 was officially retired in 2008, after which its program history became significantly more public. Its lifecycle illustrates the pattern: classified development, public denial despite widespread sightings, eventual acknowledgment decades later.
