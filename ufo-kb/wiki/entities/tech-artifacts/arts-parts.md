# Art's Parts

Anomalous material allegedly from the 1947 Roswell crash, received by radio host [[art-bell]] and subsequently shared with researchers. The material consists of **bismuth-magnesium nanolayers** — an unusual configuration with properties relevant to both UAP propulsion hypotheses and cutting-edge material science.

## Description

- **Composition**: Nanometer-scale alternating layers of bismuth and magnesium
- **Relevant properties**:
  - Bismuth is a **high-k dielectric** — it stores and discharges electromagnetic energy efficiently
  - In the [[biefeld-brown-effect]], bismuth as the insulator between positive/negative electrodes produces greater electrogravitic thrust
  - One material (bismuth or magnesium) is **paramagnetic**, the other **diamagnetic** — extreme magnetic property contrast
  - This diamagnetic/paramagnetic pairing is central to modern **spintronics** research in material science
- **Structure**: Nanolayer architecture — the kind of precision layering that would require advanced fabrication to produce, though modern nanofabrication can now produce similar structures

## Significance

[[jesse-michaels]] notes: bismuth appears constantly in anti-gravity literature (Townsend Brown, [[biefeld-brown-effect]]). The fact that alleged UFO crash material uses the same material that produces the most effective electrogravitic thrust in the one confirmed anti-gravity modality we know of is "really significant."

[[kevin-knuth]] adds: the diamagnetic-paramagnetic pairing is precisely what material scientists work with in spintronics breakthroughs today. The material is either extraordinarily coincidentally aligned with our frontier research directions, or suggests technology that was more advanced in these directions.

[[garry-nolan]] at Stanford has examined some pieces using mass spectrometry and mentioned to Kevin Knuth that some pieces might function as microscale waveguides for **terahertz power** (Nolan's observation). Knuth is uncertain if this is possible.

## Current Analysis

- **[[matthew-shostakus]]** at University at Albany: non-destructive neutron-activation isotope analysis; goal is to detect isotope ratios inconsistent with natural Earth/asteroid compositions
- **[[garry-nolan]]** at Stanford: mass spectrometry analysis
- Government interest in Shostakus's work confirmed ("there's interest in Matthew's work. He's had contact with government people").

## Provenance Questions

The material was received by [[art-bell]] — exactly when and from whom is unclear. It is alleged to originate from the [[1947-roswell]] crash. The chain of custody between 1947 and Bell is unknown.

In [[dzTZbSNsKV8]], the closing exchange distinguishes the on-camera bismuth sample (Roswell 1947 per Puthoff's chain) from a separate **larger** sample in Nolan's possession that Michaels attributes to the [[1945-san-antonio-trinity-crash]]. The Trinity-1945 attribution is volunteered by Michaels — Nolan does not contradict it — and frames the closing question about whether NHI interest in humanity tracks our atom-splitting milestones.

## Provenance Clarification — Army Officer / Grandfather's Diary (dzTZbSNsKV8)

In [[dzTZbSNsKV8]], Nolan could not recall the bismuth sample's provenance and called [[hal-puthoff]] on camera. Puthoff's account:

> *"Story was that it was sent anonymously by someone claiming to be an army officer. Long story short, this army officer was going through his grandfather's archives when he found this rare sample. And then written in the diary was that it was a piece from Roswell."*

This is hearsay (Puthoff relaying an anonymous sender's claim), but it is the primary provenance chain on record. The bismuth-magnesium layered structure was already in the possession of Nolan's network; this episode adds the first named source for the backstory.

## Terahertz Waveguide Properties (dzTZbSNsKV8)

[[hal-puthoff]] claims the bismuth-magnesium layered material has practical terahertz microwave guide properties:

- Reduces required microwave guide size to **~1/30th of the terahertz wavelength**
- Enables fitting **30 waveguides in the volume of a single conventional waveguide** at terahertz frequencies
- Practical implication: much denser information packing in terahertz-range communication systems

Puthoff: *"Terahertz is the next thing for communication — if we can get terahertz waves working efficiently, there's a whole slew of other electronic and radio communication things that could be done that can't be done now."*

This is independent of the Biefeld-Brown electrogravitic bismuth application — the same material may serve multiple exotic functions simultaneously.
