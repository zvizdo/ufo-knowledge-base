# Tag-Along Orbs

## Description

"Tag-alongs" is the working military slang used by personnel at the highest clearance levels to describe UAPs that closely followed nuclear missile tests, high-altitude detonations, and other advanced weapons tests. The term was in common use by 1962 among officials with access to the full picture of nuclear test programs.

[[harold-malmgren]] relays the term with striking casualness: "When I saw this object, which I call a tag-along, that's what you would call them, tag-alongs. What else?" The matter-of-fact tone implies the phenomenon had been normalized as an operational constant — something military planners accounted for without public acknowledgment.

## Behavioral Profile

Based on the accounts in [[09KP8XVf5nY]]:
- Persistent proximity to nuclear and missile tests
- Apparent monitoring function: closely observing detonations
- Anomalous flight characteristics (capable of matching test vehicle envelopes)
- Not always visually obvious; detected by instruments as well as visually
- The [[1962-bluegill-uap-shootdown]] resulted when a tag-along monitored too closely and was struck by the X-ray pulse from the detonation

## Patrick Jackson's Classification System (V00WcEiKRAY)

[[patrick-jackson]], a Texas-based sphere researcher, proposes a **type one / type two** classification for metallic UAP spheres. His interpretation: the spheres constitute a **global alien defense system** protecting Earth. Coulthart treats this as speculative but notes photographs of UAP events often show large objects accompanied by smaller metallic sphere dots.

[[ross-coulthart]]'s 2021 Australian television story on the metallic spheres received over **43 million views** across two iterations (one taken down due to music copyright). The response included accounts from people globally, confirming the sphere phenomenon is widespread.

## Significance

The normalization of the term "tag-along" among cleared officials represents significant evidence for the [[ufo-nuclear-nexus]] thesis: the phenomenon was not a sporadic anomaly but a recognized operational pattern that warranted a name and a cognitive category among those running the nuclear test program.
