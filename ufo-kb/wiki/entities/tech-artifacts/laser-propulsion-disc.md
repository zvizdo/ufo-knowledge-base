# Laser Propulsion Disc

## Description

A disc-shaped aircraft — specifically designed in the flying-saucer form factor — propelled from below by a ground-based pulse laser. The laser ablates or pressure-heats the disc's bottom surface, creating thrust. The disc is lifted and stabilized by the pulsed laser energy without any onboard fuel or moving parts.

[[eric-haseltine]] learned about this device from direct personal interaction with an unnamed scientist at Sandia National Lab who demonstrated it for the US government at White Sands.

## Significance: Shape is Not Coincidental

The flying-saucer disc shape is **optimal for laser propulsion geometry** — the curved underside maximizes laser coupling and provides stability. This means:

- The iconic "flying saucer" silhouette is not culturally arbitrary — it may be aerodynamically and propulsion-optimal
- A classified laser-propelled craft would look exactly like what UFO witnesses describe
- The government has actually demonstrated such a craft at White Sands

Haseltine notes that with modern adaptive optics, the range of a ground-based laser propulsion system could be significantly extended beyond the original short-distance demonstration range.

## Propulsion Advocacy

The unnamed Sandia scientist advocates for laser propulsion as a technology for:
- Civil aviation (dramatically cheaper per ton-kilometer than chemical propulsion)
- Low-Earth orbit insertion (replacing chemical first stages)

The scientist is described as "a little private" — hence unnamed.
