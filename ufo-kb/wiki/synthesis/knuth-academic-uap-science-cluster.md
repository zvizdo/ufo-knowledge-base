## The JAL 1628 Physics Analysis

The 1986 Japan Air Lines flight 1628 case ([[1986-jal-1628]]) yielded the most quantitatively rich radar data in the public UAP record. [[john-callahan]] (FAA Division Chief) preserved the radar tapes from destruction after being ordered to hand them over; he kept a personal copy.

[[daniel-koumbi]] analyzed the trajectory data and calculated:
- **~250,000 mph** maximum velocity
- **9,000–11,000 G accelerations** — far beyond any biological tolerance or known propulsion
- Radar track corroborated independently by ground and aircraft instrumentation

Knuth considers JAL 1628 his highest-conviction case precisely because it has preserved, quantifiable, multi-source radar data — not witness testimony.

## The Nimitz Energy Analysis

For the 2004 Nimitz Tic-Tac incident ([[2004-nimitz-tic-tac]]):
- Knuth calculated: to achieve observed F-18 intercept performance differentials in a craft of Tic-Tac size → **~1,100 GW** required
- That is more than the total electricity-generating capacity of the entire United States
- The Tic-Tac was approximately the size of an F-18; the energy density requirement rules out any conventional propulsion

This is the single most important quantitative argument against the "advanced drone" hypothesis.

## The SCU Nuclear Pre-Construction Study
