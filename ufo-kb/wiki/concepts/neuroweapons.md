# Neuroweapons

Neuroweapons are technologies designed to manipulate, degrade, or exploit human neural function as a military or intelligence objective. The category spans multiple modalities and delivery mechanisms, from early Cold War microwave beaming programs to contemporary directed energy weapons research.

**Historical roots**: The [[soviet-microwave-weapons]] program — which beamed microwave energy at the US Moscow embassy beginning in the 1950s — represents the earliest documented state-level deployment. The program produced documented health effects in embassy staff including neurological symptoms, cancers, and cognitive disruption. US intelligence monitored it, and the [[1971-salt-talks-unknowns]] transcripts show US-Soviet diplomatic acknowledgment of RF weapon health effects decades before public disclosure.

**John Norsine's work**: [[john-norsine]], a Navy pilot turned neuroengineer at Lockheed Martin, worked on what colleagues described as "manipulation of the mind" — neuroweaponry at the intersection of biometrics, neural pattern recognition, and directed influence. His concept of "brain prints" (unique brain-wave signatures analogous to fingerprints) was intended both for identification and potentially for targeting.

**Havana Syndrome connection**: The [[2016-havana-syndrome]] cases — US diplomatic and intelligence personnel experiencing sudden headaches, hearing damage, cognitive disruption, and memory effects — are widely attributed to directed energy weapons in the neuroweapon category, likely RF or microwave-band devices. The pattern is consistent with the [[1976-moscow-embassy-microwave-incident]] and subsequent Soviet/Russian directed energy weapon development.

**Voice of God**: The [[voice-of-god-technology]] is a subset — using microwave auditory effect (Frey effect) to transmit audio directly into a target's skull, bypassing ears. This was documented as early as the 1960s and has been operationalized in both PSYOP and potential neuroweapon applications.

**Dual-use architecture**: Neuroweapon technologies occupy the same dual-use spectrum as pharmaceutical agents and signal processing systems — the research that enables therapeutic neural implants or hearing aids operates on identical physical principles as systems designed to disrupt or monitor neural function at a distance.
