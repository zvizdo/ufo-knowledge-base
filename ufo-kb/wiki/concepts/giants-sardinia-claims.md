# Sardinian Giant Skeletal Remains (Undated)

Sardinia, Italy, is one of the locations cited in researcher accounts of anomalously large skeletal remains from antiquity — the Sardinian giant phenomenon. The claims arise in connection with Sardinia's Nuragic civilization (c. 1800–238 BCE), which built thousands of nuraghe (stone tower structures) across the island and has left the most densely nuraghic archaeological record in the Mediterranean.

**Archaeological context**: Sardinia has produced skeletal remains and oral traditions associated with unusually large individuals. Classical sources — including Diodorus Siculus — describe Sardinian inhabitants of unusual physical stature. Modern archaeological investigations have occasionally recovered bones described as significantly above contemporary norms, though interpretations vary widely between researchers accepting these as statistically anomalous ancient humans and mainstream archaeologists attributing discrepancies to measurement error or post-depositional distortion.

**Nuragic civilization**: The Nuragic people built approximately 7,000 surviving nuraghe towers using megalithic construction techniques, alongside "giants' tombs" (*tombe dei giganti*) — collective burial structures with distinctive facade designs. The term "giants' tombs" is a cultural name for the structure type, not an assertion of giant occupants, but this nomenclature feeds into researcher interpretations connecting Sardinia to the nephilim/giant phenomenon.

**KB connection**: The Sardinian giant claims are cited in the [[2002-kandahar-giant]] context as a parallel evidence strand — archaeological rather than military encounter — for the broader [[nephilim-giants-thesis]] that argues for the historical physical reality of giant humanoids. The KB treats this as contested claims-thesis evidence.
