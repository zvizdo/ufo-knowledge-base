# Hydrothermal Vents

Submarine geothermal openings that deposit metal-rich mineral veins in surrounding rock; in the chemical-manufacturing reading of Giza, ancient Tethys-Sea hydrothermal vents created the iron-ore and rare-earth deposits the pyramid complex was allegedly engineered to extract.

Hydrothermal vents form where superheated water saturated with dissolved minerals escapes through seafloor cracks. As it cools, dissolved metals — gold, silver, copper, iron, platinum-group elements, and rare earths — precipitate into veins in the surrounding rock. [[jeffrey-drum]]'s pyramid-as-chemical-factory thesis identifies Tethys-period deposits of this type as the ore body beneath the Giza plateau: iron-bearing veins accessible from the deep karst cave system, amenable to [[acid-mine-drainage|acid-leach extraction]] using [[hydrogen-sulfide|H2S-derived sulfuric acid]] from the same karst system. The presence of [[fulgurite|fulgurites]] within sealed sections of these veins is interpreted as evidence that high-voltage current was historically distributed through the iron network to drive electrochemical metal recovery.
