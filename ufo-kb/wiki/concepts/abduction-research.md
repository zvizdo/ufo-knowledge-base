# Abduction Research

The systematic study of individuals reporting alien abduction experiences. Distinct from simple witness testimony collection, abduction research involves clinical and quasi-clinical methods to investigate reports of physical transport, medical examination by non-human entities, missing time, and (often) ongoing contact.

**Primary methodologies**:
- **Hypnotic regression** (→ [[hypnotic-regression]]): Using hypnosis to recover memories of experiences the subject cannot consciously recall. Controversial because hypnosis can produce false memories; proponents argue it sometimes surfaces physiologically corroborated detail.
- **Structured interviews**: Systematic debriefing without hypnosis; preferred by researchers skeptical of regression artifacts.
- **Physiological examination**: Documenting physical evidence — unexplained scars, implants, radiation-consistent skin reactions, unexplained pregnancies — that may corroborate reported contact.

**Key researchers** in the KB:
- [[john-mack]] — Harvard psychiatrist; took abduction reports seriously as genuine experiences; killed 2004
- [[budd-hopkins]] — artist turned researcher; documented thousands of cases; developed the "missing time" concept
- [[david-jacobs]] — historian turned researcher; focused on NHI genetic program hypothesis
- [[leo-sprinkle]] — early researcher; regressed Betty and Barney Hill
- [[john-edgar-hoover]] — peripheral; abduction cases intersect with MK-Ultra survivor research

**Missing time** (→ [[abduction-missing-time]]): The core anomaly that initiates most formal abduction research — witnesses finding that more time has passed than their conscious experience accounts for, often correlated with vivid encounter fragments or physiological after-effects.

**Institutional status**: Abduction research sits in an uncomfortable position between clinical psychology (where the experiences are taken seriously as trauma requiring treatment), parapsychology (where the question of literal reality is bracketed), and mainstream UAP investigation (where physical evidence and corroboration are prioritized over subjective testimony).
