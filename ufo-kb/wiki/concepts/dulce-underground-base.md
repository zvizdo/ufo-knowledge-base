# Dulce Underground Base

The claim — one of the most persistent in UFO mythology — that a joint human-alien underground facility operates beneath Archeleta Mesa near [[dulce-new-mexico]] on the Jicarilla Apache reservation in northwestern New Mexico.

## Origin and Epistemic Status

The Dulce underground base claim has a traceable and largely discreditable origin chain, documented in detail by [[greg-bishop]] in *[[project-beta]]*:

### 1. Myrna Hansen's Regression (early 1980s)
The account originates with [[myrna-hansen]], an abductee whose case [[paul-benowitz]] investigated. Under hypnotic regression sessions conducted by psychologist [[leo-sprinkle]], Hansen described being taken to an underground facility. Benowitz, who was simultaneously being subjected to AFOSI disinformation, interpreted this as confirming an alien base near Dulce. (Greg Bishop, firsthand research, via [[WBTqsbwJyqU]])

The regression was conducted in a contaminated environment: Benowitz was already paranoid and was being fed false information. Whether the regression reflects genuine experience, confabulation, leading questioning, or contamination by Benowitz's existing beliefs cannot be determined.

### 2. AFOSI Active Amplification
[[rick-doty]] and AFOSI colleagues, running the disinformation campaign against Benowitz, actively redirected his attention toward Archeleta Mesa and the Dulce area — away from [[kirtland-afb]] where his original genuine observations had occurred. This included:
- Flying Benowitz by helicopter to the area to show him "what was happening there"
- Staging physical props (jeeps, water tanks) at the site for him to photograph
- Feeding him information about alien activity in the area through the disinformation pipeline

### 3. John Lear's Admitted Fabrication
[[john-lear]] — who had CIA/Air America background and communicated with Benowitz after the initial AFOSI operation — propagated the "Dulce firefight" story: an alleged battle between humans and aliens at the underground base. Bishop reports that Lear privately admitted to an interlocutor before an early 1990s congressional UFO hearing: "I made that up." This admission was not public at the time. (Greg Bishop, [[WBTqsbwJyqU]])

## What May Have Been Real

- **Myrna Hansen's subjective experience**: Something happened to Hansen that she interpreted as abduction; whether the specific underground base details are accurate, confabulated, or shaped by Benowitz's existing theories is unknown.
- **A real crash at Archeleta Mesa**: [[gabe-valdez]] found physical evidence of a crash-site cleanup in the area — burned brush, removed debris, a US Government pen. This was likely a classified aircraft (stealth drone or similar). AFOSI may have used this real event to further focus Benowitz's attention.
- **Cattle mutilations**: Valdez investigated real cattle mutilations in the Dulce area. The connection between mutilations and an underground base is asserted by many but unverified.

## Assessment

Greg Bishop does not flatly dismiss all Dulce-related claims but notes that the mythology was substantially manufactured or amplified by a documented intelligence operation. The claim functions as a test case for the principle that UFOs and their associated lore can be weaponized by intelligence operators.

> "A lot of the tropes kind of started back then." — Greg Bishop, on how the Benowitz-era disinformation seeded lasting UFO mythology ([[WBTqsbwJyqU]])

Independent corroborations of the underground base (people claiming similar experiences) may reflect genuine accounts or secondary contamination from the same disinformation environment. Bishop cannot distinguish between these.
