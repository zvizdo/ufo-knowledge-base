# Biophotons

Ultraweak photon emissions (UPE) from living cells and organisms — light produced at extremely low intensities by biological systems through metabolic processes, reactive oxygen species (ROS) activity, and possibly other mechanisms. Biophoton emission is documented in mainstream biophysics; the disputed questions concern its functional significance.

**Mainstream status**: Biophoton emission from organisms is well-established: single-photon counting devices can detect photons emitted by cells, tissues, and whole organisms. The emission is correlated with metabolic activity and stress. Germinated seeds, injured tissue, and metabolically active cells all show elevated biophoton emission.

**Speculative functional claims**: Beyond the documented emission, researchers including Fritz-Albert Popp (Germany) and others have claimed that biophoton emission is coherent (laser-like) and functionally involved in cellular communication and biological organization — that cells use light signals to coordinate. This claim is more contested; coherence of biophoton emission is debated.

**Consciousness speculation**: Some researchers have proposed that biophoton emission from neural tissue may be a correlate or substrate of consciousness — that the brain emits coherent photons that participate in information processing beyond electrochemical signaling. This connects to the broader KB theme of electromagnetic substrates for consciousness.

**DNA electromagnetic context**: In the KB, biophotons appear adjacent to the [[dna-phantom-effect]] and [[dna-intelligence-encoding]] cluster — the thesis that DNA has electromagnetic information properties beyond its chemical sequence. Biophoton emission from DNA is cited as evidence that biological molecules communicate electromagnetically.
