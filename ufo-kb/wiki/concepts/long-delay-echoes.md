# Long Delay Echoes (LDEs)

## Core Phenomenon

Beginning in 1927, Norwegian shortwave radio operators began noticing anomalous echoes of their own transmissions. Normal shortwave radio naturally travels around the globe and returns as an echo approximately 1/7th of a second after transmission — the time for radio waves (traveling at the speed of light) to circumnavigate the Earth. These normal echoes are well understood.

**Anomalous echoes**, however, returned anywhere from 3 to 30 seconds after the original transmission. Norwegian physicist Carl Störmer immediately began investigating. No definitive explanation has been established for the anomalous subset.

## Scale and Distribution

LDEs have been documented consistently since 1927 across different radio operators, frequencies, and locations. This is not an artifact of a single transmitter or location.

## Proposed Explanations

- **Plasma in the ionosphere**: The most common mainstream explanation. Accounts for many LDEs, but not all — particularly those with the longest delays and the spatial correlation with Lagrange points.
- **Earth-Moon Lagrange point reflection**: Some LDE delay times match the travel time to the Earth-Moon L4 and L5 Lagrange points. One study found a statistically significant increase in LDE occurrence when the **Earth-Moon L5 Lagrange point** was above the horizon — suggesting a reflecting source at that location.

## Connection to Bracewell Probe Hypothesis

The [[bracewell-probe-hypothesis]] predicts that an interstellar probe parked in a Lagrange point would make its presence known by intercepting local radio transmissions and bouncing them back — exactly what LDEs describe. The temporal overlap is striking: LDEs began being documented in 1927, decades before the dawn of the space age, before any human reflective satellites existed.

If even a subset of LDEs represent Bracewell probe responses:
- The phenomenon predates all human space hardware
- The reflecting source is in the inner solar system, near Earth
- The behavior (reflecting Earth's own transmissions back) is exactly Bracewell's predicted first-contact protocol

## Connection to Palomar Transients

[[beatriz-villarroel]]'s pre-Sputnik transients ([[pre-sputnik-uap-transients]]) show mirror-like reflective objects in geosynchronous-range orbit. If these are the same population as Lagrange-point Bracewell probes, they would be expected to also reflect radio signals — producing LDE-type echoes. The two phenomena may be two different detection signatures of the same underlying objects.
