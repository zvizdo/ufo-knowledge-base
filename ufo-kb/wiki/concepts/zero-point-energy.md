# Zero-Point Energy

The minimum energy of a quantum field at zero temperature — the energy of the vacuum state. In ordinary quantum field theory, the harmonic-oscillator lower bound (½ℏω per mode) summed over all field modes diverges, with the cutoff (and hence the "real" magnitude) at the heart of the cosmological-constant problem.

In UAP propulsion discourse, ZPE is invoked at two levels:
1. **Energy source.** Speculative proposals that anomalous craft tap a small fraction of the vacuum energy density as their power source — sidestepping the conservation-of-energy bottleneck on conventional propulsion.
2. **Substrate of metric engineering.** In [[hal-puthoff]]'s **polarizable-vacuum (PV) model**, ZPE is the substrate whose local parameters can in principle be modified to produce gravitational and inertial effects — the conceptual bridge from quantum-field-theory ground state to engineerable spacetime.

The **Casimir effect** is the canonical empirical demonstration that vacuum-state energy is real and measurable; its scaling is far below what propulsive applications would require, so the ZPE-as-source claim is structurally optimistic relative to known physics.
