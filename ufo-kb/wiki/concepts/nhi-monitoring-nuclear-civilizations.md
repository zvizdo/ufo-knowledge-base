# NHI Monitoring Nuclear Civilizations

The NHI nuclear monitoring hypothesis proposes that non-human intelligences specifically monitor, respond to, and in some cases directly interfere with human nuclear weapons and nuclear energy programs. This is one of the most empirically supported of the NHI behavioral hypotheses, because the pattern — UAP appearing at nuclear facilities, particularly ICBM launch control centers and nuclear weapons storage sites — has been reported across multiple countries, time periods, and military contexts.

**Evidence base**:
- **Malmstrom AFB (1967)**: On March 16, 1967, Captain Robert Salas was duty officer at Malmstrom Air Force Base, Montana, when a glowing red oval-shaped object was observed by security forces over the front gate. Within 90 seconds, all 10 of the Minuteman ICBMs assigned to his flight went offline. Similar events occurred at a second launch control facility the same week. Salas testified publicly and to Congress.
- **Minot AFB, Loring AFB**: Similar incidents at other USAF nuclear installations during the 1970s have been documented.
- **UK Bentwaters**: UAP activity at RAF Bentwaters/Woodbridge (1980) occurred at a base that stored US nuclear weapons.
- **Soviet nuclear facilities**: Soviet/Russian military reports include similar patterns at their ICBM and nuclear weapons facilities, suggesting a cross-ideological consistency.

**Robert Hastings research**: Author Robert Hastings conducted the most extensive cataloging of nuclear-UAP correlations in *UFOs and Nukes* (2008), compiling testimonies from over 100 US military veterans who witnessed UAP activity at nuclear weapons sites.

**Garry Nolan's framing**: [[garry-nolan]] has referenced the nuclear monitoring pattern as one of the most empirically consistent behaviors in the UAP dataset — suggesting it provides meaningful information about NHI objectives or interests, as a civilization that monitors nuclear activity specifically would be demonstrating concern about human nuclear weapons development.

**Implications**: If genuine, NHI nuclear monitoring suggests: (a) NHI have been present and observant since at least 1945; (b) they are specifically concerned with nuclear weapons (vs. nuclear energy, which has received much less documented attention); (c) their interest may reflect concern about the same existential risk that the [[great-filter]] framework addresses.
