# FFRDC / UAP Architecture

The hypothesis that the network of Federally Funded Research and Development Centers (FFRDCs) — MITRE Corporation, RAND Corporation, Center for Naval Analyses, Institute for Defense Analyses, Aerospace Corporation, MIT Lincoln Laboratory — constitutes the organizational spine through which the unacknowledged UAP research and reverse-engineering programs are funded, staffed, and protected from oversight.

Developed in detail by [[uap-gerb]] (documented in the [[d2tkcfmjjks]] and [[ntifs8luduo]] transcripts), the thesis rests on several structural features of FFRDCs:
- They receive direct Congressional appropriations without the competitive procurement required for contractors
- They operate under indefinite government contracts; their work products are government property and can be classified without triggering the reporting requirements that apply to defense contractors
- They can hire cleared personnel across agency lines, making them natural nodes for cross-agency programs
- Their funding is difficult to track through standard Congressional appropriations analysis

The specific claim: $34–40 billion per year in DoD funding moves through FFRDC/UARC structures that are not visible in standard budget analysis. The Nevada Test Range / Edwards AFB complex is the physical node; FFRDCs provide the institutional cover.

This architecture would explain how a program could survive across administrations and be inaccessible to AARO: if the program is housed in an FFRDC under a classified contract with no statutory reporting requirement, even a president cannot easily identify or access it through normal executive-branch channels.
