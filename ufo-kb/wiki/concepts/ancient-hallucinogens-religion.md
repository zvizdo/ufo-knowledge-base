# Ancient Hallucinogens and Religion

## Core Thesis

Across multiple ancient civilizations, pharmacologically active substances were central to — possibly constitutive of — what people described as encounters with gods, divine inspiration, and civilizational advancement:

| Culture | Substance | Context |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Ancient Egypt | Lotus flower, opium, possibly DMT compounds | Temple rituals, mystery cults witnessed by Herodotus |
| Greek Eleusinian Mysteries | Kykeon (ergot fungus on grain; possibly containing LSA) | Initiation rites for Plato, Socrates, Alcibiades |
| Amazon (ayahuasca belt) | DMT + MAOI vine combination | Consistent "goddess" entity reports; shamanic tradition |
| Mesoamerica / worldwide | Psilocybin mushrooms | Stoned Ape hypothesis; pre-agricultural use |
| Mystery schools broadly | Various | Herodotus participated in Egyptian mystery rites and never spoke of them again |

## Key Arguments

### The Improbable Discovery
Combining DMT with an MAOI inhibitor (the pharmacological basis of ayahuasca) is a chemical discovery requiring precise botanical knowledge across two unrelated plant species. [[luke-caverns]] and Jesse Michaels note this seems unlikely to arise from pure trial and error — either divine guidance was received *while taking the substance*, or an earlier tradition of knowledge encoded the recipe.

### The Agricultural Ergot Connection
Ergot (*Claviceps purpurea*) grows on wheat, barley, and rye — precisely the crops of the agricultural revolution. [[brian-muraresku]] (*The Immortality Key*) argues that ergot-laced kykeon (the mystery drink at Eleusis) goes back to Egypt and possibly the origins of agriculture. Luke and Jesse raise the possibility: **was the agricultural revolution itself a function of visionary experiences**, not merely increased leisure time from food surpluses?

The "more time → civilization" narrative doesn't feel right to Luke: "When have you ever felt like, 'Oh, I just had this abundance of time. I'm going to go erect a megalithic temple.'"

### Stoned Ape Hypothesis
[[terence-mckenna]]'s thesis (mentioned briefly): psilocybin mushrooms growing in megafaunal dung on the African savanna catalyzed a doubling of human cranial size (~2M years ago → 300K years ago) through neurogenesis and epigenetic change.

### Cross-Cultural Entity Consistency
Ayahuasca practitioners worldwide meet a consistent "goddess" entity that knows their health issues and can address them therapeutically. [[luke-caverns]] offers two explanations: (1) all human brains are wired identically to produce this archetype, or (2) the entity is real. This directly connects to the DMT-NHI debate in [[dmt-nhi-hypothesis]].

### Ancient Egypt Specifically
Herodotus witnessed Egyptian mystery cults and participated — then never described them. Lotus flower remains an "incredible mystery" as a psychoactive. Opium use is documented. The Amarna period's Akhenaten may have had divine encounters (with the Aten) that were chemically mediated — or NHI-contact-mediated (see [[akhenaten-pharaoh]]).

### Bicameral Mind Connection
[[julian-jaynes]]'s theory that pre-1000 BC humans heard divine voices as literal auditory hallucinations connects: hallucinogens may have been the *ritual technology* that reproduced or amplified an increasingly rare bicameral capacity after ~1000 BC.

### Eleusis — Secrecy Under Penalty of Death

From [[I9VyyV4-3Js]]: the Eleusinian Mysteries were explicitly secret — **revealing what happened was punishable by death**. Muraresku calls it "the fight club of the ancient world." What little survived in the ancient record: people went to [[eleusis]] as human beings and walked away thinking they were immortal. The penalty for disclosure means we cannot directly confirm the psychedelic hypothesis from testimony — hence the importance of the organic chemistry data from chalices.

### The Dionysus Question

[[carl-ruck]] (Boston University) asked in *[[the-road-to-eleusis]]* (1978): **Was Dionysus the god of wine or the god of psychedelics?** The standard classicist view is that Dionysus is the wine god. Ruck (with Wasson and Hofmann) argued that "wine" in the ancient world may have been a general term for any fermented or psychoactively enhanced drink — and that Dionysian worship was centered on the psychedelic experience, not intoxication per se.

### Christianity as Mystery Religion

Muraresku's argument (endorsed by Martin Luther King Jr. writing in 1950 on the influence of mystery religions on Christianity): early Greek-speaking Christian communities may have adapted the Eleusinian mystery tradition into the Eucharist. Two textual anchor points:
1. Jesus uses "musterion" (mystery, secret confided only to initiates) when asked why he speaks in parables
2. John 6 ("has eternal life" — present tense) parallels the **immediate** immortality-grant of Eleusinian initiation, not a deferred afterlife

## Hancock on Psychedelics and Early Christianity (dfPfPB601hw)

[[graham-hancock]] in [[dfPfPB601hw]] extends the Christianity-as-psychedelic-religion argument:

- Early Christianity was "very much a psychedelic religion" — Christ was seen more as a teacher than as the Son of God
- **Council of Nicaea (325 AD)** was a political consolidation that cut down Christianity into an accepted canon on political grounds; the very first groups the Roman Catholic Church persecuted were other Christian sects that had used psychedelics
- The kykeon at [[eleusis]]: initiates were given this drink, then descended into "a deep dark hallway." The result — universally documented — was the **loss of fear of death**. "Many of those great philosophers said that what they lost was their fear of death — they had had fear of death before, and after this experience they no longer feared death."
- [[plato-philosopher]], [[socrates-philosopher]], and Aristotle all went to [[eleusis]] and were initiated.
- [[brian-muraresku]]'s *[[the-immortality-key]]* makes the case for early Christianity as psychedelic religion; Hancock wrote the foreword.

## SSRI and Mainstream Depression Treatment (Personal Account)

Hancock speaks from personal experience with severe depression and SSRI antidepressants (seroxat/Prozac). His assessment: SSRIs are "absolutely useless" for depression. He gradully withdrew over six months, slicing pills with a razor blade. He cites the scientific literature: placebos are as effective as SSRIs; the serotonin-deficiency/depression link has been debunked after 50 years of promotion.

This connects to the broader argument that psychedelics (psilocybin in particular) are more effective for depression than pharmaceuticals — and that the suppression of psychedelic research has left depressed patients with inferior options.
