# Trauma-Based Mind Control

Trauma-based mind control (TBMC) refers to the alleged deliberate use of extreme trauma to fragment a subject's personality into dissociated compartments — alters — that can be separately programmed, activated by triggers, and isolated from one another. The mechanism exploits [[dissociative-identity-disorder]] (DID): severe early childhood trauma is a known risk factor for DID, and TBMC claims that this can be intentionally induced.

**Project Monarch claims**: The most specific TBMC claim is "Project Monarch" — an alleged [[mk-ultra]] subproject in which DID was deliberately induced in child subjects for intelligence purposes: creating couriers who could carry information in one alter without the host personality having conscious access to it, and creating assassins whose violence could be compartmentalized. Project Monarch is not documented in the MK-Ultra files released to Congress, and its existence is disputed; it is primarily based on claims by alleged survivors and researchers like Cathy O'Brien (*Trance Formation of America*).

**Relationship to MK-Ultra**: The documented [[mk-ultra]] program did conduct experiments on trauma, hypnosis, and dissociation. The transition to "deliberate DID induction" as a systematic program is where documentation ends and contested claims begin. The KB treats Project Monarch as a claims-thesis: some survivors and researchers assert it; it is not confirmed by declassified documents.

**Abduction research adjacency**: In the KB, TBMC appears adjacent to [[abduction-research]] — some researchers have proposed that apparent abduction memories recovered via hypnosis may be trauma-induced false memories, possibly from TBMC programs, rather than genuine NHI contact. This creates a bidirectional epistemological problem.
