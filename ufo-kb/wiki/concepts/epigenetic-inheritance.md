# Epigenetic Inheritance

Epigenetic inheritance is the transmission of phenotypic traits across generations through non-DNA-sequence mechanisms — specifically through heritable epigenetic marks such as DNA methylation patterns and histone modifications. Unlike classical Mendelian inheritance, epigenetic inheritance involves changes to gene expression patterns rather than to the genetic sequence itself. The primary mechanisms are: (1) DNA methylation — methyl groups attached to cytosine bases that suppress gene expression; (2) histone modification — chemical tags on the protein spools around which DNA is wound, altering how tightly DNA is packaged and how accessible it is for transcription.

**Scientific status**: Transgenerational epigenetic effects are recognized in mainstream biology, though the scope and persistence of the effect is debated. The classic example: children and grandchildren of Holocaust survivors show altered cortisol stress-response profiles consistent with their grandparents' trauma exposure. Plant studies have documented epigenetic transmission across multiple generations under stress. The question of how many generations the effect persists is a live research area.

**Lamarck connection**: Epigenetic inheritance is sometimes called "neo-Lamarckian" because it provides a mechanism for acquired characteristics to be heritable — which classical Darwinism denies. The resemblance to Lamarck's theory of inheritance of acquired characteristics is noted but the mechanisms are distinct (no direct environmental modification of DNA sequence). This positions epigenetic inheritance as a partial rehabilitation of a theory long dismissed as pre-scientific.

**Sheldrake's critique**: [[rupert-sheldrake]] argues in the KB context (via [[morphic-resonance]]) that many phenomena described as epigenetic inheritance are actually morphic resonance — information transmission through a non-material morphic field rather than through chemical epigenetic marks. In Sheldrake's view, mainstream biology is using "epigenetic inheritance" as a mechanistic label for what is actually field-based information transfer, and the chemical mechanisms (methylation, histone modification) are the local readout of a non-local field pattern rather than the information storage system itself.

**Abduction and generational claims**: Within the KB's abduction research thread, some abductees report that their parents, children, and grandchildren have similar experiences — a generational clustering that is difficult to explain through simple social transmission. Epigenetic inheritance offers a speculative biological mechanism: if NHI contact events produce heritable physiological changes (altered stress response, modified neurological thresholds), those changes might predispose subsequent generations to similar contact states or experiencer profiles.

**DOE human genome connection**: The [[doe-human-genome]] project, in some KB framings, is linked to research into non-standard biological inheritance. Epigenetic inheritance represents a layer of biological information that the Human Genome Project did not capture — the genome sequence is necessary but insufficient to explain phenotypic transmission.
