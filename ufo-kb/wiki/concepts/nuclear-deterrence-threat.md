# Nuclear Deterrence Threat

**Type**: analytical framework / suppression-motive concept

The doctrine of nuclear deterrence — that possessing preponderant nuclear weapons prevents adversaries from launching attacks — is the stated "backbone of US national security" per the annual DOD Nuclear Posture Review. [[robert-salas]] identifies this doctrine as the primary *motive* for suppressing UFO-nuclear evidence.

## The Logic

If non-human craft can disable nuclear missiles at will (as documented in [[ufos-disable-nuclear-weapons]]), then:
1. Nuclear deterrence is not absolute — a third party can neutralize it
2. The DOD would be obligated to explain what it is doing about this threat
3. The DOD has already admitted it cannot do anything about it
4. This admission would undermine the entire rationale for the $trillions spent on nuclear weapons since 1950
5. It would also undermine the arms-race logic that produced 32,000 nuclear weapons at the Cold War's peak (20,000 remain across 9 nuclear states as of 2024)

Therefore, suppressing the evidence protects the institutional, financial, and geopolitical foundations of nuclear deterrence.

## Salas's Extended Argument

Salas argues that nuclear deterrence is itself not sustainable: as long as deterrence logic holds, every nation wants nuclear weapons, producing proliferation (now 9 nuclear states, with Iran potentially becoming the 10th). He describes the current multipolar nuclear landscape (US/Russia/China/Pakistan/India/Israel/North Korea + Iran) as more dangerous than Cold War bilateral deterrence, with multiple crisis vectors active simultaneously.

He speculates that NHI intervention — interference with nuclear systems — may be intended to push humanity toward abolition of nuclear weapons, consistent with a civilizational pattern they themselves may have passed through.
