# RIAS: Golden Age of General Relativity

The **Research Institute for Advanced Study (RIAS)** was a division of the **Martin Corporation** (aerospace, Maryland) that operated in the late 1950s–1960s. [[eric-weinstein]] describes it as "a Manhattan Project for Gravity" — a concentrated, well-funded effort to apply the era's leading mathematical physicists to problems in general relativity and gravity.

## Personnel

Notable figures at or associated with RIAS:

- [[lewis-whitten]] — physicist; father of [[edward-witten]] (string theory's architect)
- [[sheldon-glashow]] — later Nobel laureate (electroweak unification)
- Rudolf Coleman
- [[solomon-lefschetz]] — topologist
- Deser and Arnowitt — of the ADM formalism in GR
- [[freeman-dyson]]

## The Shutdown Problem

The RIAS program went cold in the **early 1970s** — precisely when quantum gravity emerged as the dominant paradigm in theoretical physics. When an APS (American Physical Society) historian looked into this transition in 1984, there was no satisfactory explanation for why this concentrated gravity effort simply stopped. The timing coincides with:

1. The appearance of "quantum gravity" as an institutional mandate (~1972), which had not been a central framing before
2. The cold shutdown of RIAS-era aerospace gravity research
3. No Nobels in fundamental gravity or GR since the early 1970s

Weinstein's hypothesis: the shutdown was not coincidental — RIAS-era research had reached a point where continued open work was considered too dangerous, and the quantum gravity mandate served as a blocking mechanism to redirect academic talent away from productive gravity directions.
