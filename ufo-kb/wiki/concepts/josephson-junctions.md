# Josephson Junctions

## Concept

A Josephson junction is a quantum-electronic device consisting of two superconductors separated by a thin insulating barrier through which Cooper pairs tunnel coherently. Predicted by Brian Josephson (Nobel Prize 1973), they are the foundational building blocks of superconducting quantum computing, SQUID magnetometers, and a range of other cryogenic quantum-sensitive instruments. They operate near absolute zero (typically below ~4 K).

## UAP-Adjacent Use

[[hal-puthoff|Hal Puthoff]] in [[Gf_tKn9TaP8]] identifies Josephson junctions as the receiver technology he is using in his current [[subrosa-quantum-communication|quantum-communication]] research program with [[john-paul-dejoria|John Paul DeJoria]]. Puthoff's communication scheme suppresses ordinary E and B field components while preserving an underlying quantum signal; ordinary EM receivers therefore can't detect it. Josephson-junction-based receivers, operating at ~3.7 K, can.

Per Puthoff, the 1990s blocker on deploying his quantum-communication architecture was the immaturity of cryogenic detection. Quantum-computing investment has matured the hardware to the point that the architecture can be revived.

In [[Rfmy5oW_r9c]], Jesse Michaels also names Josephson junctions in the context of the proposed physical implementation of Puthoff's [[extended-electrodynamics]] / scalar-field-communication framework.
