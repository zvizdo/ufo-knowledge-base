# Radar-Induced UAP Crashes

## The Hypothesis

Proposed by [[john-blitch]], drawing on his DARPA engineering background: the cluster of reported UAP crash-retrievals in the late 1930s through late 1940s is not coincidental with the rapid development of military radar. Early radar engineers were experimenting with **maximum transmitter power** — not to shoot anything down, but simply to extend detection range. The hypothesis:

1. NHI craft operating in Earth's atmosphere were **functionally invisible** to human sensors and human awareness at the time
2. The high-power radar pulses created an **electromagnetic interference environment** that disrupted the craft's propulsion or navigation
3. Craft went down within several hundred kilometers of the radar installation — consistent with the observed crash geography
4. Military authorities quickly recognized the connection and moved **radar development to isolated desert locations** (White Sands being the obvious choice) to ensure future crashes did not occur in populated or foreign territory

Blitch notes this is "Occam's Razor applied" — the simplest explanation that accounts for the correlation without requiring deliberate hostility.

## Supporting Cases

### 1941 Cape Girardeau, Missouri

[[john-blitch]] personally drove to Cape Girardeau during a cross-country move to the AFRL in Dayton, Ohio, and walked the ground. He finds the account credible — specifically the detail of a priest being called to administer last rites to surviving diminutive beings. Cape Girardeau is approximately 5 hours by road from [[wright-patterson]], which fits the model of a craft coming down within collection range of early Army Air Corps radar installations.

See [[1941-cape-girardeau-crash]].

### 1933 Magenta Crash (Lombardy, Italy)

The [[1933-magenta-crash]] also fits the radar-crash model: Blitch notes that Gugliemo Marconi was a prominent electrophysicist based in northern Italy at approximately this time, and suggests a research project to determine whether any Marconi-era high-power radio/radar experiments were occurring within a 5-hour drive of the Lombardy crash site would be a productive avenue of investigation.

### Roswell (1947)

Blitch implies — without making it explicit — that if radar-plus-weather was the mechanism at Roswell (a storm being one factor), this is consistent with the radar-crash hypothesis. White Sands was already active by 1947, suggesting the Army Air Corps had moved experiments to the desert precisely to isolate crash effects from populated areas.

## Implications

If the hypothesis is correct:

1. The initial crash wave was **accidental** on both sides — no deliberate NHI provocation, no deliberate human shoot-down
2. Human authorities recognized the cause quickly, which explains both the rapid institutional response (Project Blue Book, MJ-12 alleged structure) and the subsequent shift of radar development to remote areas
3. The current era of alleged deliberate shoot-downs (per [[jake-barber]] and others) represents a later, more intentional phase — not the original pattern
4. Marconi's work and the European crash wave (Magenta 1933, Italian reports, German foo fighter encounters) may be connected to the same pre-war radar experimentation
