# Extended Electrodynamics

The physics framework that restores the **full original Maxwell electromagnetic equations**, including scalar and vector potential terms that were mathematically simplified away by Oliver Heaviside and Heinrich Lorenz in the late 19th century. Also called "scalar physics," "higher-symmetry electromagnetics," or U(1) → SU(2) EM extension. The core claim: the simplification was mathematically convenient but physically wrong — it discarded a real domain of electromagnetic phenomena with profound technological implications.

## The Lorenz Re-Gauging Problem

Maxwell's original equations (20–21 equations in quaternion form) describe the full electromagnetic field, including:
- The conventional **transverse Hertzian (E, B) waves** used in all standard electronics
- **Scalar and vector potentials** without accompanying conventional fields — the "pure gauge" or longitudinal terms

When Heaviside reformulated Maxwell's equations into compact four-vector form, Lorenz simultaneously imposed what is now called the **Lorenz gauge condition**: setting the sum of the four-vector potentials to zero. This was mathematically elegant and made engineering tractable for 19th-century practitioners — but Lorenz explicitly acknowledged that the dropped terms don't physically disappear. His stated justification: they "have no physical significance."

[[dave-rossi]]'s analogy: "Say you have a pizza with 12 slices. Lorenz told the engineers there's only 1 slice. He didn't deny the other 11 existed — he just said they have no physical significance."

The **Aharonov-Bohm effect** (1959) provides experimental proof that the potentials *are* physically significant — electrons are affected by vector potentials even in regions where E and B fields are zero. This demonstrates the dropped terms have measurable consequences.

## What Extended Electrodynamics Unlocks

When scalar and vector potentials are restored to the equations, three new categories of wave solution emerge:

1. **Longitudinal scalar waves** — pressure-wave-like propagation along the field direction; exist in the quantum vacuum but require curved spacetime to access
2. **Helicoidal waves** — longitudinal waves with helical geometry structurally matching double-helix DNA; proposed by [[constantine-meyl]] as the natural frequency for DNA communication
3. **Caduceus waves** — two opposing helical waves; arise when spacetime is locally curved via electromagnetic induction; associated with neg-entropy effects (cold currents, reversed entropy)

## Curved Spacetime as the Gateway

The key engineering insight: flat-spacetime EM (U1 model) cannot produce these effects. To access them, spacetime must be **locally curved**. Mechanisms to do this without astronomical mass:

- **Spinning capacitor** (mechanical): Rotation induces local curvature in its vicinity
- **Superconductor lattice polarization** (solid state): Breaking the symmetry of the superconductor's lattice holes puts electrons on non-linear paths — equivalent to curving the local spacetime from an EM standpoint
- **High voltage** ([[thomas-townsend-brown]], [[nikola-tesla]]): Very high voltages can polarize the quantum vacuum locally — the "Ebner effect" (plants growing anomalously in 500kV+ fields)
- **Plasma** ([[salvatore-pais]]): Plasma as a conduit that slows quantum vacuum fluctuations, making them accessible as a physical medium
