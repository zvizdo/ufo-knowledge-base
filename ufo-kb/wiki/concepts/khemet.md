# Khemet

Ancient Egypt's own name for itself, conventionally translated as 'the Black Land' (referring to fertile Nile silt); Drum reframes it as 'land of chemistry' — root of the word 'alchemy' — describing the pyramid complex's industrial function rather than the soil color.

Khemet (km.t in Egyptian transliteration) is conventionally read as 'the Black Land,' referring to the fertile dark Nile silt as opposed to the red desert sand (deshret). Drum's alternative reading: kh-mt as 'land of chemistry' — root of khēmeia → alchemy — describing the industrial chemical function of the pyramid complex. The diluted sulfuric acid byproduct of metal extraction reacted with limestone to produce gypsum, a soil amendment that created the fertile agricultural land documented as Khemet — connecting the chemical-manufacturing thesis to the agricultural infrastructure of the Saharan Humid Period population.
