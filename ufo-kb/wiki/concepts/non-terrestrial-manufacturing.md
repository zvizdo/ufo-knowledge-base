# Non-Terrestrial Manufacturing

The claim that some recovered materials associated with UAP cannot be attributed to any known human manufacturing process, nation-state, or historical technology. First surfaced in classified crash-retrieval contexts; brought into public testimony by [[david-grusch]], who stated that recovered materials showed "non-terrestrial" provenance — meaning the manufacturing signatures, isotope ratios, crystalline structures, or engineering tolerances had no match in the human industrial record.

The concept is distinct from "extraterrestrial" in that it is agnostic about origin: materials could theoretically be produced by an ultraterrestrial intelligence, a time-displaced human civilization, or an off-world source. The claim is what justifies the extreme classification levels applied to crash-retrieval programs: if materials were merely foreign adversary technology, standard classification would suffice; non-terrestrial provenance triggers a categorically different secrecy logic.

T. Townsend Brown's work on [[electrogravitics]] and the broader [[anti-gravity-manhattan-project]] context suggests that even in the 1950s, contractors were attempting to understand and reproduce propulsion and materials effects they believed were beyond the human baseline.
