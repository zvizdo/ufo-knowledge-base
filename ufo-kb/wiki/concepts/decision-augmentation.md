# Decision Augmentation

A hypothesis developed by parapsychologist [[ed-may]] to explain positive results in micro-PK (micro psychokinesis) experiments — trials in which subjects apparently influence the output of random number generators (RNGs) with their minds.

## The Phenomenon Being Explained

Decades of parapsychology experiments show statistically significant correlations between subject intentions and RNG output — suggesting consciousness may directly affect physical outcomes (psychokinesis). This is the "psi" effect most resistant to conventional explanation and most challenging to physics.

## May's Alternative Explanation

Instead of subjects' consciousness affecting RNG output, May proposes: **the experimenter's unconscious precognition guides them to start a trial at a moment when the RNG will, by chance, generate output in the desired direction**.

No mind-over-matter occurs. No physics violation. The experimenter is not affecting the RNG — they are *retrocausally navigating* to start-times where the RNG outcome already is what they want.

**Decision augmentation** = the researcher's precognition augments their start-timing decision.

## Why This Is Hard to Rule Out

Standard double-blind protocols cannot eliminate decision augmentation because:
- The researcher still decides *when* to begin recording
- This decision is made by a human who may be unconsciously precognitive
- No blinding procedure prevents a human from having precognitive intuitions about start-times

Ruling out decision augmentation would require fully automated trial initiation with no human decision point — a methodological standard rarely applied in micro-PK research.

## Implications for Science at Large

[[eric-wargo]] considers decision augmentation theory "potentially hugely important across science" — contingent on accepting precognition as real (a prerequisite most mainstream scientists reject):

- Any experiment with a human decision about when to begin recording could be subject to decision augmentation
- This includes biophysical experiments, clinical trials, psychology studies — anywhere a researcher starts a trial at a moment of their choosing
- It could be a previously unsuspected hidden factor in the **replication crisis** — systematic positive results in original studies that cannot be replicated when the start-time is automated or randomized

Wargo: "If this is an effect, what kind of effect is this happening in any kind of biophysical experiment or health sciences or psychology? It could be hugely important — but you have to accept precognition first."

## Relationship to Wargo's Model

Decision augmentation is a *specific application* of [[quantum-brain-precognition]] — the general model that the brain's quantum subsystem can access future knowledge states. May restricts this to the narrow case of RNG experiment timing; Wargo's model predicts it should appear wherever a precognitive person makes decisions with real future stakes.

## Status

May explicitly does not believe in macro-PK (levitation, affecting large physical objects at a distance) and is skeptical of many PK claims. He takes precognition seriously as an empirically demonstrated effect. Decision augmentation is his attempt to give a precognition-only explanation that avoids positing psychokinesis as a distinct force.

The [[stargate-program]] context: May ran the research arm for its final decade, so this represents an insider's attempt to reformulate what the program demonstrated.
