# Electrogravitics

The field of research investigating whether high-voltage electrical fields can produce thrust or gravitational modification. Named and developed primarily by [[thomas-townsend-brown]], who discovered in the 1920s that a sufficiently charged capacitor with asymmetrical electrodes moves toward its positive pole — an effect he called the Biefeld–Brown effect after his mentor Paul Biefeld.

Brown spent decades investigating whether this effect was purely electrostatic (ion wind) or involved a genuine coupling between electromagnetic fields and gravity. His Project Winterhaven proposal to the U.S. Navy in the early 1950s explicitly described a disc-shaped electrogravitic craft capable of Mach 3+ performance. The Navy declined the proposal publicly but Brown's subsequent career placed him inside classified contractor networks that included Martin Company, the predecessor organization to Lockheed Martin.

The KB contains substantial documentation of the [[anti-gravity-manhattan-project]] thesis: that by the mid-1950s, the major U.S. aerospace contractors had parallel electrogravitics research programs. Aviation journalist Bill Gunston ([[b2-stealth-bomber]]) asserted that the B-2 Spirit's anomalous performance figures only make sense if its leading and trailing edges are charged to millions of volts — matching Brown's capacitor-thrust mechanism exactly.

Within the UAP frame, electrogravitics is the strongest proposed bridge between documented classified research and observed UAP flight characteristics: silent propulsion, instant acceleration without visible exhaust, plasma sheaths, and apparent gravity-immune maneuvering all match electrogravitic predictions.
