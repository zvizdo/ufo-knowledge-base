# Taurid Meteor Stream

## Definition

The Taurid meteor stream is a broad, ecliptic-plane stream of cometary debris that Earth crosses **twice per year**:
- **Summer Torids**: Late June / early July (stream coming from perihelion direction, near the Sun; meteors hard to observe)
- **Fall/Halloween Torids**: Late October / early November (stream incoming toward perihelion; radiant visible in Taurus/Pleiades; Southern and Northern Torids)

The stream's **radiant point** is centered on the constellation Taurus, specifically near the Pleiades (the shoulder of the bull in classical depictions).

## Progenitor and Formation

[[bill-napier]], working with Victor Clube and William Asher (going back to ~1980), proposed that the Taurid stream originated from a giant progenitor comet (~50–60 miles diameter) that was captured into a sub-Jovian orbit 25,000–30,000 years ago. This comet — related to but distinct from comet Encke — underwent a hierarchical fragmentation cascade:

1. Giant progenitor → secondary nuclei
2. Secondary nuclei → tertiary nuclei (comet Encke is among the survivors)
3. Ultimately generating the diffuse Taurid debris stream

In the early stages (~10,000–20,000 years ago), the stream was far denser than today, with larger and more numerous fragments. The stream is not uniform — it contains **clumps** (like traffic bunching behind a red light) that make some crossings dramatically more hazardous than others.

## Orbital Mechanics (Detailed per Carlson)

The stream's elliptical orbit takes it from a perihelion near the Sun to aphelion near (or just inside) Jupiter's orbit. Objects in the stream are in a dynamic game between Jupiter's gravity (which can throw them inward or outward) and the Sun's gravity. Cometary nuclei in the Kuiper disc are "quasi-stable" — minor perturbations from outer-planet conjunctions (especially Uranus-Neptune) can destabilize them inward, toward Jupiter's influence, and then toward the Sun.

As the stream objects age:
- Smaller fragments sublimate and disperse (reducing density over millennia)
- Surviving nuclei (like Encke) remain as periodic comets
- Residual debris constitutes today's diffuse Taurid shower

## Earth-Crossing Hazard

The June crossing is more dangerous for radar-tracked impacts (objects coming from near the Sun direction are harder to spot). The October crossing is visually observable — the "Halloween meteors."

[[randall-carlson]] frames the Taurid stream as an analogy for a highway intersection: Earth isn't in constant danger, but when it crosses the intersection (stream), and especially when a high-density clump is present, the probability of a catastrophic impact rises by orders of magnitude. The civilizational response should be:
1. Deflection technology (cf. NASA DART mission as a proof of concept)
2. Places of refuge — potentially underground or off-world (the Moon)

## Historical Evidence

- **1908 Tunguska Event** ([[1908-tunguska]]): Occurred June 30 — the peak of the summer Taurid crossing. Radiant point consistent with Taurid origin. Almost certainly a Taurid member.
- **Younger Dryas Boundary (~12,900 years ago)**: The catastrophe Carlson and Napier argue was caused by a dense Taurid clump impact/airburst over the Laurentide Ice Sheet. The stream was orders of magnitude denser 12,900 years ago than today.
- **Halloween Mythology**: Carlson gave a full presentation connecting the ancient origins of the Halloween celebration to the Taurid meteor shower, which peaks in late October when it was most visually dramatic and terrifying to ancient observers.

## Mythology Connection

For ancient civilizations living 5,000–10,000 years ago when the stream was denser:
- Regular spectacular displays (fireballs, airbursts) would have been visible
- Carlson proposes that many "sky god" narratives, fire-deity myths, and celestial-catastrophe traditions encode real memories of Taurid-stream bombardments
- The Evenki peoples' response to the 1908 Tunguska event (attributing it to their fire god Agni) illustrates exactly how real events become religious mythology
