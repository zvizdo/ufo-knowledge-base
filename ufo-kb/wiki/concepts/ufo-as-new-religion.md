# UFO as New Religion

[[diana-pasulka]]'s central predictive thesis, most fully articulated in [[tS_64sTN5AU]]: the current UAP moment constitutes not merely a UFO belief system (UFO cults already exist) but the emergence of a **new structural form of religion** — a religiosity that spreads through media screens, defines itself through the convergence of science and spirituality, and is constitutively neither purely empirical nor purely supernatural.

## Pasulka's Original Prediction

Pasulka states she made this prediction *before it happened*, and watched it validate:

> *"This is a new form of religion. It's not just a religion because we do have UFO religions right. But this is a new form of religion and this is going to happen."*

The key distinctions from prior UFO cults (Heaven's Gate, Raëlism, etc.):
- This new religiosity does not require a specific sect or founder
- It spreads through **secular media infrastructure** (podcasts, YouTube, documentaries, social networks)
- It is characterized by the **convergence of science and spirituality** — nuts-and-bolts hardware + angels-and-demons spirituality — which Pasulka identifies as uniquely charismatic in an age of disenchantment
- Participants include credentialed scientists, academics, military officials, and ordinary experiencers — not a marginal sub-culture

## Science and Religion as Twin Attractors

Pasulka and [[jesse-michaels]] jointly articulate in [[tS_64sTN5AU]] why UFO religiosity has charismatic pull in the current moment:

- In a **secularized age**, even traditional believers often don't genuinely believe in the metaphysical reality of their religion (they are "secular religious")
- The UFO phenomenon is **uniquely charismatic** because it marries two things normally in tension: hard science (craft, materials, physics) and spirituality (angels, NHI, consciousness)
- Science itself, as Jesse formulates, has become "the pervasive mythology of our time" with its own founding myth (Big Bang), teleology (evolution = progress), and initiation rituals (lab coats, peer review) — and UAP destabilizes *that* religion from below
- The convergence makes the UAP topic the most **epistemologically interesting** object in the culture: it is the only domain where a credentialed Stanford microbiologist and a religious studies professor are doing their most serious work on the same phenomenon

## Media as the Infrastructure of Dissemination

Pasulka's earlier work (before the field work of *American Cosmic*) was focused on how **entertainment media subtly shapes UFO phenomenology**:
- Betty and Barney Hill's alien descriptions (1961) overlap with CBS *Outer Limits* aliens (same horizontal-slit eyes, show premiered weeks before the abduction report)
- The [[simulation-theory]] cluster in the culture is partially driven by the monolith in *2001: A Space Odyssey* — which director Stanley Kubrick said was the film's "most superficial interpretation" (he experimented with it literally being a screen)
- The French philosopher Aimé Michel is cited: "the eyes can only see what the mind is prepared to comprehend" — the cultural meme library shapes how contact is perceived and recalled

## The "Both/And" Problem

The most difficult argument in this cluster, per Pasulka: the UFO phenomenon is **both** a manufactured scop (media-shaped belief) **and** a real phenomenon. The evidence for active intelligence-community manipulation of the UAP narrative (Walter Bedell Smith's 1953 memo: "we will play up the phenomena for psychological warfare purposes") exists alongside the evidence for real underlying encounters. The manipulation uses an existing real phenomenon for its own purposes. Disentangling these is the core epistemological challenge.

## Relationship to [[ufo-religion-convergence]]

*UFO as new religion* differs from the [[ufo-religion-convergence]] concept:
- **Convergence**: the historical thesis that contact experiences across millennia (angels, fae, aliens) are phenomenologically continuous — the same phenomenon in different cultural idioms
- **New religion**: the sociological/structural thesis that the current UAP moment is generating a *novel* religious form, not merely repeating old patterns

Both concepts originate primarily from Pasulka's work. The convergence thesis is more historical; the new-religion thesis is more sociological and forward-looking.
