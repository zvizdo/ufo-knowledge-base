# Diatomaceous Earth

A naturally-occurring sedimentary rock formed from the fossilized cell walls of diatoms (single-celled algae with silica skeletons). Functions as a powerful desiccant — it pulls water out of organic tissue rapidly enough to halt decomposition before microbial action can degrade soft tissue.

**Why it matters in the Nazca case**: per the [[HxQN2tkQHs8|American Alchemy Peru episode]], the cave [[leandro-gravedigger]] discovered in 2015 contained 200+ tridactyl body parts all coated in diatomaceous earth. This is why the [[nazca-tridactyl-mummies]] are technically *not* mummies in the conventional sense — many retain their organs, tendons, cartilage, and other soft tissue fully intact. The diatomaceous earth desiccated the bodies fast enough to prevent the loss of internal anatomy that conventional mummification (Egyptian-style preparation, natural dry-cave preservation, etc.) usually entails. Carbon dates span 700 to 18,800 years ago, demonstrating diatomaceous earth's preservation effectiveness across deep timescales.

**Implication for the debunk debate**: the preservation quality is part of why credentialed forensic experts ([[john-mcdow]], [[jim-caruso]], [[david-ruiz]], [[jose-zalce]]) treat the M-types as anatomically self-consistent organisms rather than reconstructions. With organs and capillary structures intact, a fraud would need to fabricate full internal anatomy, not just external morphology — a much harder lift.

**Other context**: Leandro initially called the chamber "full of [music] datoms" (the verbal artifact of "diatoms" in the audio transcript) — distinct from a normal cave-burial environment.
