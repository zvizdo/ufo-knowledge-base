# Disclosure Narratives

**Type**: meta-framework / concept  
**Applied to**: sources, people, and events that frame *why* information is coming out now, or *why* it's been suppressed

These are the competing models in the UFO/UAP discourse for explaining the trajectory of information release. They are not mutually exclusive — multiple can be operating simultaneously, and specific actors may be consciously or unconsciously serving one model while believing they serve another.

## Core Narrative Models

### 1. Slow / Managed Disclosure
The government (or the custodian group) is deliberately releasing information in calibrated amounts to avoid societal shock. Each UAP hearing, AARO report, and declassified video is a controlled step in a pre-planned reveal sequence.

- Proponents: [[lue-elizondo]], [[david-grusch]] (implicitly — both operate as if official channels are the right path)
- Criticism: The "managed" reveal has no clear end state and could be indefinitely extended.

### 2. Controlled Opposition
Key witnesses and disclosure advocates are intelligence assets, consciously or unconsciously channeling the narrative toward a prepared false-disclosure endpoint. The "real" secret is protected while a decoy story is revealed.

- Referenced by: [[jesse-michaels]] (skeptically), [[harold-malmgren]] (implies CIA compartmentalization makes even insiders into unwitting assets)
- Evidence pattern: many prominent figures have intelligence or military backgrounds; some advocated for a "break" from official channels, then returned to testifying before Congress.

### 3. Psyop / False-Flag Preparation
The UAP narrative is being deliberately seeded or amplified to prepare a future false-flag event (fake alien invasion, manufactured crisis) that would justify a global security state or permanent emergency powers.

- Referenced by: fringe critics; not asserted by main KB sources, but mentioned as the "catastrophic disclosure fear" logic runs in this direction
- Cross-link: [[catastrophic-disclosure-fear]] — the fear that disclosure itself, whether real or staged, has civilizational consequences.

### 4. Genuine / Spontaneous Reveal
The information is coming out despite — not because of — the custodian group's wishes. Congressional pressure, whistleblower protections (NDAA 2024), and journalists like [[ralph-blumenthal]] and [[leslie-kean]] have forced the hand of institutions that preferred silence.

- Proponents: [[marco-rubio]] (legislated mandatory reporting), [[john-ratcliffe]] (endorsed the reality of UAP on record)
- Evidence: the 2017 NYT article broke outside normal institutional PR channels.

### 5. Competitive Disclosure Pressure
A geopolitical race: the U.S. must disclose before China or Russia does, lest the strategic advantage of alien-derived technology be forfeited, or lest an adversary use the moment to delegitimize U.S. power.

- Referenced by: [[harold-malmgren]] directly — argues the U.S. stole a march on everyone else post-WWII; the risk now is that China's VASCO program ([[vasco]]) and related astronomical-anomaly research could put them ahead on disclosure optics.
- Cross-link: [[catastrophic-disclosure-fear]], [[ufo-secrecy-cabal]]

### 6. Breakaway Civilization Model
A secret technical program, using off-books funding and alien-derived technology, has advanced so far beyond the official state that the "cover-up" is less about hiding aliens and more about hiding the existence of a shadow economy and power structure.

- Referenced implicitly by: [[danny-sheehan]], [[lue-elizondo]]

### 7. Insider-Outsider Pressure Loop (articulated by Graves)

[[ryan-graves]] describes a dynamic neither purely managed nor purely spontaneous: insiders with knowledge coordinate partial releases, which motivate outside civilian and commercial researchers, who generate external pressure, which enables further insider releases. The two groups are in a **mutual amplification loop**. Graves explicitly frames this as the primary mechanism driving the current acceleration of disclosure and does not think a single "big reveal" will be the endpoint.

Key implication: neither the government nor the public "owns" the disclosure trajectory — it's emergent from the interaction between the two. Graves adds that he believes NHI may themselves be a factor not taken into account in any purely human-centered model of how this plays out.

## Open Questions

- Are these narratives mutually exclusive in practice, or can a disclosure environment be simultaneously genuine and managed?
- Who benefits most from *each* narrative being believed?
- Is the Grusch testimony part of Narrative 4 (genuine) or Narrative 2 (controlled opposition)? The evidence is currently indeterminate.
