# Contactee

The contactee tradition refers primarily to individuals in the 1950s and 1960s who claimed ongoing, voluntary contact with benevolent extraterrestrial beings — commonly called "Space Brothers" — who delivered spiritual and philosophical messages about human evolution, nuclear weapons, and cosmic brotherhood. This distinguishes contactees from abductees (involuntary, often traumatic, medically oriented) and from whistleblowers (government/military insiders).

**George Adamski**: The canonical contactee figure. Adamski claimed contacts beginning in 1952 with a Venusian named Orthon in the California desert, followed by contacts with beings from multiple planets. He photographed alleged craft and wrote widely read books (*Flying Saucers Have Landed*, 1953). His claims were treated skeptically by early UFO researchers including J. Allen Hynek, but attracted a large following.

**Common contactee pattern**: The Space Brothers typically conveyed: (1) concern about nuclear weapons testing; (2) a cosmological framework in which humanity is a young civilization under observation; (3) spiritual evolution as the purpose of the contact; (4) warnings about humanity's self-destructive trajectory. This pattern recurs across unrelated contactees.

**Intelligence interest**: Some contactees were investigated by FBI and Air Force intelligence — partly to assess whether they were Soviet-influenced disinformation agents, partly to assess whether they had genuine intelligence from classified programs. The contactee phenomenon's intersection with early Cold War security concerns is documented in declassified FBI records.

**Continuity**: The contactee tradition continues through the New Age movement, [[steven-greer]]'s [[ce-5-protocols]] (voluntary contact via meditation), and experiencer communities that emphasize positive, spiritually transformative contact rather than the traumatic abduction narrative.
