# Saharan Humid Period

Climatologically documented epoch (~8500–5300 BC) during which the Sahara region received significantly more rainfall, supported vegetation and large animal populations, and was inhabitable by complex human societies. Sometimes called the "African Humid Period" or "Green Sahara."

## Relevance to Alternative Archaeology

[[jeffrey-drum]] and other alternative researchers argue the Saharan Humid Period is the most plausible construction window for the [[giza-plateau]] pyramid complex. Key argument: the Giza plateau would have been a well-watered, resource-rich, accessible region during this epoch rather than the hyper-arid desert context of dynastic Egypt (~3000 BC onward). The construction logistics — water transport, large labor forces, continuous supply chains — are far more tractable in a humid environment.

This dovetails with the [[sphinx-water-erosion-theory]] (Robert Schoch's argument that the weathering patterns on the Sphinx are consistent with heavy rainfall erosion, implying construction before the desertification) and the broader [[lost-civilization-hypothesis]] that attributes the pyramids to a pre-dynastic advanced civilization.

The Saharan Humid Period ended gradually around 5300 BC, with full desertification complete well before the First Dynasty (~3100 BC). If construction occurred during the humid period, the pyramids are at least 7,500 years old — roughly triple the mainstream Egyptological estimate.
