# Homopolar Generator

A homopolar generator (also called a Faraday disk or unipolar generator) is an electromagnetic generator using a rotating conductive disk in an axial magnetic field to produce continuous DC electrical current without the commutation required by conventional generators. First demonstrated by Michael Faraday in 1831, it is the simplest electrical generator and the only one producing truly constant DC output.

**Operating principle**: A conductive disk rotates in a magnetic field aligned with the disk's axis. Free electrons in the disk experience a Lorentz force from their rotational velocity through the magnetic field, pushing them radially outward (or inward). Current flows from center to rim (or reverse) continuously. No commutator is needed because the geometry never reverses.

**UAP propulsion model**: In [[iPxbILOMQ08]], the homopolar generator principle is cited as a model for UAP propulsion — specifically, the hypothesis that the rotating perimeter of a UAP craft (modeled as a molten iron-silicon conductor at the craft's hull) combined with a perpendicular magnetic field produces an electromagnetic field around the craft. This would explain:
- Electromagnetic disruption of nearby vehicle electrical systems (engines stalling, lights failing)
- Radar return characteristics
- The rotating appearance of disc-shaped UAP

**Historical significance**: The homopolar generator is also cited in the context of [[electrogravitics]] research — T.T. Brown's early experiments involved rotating charged capacitors (the Biefeld-Brown effect), and homopolar geometry has been proposed as one physical model for achieving EM-gravity coupling.
