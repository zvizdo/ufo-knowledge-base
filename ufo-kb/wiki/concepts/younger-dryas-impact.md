# Younger Dryas Impact

## Definition

The Younger Dryas Impact Hypothesis (YDIH) proposes that a comet or fragmentary asteroid impacted or airburst over the Laurentide Ice Sheet ~12,900 years ago, triggering:

- A rapid cooling event (Younger Dryas stadial, ~12,900–11,700 years ago)
- Catastrophic meltwater pulses into the world's oceans
- Megafaunal extinction (mammoths, giant ground sloths, etc.)
- Possible collapse of any advanced pre-Ice-Age human settlements

The end of the Younger Dryas (~11,700 years ago, roughly 9,700 BC) corresponds to a massive climate shift and rising sea levels — what Plato's 9,000-years-before-Solon (c. 9,600 BC) dating for Atlantis's destruction tracks with suspicious precision.

## Flood Myth Convergence

[[ed-barnhart]] and [[luke-caverns]] both note that **hundreds of global flood myths from unconnected civilizations** tell structurally identical stories of civilization-ending catastrophic floods. While skeptics argue this reflects the universal human experience of living near water bodies prone to flooding, the specificity, convergence, and cultural isolation of many traditions suggest a common catastrophic memory. This is the "Meltwater Pulse 1B" hypothesis in popular discourse.

## Plato's Dating

Plato (via Solon's Egyptian sources, c. 600 BC) dates Atlantis's destruction to ~9,600 BC. [[luke-caverns]] notes this matches the end of the Younger Dryas with uncomfortable precision — though Plato's historical reliability for events 9,000 years before his time is highly uncertain.

## CME Alternative (Matt Lroy)

[[matt-lroy]] argues in [[A_LsUSxTm7A]] that the Younger Dryas mechanism was a **coronal mass ejection (CME)** rather than a Taurid comet impact:
- No confirmed impact craters from this period
- Vitrification at [[kepkesi]] and other sites is consistent with plasma discharge, not impact ejecta
- The CME is driven by the binary dead-star companion's perihelion ([[binary-star-companion]])

Lroy also emphasizes the paired **[[older-dryas]]** (~14,500 BP) as a companion catastrophe — the Younger Dryas on its own understates the scale of the civilizational reset.

## Taurid Mechanism (Carlson / Napier — JGE1NIGhBzw)

In [[JGE1NIGhBzw]], [[randall-carlson]] provides the most detailed account in this KB of how the [[taurid-meteor-stream]] connects to the Younger Dryas:

- **Carlson pre-dated the formal paper**: Five lectures at **Warren Wilson College in 1995** argued cosmic impacts were the most likely Younger Dryas trigger — 12 years before Firestone et al. (2007).
- **[[bill-napier]]** (Armagh Observatory) is credited with first proposing the Taurid stream as the specific Younger Dryas mechanism — a large progenitor comet fragmented into the stream, with Earth crossing dense clumps during the 12,900 BP period.
- **Proxy evidence**: Carlson describes at least 8–10 independent teams confirming cosmic signatures at the lower Younger Dryas boundary: iridium spikes in ice cores (~12,900-year layer), platinum spikes, nanodiamond layers (requiring 2,000–3,000°C), microspherules, the "black mat layer" at quarry sites worldwide.
- **1908 Tunguska** ([[1908-tunguska]]): Occurred June 30 — peak of the summer Taurid crossing — is "almost certainly" a Taurid member, providing a modern empirical demonstration.
- **Clumps vs. diffuse**: The stream is not uniform; clumps mean some crossing years are dramatically more dangerous. Carlson's "highway intersection" analogy: civilization approaching a dense clump = approaching an intersection with heavy traffic.
- **Civilizational response**: Knowing this cycle, an advanced civilization would (1) develop deflection technology and (2) establish refugia — logically the Moon.
