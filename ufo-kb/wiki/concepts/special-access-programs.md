# Special Access Programs (SAPs)

A classification structure above Top Secret/SCI (Sensitive Compartmented Information) that governs the most sensitive US government programs. Critically important to the UAP disclosure landscape because UAP-related programs are believed to operate at the SAP level or above, placing them outside normal congressional oversight.

## Types of SAPs

[[eric-davis]] explains in [[LnAiNChnuEQ]] that there are four (possibly three) types:

1. **Regular SAP** — standard special access program
2. **Waived SAP** — congressional notification not required; "waived" from reporting
3. **Unacknowledged SAP** — existence is not acknowledged even to most cleared personnel
4. **Waived Unacknowledged SAP** — combines both; the most restricted category

Davis notes that types 2 and 3 may effectively be combined in practice: "I think more likely it's waved and un acknowledged at the same time so that might be just a third category."

## SAPs vs. Hidden SCIs — The Equivalence Argument

Davis and [[david-grusch]] independently reached the same conclusion:

> "Dave and I actually came to the conclusion that saps are just the same thing as a hidden seci."

The argument:
- **Hidden SCIs** are SCI compartments that are not publicly acknowledged or registered in standard oversight channels
- **SAPs** are programs with restricted access, separate from standard SCI management
- The distinction is actively debated within the IC/DoD security community
- In practice, both serve the same function: controlling access to information that bypasses normal oversight

This equivalence has significant disclosure implications: it means the number of oversight-evading programs may be larger than the known SAP count, and that some programs may be classified as hidden SCIs to avoid even the limited SAP oversight frameworks.

## SAP Creation Process

Davis describes the correct process for elevating a program to SAP status:

1. Security channels and program management create a formal justification
2. The need is documented and reviewed through proper bureaucratic channels
3. Presidential authorization (through the Commander-in-Chief or National Security Advisor) is the correct authority
4. Congress has no direct authority to create a SAP — they can appropriate money but cannot direct the executive branch on SAP creation

This is why [[harry-reid]]'s letter to [[william-lynn]] failed: political muscle from Capitol Hill is not the correct channel. The president (directly, or via [[steven-hadley]] as NSA clearing house) is.

## PEAD Connection

[[pead]] (Presidential Emergency Action Documents) are one mechanism for creating Waived Unacknowledged SAPs. Because PEADs are classified themselves and issued directly by the president, they bypass even the limited oversight structures that regular SAPs face. This is how the most sensitive UAP programs are believed to have been created and sustained outside congressional visibility.

## Compartmentalization Problem

[[bill-sweetman]] documented in the 1980s that SAP compartmentalization created a duplication problem: two programs could be doing the same work, spending taxpayer money twice, without being able to communicate or merge because they cannot acknowledge each other's existence.

Davis confirms: "you end up duplicating programs doing the same thing so we're now spending taxpayers dollars at twice the magnitude doing the same thing between two programs and they can't communicate."
