# Derivative Technology

**Derivative technology** is the term [[uap-gerb]] prefers over **ARV** (alien reproduction vehicle) when discussing US exploitation of non-human-origin hardware. The shift in terminology reflects a more nuanced understanding: not every instance of program exploitation involves attempting to build a complete copycat vehicle — sometimes individual technologies or capabilities are stripped from recovered craft and adapted to existing US airframes.

## Why the Term Matters

"ARV" implies a complete reproduction of an alien vehicle. Derivative technology acknowledges a spectrum:

1. **Full ARV / copycat** — attempting to build a functional replication (the [[mark-mccandish]] flux liner / Northrop Grumman approach)
2. **Subsystem adoption** — taking a specific capability (e.g., electrooptic cloaking) and adapting it to a US airframe
3. **Materials research** — studying recovered materials (metamaterials, bismuth layers, element 115) without necessarily deploying them as hardware
4. **Propulsion elements** — partial adoption (e.g., internal mass reduction for TR3B rather than full anti-gravity)

## Key Derived Capabilities Named

- **Electrooptic cloaking / signature management** — observed on [[dylan-borland]]'s Langley triangle; early-2000s US Army interest in applying to M1 Abrams-class vehicles; UAPGerb has an anonymous trusted witness who confirms NHI provenance of the underlying technology
- **Internal mass reduction** (TR3B) — not full anti-gravity but a 89% mass reduction for the craft's propulsion system; reverse-engineered at [[sandia-national-laboratories]] and [[lawrence-livermore-national-laboratory]]
- **Bismuth/high-K dielectric effects** — [[thomas-townsend-brown]]'s anti-gravity research using bismuth; [[garry-nolan]] has bismuth-magnesium parts allegedly from Roswell; the overlap suggests NHI material informed known terrestrial anti-gravity experiments in the 1950s
