# Non-Terrestrial Officers

The "Non-Terrestrial Officers" spreadsheet is [[gary-mckinnon]]'s most widely cited and analytically specific disclosure claim. It is a single-witness claim supported by no recovered physical documentation, and it connects to a coherent (if unconfirmed) model of a classified human space program. This page aggregates who makes the claim, what the evidence and disputes are, and what the claim connects to.

## The Claim

During his 2001 unauthorized access to US military computer networks, McKinnon accessed a US Navy network and found an Excel spreadsheet explicitly titled **"Non-Terrestrial Officers."** According to McKinnon's account (consistent across multiple interviews over more than 15 years):

### Contents of the Spreadsheet

**Tab 1 — Officers:**
- Approximately **30–40 names** in initial-plus-surname format (e.g., "J. Smith")
- McKinnon did not record or remember any specific names
- The names did not match any officers in the publicly known US Navy officer corps that McKinnon was aware of

**Tab 1 — Ships:**
- **Ship names** — none of which matched any vessel in the publicly registered US Navy fleet
- McKinnon interpreted these as designations for platforms not publicly acknowledged to exist

**Tab 2 — Transfers:**
- **Fleet-to-fleet and ship-to-ship transfer records** of exotic chemicals
- Materials specifically identified by McKinnon: **barium**, **strontium**, **molybdenum**
- The records appeared to document a logistics/supply chain for these materials in space or near-space context

### McKinnon's Interpretation

McKinnon is explicit: he does not interpret "Non-Terrestrial Officers" as meaning alien officers in uniform. He interprets it as meaning **officers not stationed on Earth** — a human crew of a classified space-based platform or fleet. His interpretation is Variant 1 of the [[secret-space-program]] claim: conventional human space presence, classified, publicly unacknowledged.

He is equally explicit that this is **not Solar Warden**. The Solar Warden narrative — which circulates online and attributes a vast classified space fleet to McKinnon's hack — was, by McKinnon's account, seeded after his story became public, and he considers it likely disinformation designed to discredit the more specific and verifiable claim.

## What Happened to the Evidence

McKinnon downloaded the spreadsheet to his computer. When he was arrested in March 2002, the [[office-of-naval-intelligence]] — not the FBI or a civilian law enforcement agency — physically seized all his hard drives. The drives have never been returned or their contents publicly disclosed.

This is significant for two reasons:

1. **Jurisdictional anomaly**: A UK-based computer crime case would normally involve FBI liaison, not ONI physical seizure. ONI's direct involvement suggests specific pre-arranged institutional interest in the material.

2. **Evidence absorption**: The one physical copy of the spreadsheet data that existed outside a classified government system was seized and disappeared into ONI custody. No Freedom of Information request has produced it. The data is effectively irretrievable.

## Disputes and Limitations

**Single-witness**: McKinnon is the sole witness to the spreadsheet's existence. There is no corroborating document, no second-hand account from anyone else who has seen it, and no physical evidence.

**Memory reliability**: McKinnon accessed the spreadsheet during a period when he was conducting hundreds of similar sessions. He did not record specific names or ship designations at the time — he browsed and then moved on. He recalls the title, general format, quantity (30-40 names), and the chemical names — but not individual officer names or ship names that could be independently verified.

**Disproportionate response as indirect evidence**: The US government's response — a decade-long extradition fight, 70-year potential sentence, ONI physical seizure rather than FBI handling — is anomalous for a computer crime that produced no financial harm and no confirmed espionage. This disproportionality is the strongest indirect evidence that McKinnon found something of genuine institutional significance. It is not proof of the spreadsheet's content, but it is evidence that something warranted extraordinary response.

## The Chemical Connection — Bifield-Brown Dielectrics

The specific chemicals McKinnon identified — **barium, strontium, molybdenum** — are not random exotic materials. They are precisely the compounds most effective as high-K dielectrics in [[biefeld-brown-effect]] capacitor experiments:

- **Barium titanate** (BaTiO₃): one of the highest-K dielectrics known; central to Brown's experiments
- **Strontium titanate** (SrTiO₃): comparable K values; used in high-performance capacitor research
- **Molybdenum alloys**: refractory metals used in advanced capacitor electrodes and high-temperature dielectric applications

[[jesse-michaels]] and McKinnon develop this in [[2ttdlCa5ZCI]]: if a classified space program were manufacturing high-K dielectric metamaterials in zero-gravity (where the absence of convection allows more perfect crystal growth), the supply chain would look exactly like what McKinnon found. The program would:

1. Have a crew of officers not stationed on Earth ("Non-Terrestrial Officers")
2. Use vessels not in the public Navy registry
3. Move barium, strontium, and molybdenum compounds between manufacturing platforms

[[paul-laviolette]]'s research and [[charles-buhler]]'s vacuum thrust results provide the theoretical and experimental framework suggesting these materials are relevant to a real propulsion effect.

## What Remains Unresolved

1. **Can any ship names be independently identified?** McKinnon did not record them, making this impossible unless another source names them
2. **Are the exotic chemical transfers traceable to classified aerospace procurement?** If DoD procurement records for barium titanate or strontium compounds spike in the late 1990s–2000s in anomalous ways, that would be indirect corroboration
3. **Why ONI?** The specific jurisdictional decision to involve ONI rather than FBI has never been officially explained
4. **What did the NHTCU officers see at ONI in Washington?** Their visit preceded the legal escalation; what they were shown or told has never been disclosed
