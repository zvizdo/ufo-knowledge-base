# Quantum Mechanics

Quantum mechanics (QM) is the fundamental physical theory governing matter and energy at atomic and subatomic scales. It supersedes classical (Newtonian) mechanics for phenomena at small scales and underlies all of atomic, molecular, nuclear, and particle physics, as well as the behavior of macroscopic quantum systems (superconductors, lasers, topological insulators).

**Core principles**:
- **Wave-particle duality**: Quantum objects exhibit both wave and particle properties depending on how they are observed
- **Superposition**: A quantum system can exist in a superposition of multiple states simultaneously until measured
- **Entanglement**: Quantum systems can be correlated such that measuring one instantly determines the state of the other, regardless of distance
- **Uncertainty principle**: Position and momentum (and other conjugate variables) cannot both be precisely determined simultaneously
- **Measurement problem**: The act of measurement collapses the wavefunction from superposition to a definite state — the mechanism for this is unresolved (see [[many-worlds-interpretation]])

**von Neumann contribution**: [[john-von-neumann]] provided quantum mechanics with its rigorous mathematical foundation, proving in 1932 that quantum mechanics cannot be completed by adding "hidden variables" without violating the Bell inequality constraints — a result later empirically confirmed by Alain Aspect's 1982 experiments.
