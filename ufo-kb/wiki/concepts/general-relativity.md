# General Relativity

Einstein's theory of gravitation (1915): gravity is not a force but a curvature of spacetime produced by mass-energy. Described by the Einstein field equations G_μν = 8πG T_μν, relating the spacetime curvature tensor (left side) to the stress-energy tensor (right side). Predicts gravitational waves, black holes, frame-dragging, and gravitational lensing — all confirmed experimentally to high precision.

**Mathematical structure**: GR describes gravity through the curvature of a pseudo-Riemannian manifold. The key objects are the metric tensor (describing distances in curved spacetime), the connection (describing how vectors are parallel-transported), and the Riemann curvature tensor (measuring how curved the manifold is). The connection on the spacetime manifold is mathematically a connection on a fiber bundle — exactly the same structure that Yang-Mills gauge theories use to describe particle forces.

**Wu-Yang correspondence**: The [[wu-yang-dictionary]] (1975) demonstrated that GR's fiber bundle structure (gravity as curvature of a bundle over spacetime) and the [[standard-model]] gauge theory structure (forces as connections on bundles) are mathematically the same framework. This is why GR and Yang-Mills theory can be written in the same language even though they describe apparently different physical phenomena — gravity vs. nuclear/electromagnetic forces.

**KB relevance**: In the KB, general relativity appears primarily through the Wu-Yang correspondence as the theoretical foundation for attempts at gravity-electromagnetism unification. The claim that UAP propulsion involves gravity manipulation presupposes a physics in which gravity can be coupled to electromagnetic fields — not standard GR (where they are separate) but the target of unification proposals that the Wu-Yang dictionary approach advances (→ [[gravity-electromagnetism-unification]]).

**Frame-dragging and UAP**: General relativity predicts frame-dragging — the dragging of spacetime around a rotating massive body (Lense-Thirring effect, confirmed by Gravity Probe B in 2011). Some UAP propulsion theories invoke frame-dragging-like effects at much smaller scales than GR would normally allow, requiring new physics beyond standard GR.
