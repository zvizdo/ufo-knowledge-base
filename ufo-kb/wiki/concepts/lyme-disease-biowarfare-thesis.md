# Lyme Disease Biowarfare Thesis

The Lyme disease biowarfare thesis holds that Lyme disease — a tick-borne bacterial illness caused by *Borrelia burgdorferi*, first identified in Lyme, Connecticut in 1975 — may have originated from US government biological weapons research at the [[plum-island]] Animal Disease Center, located approximately 9 miles from Lyme.

**Plum Island connection**: Plum Island operated classified biological research programs during the Cold War, including research on tick-borne diseases. The island is located in Long Island Sound, accessible from both Connecticut and Long Island (Old Lyme is across the water from Plum Island). The geographic proximity — classified tick research facility, new tick-borne disease first identified nearby — is the core of the circumstantial case.

**Erich Traub**: The thesis received its most detailed development in Kris Newby's book *Bitten* (2019). Newby identified German virologist Erich Traub, a specialist in tick-borne and other vector-borne pathogens who worked on biological weapons for Nazi Germany, was brought to the US via [[operation-paperclip]], and subsequently worked at Plum Island. Traub's expertise in infecting ticks with pathogens is central to the thesis.

**Congressional investigation**: In 2019, the US House of Representatives passed an amendment to the National Defense Authorization Act directing the Department of Defense Inspector General to investigate whether the Defense Department had experimented with tick-borne diseases as biological weapons. This constituted formal acknowledgment that the question was not frivolous.

**Scientific status**: The scientific mainstream regards Lyme disease as a naturally emerging zoonosis — *Borrelia burgdorferi* has been identified in ticks from the 1940s, predating any proposed weaponization program. The biowarfare thesis is not accepted by the medical establishment but is not definitively disproven.
