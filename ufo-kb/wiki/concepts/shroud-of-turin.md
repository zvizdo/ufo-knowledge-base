# Shroud of Turin

## Description

A linen burial cloth (~4.4m × 1.1m) bearing a faint, photographic-negative image of a man. The image shows:
- Full-body front and back
- Wounds consistent with crucifixion: nail punctures at wrists (not palms), crown of thorns, lance wound at the side, scourging marks on the back, ankle wounds

[[randall-carlson]] notes: "All of the wounds are 100% consistent with the wounds described in the passion in the gospels." This consistency is either evidence of authenticity or evidence of a medieval forger who understood the passion narrative in forensic detail.

## Historical Provenance

The Shroud's documented history begins in the mid-14th century, but [[randall-carlson]] tracks it back through the [[knights-templar]] lineage:

1. **Jacques de Molay** — last Grandmaster of the Knights Templar; executed 1314 by burning (the famous "curse of de Molay")
2. **Geoffrey de Charney** (spelled variously as Desarie, de Charné) — associate of de Molay (possibly his nephew); the first recorded possessor of the Shroud; burned with de Molay
3. Ownership passed through successive families
4. Eventually donated to the House of Savoy; transferred to the **Cathedral of Saint John the Baptist in Turin**, where it has remained

The Templar provenance is significant because the Templars were sophisticated enough to distinguish a forgery from an extraordinary artifact.

## Scientific Controversies

### 1988 Carbon Dating (Vatican-Approved)

The Vatican permitted carbon-14 dating in 1988. Results dated the cloth to 1260–1390 CE — consistent with a medieval forgery. The result was widely reported as "debunking."

### Carlson's Critique of the Debunking

Carlson argues the 1988 dating was flawed:
- The sample taken was from a **repaired/restored corner** of the Shroud, not the original linen — medieval repairs using different-age thread would skew the date toward more recent
- Subsequent studies have contradicted some of the debunking claims, though Carlson acknowledges he hasn't followed the most recent work

### Post-1988 Studies

Multiple subsequent scientific studies (which Carlson says he hasn't fully tracked) have reportedly:
- Found evidence of 1st-century pollen species on the Shroud
- Documented that the image cannot be replicated by any known medieval technique (no known pigments; the image is a surface oxidation)
- Found Roman coin images from Tiberius Caesar's reign over the eyes (Lepton coins, 29 CE)

> ⚠ The Shroud's authenticity remains scientifically unresolved. The 1988 carbon dating is the strongest argument for medieval forgery; the image-formation mechanism and Templar provenance are the strongest arguments for antiquity.

## Open Questions

- Have luminescence or other non-carbon dating techniques been applied?
- What is the current scientific consensus on the image-formation mechanism?
- Why was the sample taken from the corner (known restored area) rather than from the body proper?
