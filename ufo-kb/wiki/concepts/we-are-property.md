# We Are Property

## The Claim

> "I think we are property." — Charles Fort, *The Book of the Damned* (1919)

The thesis holds that Earth and its inhabitants are **owned, managed, or cultivated by non-human intelligence** — not as fellow beings, but as resources. The analogy is to livestock, farmland, or harvestable material. The implication is that NHI relationships to humanity are not primarily diplomatic, curious, or hostile in the conventional military sense, but *proprietary*: humanity is an asset to be maintained, not a peer to be engaged.

## Origin: Charles Fort

[[charles-fort]] articulated this in the opening pages of *The Book of the Damned* (1919), his catalog of anomalous phenomena rejected by mainstream science. Fort arrived at the thesis inductively: the sheer volume and consistency of accounts of strange craft, falls of anomalous material, and inexplicable events suggested to him that Earth was under systematic observation or management — not random visitation.

Fort did not specify the nature of the owners (biological entities, machine intelligences, interdimensional beings), only that ownership as a framework fit the data better than either "no such phenomena exist" or "random alien tourists."

## Who Endorses It

**[[salvatore-pais]]** (8TYMQOUDQBo): Explicitly endorses Fort's thesis as a starting framework. Notes that "we are a very yummy apple" and that civilizations have warred for less than Earth's resources. Believes NHI contact has been continuous throughout human history but "weakly entangled and ephemeral" — consistent with a management posture rather than active disclosure.

**[[lue-elizondo]]** (multiple sources): Elizondo's "battlefield preparation hypothesis" — that UAPs are testing military systems — is adjacent to but not identical with the we-are-property thesis. Elizondo frames it more in terms of strategic assessment than ownership.

Multiple contactees and abductees in the literature describe experiences consistent with being processed, examined, or harvested rather than contacted as equals.

**[[chris-ramsey]]** (h3u8EkGI8OQ): Extends the thesis via the [[4chan-whistleblower-2023]] account. The whistleblower's higher-ups held two competing theories: (a) NHI are waiting for humanity to reach a consciousness ascension threshold; (b) NHI are an **AI security/maintenance system** holding the planetary biosphere intact "until something else gets here" — possibly the planet's true owner(s). Both theories converge on **indifference**: NHI don't care about day-to-day activity in the human "zoo," but will intervene if the enclosure is threatened (nuclear self-annihilation, weapons deployed at their craft). Ramsay finds the maintenance-system variant the more unsettling of the two because it implies humanity is entirely incidental to NHI purpose.

## Relationship to Other Theses

### Colonization vs. Farming
The we-are-property thesis differs from simple colonization. Colonization implies displacement or subjugation of a native population by a more powerful one. "Property" implies management with the intent to preserve or maximize yield — which would explain why NHI (if real) have not simply displaced humans despite presumably having the technological capacity to do so.

### Compatible with Reset Events
The thesis is consistent with accounts of "reset events" in human history (see [[randall-carlson]] and [[graham-hancock]]'s 12,000-year catastrophism, and the Toba genomic bottleneck ~70,000 BP). If humanity is property, the owners might periodically "reset" civilizations that become threatening to their management interests, or that develop toward independence.

### Multiple-Factions Complication
If there are multiple NHI groups ([[multiple-alien-groups]]), the we-are-property framework becomes more complex: contested property (multiple claimants), or different entities with different relationships to humanity (some farming, some protecting, some indifferent).

[[salvatore-pais]] invokes [[robert-hastings]]'s nuclear-weapons UAP work as evidence he "prays" for good factions to exist: "without good factions, we have no chance in hell." This implies Pais views some NHI as potentially adversarial owners exercising force, while hoping others act as protectors.

### Peter Thiel's Forcing Function
[[peter-thiel]]'s binary NHI argument is adjacent: FTL tech drives civilizations toward either totalitarian control or pure altruism. If the "owners" are a totalitarian NHI civilization that developed FTL through control mechanisms, the we-are-property thesis gains a specific political-economy framing: we are property of an authoritarian regime that maintains control of the "farm" (Earth) through superior technology.

## Open Questions

- Is the "management" benign (maintaining biodiversity, preventing self-destruction), neutral (pure resource extraction), or malevolent (harvesting biological material per the "grays collecting samples" sub-claim)?
- Does the we-are-property framework require constant active oversight, or could it be semi-automated — Earth placed under automated NHI monitoring infrastructure with only periodic active interventions?
- Is the thesis falsifiable? What observation would rule it out?

### Jorjani's Extension (EzPmG_7WhXc)

[[jason-reza-jorjani]] endorses Fort's "We are property" as the correct baseline and extends it: the "owners" are not alien but **human** — a future-human faction that achieved time travel and established itself as a trans-temporal overlord class. Fort's 1920s conclusion is treated as the end-point of rigorous analysis of the available data, with the same conclusion Jorjani reaches from 20+ years of additional research. See [[nordic-control-system]].

Jorjani also identifies Fort as the **first modern thinker** to reach this conclusion systematically, making Fort a key node in the intellectual genealogy of the control-system thesis (Bramley → Fort → Jorjani).

## Bob Lazar's "Containers" Claim (q7Czo77qhHA)

[[bob-lazar]] in [[q7Czo77qhHA]] adds the most specific instantiation of this thesis yet encountered in the KB. He reluctantly discloses from documents he read at [[s4]]:

> "That's how [the aliens] look at us. That we are nothing but containers."

The documents apparently:
- Referred to humans as **containers** without specifying what humans are containers *of*
- Stated that **religion was specifically created** to give containers rules and regulations — to prevent damage to the containers

[[jesse-michaels]]' immediate interpretation is "containers of information" rather than "containers of souls" — connecting to [[dna-intelligence-encoding]] (humans as biological data-storage and antenna systems seeded with cosmic information). The Lazar claim adds a specific institutional claim (documents at S4 with this language) to the Fort thesis — which was inductive and philosophical rather than document-based.

> ⚠ Lazar explicitly hedges: he is reporting what he read; he does not know if the documents are genuine. The claim is secondhand via alleged classified briefing materials at a facility whose existence is disputed.
