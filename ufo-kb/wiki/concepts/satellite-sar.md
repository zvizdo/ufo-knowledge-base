# Satellite Sar

Synthetic Aperture Radar from orbit; the detection method underlying Filippo Biondi's [[beyond-protocol|Beyond Protocol]] discovery of vertical tubular structures ~1 km deep beneath the Giza plateau.

SAR (Synthetic Aperture Radar) is an active microwave imaging technique that synthesizes a virtual large antenna from satellite motion. Biondi's adaptation, using the Italian [[cosmos-skymed]] satellite constellation, measures surface micro-vibrations (phonon responses) induced by radar pulses — extracting information about subsurface structure from a sequence of micro-vibration snapshots. This is fundamentally different from attenuation-based [[muon-tomography]] and can image to depths exceeding 1.4 km (validated at [[gran-sasso-laboratory|Gran Sasso]]).
