# Analytical Overlay

In the remote viewing literature (→ [[remote-viewing]]), analytical overlay (AOL) is the contamination of raw, direct sensory impressions by the viewer's analytical mind — the tendency to impose familiar conceptual categories on ambiguous raw data. A viewer receiving an impression of "large, metallic, rotating" may mentally interpret this as a "turbine" or "helicopter" before recording it, losing the raw perceptual accuracy.

**In remote viewing protocol**: Structured remote viewing methodology explicitly trains viewers to separate raw impressions (ideograms, coordinates, basic sensory data) from analytical interpretations. AOL is flagged when a viewer catches themselves generating a concept rather than a percept. Advanced viewers learn to note "AOL: airplane" to flag that the concept appeared, while preserving the underlying raw data for later analysis.

**Application to UAP witness testimony**: The Kenneth Arnold case ([[kenneth-arnold]], 1947 Mount Rainier sighting) is the canonical example of how AOL can shape the entire cultural framing of a phenomenon. Arnold described the objects as moving like a "saucer skipping on water" — describing the flight pattern, not the shape. Press reporting translated this to "flying saucers" as the object description, establishing a cultural template that influenced how all subsequent witnesses described what they saw. Arnold's own later descriptions suggest the objects were crescent or heel-shaped, not saucer-shaped.

**Significance**: AOL is the mechanism by which cultural expectation, media framing, and witness expectation contaminate raw observation — making it difficult to reconstruct what was actually perceived from what was reported. It applies to both:
1. Individual witness reports (conscious or unconscious interpretation before articulation)
2. Population-level reporting (later witnesses unconsciously shaping descriptions to fit established templates)
