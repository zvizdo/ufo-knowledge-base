# PTSD in Military UAP Witnesses

A recurring observation across firsthand military-witness reports: personnel involved in close-encounter UAP events disproportionately develop **PTSD-spectrum symptomatology** matching the diagnostic profile of combat trauma — flashbacks, hypervigilance, sleep disruption, dissociation. The pattern recurs across [[1980-rendlesham-forest]], [[1967-echo-flight]], [[2004-nimitz-tic-tac]], and the [[1992-cape-canaveral-saucer]] / [[greg-rogers]] account.

The credibility-framework reading: PTSD is a poor candidate for fabrication or hallucination explanations — the body's response system is engaged in a way consistent with **a real, severe threat event having occurred**. The pattern functions as embodied corroboration of witness sincerity even when the propositional content of the encounter is contested.

The skeptical counterargument is that pre-existing trauma vulnerability or general operational stress can produce equivalent symptomatology without requiring the encounter narrative to be veridical.
