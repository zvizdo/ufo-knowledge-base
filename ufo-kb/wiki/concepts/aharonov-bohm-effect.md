# Aharonov-Bohm Effect

## The Effect

The Aharonov-Bohm (AB) effect, predicted by Yakir Aharonov and David Bohm in 1959 and experimentally confirmed, demonstrates that a charged particle (e.g., an electron) can be influenced by an electromagnetic vector potential **even in a region where the electric and magnetic fields (E and B) are zero**.

The canonical experiment:
- An electron beam is split and passed around a perfectly insulated solenoid carrying a current
- Outside the solenoid: E = 0, B = 0 (perfectly shielded)
- Yet the electron beam shows a measurable phase shift — a **holonomy effect** — that depends on the current through the solenoid

This is not a classical effect. It cannot be explained by the Lorentz force (which depends on E and B directly). What's doing the work is the **electromagnetic vector potential** — a field quantity that classical physics treated as a mathematical convenience, not a physically real entity.

## Puthoff's Framing (iQOibpIDx-4)

[[hal-puthoff]] uses the AB effect as his primary physics analogy for **metric engineering** — the project of manipulating spacetime geometry without conventional energy densities:

> "There's only one analog that even smells vaguely like this in electromagnetism. Aharonov and Bohm argued that if you passed an electron beam around a solenoid... you would have no E or B fields outside of the solenoid, but yet you'd get a phase shift in the electron beam."

His argument:
1. Prior to the AB effect (1959), physicists thought they understood electromagnetism fully from Maxwell's time — E and B fields were the complete story.
2. The AB effect revealed that the **electromagnetic 4-potential** (vector and scalar) was the real physical actor, not just a mathematical intermediary.
3. This was *frightening* at the time — it meant a level of physical reality beneath the observable fields, accessible through quantum coherence.
4. The analogy for gravity: Einstein's GR uses metric coefficients the way Maxwell's equations use E and B fields. But there may be **deeper variables** (analogous to the 4-potential) that the metric coefficients are derived from — and those could be manipulated without the extreme energy requirements of engineering E or B directly.

In Puthoff's formulation, the **polarizable vacuum model** is his specific theoretical approach to making this analogy precise: the vacuum has dielectric properties (ε₀ and μ₀) that can in principle be locally altered, thereby changing c locally, and thereby inducing all effects of curved spacetime without a mass source.

## Vector and Scalar Potentials as Primary Actors

Puthoff notes that since the AB effect, physics has effectively moved on to recognizing potentials as primary:

> "Nobody [in modern physics] even wants to call it vector and scalar. Just potentials. They're different connections on fiber bundles."

This framing — potentials as connections on fiber bundles — is the geometric/gauge-theoretic language in which modern physics understands electromagnetism. In this language, the AB effect is a holonomy: the failure of parallel transport to return to the starting point when transported around a closed loop enclosing flux.

Puthoff has **two patents** on engineering systems that work exclusively with vector and scalar potentials (no E or B fields required) and has started a new company around this approach.

## Toroidal Geometries and Casimir Effect

Puthoff extends the AB effect to toroidal field configurations:
- A toroid can be arranged with zero E and B fields everywhere externally, yet strong internal vector and scalar potentials
- Any quantum detector sensitive to phase shifts (not just Lorentz force) can detect the potentials even in the absence of fields
- This opens an engineering space — **potential-only field manipulation** — that conventional electromagnetic engineering ignores entirely

He connects this to the [[scalar-waves]] / extended electrodynamics tradition, where the dropped terms in Heaviside's simplification of Maxwell are precisely the potential terms that the AB effect reveals are physically real.
