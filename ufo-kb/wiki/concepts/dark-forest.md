# Dark Forest Hypothesis

The Dark Forest Hypothesis is a solution to the [[fermi-paradox]] proposed by Chinese science fiction author Liu Cixin in his 2008 novel *The Dark Forest* (second volume of the Three-Body Problem trilogy). It argues that the apparent silence of the universe — the absence of detectable extraterrestrial civilizations — is explained not by civilizational rarity but by universal preemptive predation.

**Core logic**: The argument rests on two axioms:
1. Every civilization's first priority is survival.
2. Resources in the universe are finite; civilizations can expand indefinitely but resources cannot.

From these, Liu Cixin argues that any sufficiently advanced civilization, upon detecting another, must destroy it preemptively — because:
- Communication takes time; the other civilization may develop faster than expected
- You cannot verify the other civilization's intentions
- Waiting gives the other a chance to strike first
- Therefore rational self-preservation requires striking first every time

The universe becomes a dark forest: every hunter hides, and anything that broadcasts its location gets destroyed.

**Implications for SETI**: If the Dark Forest is correct, Active SETI (deliberately broadcasting our location) is catastrophically dangerous. Several METI (Messaging Extraterrestrial Intelligence) projects have been opposed on similar grounds, though the opposition usually invokes general risk rather than the specific Dark Forest logic.

**UAP relevance**: In the UAP context, the Dark Forest hypothesis provides one explanation for why NHI, if real, might avoid open contact — or why contact might be covert, controlled, or through intermediaries. It also suggests that humanity's current radio signature may already have attracted attention, making ongoing covert interaction more plausible than it might otherwise seem.

**Contrast with other Fermi resolutions**: The Dark Forest contrasts with the [[great-filter]] (which locates the silence in civilizational rarity) and with the Zoo Hypothesis (which locates it in deliberate non-interference rather than predation).
