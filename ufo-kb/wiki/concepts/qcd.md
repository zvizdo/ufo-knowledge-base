# Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD)

Quantum Chromodynamics is the quantum field theory of the strong nuclear force — the force that binds quarks into protons, neutrons, and other hadrons, and holds atomic nuclei together. QCD is a Yang-Mills gauge theory based on the SU(3) color symmetry group, where "color" charge (red, green, blue) is analogous to electric charge in electromagnetism. Gluons are the force carriers, analogous to photons, but unlike photons they carry color charge themselves and can interact with each other — making QCD a strongly interacting, non-linear theory.

**Confinement and asymptotic freedom**: Two of QCD's key properties are (1) quark confinement — quarks cannot exist as isolated particles; they are permanently bound in hadrons — and (2) asymptotic freedom — at very short distances the strong force weakens, allowing quarks to behave nearly freely at high energies. These properties are the opposite of electromagnetic behavior and arise from the non-Abelian (self-interacting) structure of the SU(3) gauge group.

**Chern-Simons theta term**: The Chern-Simons term appears naturally within the QCD Lagrangian as the "theta term" (θ-term), multiplying the Chern-Simons topological density. This term is connected to the strong CP problem — the unexplained observation that QCD does not appear to violate CP symmetry even though the theta term would generically produce such violation. The Chern-Simons structure within QCD is one of the paths by which topological mathematics (developed in pure mathematics by [[jim-simons]]) entered physics, and is the connection the [[chern-simons-gravity-connection]] page traces to potential gravity-coupling implications.
