# Standard Model

The Standard Model of particle physics is the quantum field theory describing fundamental particles (quarks, leptons, gauge bosons, Higgs boson) and three of the four fundamental forces: electromagnetic, weak nuclear, and strong nuclear. It is a gauge theory based on the symmetry group SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1) — the direct product of three Lie groups corresponding to the strong, weak, and electromagnetic forces respectively.

**Particle content**: The Standard Model organizes particles into: (1) fermions (matter particles) — 6 quarks and 6 leptons and their antiparticles; (2) gauge bosons (force carriers) — photons (EM), W/Z bosons (weak), gluons (strong); (3) the Higgs boson (confirmed 2012), which gives mass to W/Z bosons through spontaneous symmetry breaking.

**Mathematical structure**: The Standard Model is built on Yang-Mills gauge theories — the non-Abelian generalizations of electromagnetism that C.N. Yang and Robert Mills developed in 1954. The Yang-Mills field equations describe force carriers in terms of connections on fiber bundles over spacetime. This fiber bundle structure is what the [[wu-yang-dictionary]] recognized as identical to the mathematical structure of [[general-relativity]].

**What's missing**: The Standard Model does not include gravity. A complete "Theory of Everything" unifying SM with GR has been sought since the 1970s but remains elusive. This gap — the missing gravitational sector of the Standard Model — is precisely where UAP-relevant physics may live. If gravity can be coupled to Standard Model fields in ways not yet formalized in public physics, that coupling would be the mechanism for proposed UAP propulsion systems.
