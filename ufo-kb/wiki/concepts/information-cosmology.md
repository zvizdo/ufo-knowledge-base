# Information Cosmology

The thesis, developed across multiple thinkers and synthesized by [[jason-reza-jorjani]] in [[EzPmG_7WhXc]], that **matter, energy, and information are three states of the same underlying thing** — and that we may live inside a quantum computational system whose structure is detectable through extreme physical events.

## The Chain of Reasoning

### 1. Konrad Zuse (WWII): Calculating Space
[[konrad-zuse]], who built the world's first programmable computer before Turing, proposed during World War II that quantum anomalies indicate we live in a **Rechnender Raum** (Calculating Space). His specific prediction: nuclear detonations are high-entropy enough to reveal the **mesh of the matrix** — the computational substructure of reality.

### 2. Ralph Landauer (1961): Data Has Mass
Ralph Landauer proved that **erasing a bit of information releases energy** (Landauer's principle) — because erasing data locally decreases entropy, requiring compensating entropy increase elsewhere, per the second law of thermodynamics. This implies: before erasure, data stored in a physical system **is a form of mass** (via E=mc²). Landauer's erasure → energy release → implies data = hidden mass.

### 3. Claude Shannon (1948): Formalizing Information
Claude Shannon's mathematical theory of information established precise quantification of data. Landauer's calculations use Shannon's formalism to determine how much energy is released per bit erased.

### 4. John Archibald Wheeler (late 1970s–80s): "It from Bit"
[[john-archibald-wheeler]] took the Landauer chain to its logical conclusion: if data is mass, and mass is energy, and energy is data, then **matter and energy are different states of information**. His phrase "It from Bit" — every physical entity derives its existence from information-theoretic answers to yes-or-no questions. We live in a participatory informational cosmos.

### 5. Melvin Vopson: Data Accumulation as Geophysical Threat
Physicist Melvin Vopson extended Landauer's calculations to show:
- All world data today ≈ **1 kilogram** of hidden mass (too small to measure directly)
- At current data growth of **25% per year**, in approximately **340 years** Earth's data centers will accumulate a **moon's worth of mass**
- A moon's worth of mass concentrated on Earth's surface (at data centers in Silicon Valley and China) would be a **geophysical catastrophe**: earthquakes, volcanism, tsunamis as oceans are pulled toward the concentrations

This creates an exponential information catastrophe horizon — a hard physical limit on digital civilization.

### 6. Dark Matter as Computational Cloud
The episode proposes (Jesse Michaels contributing) that **dark matter** — the undetected mass accounting for ~27% of the universe — may be **unrendered computational space**: data stored in the system that hasn't been activated/observed. This would explain why dark matter doesn't interact electromagnetically (it's not rendered) but has gravitational effects (mass is mass regardless of render state).

Wave-particle duality and the observer-collapsing-the-wave-function are reinterpreted as **rendering optimization**: the system only fully instantiates physical reality at the observer's point of measurement — like level-of-detail in video game rendering.
