# DNA Phantom Effect

A set of experiments conducted by Russian scientists (primarily attributed to Pyotr Gariaev and colleagues in the 1990s) in which laser light continued to scatter in a container after a DNA sample was physically removed. The scattering pattern persisted for hours or days — as if the DNA were still present — despite the physical absence of any material to cause it.

**Experimental claim**: A DNA sample was placed in a chamber and illuminated with a laser. When the DNA was removed, the laser scattering pattern did not immediately revert to baseline but continued in the pattern characteristic of DNA presence, gradually dissipating over hours. This "phantom" was interpreted as evidence of a non-material energetic residue — a field or informational imprint left in space by the physical DNA.

**Wave genetics context**: Gariaev developed a theoretical framework ("wave genetics") proposing that DNA has a dual nature — material (the physical double helix) and wave (a field-based information structure that can exist somewhat independently of the physical molecule). The phantom effect was offered as experimental evidence for this wave component.

**Replication status**: The DNA phantom experiments have not been widely replicated by independent laboratories using rigorous controls. The interpretation of residual laser scattering as "phantom DNA" rather than as instrument artifact, contamination, or surface coating effects is disputed. No peer-reviewed replication in major Western physics or biophysics journals confirms the original result.

**KB relevance**: In [[dna-intelligence-encoding]], the phantom effect is cited alongside [[luc-montagnier]]'s DNA teleportation experiments as evidence for the thesis that biological information has electromagnetic or field substrates beyond the physical molecule. The convergence of two independent claims — Russian phantom experiments and French electromagnetic teleportation — is presented as mutually reinforcing, though both remain scientifically unconfirmed.
