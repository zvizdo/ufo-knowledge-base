# Eminent Domain

## Concept

Eminent domain (in US law) is the power of the government to take private property for public use, contingent on payment of "just compensation" to the property owner under the Fifth Amendment. It is most commonly invoked for infrastructure (highways, utilities, rail), but the language is general enough to apply to any class of property.

## UAP-Disclosure Application

In the [[schumer-uap-amendment|UAP Disclosure Act of 2023]] (Schumer-Rounds amendment to the NDAA), eminent domain is the mechanism that would allow the federal government to compel aerospace contractors and other corporate custodians of recovered UAP materials, technologies, and information to surrender them to a presidentially-appointed nine-person panel. The mechanism is explicit precisely because the assumed default is that custodian corporations would not volunteer the materials.

[[hal-puthoff|Hal Puthoff]] in [[Gf_tKn9TaP8]] notes that the eminent-domain clause "turns people's hair on fire when they think they've got something and don't want to share it" — i.e., the clause is precisely targeted at the political base of resistance to disclosure: corporate custodians whose business model depends on continued sequestration.

The 2023 amendment passed the Senate and was killed in the House. The 2024 reintroduction retained most provisions but stripped the operational teeth; the [[national-archives]] release track survived. The eminent-domain provision is the most-discussed structural element of the bill because it would convert the disclosure question from a voluntary-cooperation problem into a property-transfer-mandate problem.
