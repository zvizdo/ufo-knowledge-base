# Block Universe

The physical model, rooted in Einstein's general relativity, in which **all spacetime events — past, present, and future — are equally real and fixed**. The "block" is the four-dimensional spacetime manifold containing all events. There is no metaphysically privileged "now" flowing forward; the flow of time is a feature of consciousness, not of physics.

Popularly associated with determinism and fatalism (the future is "already written"). [[eric-wargo]] argues this association with nihilism is a misreading — the block universe is *required* for precognition to be possible and for evolution toward richer consciousness to be meaningful.

## Relevance to Precognition

In an open, indeterminate future, backward information flow carries no information — there is no fixed future state to signal from. In a block universe:

- Future events exist and are fixed (in the same sense past events are fixed)
- Retrocausal signals from future states are in principle transmissible
- The [[self-fulfilling-prophecy-mechanics]] structure is physically necessary — the signal cannot change history, only be part of it

Wargo: "Events in the future are fixed in the same way events in the past are fixed. Any kind of precognition is a form of informational time travel — and it cannot change history."

## No Paradox: The 1980s Physics Result

Physicists modeling wormholes and closed timelike curves in the 1980s — stimulated by work on traversable wormholes by Kip Thorne and colleagues — found mathematically that paradox-generating interventions in closed timelike curves are **self-correcting**. A billiard ball sent through a wormhole to deflect itself away from the entrance will always be deflected back into the entrance. The universe is self-consistent.

Result: time travel and retrocausation **do not lead to grandfather paradoxes**. They produce consistent loops in which the time traveler was always part of the backstory. The grandfather paradox is based on faulty reasoning about what time travel implies.

## Free Will in a Block Universe

The standard objection: if the future is fixed, free will is an illusion. Wargo's response:

1. The block universe does not require that you *know* your future — only that it exists
2. Your choices are real causal events; they are part of the block
3. The feeling of freedom is real; it is part of the causal chain that produces the fixed future
4. The precognitive signals you receive are symbolic and oblique precisely *because* free will must be preserved for the self-consistent loop to close — you must make the choice that produces the outcome without consciously knowing you are doing so

This is the ironic logic Wargo calls the "edlic" (Oedipal) structure of fate: we fulfill prophecies precisely by making free choices in response to oblique signals we misinterpret.

## Relationship to Many-Worlds / Everett

Wargo explicitly rejects the many-worlds / alternate timelines model. His objections:

- Everettian many-worlds implies practically *infinite* parallel universes (not just "accident" vs. "no accident" but infinite variations) — making "I saw an alternate timeline" scientifically untestable
- The alternate timeline framing is a "cop-out" that avoids confronting the evidence for genuine precognition of a *fixed* future
- Block universe + retrocausation is the simpler, more testable model

He acknowledges the many-worlds interpretation is mathematically coherent but finds it phenomenologically uninteresting and scientifically unproductive.

## Implication for Evolutionary Direction

Wargo: an artist reaching forward into their future for creative inspiration is drawing from a future that exists and is accessible. If artistic precognition is real, the future it accesses is not empty — it is rich with information about evolved consciousness. This is what makes the block universe *not nihilistic*: the fixed future is not a prison but a resource.
