# Extramission Hypothesis

**Extramission** is the hypothesis that the human eye does not only receive light (intromission) but also **emits** measurable electromagnetic signals. This contrasts with the dominant scientific model (intromission only, dating to John Locke) in which no physical signal exits the eye.

## The Intromission / Extramission Debate

- **Intromission** (accepted): light enters the eye → hits retina → processed by visual cortex. This is how human vision works; no controversy.
- **Extramission** (contested): something also exits the eye — historically attributed to spiritual "fire" in ancient and medieval thought, now reframed by [[colin-ross]] as measurable ELF electromagnetic signals.

Extramission is "not allowed" in mainstream academia, per Ross — "going all the way back to John Locke, it's just forbidden."

## Ross's Experimental Evidence

[[colin-ross]] designed and conducted an experiment:

1. A **floating electrode** (no body contact) was suspended in front of his eye inside electromagnetic-insulated ski goggles (tin foil + copper mesh from Target)
2. A second **control electrode** was placed in open space nearby
3. Standard EEG leads (20 frontal leads) were attached to his head
4. **Result**: the floating electrode in front of the eye captured a **physiologically consistent signal** — similar in character to the two frontal-lobe EEG leads — while the control electrode showed only background noise

This was published in a **peer-reviewed electrical engineering journal**.

## The Physics Case

Ross's physical argument:
- Every atom emits electromagnetic fields (undisputed basic physics)
- The **optic nerve** is a large electrical cable exiting the brain directly through the skull — unlike other brain regions where signals must propagate through bone
- **ELF (extremely low frequency)** electromagnetic waves propagate for **thousands of kilometers** without normal inverse-square-law decay — established physics, published without controversy
- Therefore: ELF brain waves exiting through the optic nerve propagate far enough to account for the sense of being stared at, the evil eye phenomenon (universal cross-cultural), and other scopesthesia effects

## Scopesthesia — The Sense of Being Stared At

[[rupert-sheldrake]] has conducted extensive research on **scopesthesia** (the scientific term for the sense of being stared at). Ross cites Sheldrake's work as parallel evidence for extramission: if no signal exits the eye, scopesthesia has no physical mechanism. If ELF signals exit, scopesthesia is predictable.

Cross-cultural evidence: the "jettature" (evil eye practitioners) in Italy; evil eye beliefs across all cultures; Ross's personal experiences sensing being stared at (and staring) across distances.

## Sheldrake's Transactional QM Mechanism (wT5-hXWIkzM)

In [[wT5-hXWIkzM]], [[rupert-sheldrake]] provides the most specific physical mechanism for extramission in the KB to date: the **transactional interpretation of quantum mechanics** (John Cramer; developed further by Ruth Kastner). The standard quantum mechanical description of light absorption implies a single photon traveling from source to eye. The transactional interpretation adds a **reverse "anti-photon"** (a wave moving from the future toward the past) emitted by the absorbing atom at the moment of absorption. The two waves form a **standing wave** — a physical link between the seer and the seen.

Under this model:
- The standing wave physically overlaps with the object being viewed (the seer is "touching" the object via this link)
- The mental projection of an image toward the object is not metaphor but a description of the reverse wave
- If you look at someone from behind, your projections (via reverse photons) overlap with their back, providing a physical substrate for scopaesthesia

This is distinct from Colin Ross's ELF extramission model (which posits direct emission of ELF brain waves), but the two are not mutually exclusive. The transactional interpretation is mathematically rigorous (part of quantum electrodynamics) and was developed by Feynman and Wheeler in 1945 and formalized by Cramer.
