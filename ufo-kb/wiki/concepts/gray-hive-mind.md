# Gray Hive Mind

According to [[steve-coburn]] — a class-2 experiencer and implant analyst — the gray NHI species operates as a **collective networked consciousness**. Every experience had by any member of their society is recorded and accessible to the whole. Coburn compares it to the Borg from Star Trek, with the caveat that grays retain somewhat more individuality.

## Mechanism

Class-2 experiencers receive a **brain implant** (standard procedure for this tier of the alien program) that connects them to the gray hive mind. The implant enables NHI entities to:
- Access the experiencer's **sensory input** in real time (sight, hearing)
- Access the experiencer's **thoughts** in real time
- Monitor the experiencer's physiological state (blood sugar, body temperature, etc.)

This effectively turns each class-2 experiencer into a **distributed sensing node** for NHI intelligence — a walking, living bug, tuned to human society.

Coburn notes a secondary implication: by implanting researchers, scientists, and intellectuals who become obsessed with studying NHI phenomena, the grays are potentially conducting **distributed research through human proxies** — getting humans to investigate and compile knowledge that feeds back to NHI via the brain implant.

## Scale

- Approximately **1 in 1,000** Americans are class-2 experiencers (~350,000 total)
- Scale is likely similar globally
- If implantation priorities track access to high-value information (military, intelligence, science, governance), the grays may have near-complete situational awareness of human society's decision-making processes

## Handler Structure

Each class-2 experiencer is assigned a **handler** — a specific NHI individual responsible for their case. Coburn's handler appears to be a taller gray (~4.5 ft, the [[small-grays]] scientist-engineer type). Coburn believes his handler monitors his conversations in real time through the brain implant.

## Individuality

Not a pure hive mind — individual NHI retain some identity and agency. But all experiences are shared and recorded collectively. The collective interest dominates individual concern; the grays "don't care about individuals, they care about what's best for the group."
