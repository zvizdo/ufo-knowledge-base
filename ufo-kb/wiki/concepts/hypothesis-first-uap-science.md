# Hypothesis-First UAP Science

## The Claim

[[beatriz-villarroel]] argues that the dominant methodology in civilian UAP research — the **exclusion principle** — is scientifically inadequate. The exclusion principle works by cataloguing all observations, filtering out everything identifiable (drones, birds, satellites, weather), and declaring what remains as UAP. Villarroel's objection: this produces a barely falsifiable hypothesis because you can "always go on forever" expanding the list of exclusions without ever reaching a positive claim.

Her proposed alternative: start with a **concrete prior hypothesis** — "I am looking for a flying saucer" — and design the experiment around the predicted signatures of that specific object.

## The Flying Saucer Argument

Villarroel uses "flying saucer" (rather than the broader "UAP") deliberately:

> "When you have flying saucer, you directly can see the flying saucer in your head. But when you see it in your imagination, you can imagine how it would look in your data, right?"

The concreteness of the image enables:
1. **Experimental design**: What instruments, wavelengths, and baselines would detect a flying saucer?
2. **Failure mode analysis**: In what conditions would the experiment miss a flying saucer? What would a false positive look like?
3. **Falsifiability**: The experiment can return a negative result that actually constrains the hypothesis.

She applies this to [[exoprobe]] — specifically looking for solar reflections and emissions consistent with the geometry of a mirror-like disk (per [[pre-sputnik-uap-transients]] findings), rather than "anything anomalous."

## Critique of UAP as Category

Villarroel: *"I don't like the definition of UAP because it fits almost everything now. It's so broad."*

The broader the search category, the more potential false-positive sources exist and the less any finding constrains the hypothesis. This makes UAP programs vulnerable to the criticism that they can never produce conclusive results — providing institutional cover for indefinite inconclusion.

## Application to Specific Search Modalities

- **Metallic orbs**: Design a detection system for orb-specific reflective signatures
- **Flying saucers**: Design for the flat, mirror-like flash profile identified in VASCO data
- **Infrared caution**: Villarroel is skeptical of the common claim that UAP are "more visible in infrared" — she suspects this reflects human inexperience classifying IR objects rather than genuine signature enhancement
