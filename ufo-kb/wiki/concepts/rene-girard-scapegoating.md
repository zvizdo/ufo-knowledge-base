# René Girard's Scapegoat Mechanism

French literary theorist and anthropologist [[rene-girard]] (1923–2015) advanced two coupled theses that recur in UAP-religion-convergence discourse:

1. **Mimetic desire** — human desire is not autochthonous; it is *learned by imitation* of others. This generates rivalry, since two imitators converge on the same desired object.
2. **The scapegoat mechanism** — when mimetic rivalry generalizes into a community-wide crisis, the conflict is resolved by collectively externalizing it onto a single victim whose sacrifice restores order. Girard reads myth, ritual, and the historical Crucifixion as variations on this single substrate, with Christianity uniquely *exposing* (rather than concealing) the mechanism.

In UFO-as-new-religion discourse, Girard's framework is invoked to explain:
- Why ridicule of UAP witnesses follows a sacrificial-victim pattern (the witness as scapegoat for institutional anxiety).
- Why disclosure-revelation is structurally analogous to revelatory religious events that generate, then displace, social conflict.
- Why the field's recurring "messengers" (Bledsoe, Strieber, contactees) end up bearing community projection.
