# Interstellar Probe Hypothesis

The proposition — developed most systematically by [[avi-loeb]] — that the most physically plausible mode of interstellar travel for any advanced civilization is not biological spaceflight but technological probes carrying artificial intelligence. Biological beings cannot survive interstellar transit exposed to cosmic radiation. Equipment can.

## Loeb's Formulation

> "The way I see our future in space is by sending our technological kids — equipment with artificial intelligence. If we can imagine sending AI systems that currently drive cars into space as AI astronauts, then maybe other civilizations have done that already." — Avi Loeb, [[_bM9eAtCOGw]]

The argument has two components:

**1. Biological impossibility of interstellar transit.** Cosmic radiation at interstellar distances would destroy biological organisms. Mars colonization (a comparatively trivial distance) already requires solving the absence of Earth's magnetic shielding. Interstellar travel for living creatures is orders of magnitude harder. This is not a speed problem but a biology-in-space problem.

**2. AI as civilizational heir.** Homo sapiens has been physiologically the same for ~200,000 years. In the last 5% of that time (10,000 years), it developed the agricultural revolution and is now developing AI. The trajectory from biological to technological intelligence is a universal civilizational arc. Once a civilization creates AI sophisticated enough to navigate, it becomes the obvious medium for interstellar expansion. Equipment doesn't age, doesn't need food, and handles cosmic rays differently than biology.

## AI as Better Classifier

Loeb extends this to UAP analysis: AI will not be attached to its ego. A human scientist who has built a career on "all objects in the solar system are rocks" cannot easily update to "this is equipment." An AI system would say: "It doesn't look like any rock we've seen before — therefore maybe it's a piece of equipment produced by someone else." This is his partial response to the caveman-and-cellphone problem. See [[_bM9eAtCOGw]] for the full argument.

## Interstellar Archaeological Trash

A corollary: if civilizations have been sending probes for billions of years, interstellar space should contain detectable artifacts — non-functional, drifting. Loeb's term: "plastic bottles released into the ocean of interstellar space." Over billions of years, this debris accumulates. Searching for anomalous interstellar objects (like [[2017-oumuamua]]) is therefore a form of cosmic archaeology.

The Galileo Project's second research track — interstellar object characterization — is operationally instantiated on this hypothesis. If ʻOumuamua is technological trash (a defunct lightsail), finding more such objects and studying them physically would be evidence of past civilizations without requiring any living NHI to be present.

## Relation to Metaverse Fermi Resolution

Loeb explicitly connects this to the [[fermi-paradox]]: advanced civilizations that retreated into virtual reality still obey the second law of thermodynamics — they still produce entropy/waste that escapes into physical space. Archaeological trash from such civilizations would be detectable. Even a civilization that had "closed itself in a cocoon" of virtual reality would leave an interstellar junk record. This keeps the probe hypothesis viable even under the metaverse scenario.

## Contrast with Contact via Biology

This hypothesis contrasts with the "biological NHI physically present" model (cf. [[nhi-biologics]], crash-retrieval programs). Loeb does not dismiss crash-retrieval claims but his prior is that any genuine NHI hardware would be automated/AI-controlled equipment rather than crewed. If the government has recovered hardware, that hardware is more likely to be an unmanned probe than a vessel with biological occupants.
