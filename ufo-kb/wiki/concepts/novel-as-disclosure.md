# Novel as Disclosure

## The tactic

A current or former cleared individual ([[jake-barber]], [[tom-delonge]]) writes a novel that intentionally embeds classified UFO / NHI content. They submit the manuscript through their host government's pre-publication review system ([[dopsr]] in the US case). Two outcomes are useful:

1. **Pass.** The material is released as "fiction" but contains true detail — readers in the know recognize it, the public absorbs it as imagination, the legal exposure is minimal.
2. **Redaction.** The redaction itself authenticates the content. If the Pentagon redacts a fiction novel, the fiction was real enough to redact.

By drip-feeding chapters and varying the level of detail, an author can also use the review system *backwards* — as a real-time classification map of what is actually classified, then re-incorporate that information through paraphrase or fictional reframing.

## Precedent

- [[tom-delonge]] + Peter Levenda — *[[secret-machines]]* — first canonical public deployment of the tactic; advised by [[charles-chase]], [[robert-weiss]], [[james-ryder]], and AFRL Commander Neil McCaslin.
- [[jake-barber]] (with the pen-name [[alva-douglas]] ghost writer) — *[[sentinels-of-ether]]* — submitted in 20-page chunks to [[dopsr]]; first chunk took six months and returned seven pages of redactions on 10% of the manuscript.

## Why it works

- The fiction wrapper provides plausible deniability for the author.
- The review system creates a *signed* trail of redactions that operates as covert authentication.
- "Self-stigmatizing" — "the best-kept secrets don't need to be classified. They're just too insane to be taken seriously."
