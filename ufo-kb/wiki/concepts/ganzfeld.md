# Ganzfeld

A parapsychological experimental protocol for testing telepathy (and related psi phenomena) by eliminating sensory noise — creating conditions in which weak signals can be detected against a clear background. "Ganzfeld" is German for "total field" — uniform, unvarying sensory stimulation.

**Protocol**: The receiver wears halved translucent ping-pong balls over their eyes (creating uniform diffuse light) and listens to white noise through headphones. In this state, the brain detects no change in the sensory environment, stops filtering the white noise, and enters a relaxed, internally-focused state. The sender is physically isolated and attempts to "send" a target image (randomly selected from a pool). The receiver describes mental imagery; after the session, the receiver views four images and selects which matches their impressions.

**Statistical results**: Meta-analyses of ganzfeld experiments (most comprehensively by Honorton in 1994 and subsequent analysts) find hit rates of approximately 32–35% against a theoretical 25% chance baseline — a statistically significant effect across hundreds of experiments. The debate over methodological adequacy (randomization, blinding, selective reporting) continued through the 1990s with the "autoganzfeld" (fully automated) series.

**KB relevance**: In [[synesthesia-remote-viewing]], the ganzfeld mechanism is described as working by **eliminating entropy** — the brain stops processing predictable stimulation and can detect weaker signals. This is compared to remote viewing's altered state protocol: both achieve psi-conducive states by reducing sensory noise rather than by increasing concentration. The ganzfeld results constitute one of the few genuinely replicated parapsychological effects and are cited in credibility arguments for [[remote-viewing]].
