# Landing Trace Evidence

Landing trace evidence refers to physical marks and anomalies documented at sites where UAP are reported to have landed or hovered close to the ground. These include compacted or depressed soil, burned or desiccated vegetation, elevated radiation readings, soil with anomalous mineral crystallization, and broken or twisted branches at consistent heights. Unlike eyewitness testimony, landing traces provide physical evidence that can be analyzed independently of the witness.

**Ted Phillips catalog**: American researcher Ted Phillips compiled the most comprehensive catalog of landing trace cases, documenting over 5,000 cases from approximately 90 countries. Phillips began systematic collection in 1968 and collaborated with J. Allen Hynek. His catalog represents the largest database of UAP physical trace evidence ever assembled.

**Trans-en-Provence (1981)**: The most scientifically rigorous analysis of landing traces was conducted on the Trans-en-Provence case (January 8, 1981, Var, France), where a witness reported a disc-shaped object landing briefly on his property. The French national laboratory GEPAN/SEPRA conducted a multi-year study of the site, analyzing the soil and vegetation. Results showed: anomalous soil compaction, measurably different chlorophyll content in vegetation at the impact site vs. control samples at varying distances, and other chemical anomalies. The GEPAN report is one of the most scientifically credible in the UAP literature.

**Voronezh landing (1989)**: The [[1989-voronezh-landing]] in the Soviet Union produced a landing trace site investigated by Soviet Academy of Sciences researchers, who reported unusual radiation readings and soil anomalies at the reported landing site.

**Evidentiary value**: Landing traces are the most valuable form of UAP physical evidence because they are place-specific, can be compared against control samples, and are not dependent on the reliability of witness memory. However, the chain of custody between the sighting and the scientific analysis is often broken, limiting conclusiveness.
