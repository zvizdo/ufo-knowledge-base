# Ancient Mega-Engineering

The body of monumental ancient construction that poses unsolved engineering problems: structures built to tolerances of fractions of millimeters, using stones weighing hundreds of tons, assembled without mortar, positioned with astronomical precision, and in some cases distributed globally with near-identical design principles before any known cross-cultural contact.

Canonical examples in the KB:
- The [[great-pyramid]] of Giza: aligned to true north within 3/60 of a degree, contains ~2.3 million blocks, original casing stones fitted with 0.02mm gap tolerances
- Sacsayhuamán, Peru: interlocking polygonal stone blocks of up to 200 tons, multi-sided with no two stones identical, no mortar
- Puma Punku, Bolivia: H-shaped andesite blocks machined with vitrified surfaces, precise drill holes, and right-angle cuts
- [[gobekli-tepe]]: 12,000-year-old stone circles requiring coordinated labor from a supposedly pre-agricultural society

The unresolved question is mechanism: how were stones of this weight moved, lifted, and placed with such precision using only copper tools, wood, and manpower? Conventional explanations involve ramps, sledges, and large labor pools, but do not fully account for the observed tolerances or the choice of extremely hard stone. Alternative theories invoke lost technology — acoustic levitation, electrogravitic effects (per [[thomas-townsend-brown]] lineage), or NHI assistance.

In the KB, ancient mega-engineering is the empirical puzzle that the [[lost-civilization-hypothesis]] and [[catastrophism]] thesis are proposed to explain.
