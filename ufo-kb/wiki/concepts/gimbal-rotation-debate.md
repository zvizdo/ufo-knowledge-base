# Gimbal Rotation Debate

The central technical dispute in modern UAP video analysis: whether the smooth, sustained rotation of the object in the [[2015-gimbal]] video is (a) a physical property of a real anomalous craft, or (b) an artifact of the FLIR sensor system.

## Claim: Rotation is a Camera Artifact (Mick West)

[[mick-west]] argues the rotation results from either:
1. An optical glare phenomenon occurring before the sensor — the object is a distant jet, and the apparent shape and rotation are a sensor artefact of how bright IR sources are processed
2. A digital de-rotation algorithm in the FLIR pod (described in a FLIR Systems patent) that compensates for aircraft bank — as the aircraft banks to follow the object, the de-rotation system counter-rotates the image, producing apparent rotation of the object

Supporting evidence cited by West:
- The Chilean Navy video (a similar-looking case) was solved as a distant plane with exhaust
- The object looks different in white-hot vs. black-hot modes, suggesting it's not a fixed physical shape
- One infrared expert (cited by Merrick) also mentioned it resembled an SR-71 — suggesting the expert's "first impression" assessment was imprecise

## Claim: Rotation is Physical (Marik von Rennenkampff / Pro-Anomaly Position)

[[marik-von-rennenkampff]] argues the rotation is genuine physical motion of a real object, citing:
1. Air-to-air infrared experts called the rotation "bizarre" and said they had "never seen anything like this" — rejecting both the glare and blooming explanations
2. The object grows ~15% larger as the F/A-18 closes distance, consistent with real perspective change
3. Visual similarity to the [[2008-iraq-gimbal]] video — same morphology appearing in a completely different sensor system 7 years earlier
4. Correct technical term is "blooming" (not "glare"), and blooming does not produce rotation
5. [[aaro]] has not officially endorsed West's explanation despite the video being known for years

## The Expert Opinion Dispute

Both sides invoke expert opinions. The dispute has meta-level complications:
- The experts were contacted by Merrick and provided preliminary impressions, not formal assessments
- They apparently did not want their communications shared publicly
- One may have said West was "right but for the wrong reason" — both sides dispute this characterization
- The experts' SR-71 comparison, cited by West as evidence of imprecision, was acknowledged by Merrick as incorrect but framed as a side comment, not the substantive finding

## Current Status

As of late 2024, [[aaro]] has not publicly ruled on the Gimbal video. The [[2015-gimbal]] video remains in the "unresolved" category by the US government's own accounting.

> ⚠ **Conflict**: West claims expert opinion tentatively supported his position; Merrick claims the same experts explicitly rejected West's position. The expert communications have not been publicly released in full.
