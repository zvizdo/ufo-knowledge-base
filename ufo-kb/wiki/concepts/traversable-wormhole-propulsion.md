# Traversable Wormhole Propulsion

## Core Concept

A traversable wormhole is a theoretical spacetime topology that would allow passage between two distant points in spacetime — potentially enabling faster-than-light travel or interstellar displacement without requiring a vessel to physically cross the intervening space. Unlike a Schwarzschild (black hole) wormhole, a traversable wormhole would remain open long enough for matter to pass through.

The concept was formalized by Kip Thorne and colleagues in 1988 (the Morris-Thorne wormhole), and requires **exotic matter** with negative energy density to hold the throat open against gravitational collapse.

## Eric Davis's Research

[[eric-davis]] has published peer-reviewed work on traversable wormholes and **metric engineering** — the engineering of spacetime geometry to produce desired propulsion effects. In [[xnxasfyHtfo]], Davis states:

> "You're going to create a wormhole on demand… You should be able to. That's what my research showed."

Davis frames this within the analogy that General Relativity is to the true underlying physics as Maxwell's equations are to quantum mechanics — an effective theory waiting to be superseded. Just as quantum mechanics produced phenomena (photoelectric effect, etc.) that Maxwell's equations couldn't explain, the correct physics beyond GR would enable metric engineering that GR merely points toward.

This framing directly mirrors [[eric-weinstein]]'s Geometric Unity argument: GR is a limiting case of something broader, and the FTL constraints are GR-level constraints that do not apply in the broader framework.

## Relationship to UAP Propulsion

The traversable wormhole framework is relevant to UAP observed behaviors — specifically:
- Instantaneous or near-instantaneous displacement between distant points
- Apparent materialization/dematerialization (entry/exit from a wormhole mouth)
- Physics that defies classical spacetime constraints (no inertia, no sonic boom, no aerodynamic heating)

Davis's work at [[earthtech]] includes co-authoring an AIAA survey paper on warp drives with [[hal-puthoff]]; these represent the publicly visible portion of metric engineering research whose classified counterparts may have been developed via the [[crash-retrieval-research]] program.
