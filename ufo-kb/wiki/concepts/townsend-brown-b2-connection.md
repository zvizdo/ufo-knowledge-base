# Townsend Brown B2 Connection

**Claim**: [[thomas-townsend-brown]]'s electrogravitics work — specifically the Biefeld-Brown effect using charged leading/trailing edges — was incorporated into the design of the B2 Spirit stealth bomber, produced by Northrop.

## Who Makes This Claim

- **Bill Gunston** (Britain's premier aerospace journalist, writing in *Air International* magazine, 2000): Stated he was "very sure that Towns and Brown's work made it into the B2 stealth bomber" and noted the specific mechanism: leading edge charged to millions of volts positive, trailing edge to millions of volts negative — the precise negative-to-positive thrust pattern of Brown's gravitator experiments.
- **A 1968 Northrop paper** on "electro-aerodynamics" (Brown's field) allegedly exists before "mysteriously disappearing."
- The name "B2" has been interpreted by some researchers as a reference to "Byfield Brown" (Brown's capacitor experiments) — though this is unverified.

## Supporting Evidence

- Brown was identified by the FBI in a declassified 1943 document as the **Navy's top radar expert** — establishing him as a credentialed scientist, not a fringe inventor
- His work is consistent with known principles: asymmetric capacitor thrust in near-vacuum has been demonstrated in small-scale experiments
- Gunston's status as Britain's most respected aerospace journalist gives his claim institutional weight

## Limiting Factors

- The claim is Gunston's personal assessment based on his knowledge of the technical principles; no declassified documentation directly connects B2 design to Brown's patents
- The Northrop electro-aerodynamics paper allegedly disappeared, removing the most direct supporting document
- Stealth bombers are classified enough that absence of public corroboration is not dispositive
