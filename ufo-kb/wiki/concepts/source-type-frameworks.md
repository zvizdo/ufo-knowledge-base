# Source-Type Frameworks

**Type**: methodology  
**Category**: credibility-frameworks

A core analytical lens for this KB: before evaluating the content of any claim, evaluate the *type* of source making it. Different source types have systematically different strengths, weaknesses, and contamination modes.

## The Five Source Types

### 1. Official
Government documents, congressional testimony, declassified files, FOIA releases, agency press statements.

**Strengths**: Institutional accountability; document trail; harder to fabricate without detection.  
**Weaknesses**: Strategic omission; designed-for-disclosure releases may cherry-pick; classification itself is a form of source manipulation.  
**Examples**: [[twining-memo]], [[estimate-of-the-situation]], [[pentacle-memo]], AARO reports.

### 2. Leaked
Documents obtained without authorization: whistleblower dumps, anonymous drops, alleged insider photographs.

**Strengths**: May reveal what Official sources conceal.  
**Weaknesses**: Provenance is inherently uncertain; disinformation campaigns use leaks as a preferred vector; forensic document analysis is the only mitigation.  
**Examples**: [[majestic-12]] documents (disputed authenticity), [[wilson-davis-memo]], [[kettle-footage]].

### 3. Witness
Firsthand testimony from individuals with direct access to the phenomenon or to classified programs.

**Strengths**: Eyewitness specificity; hard to fabricate consistent sensory detail across multiple independent witnesses.  
**Weaknesses**: Memory contamination; motivated misremembering; social contagion; intelligence assets embedded in witness communities.  
**Apply**: [[credibility-frameworks]] to assess individual witness quality; distinguish firsthand from hearsay at point of extraction.

### 4. Researcher
Secondary synthesis by journalists, academics, and investigative researchers who compile and analyze primary sources.

**Strengths**: Cross-corroboration; institutional accountability (for academics/journalists at named outlets).  
**Weaknesses**: Depend entirely on source quality; researcher bias and motivated reasoning; no independent access to classified material.  
**Examples**: [[leslie-kean]]'s *UFOs: Generals, Pilots, and Government Officials Go on Record*; [[robert-hastings]]' *UFOs and Nukes*.

### 5. Cultural
Films, fiction, podcasts, conspiracy media — sources that shape the discourse without claiming direct access to classified or firsthand information.

**Strengths**: Reveal what the culture believes; surface narrative patterns; occasionally incorporate real testimony in entertaining formats.  
**Weaknesses**: No evidentiary weight; may seed false memories in the witness community; disinformation can enter via entertainment.  
**Examples**: American Alchemy podcast ([[american-alchemy]]), *The X-Files*, *Unidentified* (History Channel).

## Weighting in This KB

- **Official + Leaked (authenticated)**: Highest weight; treated as primary evidence.
- **Witness (firsthand, corroborated)**: High weight; note corroboration count and access context.
- **Witness (hearsay)**: Medium weight; flag explicitly in frontmatter as hearsay.
- **Researcher**: Medium weight; inherit the quality of their sources.
- **Cultural**: Low evidentiary weight; high discourse-mapping value.

## Contamination Modes to Flag

- **Deliberate disinformation**: Intelligence agencies have documented history of injecting false UAP stories (Doty/AFOSI operations; [[paul-benowitz]] case).
- **Social contagion**: Later witnesses adopt details from earlier published accounts.
- **Researcher laundering**: A researcher repeats a hearsay claim in a published book; it then gets cited as "documented" by subsequent researchers.
