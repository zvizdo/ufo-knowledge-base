# UAP National Security Threat

The "UAP national security threat" framing positions unidentified aerial phenomena as national security concerns, enabling formal military and intelligence investigation without requiring commitment to any specific explanation.

**Adversary hypothesis**: The primary stated national security concern is that UAP may be adversary (particularly Chinese or Russian) advanced reconnaissance or weapons systems. This framing justified the 2020 Senate Intelligence Committee UAP provisions, the [[aaro]] establishment, and related legislative activity. If adversary forces have achieved electromagnetic propulsion or hypersonic maneuvering aircraft operating in US airspace over carrier groups, that would be a critical intelligence failure.

**Non-human intelligence framing**: The secondary — and politically more sensitive — national security framing involves NHI operating in US airspace with unknown capabilities and intentions. [[david-grusch]] and others frame legacy UAP programs as national security classified specifically because of what has been recovered, not adversary technology. In this framing, the national security concern is existential: entities with far superior technology interacting with humanity without any diplomatic framework.

**[[battle-space-preparation]] hypothesis**: A specific national security claim in the KB — that prolonged UAP presence near nuclear sites, carrier groups, and military installations constitutes systematic reconnaissance of strategic capabilities — the "battle space preparation" pattern.

**Political utility**: The national security framing has proven politically useful because it bypasses the stigma of "UFOs" — it reframes the question as an intelligence gap problem, not a paranormal problem, making it discussable in serious policy contexts.
