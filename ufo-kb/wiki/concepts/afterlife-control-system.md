# Afterlife Control System

Robert Monroe's term for his model of the post-death experience as a managed system — not a realm of liberation but a continuation of the [[loosh]]-generation cycle. In Monroe's out-of-body research, he encountered what he interpreted as a non-human control structure that manages the reincarnation process to maximize loosh production.

**The model**: Physical life generates loosh through emotional experience (love, suffering, fear, joy). Death does not end this cycle; the "afterlife" as encountered in OBE/NDE states is itself a staging area where souls are processed and recycled back into physical incarnation. The light at the end of the tunnel, reunion with deceased relatives, life review — Monroe interpreted these not as genuine spiritual events but as induction mechanisms that maintain the soul's willingness to return to embodied experience.

**Control mechanism**: The afterlife control system, in Monroe's framework, involves a hierarchy of non-human entities who manage the system. Some entities are themselves "harvested" — aware of the control structure and working within it. Others (including some reported NHI) may be the controlling intelligences or may also be subject to a higher control structure.

**KB implications**: This framework extends beyond Monroe to inform several KB concepts: [[nhi-as-information-source]] (what NHI-channeled entities report about post-death states), [[loosh]] (the energy commodity the system produces), and the broader question of whether human consciousness is being managed rather than evolved by its contact with NHI.

**Epistemics**: Monroe's model derives from his out-of-body experiences and those of Monroe Institute subjects. It is not empirically testable in standard scientific terms. It is treated in the KB as a first-person phenomenological report by a credentialed researcher who took the experiences seriously, not as established fact.
