# Limited Hangout

A **limited hangout** is a strategy in which an intelligence agency or institution releases a controlled subset of damaging information in order to:
1. Appear cooperative and transparent
2. Satisfy the immediate demand for accountability
3. Redirect investigation away from the larger undisclosed truth

[[colin-ross]] notes this is "actually the CIA's term" — a documented operational concept used internally, not an external criticism.

## Application to MK Ultra

The canonical limited-hangout example in this KB:

[[richard-helms]] and [[sidney-gottlieb]] ordered the MK Ultra files destroyed in 1973 before the Church Committee investigations. However, **seven boxes of documents were found in a storage facility** — supposedly overlooked in the destruction sweep. These 7 boxes (~15,000 pages) became the basis for the entire public and Congressional understanding of MK Ultra.

Ross's analysis: the 7 boxes are almost certainly the **curated limited hangout** — the amount of information that would satisfy investigators and journalists without revealing the full scope of the program. The box contents were damaging enough to appear authentic, but were selected to avoid disclosing the most sensitive operational achievements (including, potentially, the Manchurian Candidate successes [[jolly-west]] reported to CIA in 1955 — a paragraph redacted from the Congressional version of the report).

## Broader Applications

The limited hangout pattern appears throughout this KB:

- **UAP disclosure**: The AARO/UAPTF process, the Grusch DOPSR-cleared testimony, and the Schumer-Rounds amendment's subsequent gutting are consistent with a limited hangout — releasing enough to satisfy public pressure while protecting the core program
- **JFK assassination**: The Warren Commission conclusions as a limited hangout; the HSCA's partial contradictions as a second-tier hangout
- **Korean War brainwashing cover story**: The CIA claimed Bluebird was a reaction to Chinese techniques; this was a limited hangout framing that concealed the offensive/preexisting nature of the program
