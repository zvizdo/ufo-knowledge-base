# Closed Timelike Curves

A **closed timelike curve (CTC)** is a worldline in spacetime that loops back on itself — meeting itself in its own past. In general relativity, several exact solutions admit CTCs:
- **Gödel's rotating universe** (1949)
- **Kerr black hole** interior (rotating BH; CTCs in the inner ergoregion)
- **Tipler cylinder** (van Stockum / Tipler 1974) — see [[tipler-cylinder]]
- **Traversable wormholes** with appropriate stress-energy
- **Alcubierre-class warp metrics** with closed configurations — see [[alcubierre-warp-drive]]

CTCs sit at the formal heart of the [[time-travel-uap]] / [[future-humans-hypothesis]] argument: they are not exotic add-ons to GR; they are *consequences* of certain stress-energy distributions that GR allows. The empirical question is whether such distributions are physically realizable.

The standard counterargument, due to Hawking, is the **Chronology Protection Conjecture** — that quantum-field-theoretic vacuum fluctuations diverge near a would-be CTC and prevent its formation. This remains a conjecture, not a theorem.
