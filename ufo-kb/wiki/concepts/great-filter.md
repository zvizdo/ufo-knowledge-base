# Great Filter

The Great Filter is a concept introduced by economist Robin Hanson in 1998 to explain the Fermi Paradox — the apparent absence of extraterrestrial civilizations despite the high probability of their existence given the age and size of the universe. The argument: there must be some improbable step (or series of steps) on the path from simple chemistry to spacefaring civilization, and we have not yet observed any civilization that has passed it.

**Structure of the argument**: The observable universe contains hundreds of billions of galaxies, each with hundreds of billions of stars, many with planets. The universe is 13.8 billion years old. If life commonly emerges and civilization is a predictable outcome of life, the universe should be visibly teeming with civilizations, including ones billions of years old. It is not. Therefore some step in the chain from chemistry → life → complex life → intelligence → civilization → spacefaring must be improbably rare.

**Past vs. future filter**: The critical question is the location of the filter relative to humanity:
- **Past filter**: If the improbable step is already behind us (e.g., the origin of life, the evolution of the eukaryotic cell, the evolution of multicellular organisms, the evolution of language/intelligence), then we may be rare survivors, and our future is potentially open.
- **Future filter**: If the improbable step lies ahead — civilizations reliably destroy themselves through nuclear war, engineered pandemics, runaway AI, or ecological collapse — then the Fermi silence is a warning about our own future.

**UAP connection**: In the [[fermi-paradox]] context, confirmed extraterrestrial contact would have profound Great Filter implications. If NHI are real and numerous, it suggests the Great Filter (if any) is behind us — but raises the question of why spacefaring civilizations are not more visible. The [[dark-forest]] hypothesis and the [[postbiological-civilization]] model offer alternative framings.

**Fermi link**: [[enrico-fermi]]'s famous lunch-table question ("Where is everybody?") is the empirical puzzle the Great Filter attempts to resolve.
