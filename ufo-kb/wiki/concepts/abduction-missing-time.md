# Missing Time

The core temporal anomaly of the alien abduction experience: witnesses in a UAP encounter find, upon returning to normal awareness, that significantly more time has elapsed than their conscious experience accounts for — typically between one and several hours.

**Classic cases**: The [[1961-betty-barney-hill-abduction]] is the first formally documented missing-time case: Betty and Barney Hill drove from their encounter point and arrived home hours later than the drive should have taken, with neither able to account for the missing interval. The [[2007-bledsoe-cape-fear]] incident involved Chris Bledsoe and his companions — 4 hours unaccounted for in a UAP encounter at Cape Fear, North Carolina. The recovery of missing-time memories through the O'Connell protocol (cited in [[bM18PJY6_Zc]]) involved "memory packing" — the gradual, ordered recovery of compressed experiential data.

**Mechanism hypotheses**:
- **Hypnotic suppression**: NHI (or program operators in human-staging scenarios) induce a hypnotic state or chemical amnesia that the witness later interprets as time loss.
- **Altered consciousness**: The encounter itself involves states of consciousness that record differently in memory — similar to deep anesthesia or dissociative states.
- **Temporal dislocation**: Some models propose the witness literally experiences a time shift — entering a different temporal reference frame and returning.
- **Memory packing**: The experiencer's mind records the experience but compresses it inaccessibly; gradual recovery is possible through regression or natural recall.

**Research implications**: Missing time is the primary motivator for hypnotic regression in abduction research, since the hypothesis is that the experience IS occurring but is inaccessible to normal recall. The validity of regression-recovered missing-time memories is heavily debated (see [[hypnotic-regression]]).
