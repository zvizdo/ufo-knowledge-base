# Ball Lightning

> *Stub. Expand with the experimental history (Kapitsa, Tuck, Cen et al.), Project Hessdalen ties, and discussion of when "ball lightning" is invoked as a skeptical UAP-explanation vs. when the UAP corpus is invoked to extend the ball-lightning corpus.*

Anomalous luminous spheres reported across multiple centuries in the meteorological / atmospheric-anomaly literature. Typical reported size 5–30 cm diameter; occasionally meter-scale. Lifetimes typically seconds to a minute. Observations cluster around thunderstorm conditions but also include clear-sky and indoor reports. Laboratory reproduction has been claimed (most famously by Cen, Yuan, & Xue 2014; earlier by Kapitsa's WWII-era proposals; later by Stenhoff and others) but no method reliably produces objects matching the full reported observational range.

## UAP Relevance

- **Skeptical-side explanation**: Routinely invoked as a candidate prosaic explanation for luminous UAP — particularly small, free-floating, locally-visible objects. The invocation is often weak because reported UAP behaviors (extended duration, controlled trajectories, multi-witness pattern formations) exceed any documented ball-lightning behavior.
- **UAP-side cross-research**: A serious sub-thread of UAP-curious physicists has pursued ball lightning as a phenomenon-of-interest with implications for UAP physics rather than as a deflection. [[james-l-tuck|James L. Tuck]] (Los Alamos, P-Division) is documented in [[doe-uap-d2-james-tuck-correspondence-1970-1976]] as actively researching ball lightning alongside Condon-Report atmospheric vortices in the early 1970s.
- **Morphology overlap**: The orb / sphere class of UAP observations near nuclear and weapons-testing sites (cf. [[tag-along-orbs]], [[1973-sary-shagan-green-uap]], [[1948-1951-jemez-mountains-green-lights]]) shares basic morphology with ball-lightning reports — but typically exceeds in scale, duration, and behavioral complexity.
