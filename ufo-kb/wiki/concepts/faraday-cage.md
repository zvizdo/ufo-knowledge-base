# Faraday Cage

A conductive enclosure that blocks external electromagnetic fields by distributing the charge around its exterior. Named after Michael Faraday, who demonstrated the principle in 1836. Commonly used in electronics, sensitive measurement equipment, and MRI machines to prevent electromagnetic interference.

In the KB, the Faraday cage appears specifically through [[andrija-puharich]]'s research documented in the [[2-getxxi670]] transcript. Puharich used Faraday cage experiments in two ways:
1. **Diagnostic**: A mental patient claiming to hear voices was placed in a Faraday cage — voices stopped. When the door was re-opened, voices returned. This proved the phenomenon was radio transmission through metal dental work, not mental illness.
2. **Session enhancement**: In Puharich's "Space Kids" and channeling work at his Ossining compound, sessions conducted inside a Faraday cage were dramatically more productive than sessions outside it. The entities ([[the-nine]]) themselves provided specific construction instructions (copper, particular dimensions, particular metals) at first contact.

The paradox of Faraday cage enhancement deserves attention: if the "Nine" were purely psychological phenomena, shielding from external electromagnetic noise should have no effect on them. The observed enhancement is more consistent with the Faraday cage filtering out competing signals, leaving a cleaner channel for whatever transmission or reception was occurring.
