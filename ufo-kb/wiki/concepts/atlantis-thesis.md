# Atlantis Thesis

## Core Claim

Plato's Atlantis dialogues (*Timaeus* and *Critias*, ~360 BC) preserve real historical memory of a civilization destroyed and submerged beneath the sea at approximately **9,600 BC**, not a political allegory invented by Plato.

## The Date Chain

1. [[solon-of-athens]], Athenian statesman (~600 BC), visited Egypt and spoke with priests at the Temple of Neith at Sais in the Nile Delta.
2. The priests read from hieroglyphs on the temple walls: a civilization was destroyed and submerged "9,000 years ago."
3. 9,000 years before 600 BC = **9,600 BC**.
4. [[graham-hancock]] identifies this with **meltwater pulse 1B** (~11,600 years ago / 9,600 BC) — one of the largest and most rapid sea-level-rise events at the end of the last Ice Age, raising global sea levels by several meters in decades.
5. Solon did not publish this; he passed it down through family lineage to Plato, who used it in the dialogues.

The specificity of the date — not merely "ancient times" but a precise figure derivable from Egyptian records — is central to the argument that the tradition encodes real memory rather than philosophical invention.

## The Global Flood Corroboration

Archaeologists who dismiss Atlantis typically treat it in isolation. [[graham-hancock]] argues this is methodologically unjustified: there are approximately **200 independent flood myths** from across the world (North America, South America, Near East, India, Oceania, etc.). The Atlantis tradition is not unique — it is a member of this much larger set. Many of these myths share specific structural features: a great civilization, a catastrophic flood, survivors who carry knowledge to future generations.

Dismissing Atlantis as Plato's fiction requires ignoring its embeddedness in this global tradition.

## Where Was Atlantis?

[[graham-hancock]] does not identify a single location. He argues the civilization occupied areas that were:
- Hospitable during the Ice Age
- Are now inaccessible or uninvestigated

Candidate regions: the **submerged continental shelves** (above water during the Ice Age, now underwater), the **Sahara Desert** (green and fertile during the Ice Age), and the **Amazon rainforest** (evidenced by the lidar discovery of massive geometric earthworks and urban populations).

[[randall-carlson]] has pointed to the **Azores** as possible remnant of an Atlantic landmass. The **Eye of the Sahara** (Richat Structure, Mauritania) has also been proposed.

Hancock has *not* endorsed the Eye of the Sahara hypothesis and explicitly says he has never visited.

## Mainstream Position

Egyptologists and archaeologists predominantly treat Atlantis as a Platonic invention — a political fable about how governments should not be organized, set in a fictional ancient world. To defend this position, they must explain why the priests' date (9,600 BC) happens to align with a known catastrophic sea-level-rise event — an alignment Hancock considers too precise to be coincidence.

## Plutarch's Sanas — Evidence for Real History (Matt Lroy)

[[matt-lroy]] adds a layer to the Atlantis transmission argument via [[plutarch-historian]]: Plutarch's account of the Atlantis story specifically names the Egyptian priest — **Elder Priest Sanas** at the [[temple-of-sais]]. Plato's account does not name him. Lroy argues:

1. A named, specific historical figure (Sanas) is stronger evidence for real history than Plato's anonymous "priests."
2. Plato used allegory because of [[socrates-philosopher]]' execution — he feared speaking plainly about forbidden knowledge.
3. The Temple of Sais was later destroyed, erasing the original hieroglyphic record.

Lroy also places the Atlantis event at the [[younger-dryas-impact|Younger Dryas]] / Meltwater Pulse 1B boundary (~12,000 BP), driven by a [[binary-star-companion|binary-star perihelion]] CME event rather than a cometary impact.

## Atlantic Ocean Geology (Carlson — JGE1NIGhBzw)

[[randall-carlson]] in [[JGE1NIGhBzw]] adds an oceanographic dimension to the Atlantis case:

- He has studied the **marine geology and oceanography of the Atlantic Ocean basin**
- There is "overwhelming evidence of major subsidence on the floor of the Atlantic Ocean, particularly concentrated along the Mid-Atlantic Ridge"
- The present Atlantic islands (Azores, Canaries, etc.) are the **tops of mountains** — what were peaks of a much larger landmass
- This geological record of subsidence is consistent with a significant Atlantic landmass that sank — though Carlson frames this as circumstantial corroboration, not proof

This geological framing reinforces the Azores-as-remnant hypothesis that Carlson has previously endorsed.
