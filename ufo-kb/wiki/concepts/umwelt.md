# Umwelt

The umwelt (German: "surrounding world") is a concept developed by Baltic German biologist Jakob von Uexküll (1864–1944) to describe the species-specific subjective perceptual universe. Each organism lives in a world defined entirely by what its sensory apparatus can detect and what its nervous system can process — a tick's umwelt consists of warmth, butyric acid, and the texture of skin; a human's includes visible light, sound, and language.

**Radical cognitive difference**: The umwelt concept is used in [[exo-psychology]] and adjacent KB frameworks to address the problem of communicating with or understanding entities whose umwelt is radically different from the human umwelt. An NHI evolved in a different environment (deep ocean, another planet, higher dimensions, a different temporal frame) might have an umwelt so different from the human umwelt that communication requires not just language translation but sensory framework translation — understanding what aspects of reality that entity can even perceive.

**NHI umwelt problem**: If NHI perceive time non-linearly, or perceive electromagnetic fields as directly as humans perceive color, or have senses detecting phenomena humans cannot detect, then "communication" and "contact" become first-order problems of phenomenological translation, not just linguistic translation. The umwelt framework makes this problem concrete.

**KB relevance**: The umwelt concept appears in [[exo-psychology]] as part of the toolkit for thinking rigorously about the constraints on human-NHI communication — applicable whether NHI are extraterrestrial, ultraterrestrial, or interdimensional.
