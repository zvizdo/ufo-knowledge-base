# Archaeoacoustics

The study of acoustic phenomena in ancient archaeological sites. The core finding: many Neolithic and Bronze Age structures — from Newgrange and Maltese temples to Lascaux cave and Çatalhöyük — produce anomalous acoustic effects including standing wave resonance, infrasound at specific frequencies, and echo patterns that do not occur naturally in the surrounding landscape. The hypothesis is that these effects were not accidental but were engineered as components of ritual consciousness technology.

Infrasound at 18–19 Hz is below human hearing but induces feelings of unease, awe, and in some subjects, visual anomalies — effects consistent with archaic descriptions of encountering the divine or non-human. Resonance chambers that amplify specific frequencies (typically in the 110 Hz range for Neolithic chambers) alter EEG patterns toward theta-wave states associated with trance and hypnagogic imagery.
