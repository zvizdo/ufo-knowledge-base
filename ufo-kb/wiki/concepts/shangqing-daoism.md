# Shangqing Daoism

Shangqing (Supreme Clarity) Daoism is a revelatory school of Daoist practice that emerged in 4th-century China, founded on texts claimed to have been dictated by divine beings — specifically the immortal Lady Wei Huacun — to Yang Xi beginning around 364 CE. It became the dominant form of Daoist practice for several centuries.

**Origins as revealed text**: Shangqing's founding texts — the Shangqing scriptures — were explicitly claimed as received revelation rather than human composition. Yang Xi, a scribe working for a wealthy family, wrote down transmissions he received from immortal beings over several years. The texts describe specific practices for celestial ascent: visualization sequences, breathing techniques, internal alchemy, and the navigation of stellar pathways.

**Celestial ascent and stellar navigation**: Shangqing's central practices involve ascending through celestial realms to encounter divine intelligences. The Big Dipper stars serve as navigational reference points — specific stars correspond to specific celestial palaces or gatekeepers that the practitioner must pass. The structural parallel to [[merkavah-mysticism]]'s seven-palace ascent is noted by [[peter-levenda]] and others as evidence of a shared underlying contact-derived knowledge structure.

**Living teachers**: As noted in [[peter-levenda]]'s discussion, Shangqing texts are available but living teachers who can transmit the practice are extremely rare — mostly on Taiwan, outside the mainland Chinese religious establishment. This accessibility problem mirrors Merkavah's situation: the practice framework exists in texts but requires transmission.

**UAP relevance**: The Shangqing tradition is relevant to UAP consciousness research for the same reasons as Merkavah: its founding was explicitly claimed as NHI contact (divine beings transmitting knowledge), its practices are oriented toward achieving such contact again, and its phenomenological descriptions of the contact experience overlap with contemporary NHI encounter reports.
