# UAP Private Sector FOIA Exemption

The deliberate structural arrangement by which UAP investigation data is kept out of the Freedom of Information Act's reach: government programs are contracted to private aerospace companies, which then claim proprietary protections over the resulting findings. The system was apparently "set up to be that way on purpose."

## The AATIP/Bigelow Mechanism

The clearest documented example: [[aatip]] and [[aawsap]] ran "under the auspices of [[bigelow-aerospace]]" — a private company. All data gathered during the 2007–2012 investigation period at [[skinwalker-ranch]] and elsewhere is therefore:
1. **Classified** — because it was government-contracted sensitive work
2. **FOIA-exempt** — because it is held by a private company as proprietary data

The combination is uniquely powerful: the material is classified enough that government personnel can't talk about it, and private enough that FOIA requests hit a wall of proprietary exemption rather than national security exemption (which is at least a named, challengeable category).

[[brandon-fugel]] stated this explicitly in [[7W7iOt57fOo]]: the Bigelow Aerospace structure was set up deliberately to achieve this dual shield.

## Broader Pattern

[[lue-elizondo]] (and Fugel, citing Elizondo) have both indicated that UAP programs have migrated further into the private sector over time — not disappeared. The trajectory is:
- 1950s–1990s: government programs (Blue Book, AAWSAP, AATIP) at least nominally subject to some oversight
- Post-2012: research shifted to "special access programs" within aerospace corporations — Lockheed, Boeing, and others specifically named as holding UAP-area knowledge "sequestered against FOIA through proprietary labels"

The mechanism exploits the inherent tension in US information law: FOIA applies to government agencies but not to private companies. By having government money flow to private contractors who conduct the actual research, the resulting intellectual property bypasses the government's disclosure obligations entirely.

## Policy Implications

This structure is one of the core complaints of UAP disclosure advocates in Congress — including the motivation behind the Schumer-Rounds UAP amendment, which attempted to compel private contractors holding alleged UAP materials to make them available to the government for assessment. The resistance to that amendment partly came from the contractors themselves.
