# Von Neumann Probe

A **von Neumann probe** is a theoretical self-replicating spacecraft that explores the galaxy autonomously. Named after mathematician [[john-von-neumann]], who formalized the mathematics of self-replicating automata. The core concept: a single probe is launched to a star system; once there, it uses local materials to manufacture copies of itself; each copy launches to additional star systems, creating exponential coverage of the galaxy.

## Relevance to UAP

[[eric-haseltine]] cites the von Neumann probe as his preferred explanation for NHI technology, for two reasons:
1. Advanced civilizations consistently send robotic/AI infrastructure before biological life (as humans are beginning to do with the James Webb telescope, Voyager, etc.)
2. Biological life faces severe barriers to interstellar travel (distance, radiation, time, metabolism)

If NHI is visiting Earth, it is "probably technology, not biology — probably a von Neumann probe." This frames UAP as AI probes rather than piloted craft.

## Fermi Paradox Implication

The von Neumann probe hypothesis is often invoked in the Fermi paradox context: if one civilization launches even one self-replicating probe, it could cover the entire galaxy within millions of years (geologically short). The absence of obvious probes is itself a puzzle — or, alternatively, the probes are already here and UAP are evidence of them.
