# Method of Competing Hypotheses (MCH)

The **Method of Competing Hypotheses** (sometimes Analysis of Competing Hypotheses, ACH) is an intelligence community analytical framework developed to counter cognitive biases in evidence interpretation. Rather than building a case for a favored explanation, the analyst explicitly enumerates all plausible hypotheses, maps which evidence is consistent or inconsistent with each, and seeks to eliminate — not confirm — hypotheses.

## Application to UAP

[[eric-haseltine]] applies MCH as his primary analytical frame for UAP. His enumerated hypotheses for a given UAP observation include:

1. **Plasma** — natural or artificial ionized gas
2. **Conventional drones or aircraft** — misidentified human technology
3. **Classified human craft** — black programs unknown to the observer
4. **Russian Maskirovka** — deliberate deception using advanced but terrestrial technology ([[maskirovka]])
5. **Non-human terrestrial origin** — species or civilization that evolved on Earth but diverged ([[silurian-hypothesis]])
6. **NHI from outside Earth** — extraterrestrial visitation
7. **Extra-dimensional physics** — objects or effects from dimensions beyond 4D spacetime

MCH discipline requires that no hypothesis is discarded without evidence against it. Haseltine's conclusion on the [[2004-nimitz-tic-tac]]: the plasma hypothesis does not fully account for the visual signature; most mundane hypotheses are eliminated; "something real going on there."

## Significance for Credibility Assessment

MCH is significant because it is the framework of trained intelligence analysts — the same framework applied to WMD intelligence, counterterrorism threat assessment, etc. When senior IC officials apply MCH to UAP and do not eliminate NHI, the implication is methodologically significant.
