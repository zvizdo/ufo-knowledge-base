# Elite Global Governance

The elite global governance thesis holds that a relatively small transnational elite — drawn from finance, politics, intelligence, media, and technology — coordinates global policy outcomes through a nested set of semi-formal institutional structures. These range from exclusive social clubs (Bohemian Grove) to policy networks (Bilderberg Group, Council on Foreign Relations, Trilateral Commission) to public-facing international institutions (World Economic Forum). The thesis exists on a spectrum from the relatively uncontroversial (elites do network and coordinate to advance shared interests) to the conspiratorial (a single unified shadow government makes all major decisions behind democratic facades).

## Key Claims / Framings

- The Bohemian Grove, Bilderberg Group, WEF, and similar institutions function as coordination mechanisms for a transnational governing class that operates across and above national governments
- Democratic processes are managed outputs of this elite network rather than genuine expressions of popular sovereignty
- The same network that controls conventional political and economic institutions also controls access to, and information about, non-human intelligence and advanced technology (the UFO-elite governance intersection)
- The "breakaway civilization" concept posits that the innermost layer of this elite has access to technologies — including those derived from UAP reverse engineering — that have enabled them to develop a technological and informational civilization entirely separate from the one available to the public
- The UFO secrecy cabal is, in this framing, not a standalone phenomenon but a sub-component of the broader elite governance structure

**Critical framing**: [[alex-jones]] is one of the most prominent popularizers of this thesis, though his framing emphasizes the Bohemian Grove ritual dimension and the deliberate plan for global population control. More sober versions of the thesis are advanced by researchers like [[danny-sheehan]] and others in the UAP disclosure community who connect the secrecy apparatus to specific institutional structures.
