# Schumer-Rounds UAP Disclosure Amendment

Introduced by Senators Chuck Schumer (D-IL) and Mike Rounds (R-SD) in 2023 as part of the National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA), this amendment proposed a UAP Records Review Board patterned on the Assassination Records Review Board created by the JFK Records Act. The mechanism: an independent board with declassification authority to force release of UAP-related records held by government agencies and private contractors.

The amendment passed the Senate but was substantially weakened before final enactment — key provisions including contractor records access were stripped. [[chris-mellon]] and other advocates attributed the weakening to opposition from [[mike-turner]]'s House Intelligence Committee and resistance from the intelligence community's classification bureaucracy.

The amendment's fate is itself evidence in the [[disclosure-narratives]] debate: those who see controlled disclosure cite it as proof that Congress can pass reform but has it stripped by institutional actors; those skeptical of conspiracy cite normal legislative process.
