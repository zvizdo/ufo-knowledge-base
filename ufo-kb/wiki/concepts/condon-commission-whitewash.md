# Condon Commission Whitewash

**Type**: claims-thesis  
**Category**: disclosure-narratives

The claim that the [[condon-commission]] (University of Colorado UFO Study, 1966–1968) was structured from the outset to produce a null result, regardless of evidence — serving as the scientific veneer for terminating [[project-blue-book]] and shutting down official UFO inquiry.

## Evidence For This Claim

1. **Robert Low memo**: [[robert-low]] wrote an internal memo before the study began stating it would "appear to be a scientific investigation" while in fact reaching a predetermined conclusion. This memo was later obtained and publicized.

2. **Edward Condon's compromised appointment**: [[edward-condon]] had lost his security clearance in the 1950s. [[lou-branscomb]] (Jason Advisory Group, defense establishment) approached him around 1966 suggesting the Commission appointment would help restore his clearance. The American Institute of Physics oral history shows Condon was evasive when asked why he needed a clearance for an ostensibly non-classified study.

3. **Hoover letter**: J. Edgar Hoover characterized Condon in a 1949 letter to Congress as "nothing less than a Russian spy" — making him politically malleable.

4. **June 1967 meeting**: A meeting between Condon investigators and base UFO officers allegedly produced agreed language for the final report ("nothing to see here") before investigations were complete.

5. **Lewis Chase denial**: [[lewis-chase]], despite writing official reports documenting the UFO-missile connection, denied all knowledge to Condon investigator Roy Craig. The Commission never obtained the missile-shutdown evidence.

6. **Hiper/Low blocking**: [[robert-hiper]] told [[robert-low]] the missile-shutdown material was "probably too highly classified" for the Commission — cutting off access to the most compelling UFO-nuclear evidence.

7. **Colonel Hiper's letters**: Hiper reportedly wrote to Condon explicitly requesting the Commission show Blue Book was "a waste of time and money."
