# Astronomical Data Suppression

## Pattern Summary

A recurring pattern: anomalous observational data from astronomical and military tracking programs is destroyed, classified, or systematically removed before it can be analyzed — often by institutional authority figures with security clearance connections. The pattern spans from the early 1950s to the present.

## Documented Instances

### 1. Harvard Observatory Plate Destruction (Early 1950s)
[[donald-menzel]] — Harvard Observatory director, prolific UFO debunker, holder of extensive government security clearances — ordered one-third of Harvard's photographic plate archive discarded. He directed his *secretary* (not any astronomer) to throw them away. He also destroyed observational log books tracking which plates existed and when observations were made. [[dorit-hofflight]], his assistant, documented this in her memoirs and attempted to resist; Menzel retaliated. This occurred approximately two months after Menzel helped the USAF debunk the [[1952-washington-dc-flyover]].

### 2. Jacques Vallée's Retrograde Orbit Tape (July 11, 1961)
[[paul-mueller]], director of the Paris Observatory, confiscated and destroyed [[jacques-vallee]]'s tracking tape of an unidentified retrograde-orbit object (as bright as Sirius; independently photographed by US tracking stations) the morning after its observation. No systematic follow-up was possible. This experience, described by Vallée himself, was the inciting incident for his lifelong independent UFO research career.

### 3. Uncorrelated Targets — Classified Space Force Lists (1960s–present)
Since the early 1960s, military radar and optical sensors have tracked "uncorrelated targets" — anomalous objects that appear once or a few times and cannot be stably tracked into a conventional orbit. [[beatriz-villarroel]] reports learning from an insider source that NASA does not perform this filtering itself — **Space Force holds and maintains the classified lists**, and these lists (including the earliest from the 1960s) are classified. The uncorrelated targets are systematically subtracted from NASA's background count of objects in space before civilian-accessible figures are published.

### 4. Vera Rubin Observatory — Classified Satellite Removal
The Vera Rubin telescope (Rubin Observatory, LSST) processes its data with a filter that removes a background of classified satellites and other objects from its outputs before making data available to astronomers. This is the present-day continuation of the same pattern.

## Structural Feature

In most cases, the suppression is performed by individuals with security clearances who are also publicly aligned with debunking or skeptical positions. This combination — clearance + debunker public role — appears to characterize the pattern from Menzel to the Space Force/NASA filter arrangement. It suggests a deliberate operational doctrine rather than isolated personal decisions.

## Implications for Villarroel's Research

[[beatriz-villarroel]]'s ability to find 105,000 pre-Sputnik transients in [[palomar-observatory]] plates depends on the Palomar archive surviving intact — which it did because Menzel did not control it. The Harvard equivalent archive was partially destroyed. The scale of the Palomar finding suggests that a complete, unfiltered multi-observatory archive from the 1950s would yield much more.

### 5. Pioneer 10/11 Binary Star Data (1987 → 1990s)
[[thomas-van-flandern]] and [[robert-harrington]] (U.S. Naval Observatory) identified gravitational anomalies in Pioneer 10/11 telemetry consistent with a dead stellar companion. Findings appeared in a 1987 science encyclopedia. Astronomer Miles Standish subsequently discredited the work; [[matt-lroy]] claims the data was systematically removed from internet databases. Both Van Flandern and Harrington died of throat cancer (non-smokers) shortly after their investigation. See [[pioneer-10-11-data]].

### 6. NASA Astronaut Encounter Records (1960s–1970s) — Omitted from UAP Commission
[[kevin-knuth]] raises a systemic institutional suppression: NASA's own UAP commission report made **no mention** of documented astronaut encounters, despite multiple public records:
- **[[alan-bean]]** (Apollo 12): crew photographed a red flashing light that paced Skylab for part of an orbit (~1977); photographs are publicly accessible
- **[[story-musgrave]]** (shuttle pilot): reported "snakes writhing in space"
- **Gemini 11** (1966): mission transcripts record "something just fly over you" followed by battery drain and ~6 minutes of missing transcript; the Simkinson archive contains a lithograph labeled "Strange object as seen by Gemini 11 astronauts"
- **Soviet Phobos probe**: last image before disabling showed a fin-like object

The NASA UAP commission report addressed none of these documented cases. Knuth: "Why was there no section on what astronauts have seen in space?"

Also: a Villarroel contact inside NASA confirmed that NASA tracks "uncorrelated targets" internally — but Space Force (not NASA) systematically catalogs them. The information flows to Space Force but not through the civilian NASA science track.
