# Ancient Egypt

Civilization that arose in the Nile Valley by ~3100 BCE; in alt-archaeology readings, the inheritor (not originator) of much older [[pre-dynastic-egypt|pre-dynastic]] infrastructure such as the Giza substructures.

Conventional Egyptology dates pharaonic civilization from the unification under Narmer (~3100 BCE), with the Great Pyramid attributed to Khufu (~2560 BCE). Alternative-archaeology readings — driven by [[sphinx-water-erosion-theory|Schoch's water erosion analysis]] of the Sphinx, Biondi's SAR subterranean data, and the [[lost-civilization-hypothesis]] — argue that the Giza structures, especially the deep tubular shafts, predate the dynastic period by several thousand years. Under this reading, dynastic Egypt inherited and repurposed infrastructure built during the [[saharan-humid-period]] (8500–5300 BCE) by a high-technology civilization whose identity is unknown. The name [[khemet]] itself encodes this alternative: "land of chemistry" rather than "black land," pointing toward an industrial function predating the agricultural civilization that later occupied the plateau.
