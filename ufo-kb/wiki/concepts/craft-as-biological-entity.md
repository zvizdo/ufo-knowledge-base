# Craft as Biological Entity

The craft-as-biological-entity hypothesis proposes that at least some UAP are not mechanical vehicles built and operated by intelligence, but are themselves living organisms — analogous to how a jellyfish or bird is a biological system that moves through a medium. The hypothesis is suggested most strongly by behavioral evidence: UAP maneuvers are often described as fluid, adaptive, and contextually responsive in ways that are difficult to model as purely mechanical responses.

**David Fravor's observation**: Commander [[david-fravor]], who encountered the Tic-Tac UAP during the 2004 USS Nimitz incident, described the object's behavior as resembling a living organism. It mirrored his aircraft's movements in real time, anticipating changes of direction — responses that are easier to account for if the craft *is* the organism rather than a machine operated by one.

**Garry Nolan's interpretation**: [[garry-nolan]] has discussed the biological entity framework alongside the mechanical framework, arguing that the UAP evidence base is consistent with either interpretation — and that distinguishing them requires different experimental approaches than those used to study conventional aircraft.

**Overlap with conscious-craft hypothesis**: The [[conscious-craft-hypothesis]] goes further, attributing inner experience to UAP. The craft-as-biological-entity hypothesis is the less metaphysically ambitious precursor: an organism can be biological without being conscious in any rich sense. The two hypotheses are compatible but distinct.

**Evidence characteristics**: UAP described as craft-like but behaving organically include objects that divide or merge, change shape during flight, respond to pilot attention before any physical proximity change, and exhibit no visible propulsion or structural seams. These features are more consistent with biological than with known mechanical systems.

**KB context**: The hypothesis sits at the intersection of UAP phenomenology and biology — [[nhi-biology]] addresses the broader question of what NHI bodies or structures might be like, while this concept addresses specifically the possibility that craft and organism are the same entity.
