# Black Budget

The "black budget" refers to classified or unacknowledged government expenditures that do not appear in public budget disclosures. In the US context, this includes spending on Special Access Programs (SAPs) and Unacknowledged Special Access Programs (USAPs), which are reported only to a small set of cleared oversight committee members — or, per some claims, not to Congress at all.

**Acknowledged black budget structure**: The existence of classified defense programs with hidden budget lines is well-documented. The National Reconnaissance Office, certain NSA programs, and various DOD R&D efforts operate under classified budget headings. The Consolidated Cryptologic Program and various DARPA black programs are examples. The total classified intelligence budget (disclosed since 2007 under IRTPA) was approximately $70B in FY2023.

**UAP program funding**: [[david-grusch]]'s testimony to Congress alleged that legacy UAP retrieval and reverse-engineering programs operate outside normal appropriations — funded through misattributed contracts, contractor overhead pools, and mechanisms that do not flow through standard PPBE (Planning, Programming, Budgeting, and Execution) processes. This would constitute an unacknowledged program operating in violation of the congressional oversight requirements of the 1947 National Security Act and subsequent legislation.

**Yamashita gold thesis**: The [[yamashita-gold-black-budget-network]] synthesis documents a more extreme claim — that post-WWII off-books funding for covert US programs (including potentially UAP programs) drew on gold looted by the Japanese during WWII and recovered by US forces in the Philippines. The [[anderson-trust-gold]] is the specific vehicle cited by [[danny-sheehan]] in this account.

**[[compartmentalization]] mechanism**: Black budget programs use the SAP/USAP structure and [[compartmentalization]] to restrict knowledge to program participants, preventing normal budget oversight from revealing program existence.
