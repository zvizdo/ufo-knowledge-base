# Gain-of-Function Research

Research that produces a pathogen with enhanced capabilities — increased transmissibility, broader host range, resistance to countermeasures, or increased virulence. Proponents argue it is necessary for pandemic preparedness: creating more dangerous variants in controlled conditions allows vaccine and treatment development before natural evolution produces the same outcome. Critics, including some within the biosecurity community, argue it constitutes offensive bioweapon development under a civilian research framing.

In the KB (primarily the [[q7czo77qhha]] transcript), gain-of-function research is framed explicitly as a "euphemism for biowarfare" — noting that no gain-of-function experiment has ever produced a cure for the pathogen it created, only a more dangerous version. The DARPA DEFUSE proposal by [[ecohealth-alliance]] — to insert furin cleavage sites into SARS-like coronavirus spike proteins — was rejected by DARPA specifically because the proposed modifications would enhance human transmissibility.

The COVID-19 origins controversy centers on whether the [[wuhan-institute-of-virology]], funded in part through [[ecohealth-alliance]] sub-grants from [[anthony-fauci]]'s NIAID, conducted this or functionally equivalent research after DARPA's rejection. The KB treats this as a case study in dual-use research governance failures — research with obvious weaponization potential conducted through nonprofit intermediaries under civilian funding, with minimal oversight.
