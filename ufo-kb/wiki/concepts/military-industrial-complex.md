# Military-Industrial Complex

The term "military-industrial complex" (MIC) was coined by President [[dwight-eisenhower]] in his January 1961 farewell address as a warning about the growing power of the defense establishment — the interlocking network of the US military, defense contractors, research universities, and government agencies whose interests were mutually reinforcing and potentially antithetical to democratic accountability.

**Eisenhower's warning**: In his farewell speech, Eisenhower said: "In the councils of government, we must guard against the acquisition of unwarranted influence, whether sought or unsought, by the military-industrial complex. The potential for the disastrous rise of misplaced power exists and will persist." He initially planned to call it the "military-industrial-congressional complex," suggesting the legislative branch was also implicated.

**UAP secrecy thesis**: The MIC is the structural explanation invoked by most serious UAP secrecy researchers for why UAP programs might remain classified for decades without formal oversight. The argument: defense contractors like Lockheed, Boeing, Raytheon, and Northrop Grumman hold contracts for reverse-engineering programs; these programs are funded through the [[black-budget]] and shielded from oversight via Special Access Programs (SAPs) and Unacknowledged SAPs (USAPs); the financial incentive to maintain exclusivity over any recovered or reverse-engineered technology is enormous; and the personnel involved cycle between government positions and contractor roles (the "revolving door"), creating structural alignment against disclosure.

**Contractor control hypothesis**: Some UAP researchers — including claims attributed to David Grusch — argue that the most sensitive UAP programs have been moved entirely into private contractor facilities to escape congressional oversight and FOIA. This is structurally enabled by the SAP/USAP architecture and the contractor-heavy structure of the post-Cold War defense establishment.

**Relevant contractors**: [[lockheed]] (Skunk Works), [[raytheon]], Boeing, Northrop Grumman, SAIC, and Battelle are most frequently named in the UAP-contractor context.
