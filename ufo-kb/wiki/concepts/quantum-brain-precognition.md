# Quantum Brain Precognition

The thesis, developed by [[eric-wargo]] and connecting to the broader Penrose-Hameroff framework, that the **brain is a hybrid classical/quantum system** whose quantum subsystem exhibits **temporal non-locality** — allowing information from a future knowledge state to propagate backward in time, producing the phenomenology of precognition, intuition, déjà vu, and anomalous remote viewing results.

This is a materialist, physics-grounded account of precognition — it does not invoke spirits, supernatural intervention, or mystical frameworks.

## The Physics Foundation: Temporal Non-Locality

### Spatial vs. Temporal Non-Locality

Standard quantum mechanics (and popular science) emphasizes **spatial non-locality**: entangled particles separated in space appear to affect each other instantaneously, violating classical locality. But quantum mechanics also exhibits **temporal non-locality**:

In delayed-choice double-slit experiments with entangled photon pairs (Photon A and Photon B):
- Run the double-slit experiment with Photon A
- Wait days; then run the same experiment with Photon B
- If neither photon is observed: both show interference patterns (wave behavior)
- If Photon B is observed (collapsing it to a particle state): Photon A's *earlier* experiment also shows particle behavior — as if the future measurement retroactively determined the earlier outcome

This is experimentally documented retrocausality. The measurement of Photon B **backward-in-time causes** the collapse of Photon A's earlier state.

### Quantum Computing and Reversed Information

In quantum computers, cubits exist in superposition states (not 0, not 1, not both — a distinct superposition state). Crucially, cubit states can be **reversed** during quantum computation. Some theorists building quantum computers have hypothesized this creates a channel for sending information backward in time: a quantum computer left running for extended periods could theoretically communicate across time via this reversal.

## Brain as Quantum System

[[roger-penrose]] hypothesized the brain is a room-temperature quantum system. [[stuart-hameroff]]'s candidate: **microtubules** in neuronal cytoplasm — disrupt them with anesthesia and consciousness disappears while all other brain functions continue. [[efim-liberman]]'s goldfish cytoskeleton experiment independently demonstrated the cytoskeleton's necessity for spatial cognition.

[[quantum-biology]] has now established that warm biological systems routinely exploit quantum effects (enzyme tunneling, cryptochrome avian navigation, photosynthesis). The "too warm and wet for quantum coherence" objection to Penrose-Hameroff is increasingly untenable.

## The Precognition Mechanism

If the brain contains a quantum subsystem, it can access its **future knowledge state** through temporal non-locality — a "pre-memory" of knowledge it will acquire in the future. This surfaces as:

- **Intuition**: decisions that are locally irrational but globally optimal (make sense only given a future outcome you shouldn't yet know)
- **Precognitive dreams**: the dream-state may have lower decoherence filtering, making future-state signals more legible
- **Remote viewing**: the viewer may be perceiving their own future confirmation moment, not the present target
- **Death premonitions**: life-and-death situations create the strongest survival-relevant future-state signals

## The Confirmatory Feedback Model (Remote Viewing)

Wargo's most specific operational claim: **remote viewers are not seeing the present target directly** — they are seeing the **future moment when they receive confirmation** that their description was correct. This explains:
- Why feedback quality and timing affect remote viewing accuracy
- Why double-blind protocols with delayed judging still produce positive results
- Why viewers can name targets before they are randomly selected (the target-selection moment is the future confirmation event they perceive)

[[hal-puthoff]] confirmed this model is consistent with his understanding of the program's mechanics.

## Evolutionary Argument

Why would temporal non-locality evolve in biology? Because **survival under uncertainty is enormously improved by access to outcome information**. Humans are most accurate in their intuitions under life-and-death conditions — consistent with selection pressure for retrocausal signaling in contexts where the correct decision is literally the one that leads to survival.

## Anatomical Locus

[[garry-nolan]]'s fMRI research: the **caudate nucleus and putamen** (basal ganglia) light up when Go players make unintelligible intuitive moves that later prove correct. This region is the brain's **reward-prediction system** — it learns by comparing anticipated future rewards to actual outcomes. Higher neural density in this region correlates with anomalous cognitive capacity ([[basal-ganglia-uap-predisposition]]).

Wargo's interpretation: the reward-prediction system is precisely where you would expect a retrocausal quantum signal to surface — it is the brain's native architecture for processing anticipated future states.

## Statistical Evidence Base

[[jessica-utts]] (President, American Statistical Association) reviewed the Stargate Program data and broader SRI psi research and concluded: *"using the standards applied to any other area of science, it is concluded that psychic functioning has been well established. The statistical results of the studies examined are far beyond what is expected by chance."* Her co-reviewer [[ray-hyman]] (a committed skeptic) agreed the experiments were *"free of obvious flaws"* and the data too consistent to dismiss.

The quantum brain precognition model is designed to explain *why* these statistically real effects occur — not whether they occur.

## Decision Augmentation Extension (hXYdkcv5TtY)

[[ed-may]]'s [[decision-augmentation]] hypothesis extends the precognition model to micro-PK experimental design: positive results in RNG experiments may reflect researchers' unconscious precognition guiding when they start trials — not subjects' minds affecting physical systems. [[eric-wargo]] considers this potentially hugely important as a hidden confound in experimental science broadly, including the replication crisis in psychology and health sciences.

## Block Universe Requirement (hXYdkcv5TtY)

Wargo articulates the physics prerequisite more explicitly: the brain's quantum temporal non-locality only produces *usable* (self-consistent) results in a [[block-universe]]. An open, indeterminate future has no fixed state to signal from. The closed timelike curve / self-consistency requirement of GR (confirmed in 1980s wormhole physics) means all genuine precognition is necessarily self-fulfilling — it produces the outcome it signals.
