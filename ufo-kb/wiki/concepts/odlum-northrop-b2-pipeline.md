# Odlum–Northrop–B2 IP Pipeline

A documented institutional pathway by which [[thomas-townsend-brown]]'s electrogravitics intellectual property was allegedly transferred — without public paper trail — from his Santa Monica research company [[guidance-technologies]] into [[northrop]]'s classified aerospace programs, culminating decades later in the [[b2-stealth-bomber]]. The pipeline's documented vehicle is [[floyd-odlum]], who was simultaneously the sole outside investor in Guidance Technologies and majority owner of Northrop.

## The Chain

[[nick-cook]]'s account in [[G7Ns4Aq1tVc]] provides the most complete public documentation of the handoff:

1. **1965 — Teller demonstration**: Brown demonstrated his fan precipitator to [[edward-teller]] at Teller's Berkeley home. Teller told his wife: *"I don't know how this works."* Brown's daughter [[linda-brown]] was present and confirmed this account.

2. **Teller → Odlum tip-off**: [[floyd-odlum]] — described as "a prominent aerospace lawyer and investor" — was tipped off about Teller's astonishment and became the **sole outside investor** in Brown's Santa Monica company [[guidance-technologies]]. Odlum was simultaneously **majority owner of Northrop** prior to its Grumman merger.

3. **Fall 1967 — Guidance shutdown**: Brown abruptly shuts Guidance Technologies with no public explanation, claiming no results.

4. **Winter 1968 — Northrop publication**: Just three months later, Northrop publishes *"Electro Aerodynamics and Electrogravitics and Its Implications for Aviation"* — using terminology previously exclusive to Brown's work.

5. **Post-death payments**: After Brown's death, Odlum paid **$2,500/month** to Brown's wife Josephine for the rest of her life. Linda Brown: *"Mr. Odlum forced them to sign a contract with my dad that was open-ended... after daddy died it was what kept my mother going."*

## The B2 Connection

British aerospace journalist [[bill-gunston]] (the dean of Jane's Defence) claimed publicly that the [[b2-stealth-bomber]] incorporated Brown's electrogravitics:

- The B2's leading edge is charged to millions of volts positive
- The trailing edge is charged to millions of volts negative
- This configuration **precisely matches** Brown's negative-to-positive thrust mechanism

Gunston cited a 1968 Northrop paper on "electro-aerodynamics" before reportedly losing access to it; the paper was subsequently difficult to locate. [[nick-cook]] and Gunston both endorsed the thesis that the B2's anomalous performance data (engine output insufficient for observed flight envelope) is explained by an electrogravitic supplement to conventional propulsion. Gunston eventually dropped the story, citing — cryptically — *"the Tower of London."*

Some accounts go further: the **B2 designation itself** may derive from *"Byfield Brown"* — two B's.

## Significance

The pipeline is one of the most concretely documented examples of how civilian advanced-physics IP can be funneled into black aerospace programs through a single dual-role investor. It is also the primary technical bridge between Brown's mid-century electrogravitics experiments and operational US military aircraft.
