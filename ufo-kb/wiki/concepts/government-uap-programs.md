# Government UAP Programs

The category of official and semi-official U.S. government programs tasked with investigating unidentified aerial phenomena. The public history runs from Project Sign (1947) and Project Grudge (1948) through [[project-blue-book]] (1952–1969), the [[condon-commission]] (1966–1968), and into the modern era with [[aatip]] (2007–2012), [[aawsap]] (2008–2012), and [[aaro]] (2022–present).

What distinguishes the KB's treatment is the alleged parallel tier: unacknowledged special access programs (USAPs) that operate outside this public record, manage recovered materials, and have allegedly been structured to evade Congressional and presidential oversight. [[david-grusch]] described this tier in congressional testimony as existing beyond AARO's access — a program architecture where the public history is real but incomplete, and the operational programs are elsewhere.

Key pattern: each public program is eventually closed with a finding of "insufficient evidence" or "no national security threat," and each closure is followed by evidence of continuing classified activity through different channels. [[project-blue-book]]'s closure in 1969 was followed by the [[aatip]]/[[aawsap]] programs; their quiet termination was followed by Grusch's claims about ongoing retrieval and reverse-engineering programs.

The [[immaculate-constellation]] reference in the KB — and the broader USAP architecture — represents what the acknowledged programs are alleged to be covering for.
