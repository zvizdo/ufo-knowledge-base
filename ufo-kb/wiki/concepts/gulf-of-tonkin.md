# Gulf of Tonkin Incident

The Gulf of Tonkin incidents (August 2 and 4, 1964) involved alleged North Vietnamese naval attacks on US destroyers USS Maddox and USS Turner Joy operating in the Gulf of Tonkin. The incidents provided the political pretext for the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution (August 7, 1964), which authorized President Johnson to use military force in Southeast Asia without a formal declaration of war — the legislative basis for full US military intervention in Vietnam.

**August 2 incident**: The first incident (August 2) is generally accepted as real — North Vietnamese torpedo boats did attack the USS Maddox, which was on an intelligence-collection mission. The attack may have been provoked by covert US operations (OPLAN 34A) against North Vietnam.

**August 4 incident**: The second incident (August 4) is now known with near-certainty to have not occurred. No North Vietnamese vessels attacked US ships that night. The reported "attack" was a product of radar operator confusion, probable sonar misreads, and the heightened anxiety of the crew. Secretary of Defense McNamara and President Johnson knew within hours of the reported attack that the evidence was deeply uncertain — but proceeded to present it to Congress as definitive.

**Declassified NSA files**: NSA files declassified in 2005–2006 confirmed that the second Gulf of Tonkin attack did not occur, and that agency analysts had altered their intelligence reports to support the attack narrative rather than truthfully report the ambiguity.

**UAP relevance**: The Gulf of Tonkin incident is the most frequently cited documented historical precedent for government deception to manufacture a pretext for military action — cited in UAP research alongside [[operation-northwoods]] as evidence that the US government has demonstrated the will and capability to deceive Congress and the public about alleged military incidents.
