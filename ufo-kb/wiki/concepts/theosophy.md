# Theosophy

The Theosophical Society, founded by Helena Petrovna Blavatsky and Henry Steel Olcott in New York City in 1875, developed a synthetic cosmological system drawing on Hinduism, Buddhism, Neoplatonism, Kabbalah, and Western occultism. Blavatsky's major works — *Isis Unveiled* (1877) and *The Secret Doctrine* (1888) — proposed that all religions share an underlying esoteric tradition transmitted by a hidden brotherhood of adepts, that consciousness is a fundamental feature of the universe, and that humanity is evolving through a series of root races under the guidance of these advanced beings.

In the KB's context, Theosophy is the 19th-century bridge between ancient mystery traditions and modern NHI/UAP discourse:
- The "hidden masters" of Theosophy are structurally identical to the contactee movement's "space brothers" — advanced beings who guide human development from a higher level
- Blavatsky's cosmology of successive human civilizations (root races, including the Atlanteans) anticipates the [[lost-civilization-hypothesis]]
- The Theosophical emphasis on consciousness as primary and matter as secondary prefigures the [[science-religion-convergence]] and [[consciousness-non-local]] cluster

Timothy Alberino's analysis (in the KB) groups the founding of the Theosophical Society alongside the spiritualism epidemic and the mystery airship wave of the same era as evidence of a coordinated supernatural invitation — or, in the NHI interpretation, a systematic information-injection event.
