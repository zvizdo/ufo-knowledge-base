# Endogenous DMT Function

The set of open questions and emerging hypotheses about why the mammalian brain produces DMT endogenously — a pharmacologically active, psychedelic compound present at concentrations comparable to serotonin and dopamine.

## Key Facts (Empirically Established)

- DMT is produced endogenously in mammalian brains via two enzymatic steps from tryptophan: tryptophan → tryptamine → DMT (enzyme: INMT, indolethylamine N-methyltransferase)
- DMT concentrations in living rodent brains are roughly equivalent to classical neurotransmitters (serotonin, dopamine) — not trace amounts
- DMT **spikes at death** in rodents (measured in real-time via microdialysis as rodents are killed)
- DMT binds the **sigma-1 receptor** — an "orphan receptor" with no other known ligand; sigma-1 binding triggers intracellular signaling that protects neurons in hypoxic (oxygen-deprived) conditions
- A regulatory **peptide in cerebrospinal fluid** inhibits INMT, keeping DMT levels low; this peptide exists in mammals but its structure is unknown

## Hypotheses

### 1. Neuroprotection at Death (Partial Support)
During the dying process, as respiratory function collapses and blood supply to the brain diminishes, the brain faces oxygen deprivation. DMT's sigma-1 binding appears to confer neuroprotective effects — allowing neurons to survive longer in hypoxic conditions. This matches the observed DMT death-spike: the brain may be releasing DMT as an emergency protective response. If the body subsequently recovers (heart restart, resuscitation), the DMT-protected brain survives intact.

### 2. Death-Transit Conduit (Speculative)
[[rick-strassman]]'s "spirit molecule" hypothesis: the pineal gland releases DMT at death as a conduit by which consciousness/the soul transitions to the afterlife. [[andrew-gallimore]] gives partial support but challenges the pineal gland specifically (too small for breakthrough quantities; cortical neurons more likely) and notes the dual-function question: why does the same molecule both protect neurons AND produce an alternate-reality experience?

### 3. Dreaming Substrate (Weak Support)
[[jace-callaway]]'s 1980s hypothesis: DMT is released during REM sleep, generating dream imagery. [[andrew-gallimore]] is skeptical — dreams are nothing like DMT states, and there's no evidence of DMT release during sleep. [[terence-mckenna]] popularized this hypothesis perhaps too definitively.

### 4. Darkness and Sensory Deprivation Trigger (Under Study)
[[aubrey-marcus]] reported a full DMT-breakthrough experience after 4–5 days in a total darkness retreat. This motivates [[new-nautics]]'s current study: sending subjects into darkness retreats and sampling DMT levels at intervals. If confirmed, darkness would establish a non-pharmacological path to endogenous DMT activation — and explain the consistent entity reports from extended darkness retreats, cave rituals, and sensory deprivation practices across ancient and modern cultures.

### 5. Baseline NHI Channel (Speculative)
If the endogenous DMT system is kept suppressed by the INMT-inhibiting peptide, understanding the regulatory mechanism might reveal that the "channel" to NHI is always present but gated. Exogenous DMT bypasses the gate; [[new-nautics]]'s endoDMTX research aims to open the gate via the peptide pathway rather than by flooding the system with external molecules.

## Regulatory Peptide Research

New Nautics / University of Florida research program:
- **Goal**: isolate and characterize the INMT-inhibiting peptide in mammalian CSF
- **Significance**: understanding the peptide's structure and function would enable "endoDMTX" — using a small peptide injection to modulate the body's own DMT production system for sustained natural DMT states
- Currently: peptide is known to exist, is present in CSF, but its structure/sequence is not characterized

## Open Questions

- Is the neuroprotective function of sigma-1 DMT binding the *primary* evolutionary driver, with the consciousness effects being a byproduct?
- Does human DMT spike at death as it does in rodents? (Ethical barriers to testing; an end-of-life study with consent is theoretically possible)
- Does darkness reproducibly trigger DMT spikes? (New Nautics study in progress)
- What is the structure of the INMT-inhibiting peptide? (UF research in progress)
- Is there a regulatory pathway by which emotional state, meditation, or trauma affects INMT inhibition and thus endogenous DMT levels?
