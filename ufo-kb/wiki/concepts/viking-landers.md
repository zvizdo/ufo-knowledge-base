# Viking Landers

NASA's 1976 twin Mars-surface missions: **Viking 1** (landed July 20, 1976, Chryse Planitia) and **Viking 2** (landed Sept 3, 1976, Utopia Planitia). The first successful Mars-surface science platforms; both operated for years past nominal mission lifetime.

Two data outputs from the Viking program drive UAP-adjacent claims-threads:

1. **Gas-chromatograph atmospheric composition** — including the **Xenon-129 isotopic ratio** that [[john-brandenberg]] later argued is anomalously high relative to terrestrial baselines and consistent with ancient large-scale fission events (see [[mars-nuclear-war-thesis]]).
2. **Biology-package "labeled release" experiment** (Gilbert Levin, PI) — a positive result that NASA classified as inconclusive but Levin maintained for decades was a true detection of metabolic activity. Mainstream Mars science treats this as a perchlorate-driven false positive; a minority position holds it as the only direct life-detection result we have from Mars to date.

The Viking data are also the empirical reference baseline against which all subsequent Mars-mission isotopic and biosignature claims are calibrated.
