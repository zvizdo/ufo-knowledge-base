# Black Triangle UAP

Black triangle UAP are among the most consistently reported UAP subtypes since the 1970s: large, silent, dark triangular craft typically displaying three lights at the vertices and a central light, often observed at very low altitude and very low speed — sometimes hovering — before departing at high velocity. The combination of extreme silence, low-altitude slow movement, and sudden high-speed departure distinguishes them from conventional aircraft behavior.

**Belgium 1989–1990**: The most extensive documented black triangle wave occurred over Belgium, with thousands of civilian reports and multiple police and military witness confirmations. Belgian F-16s were scrambled and achieved radar lock, which was then lost as the objects performed extreme maneuvers. The Belgian Air Force officially acknowledged the events were unexplained.

**Woomera connection**: Aboriginal communities near the [[woomera-test-range]] in Australia have reported black triangle craft in the area, overlapping geographically with classified test activity — a pattern that appears at multiple test ranges globally.

**TR-3B hypothesis**: Edgar Fouché ([[edgar-fouche]]) and others have claimed the black triangle craft are a classified US government aerospace vehicle designated TR-3B, allegedly using nuclear-powered rotating plasma field propulsion. The TR-3B hypothesis would explain the silhouette and silence but struggles to account for the global distribution, extreme performance, and reports predating realistic classified development timelines. The [[tr3b]] claim is contested.

**Characteristics**: Witness descriptions are remarkably consistent across decades and countries:
- Equilateral or isosceles triangle shape
- Three white or amber lights at corners, one red light at center
- Size often described as football-field scale or larger
- Near-total silence at low altitude
- Ability to hover then accelerate to hypersonic speed instantaneously

**Open question**: Whether these constitute a single phenomenon, multiple phenomena, or a mix of classified US aircraft and genuinely unknown objects remains contested. The performance envelope (especially instantaneous acceleration) exceeds known aviation technology for any era.
