# Close Encounter Cases

Close Encounter cases are UAP/UFO incidents classified under the typology developed by astronomer and Project Blue Book scientific advisor [[j-allen-hynek]] in his 1972 book *The UFO Experience*. The system provides a graduated scale of proximity and interaction:

- **CE1** (Close Encounter of the First Kind): Sighting of a UAP within approximately 150 meters; no physical interaction
- **CE2** (Close Encounter of the Second Kind): Physical trace evidence — landing marks, electromagnetic effects on vehicles, radiation burns, animal reactions
- **CE3** (Close Encounter of the Third Kind): Observation of occupants, entities, or beings associated with the craft (the subject of the 1977 Spielberg film)
- **CE4** (Close Encounter of the Fourth Kind): Abduction — direct physical or experiential contact with NHI; term added by later researchers
- **CE5** (Close Encounter of the Fifth Kind): Bilateral, voluntary contact — human-initiated communication with NHI; associated with the [[ce-5-protocols]] movement

**Significance**: The Hynek classification gave the research field a shared language and allowed comparative analysis across cases. It shifted the focus from "was it a craft?" to "what class of interaction occurred?" — elevating physical-trace and entity-contact cases above simple sighting reports.

**KB examples**: The [[1994-ruwa-zimbabwe]] Ariel School incident is a canonical CE3. [[abduction-research]] cases (Hill, Hopkins's subjects, Strieber) are CE4. [[ce-5-protocols]] as practiced by [[steven-greer]] target CE5.

**Abduction as subset**: Abduction cases (CE4) are the most contested subset — they depend heavily on hypnotic memory recovery and involve claims that go far beyond what physical-trace or independent-witness CE2/CE3 cases can establish. The Hynek framework itself does not adjudicate credibility; it categorizes by reported interaction type.
