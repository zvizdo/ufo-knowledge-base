# UAP Camera Evasion Problem

The consistent pattern in which UAP encounters produce ambiguous, low-quality, or non-existent visual documentation despite occurring in environments (military cockpits, nuclear bases, radar-equipped vessels) where high-quality instrumented recording should be straightforward. This pattern is interpreted very differently depending on priors.

## Interpretation 1: Selection Effect (Mick West)

[[mick-west]] argues this is purely a selection effect — a form of survivorship bias in the evidence record:
- If a clear, high-quality video existed that unambiguously showed an anomalous craft, it would be instantly recognized and would resolve the debate
- Since no such video exists in public circulation, the only UAP footage that "survives" into discourse is inherently ambiguous
- The consistent low quality is not evidence of evasion — it is a consequence of the fact that unambiguous footage would be immediately recognized as either a UAP or a conventional object and would leave the ambiguous category
- West calls this "the low information zone"

Implication: the apparent evasion of cameras is an artifact of the epistemological process, not a property of the phenomenon.

## Interpretation 2: Genuine Evasive Behavior

[[marik-von-rennenkampff]] and others argue that the [[twining-memo]] (1947) explicitly documented evasiveness as a characteristic behavior: "UAP are evasive when approached." This pre-dates photography-focused discourse by decades.

The counter-argument to West's selection-effect theory:
- Many of the most compelling historical cases involve objects observed at close range (< 100 feet) in conditions where high-quality documentation would have been possible
- In many nuclear security incidents, personnel witnessed structured craft disabling missiles without any camera present
- The consistency of evasion across thousands of cases spanning 80 years suggests behavioral pattern, not selection effect

## Interpretation 3: Suppression / Classification

A third interpretation: high-quality footage exists but is classified. AARO director [[jon-kosloski]] stated he has seen "true anomalies" he cannot explain and pledged to release them — suggesting better footage exists inside the classification barrier. [[david-grusch]] and others allege a deliberate suppression apparatus.

## Historical Anchor

The [[twining-memo]] (1947) is the earliest official documentation of evasiveness as a UAP characteristic, 77 years before Mick West formulated the "low information zone" argument. This is either (a) confirmation that the selection effect has been operating since 1947, or (b) evidence that evasive behavior is intrinsic to the phenomenon.
