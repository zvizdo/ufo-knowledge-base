# Binary Star Companion

## Core Claim

[[matt-lroy]]'s central thesis: our sun is a binary star system. The companion is a dead ("dark") star — a white dwarf, brown dwarf, or stellar remnant — orbiting on a roughly **25,000–26,000 year cycle**, aligned with the precession of the equinoxes (the "[[great-year]]"). At perihelion (closest approach, every ~12,500 years), the companion's gravitational and electromagnetic influence stresses the sun, triggering:

- Increased solar activity
- Coronal mass ejection (CME) events
- Weakening of Earth's magnetosphere
- Civilizational reset-scale catastrophes

This framework integrates the [[younger-dryas-impact|Younger Dryas]] (~12,000 BP) and [[older-dryas]] (~14,500 BP) catastrophes as expressions of the same binary-star perihelion cycle.

## Evidence Cited

### Statistical Argument
- ~80% of known stars are binary or ternary systems.
- A sun without a companion would be a statistical outlier.
- Therefore a companion is likely; its non-visibility suggests it is a dead (non-luminous) star.

### Pioneer 10 and 11
- Pioneer probes (1970s) — first spacecraft to reach the outer solar system — detected **gravitational anomalies** consistent with a massive object beyond Pluto.
- [[thomas-van-flandern]] and [[robert-harrington]] (U.S. Naval Observatory) interpreted this as a dead stellar companion (~50 billion miles distant as of 1984 measurement).
- Findings appeared in a 1987 science encyclopedia; subsequently discredited by astronomer Miles Standish.
- Both Van Flandern and Harrington died of throat cancer (non-smokers) shortly after.
- [[matt-lroy]] claims the original data was scrubbed from the internet; the 1987 encyclopedia page is the only surviving documented source.

### Caltech Planet 9
- Caltech researchers claim ~99% confidence in a large planet beyond Neptune (Planet 9), inferred from gravitational clustering of Kuiper Belt objects.
- Lroy treats this as corroborating evidence for the outer solar system object.
- > ⚠ Conflict: Caltech's Planet 9 is a planet-mass object, not a stellar companion. The mainstream framing does not describe it as a binary star or invoke civilizational cycles.

### Ice Core Data
- Antarctic ice cores (450,000-year record) show carbon spikes and temperature fluctuations at ~25,000-year intervals.
- Greenland ice cores (~20,000 years) capture the Younger Dryas and Older Dryas events.
- Lroy interprets these as fingerprints of binary-star perihelion events.

## Great Year Alignment

The binary companion's ~25,000-year orbit coincides with the **precession of equinoxes** (26,000-year cycle). Lroy argues this is not coincidence: the precessional cycle may itself be caused by or correlated with the binary companion's gravitational influence on the solar system.

Ancient traditions encoding the Great Year ([[hamlets-mill]] thesis, Vedic [[yuga-cycles]], Egyptian Zep Tepi) would then be encoding knowledge of this binary companion cycle — civilizations warning their descendants about the next perihelion reset.

## Current Status

Per Lroy's interview (~2026): we are in the current perihelion window. Evidence: aurora borealis observed further south than ever; solar activity ramping up. The implication is that a CME event at civilizational-reset scale could happen within decades to centuries.
