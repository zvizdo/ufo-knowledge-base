# Older Dryas

## Definition

The **Older Dryas** is a cold stadial period in late glacial chronology that occurred approximately **14,700–14,000 years ago** (roughly 14,500 BP at its center), preceding the Allerød interstadial and before the well-known Younger Dryas (~12,900–11,700 BP). In mainstream paleoclimatology, it is a recognized cold event within deglaciation, less dramatic and less studied than the Younger Dryas.

## Matt Lroy's Emphasis

[[matt-lroy]] argues the Older Dryas is systematically underemphasized in the alternative history discourse — even [[graham-hancock]] and [[randall-carlson]] focus almost exclusively on the Younger Dryas. Lroy's position:

- **Carbon spike at ~14,500 BP**: Ice core data shows a massive atmospheric carbon spike at this time — dwarfing anything in modern industrial emissions — followed by a rapid temperature rise from the coldest point of the Ice Age to warmer-than-present levels within just a few hundred years.
- **Two-catastrophe bracketing**: The Older Dryas (~14,500 BP) and [[younger-dryas-impact|Younger Dryas]] (~12,000 BP) **together** ended the Ice Age. They are paired perihelion events in [[matt-lroy]]'s [[binary-star-companion]] model — the companion approached at the start of the Older Dryas (one oscillation) and delivered maximum stress at the Younger Dryas.
- **[[mount-nemrut]] eruption**: Lroy links a massive volcanic explosion near [[lake-van]] to the Older Dryas period, potentially contributing to the carbon spike and atmospheric disruption.

## Physical Evidence

From Antarctic ice cores (450,000-year record):
- Sharp carbon spike at ~14,500 BP
- Temperature rise from coldest Ice Age point to warmer-than-Holocene within centuries
- Then temperature crash (Younger Dryas) before final stabilization

This twin-spike pattern (massive warming at 14,500 BP, collapse at 12,900 BP) matches what a binary-star perihelion approach-and-retreat cycle would predict — per Lroy.
