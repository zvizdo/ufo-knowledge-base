# Scapegoat Mechanism

René Girard's theory (developed in *Violence and the Sacred*, 1972, and *The Scapegoat*, 1986) that human societies discharge accumulating mimetic desire and social tension by targeting a surrogate victim — the scapegoat — whose expulsion or destruction temporarily restores social unity. The scapegoat is typically someone already marginalized or ambiguous in social status.

## Application in UAP-Adjacent Discourse

In [[KhmUixSXxDo]], [[jesse-michaels]] explicitly applies Girard's framework to [[alex-jones]]:

> "There's this philosopher I like named René Girard. He has this theory that like there are scapegoats that can sometimes be constructed as like pressure valve releases."

Jones accepts this self-description: he was used as "the archetype of 'let's just ban Alex Jones'" to demonize the broader populist anti-establishment movement, providing a target that allowed the appearance of accountability without challenging the underlying power structure. The irony: the scapegoating amplified rather than silenced Jones.

## Structural Pattern

1. An inconvenient idea or movement gains traction
2. A specific person is elevated as the symbol/vessel of that movement
3. That person is ritually demonized and expelled (deplatformed, defamed, prosecuted)
4. The underlying movement is associated with the expelled figure and loses legitimacy
5. Power structure appears to have "dealt with" the problem without engaging its substance

This pattern recurs in the broader UFO/disclosure context wherever whistleblowers are discredited rather than answered. See [[flood-the-zone-disinfo]] and [[disclosure-narratives]].
