# Space Program Patches

Classified US space mission patches — primarily from Space Force, Space Command, and related programs — bearing **Roman gods, first-century Latin text, and other Greco-Roman religious iconography**. Documented by [[diana-pasulka]] based on direct access to insiders who showed her the physical patches.

## What Is Known

- At least one collector has **150+ patches** from various classified missions
- Patches routinely feature: Roman deity imagery, Latin mottoes using **first-century Latin** (not classical Republican Latin or later ecclesiastical Latin — specifically the idiom of the Imperial-era mystery religions and early Christianity)
- The Latin text describes the mission in opaque terms that mock outsiders ("even making fun of Outsiders" — Pasulka's phrasing)
- The patches are the institutional marker by which insiders sometimes discover their mission's full nature — Pasulka: "they didn't know they got it right until they got the patch... two years later"

## "The Sponsors"

When Pasulka asked insiders why the patches use Roman/Latin imagery, the consistent answer was that they are **dedications to "the sponsors"** — the NHI the programs believe they are working with or for.

Follow-up: **Do they read Latin?** Answer from insiders: **"Absolutely."**

## The Oral Tradition

The cosmism underlying these patches is **not transmitted through classified memos or formal briefings**. It is an oral tradition — hermetically passed from person to person. Pasulka describes it as analogous to 30,000-year-old Australian Aboriginal oral traditions about NHI: durable, non-documentary, able to persist across generations without a paper trail.

This explains why declassified documents about UAP programs "grope in the dark" — the memos capture only what the bureaucracy knows, not what the oral tradition knows.

## Historical Lineage

Pasulka suspects the tradition is **Greco-Roman with possible roots in Egyptian/Hermetic practice** — not a Christianized veneer like the Russian [[cosmism]] tradition. She notes: "It's definitely not Christian. It seems to be Greco-Roman with the recognition that there are these deities and NHI." Whether it traces back to Egypt is explicitly unresolved.

[[jack-parsons]]'s Thelema practice occupies the founding generation of this tradition on the American side; the patches represent the institutional perpetuation of the same underlying epistemology, sanitized into a different symbolic vocabulary.

## Apollo-Era Patch Symbolism (zjpvfDFc4fg)

[[jesse-michaels]] adds detail on the official Apollo program mission patch in [[zjpvfDFc4fg]]:

The Apollo patch shows Earth, the moon, a connecting path, and a large letter **"A"** — but behind the A, the **constellation Orion** is depicted, with the crossbar of the A deliberately aligned with **Orion's belt** (the three stars Mintaka, Alnilam, and Alnitak). In ancient Egypt, those three stars represented **Osiris**, god of the afterlife, death, and resurrection — outlined in blue on the patch for special emphasis.

Why name a mission after Apollo (Greek sun god) but center its symbolism in the Egyptian Osiris pantheon? Jesse asks whether the "A" stands for Apollo — or for **Asar**, the Egyptian name for Osiris.

Additional data points from [[zjpvfDFc4fg]]:
- NASA's primary launch pad at White Sands: **pad 33**
- Kennedy Space Center's only runway: **runway 33** (not runway 1)
- The number 33 is symbolically central to the **Scottish Rite of Freemasonry** (33rd degree as the highest rank)
- [[farooq-el-baz]] — who oversaw Apollo lunar science planning and selected landing sites — was nicknamed "the King" by astronauts; his father was a scholar of Egyptian religion

The **NROL-16 mission patch** (2005) is the contemporary equivalent: a classified payload with occult iconography deliberately structured around Walpurgisnacht launch timing (per [[john-ramirez]]). See [[2005-nrol-16-launch]].

**[[space-delta-7]]** (Space Force intelligence wing) emblem: a bear holding a key in front of the **Big Dipper**. See [[big-dipper-nhi-nexus]].
