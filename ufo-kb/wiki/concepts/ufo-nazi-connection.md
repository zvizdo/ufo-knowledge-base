# UFO-Nazi Connection

The UFO-Nazi connection is a cluster of claims asserting that Nazi Germany developed disc-shaped advanced aircraft ("Flugscheiben" or flying discs) with exotic propulsion systems in the late 1930s and early 1940s, using technology derived from occult research, alien contact, or advanced physics not available to the Allies. The claims lack verified documentary evidence but have been foundational to the post-war UFO mythology.

**Haunebu and Vril craft**: The most detailed versions of these claims describe specific project names — "Haunebu" (a large disc-shaped craft allegedly developed by the SS E-IV unit) and "Vril" craft (based on technology allegedly channeled by the [[vril-society]]) — with specifications, performance claims, and alleged test flights. These documents have been demonstrated to be post-war fabrications.

**Die Glocke**: The [[die-glocke]] ("The Bell") is a more plausible (but still unverified) claim about an unusual classified Nazi project involving a bell-shaped device with alleged anti-gravitational or electromagnetic anomalous properties. Journalist Igor Witkowski and Nick Cook gave the claim more serious treatment, but the original documents have not been authenticated.

**Operation Paperclip connection**: The UFO-Nazi narrative gains its most credible dimension through the actual post-war transfer of German aerospace scientists via [[operation-paperclip]]. Wernher von Braun and colleagues brought not only documented rocketry knowledge but also classified research, creating a plausible pathway for any genuine exotic technology to enter US classified programs.

**Antarctic base claims**: A persistent sub-claim involves Operation Highjump (1946–47) — the US Navy expedition to Antarctica — and the alleged existence of a Nazi Antarctic base (Neuschwabenland/Base 211) where surviving elements of the Reich continued exotic technology development. The claims lack documentation.

**Significance**: Regardless of factual accuracy, the UFO-Nazi connection framework shaped how American popular culture understood UAP for decades — implicitly earthly, government-origin, reverse-engineered — rather than extraterrestrial. This framing competed with and partially displaced the extraterrestrial hypothesis in popular understanding.
