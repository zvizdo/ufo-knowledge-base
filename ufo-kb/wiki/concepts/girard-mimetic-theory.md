# Girard Mimetic Theory

René Girard's theory that human desire is inherently mimetic: we do not desire objects independently but rather desire what another person desires, leading to rivalry between individuals pursuing the same desired object. This mimetic rivalry escalates into violence, which archaic societies resolved through scapegoating — projecting collective violence onto a single victim whose sacrifice temporarily restores social order. Girard argues that this sacrificial mechanism is the origin of both religion and culture.

In the KB, mimetic theory appears in two applications:

**Horizontal/Vertical tether framework** (via the [[avjexctajuc]] transcript): Jesse Michaels' interviewer maps Girard's mimesis to the "horizontal tether" — the axis of human attention directed toward other humans, their desires, and social status. The "vertical tether" is attention directed toward the Divine. The Christian cross is interpreted as the symbolic representation of this tension: the horizontal bar is mimesis, the vertical bar is transcendence. Healthy spiritual development requires weakening the horizontal mimetic attachment in favor of the vertical — structurally identical to [[meister-eckhart]]'s selflessness doctrine.

**Scapegoating and UAP**: [[rene-girard-scapegoating]] in the KB likely addresses how mimetic scapegoating dynamics appear in UAP disclosure contexts — the tendency to identify a single guilty party ("the government," "the military-industrial complex") as the carrier of collective blame.
