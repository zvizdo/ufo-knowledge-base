# Ultraterrestrial Hypothesis

The class of hypotheses proposing that non-human intelligences (NHI) encountered in UAP contexts are not visitors from other star systems but entities native to Earth or its immediate environment. The term "ultraterrestrial" distinguishes this position from the Extraterrestrial Hypothesis (ETH), which requires interstellar origin.

**Main variants**:
- **Subterranean** (→ [[subterranean-nhi-hypothesis]]): NHI evolved in or beneath the Earth — underground oceans, deep geological cavities, or artificial subsurface complexes. Explains why NHI might have a territorial stake in surface affairs.
- **Interdimensional** (→ [[extradimensional-beings]]): NHI exist in dimensions or branes adjacent to observable spacetime; their "craft" are dimensional intrusions rather than spacecraft.
- **Temporal** (→ [[time-travel-uap]]): NHI are future humans or evolved terrestrial descendants who have developed time travel; their apparent alienness is evolutionary drift over millennia.
- **Paraphysical** (→ [[consciousness-non-local]]): NHI are aspects of a consciousness field or informational substrate that underlies physical reality; not material beings at all.

**Key proponents**: Jacques Vallée's interdimensional hypothesis (1975) is the canonical version. [[john-keel]]'s "ultraterrestrial" coinage (1970) specifically argued against interstellar transit as unnecessary to explain the data. More recently, [[mac-tonnies]]'s "cryptoterrestrial hypothesis" (published posthumously) argued for an ancient co-evolutionary intelligence.

**Relationship to the ET Hypothesis**: The ultraterrestrial hypothesis doesn't require the NHI to be biological, to have originated elsewhere, or to have arrived recently. It allows for NHI that have always been here — which fits cases where NHI demonstrate specific interest in human territory, ecology, and genetic material rather than the behavior expected from dispassionate interstellar explorers.

**Evidential argument**: Proponents argue the ultraterrestrial framing better explains (a) NHI apparent familiarity with human psychology and mythology, (b) absence of clear physical transport vectors at the scale required for interstellar visitors, (c) the "trickster" phenomenology — NHI behavior that seems tuned to human perception rather than to purely physical goals.
