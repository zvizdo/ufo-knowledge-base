# Microwave Hearing Effect (Frey Effect)

The **microwave hearing effect** (also called the **Frey effect**, after researcher Allan Frey who first documented it in 1961) is a neurological phenomenon in which pulsed microwave radiation — even at low average power — creates auditory perceptions directly in the inner ear without passing through the normal acoustic pathway. The proposed mechanism: microwave pulses cause rapid thermoelastic expansion in the cochlear fluids (endolymph), producing pressure waves perceived as sound.

## Mechanism

- Pulsed microwave beam at frequencies of 200 MHz–3 GHz
- Very short pulse duration (nanoseconds) at high peak power, low average power
- Thermal expansion of cochlear fluid → mechanical pressure wave → perceived auditory signal
- Can produce clicking, buzzing, voice-like sounds, or tones depending on modulation

## Application to Havana Syndrome

[[eric-haseltine]] cites the microwave hearing effect as the most plausible mechanism for the directional sound component of [[2016-havana-syndrome]] (Anomalous Health Incidents). The room-specific onset (symptoms disappearing when leaving and returning on re-entry) is consistent with a targeted beam with a limited focal area. The effect could be produced by a backpack-portable device. Soviet/Russian research on high-power microwave weapons is well-documented since the 1970s ([[1976-moscow-embassy-microwave-incident]]).

## Application to Voice-of-God Technology

The microwave hearing effect is also the proposed mechanism for **[[voice-of-god-technology]]** — directed-energy systems that project voices or sounds directly into the target's auditory perception, without external speakers. Military interest in this application is documented; patents exist for directed-microwave voice-projection devices.

## Historical Context

- 1961: Allan Frey first documents the effect in humans at low power levels
- 1970s: Soviet Union beams US Moscow Embassy with microwaves; [[walter-stoessel]] later dies of rare blood cancer attributed to this exposure
- 1990s onwards: US military research into non-lethal weapons applications documented
- 2016–present: [[2016-havana-syndrome]] cases; directional sound is a consistent reported symptom

## Open Questions

- Does the microwave hearing effect at documented power levels fully account for the vestibular and cognitive damage seen in AHI cases, or is an additional mechanism required?
- Can the effect be produced at sufficient range and specificity to target a specific room in a building?
