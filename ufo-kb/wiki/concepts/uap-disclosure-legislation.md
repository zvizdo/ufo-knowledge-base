# UAP Disclosure Legislation

UAP disclosure legislation refers to the accumulated body of US Congressional action on the UAP phenomenon — primarily embedded in National Defense Authorization Acts (NDAAs) from 2020 onward — that has established reporting requirements, created oversight bodies, extended whistleblower protections, and most recently attempted to mandate the declassification and disclosure of UAP-related records.

**2020 NDAA UAP Task Force**: The 2020 NDAA directed the Department of Defense to establish a UAP Task Force (UAPTF) to detect, identify, and attribute UAP. This was the first statutory UAP mandate since the end of [[project-blue-book]] in 1969.

**2023 NDAA AARO**: The 2022–23 period brought the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), with broader mandate including cross-domain (air, sea, space) UAP tracking and a historical records review. Subsequent NDAAs added reporting requirements and Congressional briefing mandates.

**UAP Disclosure Act provisions**: Senator Chuck Schumer and other sponsors introduced UAP Disclosure Act language in 2023, modeled on the JFK Records Act — mandating a UAP Records Review Board with authority to declassify records from across agencies, with a presumption of disclosure. This language was partially incorporated into the 2024 NDAA but with key provisions weakened under pressure from the executive branch.

**Whistleblower protections**: A critical legislative development was the passage of protections for UAP whistleblowers within cleared programs — allowing individuals with first-hand knowledge to report to Congress without violating classification obligations. This provision directly enabled [[david-grusch]]'s protected Congressional testimony in 2023.

**New Paradigm Institute context**: [[new-paradigm-institute]] (Danny Sheehan's organization) operates explicitly within this legislative framework — authorized under UAP statute to recommend board members for oversight bodies, advocating for stronger disclosure requirements.

**Ongoing limitation**: As of 2026, no legislation has mandated actual public release of UAP materials beyond reports. Classified materials remain classified; the AARO reviews have been criticized for scope and access limitations.
