# Satellite Surveillance

Satellite surveillance encompasses intelligence collection using orbital platforms, primarily through:
- **IMINT** (Imagery Intelligence): optical/radar imaging satellites (KH-series, commercial imagery)
- **SIGINT** (Signals Intelligence): signals collection satellites operated by the NSA and NRO
- **MASINT** (Measurement and Signature Intelligence): nuclear detonation detection, atmospheric monitoring

**Key US programs**: The National Reconnaissance Office (NRO) operates US military and intelligence satellites. Major historical programs include the CORONA photoreconnaissance satellites (1960–1972, recently declassified) and their successors in the KH (Keyhole) series. The NSA operates signals collection satellites under programs that remain classified.

**Pine Gap**: The Joint Defence Facility Pine Gap ([[pine-gap]]), operated jointly by Australia and the US, is one of the most important ground stations for signals intelligence satellites — a critical node in the global SIGINT architecture. Its isolation in Central Australia provides optimal geometry for satellite operations covering the Indo-Pacific.

**Dual-use constellations**: Modern dual-use considerations involve commercial satellite constellations. [[spacex]]'s Starlink, while primarily commercial, has been integrated into US military communications and has inherent dual-use intelligence-collection utility. The same orbital coverage that enables broadband internet also enables passive RF collection and potentially optical observation.

**UAP correlation**: UAP events near classified satellite facilities — including [[pine-gap]], [[area-51]], and [[wright-patterson-air-force-base]] — are frequently noted in UAP research. The correlation between UAP activity and sensitive technology nodes is cited as evidence either for NHI interest in human surveillance infrastructure or for human testing of classified systems near their own secure facilities.
