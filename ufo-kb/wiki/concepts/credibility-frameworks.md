# Credibility Frameworks

**Type**: meta-framework / lens  
**Applied to**: every person and document page in the KB

How witness and source reliability is weighed in UAP discourse. No numeric scoring in v1 — credibility is captured qualitatively per page. Key dimensions:

## Dimensions

### Access
- **Documented access** (clearances, duty assignments, position on record) vs. **claimed access** (self-reported without independent corroboration).
- A USAF launch officer present in the command capsule during an incident ([[robert-salas]]) carries more weight than a secondhand account relayed from an unnamed source.

### Firsthand vs. Hearsay
- **Firsthand**: witness was physically present at the event described.
- **Hearsay chain**: track how many degrees removed the claim is from a direct witness.
- The KB captures this in `firsthand_claims[]` frontmatter on person pages, and with attribution language ("Salas states" vs. "Salas heard from X that...").

### Corroboration Count
- Single-witness claims are flagged; multi-witness or instrumented events (radar + visual + multiple crew) receive greater weight.
- Example: [[2004-nimitz-tic-tac]] — multiple pilots, shipboard radar, FLIR footage, independent intercept crew.

### Track Record
- Has the witness been caught fabricating? Has prior testimony held up under scrutiny?
- Consistency across interviews over decades is a positive signal; significant mutation of claims is a negative one.

### Institutional Affiliation
- Official (military, intelligence, government) witnesses carry institutional accountability but also have NDAs and career incentives to stay silent.
- Civilian researchers and journalists are more free but lack insider access.
- Knowing which "type" a source is (see [[source-type-frameworks]]) informs how to weight the claim.

### Motivation Analysis
- Is there a financial incentive (book sales, speaking circuit)?
- Is there a career incentive to *not* speak (NDA, security clearance retention)?
- The latter is often a positive credibility signal: witnesses who lose nothing by staying silent and yet still speak.

## High-Credibility Signals in This KB

- Military officer, documented duty assignment, corroborated by crew — e.g., [[robert-salas]], [[walt-feele]], [[david-fravor]]
- Academic with peer-reviewed record who risks reputational cost — e.g., [[james-mcdonald]], [[beatriz-villarroel]]
- Government insider with verifiable position and FOIA-corroborated claims — e.g., [[harold-malmgren]], [[lue-elizondo]]

## Low-Credibility Signals

- Anonymous or pseudonymous, no corroborating documentary record
- Significant mutation of core claims across interviews
- Financial incentive that explains the story being told
- Claims that require uniquely privileged access with no documentary trail

## Applying This Framework

Every person page ends with a Connections section that implicitly applies this lens. When a page explicitly notes credibility, the language used is analytical ("firsthand, documented access, corroborated by commander") rather than adjectival ("highly credible"). The framework is a lens, not a verdict.
