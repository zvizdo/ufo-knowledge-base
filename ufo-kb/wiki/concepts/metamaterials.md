# Metamaterials

Materials engineered to have electromagnetic, acoustic, or mechanical properties not achievable through natural material composition — achieved through precise geometric structuring at sub-wavelength scales. In mainstream materials science, metamaterials enable negative refractive index (superlenses), cloaking at specific wavelengths, and acoustic wave manipulation.

**In the UAP context**, "metamaterials" has become shorthand for alleged recovered UAP materials exhibiting properties that suggest manufacture by non-human or advanced-human technology:
- **Isotopic anomalies**: Materials with isotopic ratios that differ from any naturally occurring or industrially produced sample on Earth. This would indicate manufacture in a different stellar environment or via unknown isotopic separation processes.
- **Bi-layered composites**: Recovered fragments allegedly consisting of extremely thin alternating layers of different metals (e.g., bismuth and magnesium) at ratios achievable only through processes more precise than current terrestrial manufacturing.
- **Anomalous hardness/flexibility combinations**: Materials simultaneously harder than steel and flexible in ways crystalline metals are not.
- **EM absorption profiles**: Some alleged samples reportedly absorb or redirect electromagnetic radiation in ways consistent with radar cloaking.

**Key KB reference**: [[hal-puthoff]] has described samples allegedly recovered from crash retrieval programs as metamaterial composites. The [[tech-artifacts]] concept in the KB covers the broader category of anomalous physical evidence. The [[casimir-effect]] page discusses vacuum energy applications that might explain some metamaterial-level energy behaviors.

**Epistemics**: No verified metamaterial sample with confirmed UAP provenance is in the public domain. The most-cited alleged samples (bismuth/magnesium layered material from Art's Parts collection) have been analyzed privately by multiple researchers including Jacques Vallée, with mixed findings. Chain of custody for these materials is contested.
