# Mothman

The Mothman is an anomalous entity reported by dozens of witnesses near Point Pleasant, West Virginia, between November 1966 and December 1967. Witnesses described a large (5–7 feet tall) humanoid figure with enormous bat-like wings and bright red glowing eyes, capable of flying at speeds reportedly exceeding 100 mph. Sightings clustered in the McClintic Wildlife Management Area (the "TNT Area," a former WWII ammunition storage site) and along the Ohio River.

**Silver Bridge collapse**: The Mothman sightings ended abruptly on December 15, 1967, the night the Silver Bridge at Point Pleasant collapsed into the Ohio River, killing 46 people. The coincidence of the sightings' endpoint with the disaster became the central narrative of John Keel's investigation — the suggestion that the Mothman was either prophetically connected to or causally involved in the disaster.

**John Keel's investigation**: [[john-keel]] investigated the Point Pleasant phenomena and published *The Mothman Prophecies* in 1975, placing the Mothman within his broader "Ultraterrestrial" framework. Keel argued the Mothman was not a biological creature but a manifestation of the same phenomenon producing UFOs, Men in Black, and poltergeist activity — the Ultraterrestrial control system interacting with the local human population. The 2002 film *The Mothman Prophecies* dramatized Keel's account.

**TNT Area context**: The McClintic Wildlife Management Area where many sightings occurred contains abandoned WWII ordnance bunkers. The presence of industrial chemical contamination, underground structures, and electromagnetic anomalies in the area has been cited in both Keel's analysis and later investigators as potentially relevant to the phenomena.

**Broader pattern**: The Mothman fits into a class of entity encounters — Men in Black, the Flatwoods Monster — where anomalous non-human entities appear in cluster patterns preceding or accompanying major events, suggesting to researchers like Keel a reactive or warning function.
