# Great Year

## Definition

The **Great Year** (also Platonic Year) is the period of Earth's **axial precession** — the slow wobble of Earth's rotational axis that causes the position of vernal equinox against the constellations to shift. One complete precession cycle takes approximately **25,772 years** (commonly rounded to 26,000 years in the ancient mysteries literature).

This cycle causes the "age" of the zodiac to shift: we are currently transitioning from the Age of Pisces to the Age of Aquarius. The Sphinx's alignment with Leo at the vernal equinox points to ~10,500 BC — at the start of the Great Year where the Younger Dryas occurred.

## Application in the KB

### Astronomical Dating
The Great Year is central to [[precession-encoded-monuments]]: ancient sites were constructed to mark precessional positions, encoding the date of construction in the stars to which their chambers align. The [[great-sphinx]] aligns with Leo at ~10,500 BC; the King's Chamber of the [[giza-plateau]] aligns with Orion.

### Binary Star Companion Model
[[matt-lroy]] takes the Great Year one step further: he proposes the ~25,000-year orbit of the [[binary-star-companion]] *coincides with* the precessional cycle. On this view:
- Half-cycle = ~12,500 years = binary companion perihelion
- Each perihelion = peak solar stress = CME events = civilizational reset
- The [[older-dryas]] (~14,500 BP) and [[younger-dryas-impact|Younger Dryas]] (~12,000 BP) bracket the most recent perihelion

If this is correct, ancient civilizations that encoded the Great Year in their monuments were also encoding knowledge of the binary companion cycle — leaving warnings for future humanity.

### Yuga Cycles
The Vedic [[yuga-cycles]] framework describes civilization rising and falling across vast time scales. Lroy correlates these with the Great Year / binary companion cycle: each Yuga transition corresponds to a perihelion event.

## Randall Carlson's Detailed Structure (R7oyZGW99os)

[[randall-carlson]] provides the most arithmetically explicit account of the Great Year in this KB:

**Ancient canonical value**: **25,920 years** (modern measurement: 25,772 years; Carlson notes we don't know if the rate was constant over a full cycle).

**Subdivisions and their Platonic solid correspondences**:

| Division | Years | Platonic Solid (degrees) |
|---|---|---|
| 1/36 (1 decan) | 720 | Tetrahedron (720°) |
| 2/36 (2 decans) | 1,440 | Octahedron (1,440°) |
| 1/12 (Platonic month) | 2,160 | Cube (2,160°) |
| 1/10 (Saros base) | 3,600 | Icosahedron (3,600°) |
| 1/4 (Platonic season) | 6,480 | Dodecahedron (6,480°) |
| 1/2 | 12,960 | — (reversal of seasons) |
| Full cycle | 25,920 | — |

The astrological ages are Platonic months: each zodiacal sign corresponds to one 2,160-year period. We are currently transitioning from the Age of Pisces to the Age of Aquarius.

**Human lifespan in the Great Year**: 25,920 years ÷ 360 (sacred days) = 72 years. One human lifespan is one "day" of the Great Year.

**Saros cycle**: The Chaldean/Babylonian Saros = 3,600 years — matching the icosahedron (3,600°).

**Precessional motion**: ~50 arc-seconds per year (Carlson notes this is the ancient canonical value; modern measurement is slightly less).
