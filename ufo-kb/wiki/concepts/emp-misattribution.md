# EMP Misattribution

**Type**: claims-thesis  
**Category**: recurring-claims / disclosure-narratives

The EMP cover story has been deployed twice — 57 years apart — to attribute UFO-caused missile shutdowns at [[malmstrom-air-force-base]] to mundane electromagnetic phenomena. The recurrence of the exact same explanation framework (EMP) across two different eras suggests either a maintained institutional playbook or independent reinvention of the same implausible explanation.

## First Deployment: 1967

[[robert-hiper]] told [[robert-low]] (handwritten note, possessed by [[robert-salas]]) that the USAF believed EMP from nuclear testing caused the shutdowns. 

**Why this fails**: The Department of Energy's records (checked by Salas and James Klotz) show no nuclear tests occurred on March 16 or March 24, 1967.

## Second Deployment: 2024

Wall Street Journal article by [[joel-shectman]], allegedly sourced from [[sean-kirkpatrick]] (former [[aaro]] director), claimed a non-nuclear EMP test device was wheeled up to Malmstrom and used as an experimental test on live missiles — without the knowledge of officers like Salas.

**Why this fails** (Salas's 14-point rebuttal):
1. The specific EMP model cited was not operational until 1971–73 — four years after the incident
2. Generating the required ~6 million volts required a large, non-mobile superstructure (visible in the WSJ's own diagram as a ~60-foot test stand) taking at least 3 days to construct — impossible to conceal from base personnel
3. The Minuteman I missiles used triple-shielded 8–9 inch cable bundles specifically designed to resist EMI (electromagnetic interference)
4. [[boeing]]'s own investigation confirmed the guidance failures were caused by an *external* signal but could not explain how it penetrated the shielding
5. No test of this kind would be conducted on live, operational nuclear missiles with uninformed officers present
6. Topside guards would have reported the construction of a 60-foot test stand — but instead reported a UFO

## Third Cover Story: Air Force Hazing Rituals (2024)

The same WSJ article also introduced a second non-EMP explanation: that widespread UFO mythology across dozens of nuclear witnesses spanning 1947–present is the product of **Air Force hazing rituals**. This account:
- Cannot explain 1949 Air Force emergency meeting records (FBI, CIA, Army CIC, ONI present)
- Cannot explain the 1947 [[nathan-twining]] memo declaring UFOs "not visionary or fictitious"
- Cannot explain international civilian nuclear grid cases ([[1995-bariloche-argentina]], Fukushima, Japan)
- Cannot explain documented harassment of witnesses like [[bob-jacobs]] (mailbox bombing, career destruction) — Michaels's question: "So Jacobs and all these other nuclear base employees have been hazed for 70 years by our own government. For what?"

**Sourcing flag**: One primary source for the hazing claim is [[susan-goff]], a Booz Allen Hamilton DoD psychological operations consultant — an undisclosed conflict of interest that [[jesse-michaels]] frames as making the article itself a psychological operation.

## Significance

The same cover story appearing in 1967 and 2024 — using EMP, a plausible-sounding but technically incoherent explanation, now supplemented with the hazing narrative — suggests a maintained institutional response template rather than independent analysis. The involvement of a DoD psyops contractor ([[booz-allen-hamilton]]) as an undisclosed source adds a layer of institutional motive not present in the 1967 deployment.
