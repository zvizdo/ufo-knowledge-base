# Cellular Cognition

## Definition

The hypothesis — supported by experimental evidence — that cognition, memory, and learning are not localized to neurons but distributed throughout biological organisms at the cellular level. Individual cells and even sub-cellular systems exhibit learning and memory capacities.

## Key Evidence

### Planaria Experiment
The most cited demonstration: planaria (flatworms) are trained to navigate a maze. The worm is then cut in half, and the head (with the brain/neurons) is discarded. The tail regrows a new head with no inherited neurons. When retested, the regrown animal still knows the maze at above-chance rates. The memory was stored in the body's cells, not the neurons.

### Single-Cell Classical Conditioning
Single cells (outside of any organism) can be classically conditioned in laboratory settings — demonstrating that stimulus-response memory is a cellular-level phenomenon, not an emergent property of neural networks alone.

### Plant Learning
Plants can be classically conditioned — root systems respond to learned stimuli in ways that parallel animal conditioning responses.

## Clinical Application: The Listening Cure

[[chris-gilbert]]'s clinical practice ("The Listening Cure") is grounded in cellular cognition:
- Each organ may have "a mind of its own" — its own stored memory, stress responses, and adaptive patterns
- Physical symptoms are sometimes the body's cellular memory expressing itself
- Treatment involves "listening" to the body's cellular voice rather than suppressing symptoms

This gives Gilbert's approach a scientific foundation beyond metaphor: the claim that "the body knows" is literally supported by planaria experiments and single-cell conditioning data.

## Implications for NHI Cognition

In the context of [[exo-psychology]], cellular cognition matters for:
- NHI organisms with distributed cognition (no central brain) are biologically credible
- A von Neumann probe or dark matter entity could have distributed information processing without any human-analogous neural architecture
- The "binding problem" (why consciousness feels unified) may be resolved differently in organisms where cognition is already distributed

## Relationship to Transmission Theory of Consciousness

Cellular cognition supports the [[transmission-theory-consciousness]] in a subtle way: if memory can be stored outside neurons, then consciousness may not be fundamentally neural — which is consistent with the view that the brain is a receiver rather than a generator.
