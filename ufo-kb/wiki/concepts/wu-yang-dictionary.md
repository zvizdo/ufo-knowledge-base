# Wu-Yang Dictionary

A 1975 correspondence table published by Ta-You Wu and [[chen-yang|C.N. Yang]], building on earlier work by Yang and [[jim-simons]], establishing the formal equivalence between:
- **Physics side**: gauge fields in Yang-Mills theory and general relativity (connections on principal fiber bundles)
- **Mathematics side**: the geometric structures of connections and curvature in differential geometry (fiber bundles, holonomy groups)

**Significance**: The Wu-Yang dictionary showed that physicists and mathematicians had been independently developing the same mathematical structures — the Standard Model's electromagnetic, weak, and strong forces are, in modern language, connections on U(1), SU(2), and SU(3) principal fiber bundles respectively. General relativity's curvature (gravity) is a connection on the frame bundle. This unity — all fundamental forces as geometric structures — is a foundational result in modern theoretical physics.

**Simons connection**: The dictionary is partly the product of conversations between [[chen-yang]] and [[jim-simons]] (the mathematician/hedge-fund founder) at Stony Brook University in the early 1970s. Simons was developing Chern-Simons theory; Yang was at Stony Brook after leaving the Institute for Advanced Study. This collaboration is cited in [[xnxasfyHtfo]] as part of the lineage from pure mathematics to the physics of the Standard Model.

**[[eric-weinstein]] relevance**: Weinstein's "Geometric Unity" proposal attempts to extend this dictionary — to find a unified geometric framework that includes gravity and the Standard Model. His critique of mainstream string theory in [[physics-lockdown]] context is partly that the field abandoned this geometric-unity approach in favor of algebraic string compactification without adequate justification.
