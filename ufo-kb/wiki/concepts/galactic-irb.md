# Galactic IRB

A conceptual framework proposed by [[john-blitch]] in his 2025 interview with [[jesse-michaels]]: the reason NHI civilizations do not simply land on the White House lawn is not inability but **ethical constraint** — a galactic analog to a human Institutional Review Board (IRB) that governs experimental protocols.

## The Core Argument

In human research, an IRB exists to ensure that experiments involving subjects are conducted ethically — including a requirement for **informed consent** and a prohibition against **biasing the experiment** by revealing the study design to participants. Blitch applies this framework to the NHI-humanity relationship:

> "Maybe there's a galactic IRB — institutional review board — that's really fascinating. So it's almost like you can't bias the experiment, so you can't show yourself."

If humanity is the subject of a long-running biological/sociological experiment (see [[nhi-genetic-modification]]), then overt NHI disclosure would:
1. Invalidate all experimental results collected to date
2. Violate the conditions of informed consent (in reverse — the *subjects* cannot know they're being studied without contaminating outcomes)
3. Constitute an ethical breach under whatever galactic oversight framework exists

The framework explains why NHI might remain hidden even when they technically *could* reveal themselves — and why contact, when it does occur, tends to be oblique, individualized, or deniable.

## Relationship to Fermi Paradox

Blitch explicitly frames this against the Fermi Paradox: the paradox assumes that any sufficiently advanced civilization *would want* to reveal itself openly. He argues this assumption is wrong — just as special forces operators sometimes want to be seen (blowing up a bridge) and sometimes don't (supporting insurgencies covertly), NHI motivations for visibility are situational, not uniform.

The IRB framework provides a positive ethical reason for non-disclosure that does not require assuming NHI indifference or absence.

## Connection to Other Frameworks

- **[[threebody-problem-nhi]]** — the Dark Forest analogy offers a different (threat-based) reason for non-disclosure; the Galactic IRB offers an ethical-experiment reason. Both produce the same observable outcome.
- **[[nhi-critical-mass-strategy]]** — if NHI are waiting for humanity to reach some threshold before formal contact, that is consistent with an experimental protocol with defined endpoints
- **[[control-system-hypothesis]]** — Vallée's framework posits NHI as a control system managing human development; Galactic IRB provides the institutional structure under which such a system might operate
- **[[nhi-open-door-problem]]** — the challenge of revealing oneself without precipitating chaos; IRB framework suggests NHI have resolved this problem through protocol rather than inaction
