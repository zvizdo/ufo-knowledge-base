# Mars Nuclear War Thesis

A claims-thesis advanced primarily by [[john-brandenberg]] (plasma physicist, formerly [[lawrence-livermore-national-lab]]) that **isotopic anomalies in Martian atmospheric data**, especially elevated Xenon-129, are best explained by **ancient, large-scale nuclear events** of either natural-fusion origin or artificial origin. The Viking-mission gas-chromatograph data and follow-on remote spectroscopy provide the empirical anchor.

The thesis has two strengths and two weaknesses cited across the discourse:

**Strengths:**
- The Xe-129 isotopic signature does diverge from terrestrial-atmosphere baselines in a way Brandenberg argues fits a fission-product cascade.
- Brandenberg's plasma-physics credentials place his interpretation outside obvious crank-tier framings.

**Weaknesses:**
- Mainstream planetary-science interpretations attribute the same data to non-explosive geophysical processes (mantle outgassing, atmospheric escape) without invoking nuclear events.
- Brandenberg's published outlets are heterodox; the thesis has not been adopted in peer-reviewed planetary-science venues.

In KB-internal terms, the thesis sits in the same recurring-claims-cluster as the [[lost-civilization-hypothesis]] applied to Mars rather than Earth.
