# Vitrification Evidence

## Definition

**Vitrification**: the conversion of a material (typically stone) into glass or a glass-like amorphous solid through extreme heat. When stone is heated beyond its melting point, then rapidly cooled, it can form obsidian-like glassy surfaces.

In the ancient mysteries / catastrophism discourse, vitrified stone sites are cited as physical evidence of heat events far beyond what any known ancient technology or natural process (short of a nearby nuclear detonation or massive solar plasma event) could produce.

## Key Facts

- **Basalt melting point**: ~1,200–1,500°C (but basalt at Kepkesi is hardness 7/10; Lroy cites >2,000°C required)
- **Granite melting point**: ~1,215–1,260°C
- **Ordinary fire**: ~600–900°C — cannot melt basalt
- **Lightning**: can reach ~30,000°C locally but insufficient volume
- **Plasma discharge (CME)**: theoretically capable of sustained extreme temperatures over large areas

## Primary Sites

### [[kepkesi]], Turkey
[[matt-lroy]]'s most extensively investigated vitrification site. Giant basalt blocks with machine-cut surfaces and vitrified faces. [[robert-schoch]] confirmed the vitrification on a joint site visit — providing expert geological corroboration that the melted surfaces are real and anomalous.

### Lake Van region generally
Multiple sites in the [[lake-van]] area show similar patterns.

### Glastonbury / Scottish Iron Age hill forts (broader literature)
Dozens of Iron Age hill forts in Scotland show vitrified stone ramparts. Mainstream explanation: deliberate burning of timber-laced stone walls (possibly ritual, possibly defensive). Alternative: plasma discharge events.

## Explanatory Frameworks

### Volcanic
[[mount-nemrut]] (near Lake Van) erupted violently during the Older/Younger Dryas period. Volcanic heat could produce vitrification locally, but the geographic spread of the phenomenon argues against purely local volcanic causation.

### Plasma Discharge ([[robert-schoch]])
Schoch's [[plasma-discharge-theory]] holds that massive CME events create atmospheric plasma discharges capable of sustained extreme heat over wide areas. The surface-only melting pattern (exterior faces vitrified, stone interior intact) is consistent with directed external heat rather than internal volcanic heat.

### Nuclear
Some alternative researchers propose nuclear weapons or nuclear-powered technologies in antiquity. Lroy does not advocate this but acknowledges the heat requirement exceeds conventional explanations.

## Open Questions

- Why are some surfaces vitrified but not adjacent surfaces of the same material?
- Is the vitrification pattern directional (evidence of directed energy) or random?
- Can the observed vitrification at Kepkesi be independently dated (when did it occur)?
