# Van Allen Belt

Two concentric donut-shaped regions of high-energy charged particles surrounding Earth, trapped by the planet's magnetic field. The inner belt (1,000–12,000 km altitude) contains high-energy protons; the outer belt (13,000–60,000 km) contains electrons. Named after physicist James Van Allen, discoverer in 1958.

## UAP/UAP-Physics Relevance

- Cited as one of the barriers to conventional biological space travel (radiation dosage is lethal for extended exposure)
- Electromagnetic interactions with the belts are part of the broader UAP-geomagnetic correlation discussion
- Some UAP physics hypotheses invoke magnetohydrodynamic interaction with Earth's field as a propulsion mechanism
