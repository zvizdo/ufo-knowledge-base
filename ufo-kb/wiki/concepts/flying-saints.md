# Flying Saints

Historical accounts of Catholic and Orthodox saints reported to have levitated or flown — documented across multiple witnesses and, in some cases, argued by religious scholars to meet the evidentiary standard of verifiable history.

## Key Figures

- **St. Joseph of Cupertino** (17th century) — the most extensively documented; hundreds of eyewitness accounts of levitation across different locations and circumstances
- **St. Teresa of Ávila** — documented levitation accounts; she reportedly tried to suppress them
- **St. Francis of Assisi** — some accounts suggest levitation; less well-documented than Joseph
- **St. Maximos Kafsokalyvis** — flying monk of [[mount-athos]]; venerated for levitation, burning his huts, and life as a wilderness recluse; directly relevant to [[ralph-moat-larson]]'s Mount Athos time-travel dream character Odius Maximus

## Scholarly Treatment

In [[ATJwqp5twAg]], [[ralph-moat-larson]] cites:

- **Yale professor Carlos Eire**, *They Flew* — entered the subject skeptically; concluded that eyewitness testimony for flying saints meets the evidentiary threshold used for historical events (e.g., the War of 1812)
- A religious studies professor at UNC Wilmington who worked through St. Joseph of Cupertino's Vatican files and independently concurs
- **Jeffrey Kripal** (Rice University) gave a lecture on flying saints at MIT; a senior Skunk Works R&D executive attended and was "very interested" — suggesting aerospace-adjacent interest in the phenomenon
