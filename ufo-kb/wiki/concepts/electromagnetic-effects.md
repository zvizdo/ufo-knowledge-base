# Electromagnetic Effects

Electromagnetic effects refers to the category of instrument disruption, electrical system failure, and magnetic anomalies consistently reported in association with close UAP proximity. The pattern is one of the most reproducible and cross-referenced physical effect categories in the UAP evidence base.

**Compass and navigation failure**: The most commonly reported EM effect is compass deviation or complete malfunction — compasses spinning or stuck in the presence of UAP. [[1945-flight-19]] is the canonical aviation case: the final radio transmissions from the missing TBM Avengers suggest complete compass failure and spatial disorientation during conditions consistent with electromagnetic interference.

**Aircraft engine stall**: Multiple documented cases involve aircraft engines — both piston and jet — experiencing stall or power reduction during UAP proximity. The pattern appears in civilian, military, and commercial aviation reports from the 1950s onward.

**Radio and communication blackout**: Radio communication failures during UAP encounters are among the most consistently reported effects and have been documented in military aviation encounters, including the [[2004-nimitz-tic-tac]] incidents and Cold War-era pilot encounters.

**Vehicle electrical effects**: Ground vehicle encounters with UAP frequently involve complete electrical failure — headlights dying, ignition cutting out, electronics going blank — followed by restoration after the UAP departs. These effects have been documented in thousands of civilian close encounter reports.

**Physical mechanism**: The proposed physical mechanism for UAP EM effects is unclear. Hypotheses include:
- Intense electromagnetic field generation by UAP propulsion/power systems
- Plasma sheath effects (ionized air around a rapidly-moving object interfering with electronics)
- Directed electromagnetic pulse (EMP) either intentional or as operational byproduct
- Gravitational or space-time distortion effects on electronic systems

**Scientific significance**: EM effects are among the most potentially verifiable UAP physical signatures — if documented with calibrated instruments before, during, and after an encounter, the field strength, frequency, and directionality could constrain the physical mechanism significantly.
