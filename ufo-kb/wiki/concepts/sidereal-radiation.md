# Sidereal Radiation

A concept [[thomas-townsend-brown]] developed over his lifetime, based on his observation that the Sun and Moon's positions produced measurable effects on the output of his gravitator experiments. He built devices he called **electrometers** to detect these fluctuations, finding that anomalous readings correlated with the **sidereal calendar** (a calendar based on stellar positions, not the solar calendar).

## The Observation

Brown's gravitator showed minor but consistent variations in thrust output that correlated with the Sun and Moon's gravitational influence — positions where the local gravitational field is slightly perturbed by the combined mass of nearby celestial bodies. This was not merely "he thought he saw something"; he built specialized instrumentation to measure it and found repeating patterns correlating with sidereal timing.

He continued this electrometer work on [[catalina-island]] until the end of his life.

## The Gravitational Constant Connection

This observation connects to a broader anomaly noted by [[rupert-sheldrake]]: the **gravitational constant (Big G)** varies from laboratory to laboratory and from day to day in a pattern that no standard theory accounts for. If G were actually fluctuating — rather than measurement error — it would validate Brown's intuition that local gravitational field strength is influenced by larger astronomical configurations.

Sheldrake: "What if G were actually changing? There's already evidence that it changes throughout the day and throughout the year." The international committee standardizes G by averaging across labs — effectively suppressing the variance as noise.

## Anonymous Harvard Physicist

In [[RTEWLSTyUic]], Jesse Michaels reveals that a prominent but anonymous Harvard physicist — who requested anonymity because of the "Brown stigma" — was actively researching sidereal radiation and described the effect as still "really cutting it up." This physicist, interested in finding extraterrestrial life, told Michaels they found Brown's sidereal radiation work the most intriguing unsolved thread in his oeuvre.

## Implications

If sidereal radiation is real, it implies:
1. The gravitational field is not constant but varies with astronomical configuration — consistent with a vacuum medium ([[extended-electrodynamics]]) that transmits gravitational information from distant mass concentrations.
2. Brown's electrogravitics may have been sensitive to this background gravitational radiation in ways that standard physics does not account for.
3. The variation in the gravitational constant across different lab measurements globally is a known unexplained anomaly — possibly the same phenomenon.
