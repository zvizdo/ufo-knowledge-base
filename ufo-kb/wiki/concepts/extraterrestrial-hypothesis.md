# Extraterrestrial Hypothesis

The Extraterrestrial Hypothesis (ETH) holds that UAP/UFO phenomena are best explained as physical craft and biological beings originating from other star systems — "little green men" in the popular vernacular, extraterrestrial biological entities (EBEs) in official language. The ETH has been the dominant popular framing since the 1947 Roswell era and is the implicit assumption behind most congressional UAP legislation and disclosure-movement rhetoric.

**Origin**: The ETH crystallized in the late 1940s as the natural inference from the physical appearance of craft and recovered beings (per alleged crash-retrieval claims). The [[1947-roswell]] event and the National Security State architecture built around it assumed something physical arrived from outside Earth.

**Critiques**: [[jacques-vallee]] is the most rigorous critic of the ETH as a *sufficient* explanation. His arguments (developed in *Passport to Magonia*, 1969, and subsequent works) include:
- The frequency of UAP events is too high for interstellar craft — thousands of sightings globally suggest a non-logistical phenomenon
- UAP behavior (instant acceleration, shape-shifting, appearing and disappearing) is inconsistent with physical spacecraft
- The historical record shows anomalous phenomena predating the space age, implying the phenomenon is not keyed to human technological development
- The psychological and perceptual effects on witnesses suggest a consciousness-interface component, not purely physical contact

**Alternatives**: The ETH's main competitors in the KB include:
- [[ultraterrestrial-hypothesis]] — NHI are indigenous to Earth (subterranean, interdimensional, or temporal)
- [[interdimensional-hypothesis]] — NHI transit dimensions rather than space
- [[time-travel-uap]] — NHI are future humans or post-humans
- [[extradimensional-beings]] — NHI exist in adjacent branes or dimensions

**Official posture**: Current US government UAP discourse (AARO, UAPDA) uses the term "non-human intelligence" or "non-human biologics" rather than "extraterrestrial," deliberately remaining agnostic about origin — which some UAP researchers interpret as leaving room for the non-ETH hypotheses.
