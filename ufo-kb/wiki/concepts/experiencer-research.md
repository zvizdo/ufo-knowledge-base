# Experiencer Research

Experiencer research is the study of individuals who report anomalous contact experiences with non-human intelligence or entities. It is broader than the classical [[abduction-research]] frame: where abduction research focuses on involuntary, often traumatic encounters recovered via hypnotic regression, experiencer research also encompasses voluntary contact protocols, ongoing non-abductive contact, near-death experiences with NHI elements, shamanic or visionary contact, and the full CE (Close Encounter) spectrum.

**Distinction from abduction research**: The shift in terminology from "abduction" to "experiencer" reflects a change in framing — from victims of forced contact to participants in a spectrum of anomalous experiences. This framing is associated with researchers like [[john-mack]] (Harvard), who emphasized the transformative and spiritually significant dimensions of contact rather than the trauma-and-medical-procedure narrative dominant in earlier literature.

**Institutional context**: The academic legitimacy of experiencer research has been contested since its inception. [[john-mack]]'s work at Harvard led to a formal peer review of his conduct, though he retained his position. The [[free-foundation]] (Foundation for Research into Extraterrestrial Encounters) is a nonprofit that conducts structured surveys of experiencers and produces quantitative findings on contact phenomena.

**Methods**: Studies use structured surveys, clinical interviews, hypnotic regression (with appropriate caveats), and increasingly neurological and physiological assessment of experiencers to look for correlates.

**KB relevance**: Experiencer accounts are the primary evidentiary base for claims about NHI contact phenomenology, NHI biological interest in humanity, and the subjective characteristics of contact events. The field sits at the intersection of psychology, parapsychology, and UAP research.
