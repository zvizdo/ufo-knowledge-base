# Reverse Engineering Hypothesis

The reverse engineering hypothesis holds that the US government, military, and/or defense contractors have recovered non-human technology (primarily from alleged crash-retrieval events) and have been reverse-engineering it — attempting to understand and replicate the physics and engineering underlying UAP propulsion, materials, and possibly other capabilities.

**Relationship to crash retrieval**: The reverse engineering hypothesis is downstream of the crash-retrieval claim: if non-human craft have been recovered (cf. [[1947-roswell]], [[crash-retrieval-research]]), the logical military response would be systematic reverse engineering, particularly given Cold War pressures to assess whether the technology was Soviet.

**Defense contractor role**: In this framework, reverse engineering would most plausibly have been offloaded to defense contractors operating under Unacknowledged Special Access Programs ([[special-access-programs]]), using contractor compartmentalization to achieve even deeper secrecy than government programs. [[david-grusch]] testified that private aerospace companies have been custodians of recovered materials. [[soft-disclosure-civilian-observation]] cites the reverse engineering hypothesis as the explanation for why advanced aerospace physics appears in civilian contexts without traceable academic genealogy.

**Physics implications**: If accurate, the reverse engineering hypothesis implies that classified physics knowledge diverged from public physics — that theoretical frameworks for advanced propulsion (gravity modification, inertial frame manipulation) have been developed in classified contexts without publication. This is the explanation offered for why [[electrogravitics]] and related research disappeared from public literature in the 1950s.
