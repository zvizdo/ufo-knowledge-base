# Maskirovka

## Definition

The Russian military doctrine of strategic deception — using disinformation, camouflage, and psychological manipulation to make adversaries believe false things about your capabilities. The goal is both to hide real capabilities and to exaggerate (or fabricate) other capabilities to achieve deterrence or confusion.

From the Cyrillic маскировка — "masking" or "disguise." A core pillar of Soviet and Russian military doctrine since at least the Second World War.

## Application to UAP Discourse

[[eric-haseltine]] applies the Maskirovka frame as one competing hypothesis in UAP analysis:

> "It would be entirely consistent with Russian modus operandi to create fake UAP to mess with adversary minds."

Haseltine describes this as consistent with Russian behavior without definitively asserting it is happening. The technical mechanisms are available:
- Free-air plasma (Q-switched laser) creates glowing orbs with "impossible" kinematic signatures
- [[navy-plasma-decoy-patent]] establishes that plasma-based aircraft-shaped decoys are technically feasible
- Large balloons towed by small drones (per Cliff Wong, drone expert consulted by Haseltine) create convincing orb illusions
- Russia has been technically superior in directed energy for decades

Haseltine frames the relevant principle via Patton: "Weapons change, but man who uses them changes not at all. To defeat an enemy, you don't defeat their weapons, you defeat their brain and their heart."

## Connection to Havana Syndrome

[[2016-havana-syndrome]] is the strongest real-world example of Russia using directed energy for cognitive/psychological effects on US personnel. If Maskirovka encompasses UAP creation, then the same adversary responsible for Havana syndrome would be responsible for some UAP sightings.

The key implication: some fraction of UAP reports — particularly those near sensitive military installations or in contexts that create strategic confusion — may be deliberate Russian psychological operations rather than genuinely unexplained phenomena.

## Epistemic Constraint

Haseltine is careful not to assert Maskirovka definitively explains any specific UAP incident. It is one column in the method-of-competing-hypotheses matrix, weighted by:
- Russian technical capability (verified: directed energy)
- Russian historical use of similar techniques (verified: [[1976-moscow-embassy-microwave-incident]])
- Russian strategic incentive to confuse US military
- Lack of a specific incident where Maskirovka is the confirmed explanation
