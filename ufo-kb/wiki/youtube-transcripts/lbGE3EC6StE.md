# Meet the Astronomer Tracking UFOs

Long-form interview between [[jesse-michaels]] and [[beatriz-villarroel]] — Swedish-Spanish astrophysicist at Stockholm University, VASCO principal investigator, and founder of the [[exoprobe]] project and the [[european-crash-retrieval-initiative]]. The episode covers Villarroel's scientific origin story, the VASCO vanishing-stars methodology, the [[2011-baltic-sea-anomaly]], the Menzel–Whipple–Hynek chain at Harvard, the Carl Sagan public/private split, Villarroel's cancellation episode with [[geoff-marcy]], and her hypothesis-first philosophy for future UAP science.

## Headline Claims

- **Baltic Sea Anomaly is covered in burned biological material** with no explanation (no local volcanic activity); it could not be sampled because the object was too hard to extract a piece from; Villarroel considers it "possibly the best candidate non-human artifact you can find."
- **Menzel–Whipple–Hynek lineage** newly articulated: [[fred-whipple]] was Menzel's PhD student and created [[operation-moonwatch]] (12 citizen-science satellite tracking stations worldwide); [[j-allen-hynek]] worked under Whipple in this program; Villarroel and Michaels independently identified 36 UFO reports from this dataset.
- **Lou Branscomb → Condon clearance chain**: [[lou-branscomb]] (Menzel's PhD student) gave [[edward-condon]] his security clearance back — enabling Condon's appointment to the Condon Commission that killed [[project-blue-book]].
- **Geoff Marcy cancellation**: The National Academy of Sciences kicked out [[geoff-marcy]] the exact same week that Villarroel's paper with him (nine transients discovery) was accepted by a journal — timing she and Jesse both find suspicious.
- **ExoProbe**: Villarroel's new project to find, verify, and physically retrieve an ET probe from Earth orbit using a network of telescopes in New Mexico.
- **Hypothesis-first science**: Villarroel argues that "flying saucer" is more scientifically useful than "UAP" because a concrete hypothesis enables experimental design and failure-mode analysis; the exclusion principle is "barely falsifiable."
