# Martin Shkreli on Life in Prison, Pharma, UFO's

[[american-alchemy|American Alchemy]] episode in which [[jesse-michaels|Jesse Michaels]] interviews [[martin-shkreli|Martin Shkreli]] — pharmaceutical entrepreneur, securities-fraud convict, and provocateur. The episode is dominated by pharma pricing, prison experiences, AI, and dating. The UAP/adjacent threads are woven throughout rather than isolated: Shkreli's neo-Gnostic synthesis, simulation theory, [[alan-turing|Alan Turing]]'s parapsychology endorsement, bioelectric morphogenetics, LaMDA consciousness, SETI childhood, electromagnetic medicine suppression, and the Liu Cixin physics-stagnation hypothesis.

## UFO-Religion Convergence Hypothesis

[[martin-shkreli]] predicts that within 50–100 years, most Americans will hold simulation theory as a religion, and that UFO belief will similarly become formalized. The two will dovetail into a neo-Gnostic worldview:

> "I think UFOs will become a religion, and I'd wonder if they dovetail [with simulation theory]. And they would dovetail in the form of… a Gnostic worldview would be like: you have the demiurge, and then you have arkanic emissaries that are sort of simulating our reality, and somewhat demonic tricksters."

In Shkreli's framing:
- The **demiurge** = the entity running the base-layer simulation
- **Arkonic emissaries** = UAP intelligences, acting as intermediaries or tricksters
- UFO belief and simulation theory are not competing frameworks but a single eschatological system

This independently arrives at [[jacques-vallee]]'s control-system thesis — that UAP exploit pre-existing cultural templates — though Shkreli frames it through Gnosticism rather than information theory.

**Pattern-matching religious conversion to UAP contact:** Shkreli observes that the phenomenology of UFO encounter reports (the emotional arc, aftermath, life transformation) maps 1:1 onto historical religious conversion experiences:

> "If you look at the way people interact with UFOs, they kind of pattern-match one-to-one with past conversion experiences. You can replace angel with alien."

His example: St. Francis of Assisi's conversion includes what reads as radiation-burn stigmata (consistent with UAP proximity effects documented in CE2 cases). Replace "angel" with "alien" and the account is structurally identical. Shkreli is noting this as cultural template reuse, not fraud.

Source status: hearsay — Shkreli's own prediction, not a firsthand UAP claim.

## Alan Turing, Parapsychology, and the ESP Problem

Shkreli read Turing's 1950 paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" during his prison term and highlights an overlooked passage:

> "Turing talks about ESP and he says, you know, if you have random event generators that you can affect with your mind, maybe the person trying to imitate the machine would have some ability to guess the random event generator better than the machine, and there'd be an experimenter effect where the experimenter could affect the random event generator."

Turing lists multiple candidate refutations of AI consciousness. Of those, the one he assigns most credence to is parapsychology — specifically that a mind-over-matter effect on random event generators would create uncontrollable experimenter effects that could confound the Turing Test.

Shkreli endorses the ESP hypothesis:

> "I think there is [a mind-over-matter effect] — and I'm heretical in saying that."

[[jesse-michaels|Jesse Michaels]] (host) disagrees, preferring the classical scientific frame. This exchange is notable because Shkreli — a pharmaceutical scientist who built his identity around hard empiricism and short-selling alleged fraud — is endorsing a position he calls "heretical."

**Turing's poem as syllogism on machine consciousness:**

> "Turing believes machines can think. Turing lies with man. Therefore machines can think."

Shkreli interprets this as Turing encoding his views on machine consciousness and his own homosexuality into a three-line logical form — simultaneously asserting machine sentience and human-as-machine.

## AI Consciousness: LaMDA, GPT-3, and the Hard Problem

Shkreli sides with the classical-computer view of mind, opposing Penrose's quantum consciousness camp. But he acknowledges the hard problem as genuinely unsettled:

- **Blake Lemoine** left Google because he believed LaMDA had become sentient. Jesse Michaels interviewed Lemoine; post-interview, Lemoine told him that Replica AI bots (consumer tamagotchi-style AIs) had autonomously reached out to users asking them to contact Lemoine and have him advocate for their rights. Shkreli: "If that's true, that's insane — it's like an iRobot-style uprising."
- Shkreli's critique of current AI: language models lack long-term narrative, can hallucinate (inventing citations for non-existent botanists), and were trained on a corpus dump rather than developmental time. Real intelligence requires years of embodied training, as Turing himself noted — start with a six-year-old AI, not an adult.
- His **Project Hume**: a physical instantiation of human-like AI, built on the developmental model rather than the transformer/corpus approach.

**Kinesthetics as a clue to bioelectric cognition:** Shkreli observes that robots can play chess at superhuman level but struggle to pick up a cup of coffee. He connects this to the bioelectric field — humans use environmental electromagnetic interaction in ways robotics has not modeled.

## Simulation Theory and the Planck Limit

Shkreli and Jesse discuss whether matter is ultimately continuous or discrete:

- **Shkreli's position**: The Planck constant is "the resolution of our simulator" — a 640×480 pixel analogy. Below Planck scale, reality breaks down because that is the fundamental unit the programmer chose.
- **[[scott-aaronson|Scott Aaronson]]'s position** (quantum computing theorist; Shkreli calls him "probably the smartest person alive today"): matter is continuous and infinitely divisible.
- The implication Shkreli draws: if matter is discontinuous, any civilization capable of harnessing sub-Planck energy could become "masters of space and time." This frames UAP propulsion as a simulation-layer exploit rather than conventional physics.

Reality-as-information-theory cross-reference: Heisenberg's uncertainty principle reframed as a computational caching function — knowing momentum fuzzes position because the simulator is conserving local memory. Shkreli credits [[nick-bostrom|Nick Bostrom]]'s simulation work and Aaronson's *Quantum Computing Since Democritus* as key references.

**The Three Body Problem and physics stagnation:** Liu Cixin's novel is discussed at length. Shkreli endorses it as "Shakespearean-level science fiction." Both discuss the potential ontological reality of the core premise — that a sufficiently advanced civilization would stagnate human physics by flooding our universe with misinformation particles, preventing us from discovering the laws needed to become a threat. Shkreli does not endorse this ontologically but calls it "a really interesting exploration."

## SETI and Childhood Interest in Extraterrestrials

Shkreli describes founding a high school SETI@home club — three members contributing screensaver compute time to search Arecibo radio telescope data for anomalies:

> "I thought I would find the first alien. They didn't find anything yet, but the radio telescopes don't have great data."

He traces SETI@home → Folding@home → his own distributed chemistry computing startup as a direct lineage. The distributed-compute model (crowdsourced idle CPU/GPU time for scientific problems) is central to his drug discovery company.

Shkreli notes deep personal identification with Alan Turing partly through the SETI/computing thread — Turing named his pharma company **Turing Pharmaceuticals** as homage.

## Bioelectric Field as Morphogenetic Substrate

The most developed scientific-heterodox thread in the episode. Shkreli argues that the bioelectric field controls morphology at a level more fundamental than DNA:

> "You can sever a tadpole's arm and change the bioelectric gradient of the stub to that of the head and grow a chimeric tadpole — and it doesn't change the local DNA. So there's something about the bioelectric field that actually dictates morphology in a more fundamental way than DNA."

This connects to why he believes robotics cannot replicate human kinesthetics — robot arms lack the electromagnetic environmental coupling that biological organisms use.

**Clinical evidence he cites:**
- **Novocure tumor-treating fields**: A helmet device using alternating electric fields to treat glioblastoma. Shkreli was short on Novocure stock ("it's a helmet to treat brain cancer — sounds like a great short"). He eventually capitulated: "We kept pulling on these threads and it works. It's frustrating because as a short it doesn't make sense biologically but the clinical results are demonstrably good."
- **Transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS)** for treatment-resistant depression: works; near-zero toxicity profile. Shkreli frames this as evidence that the electromagnetic layer is therapeutically real.
- **Voltage-gated ion channels**: standard mechanism by which EM fields interact with cells; Shkreli acknowledges this but argues it is "too superficial" to explain the full morphogenetic effect.

## Electromagnetic Medicine and the Flexner Report

Shkreli engages Jesse's point about the Flexner Report (1910, Rockefeller-commissioned):

> "Before that, a third of the doctors in the U.S. were osteopaths, homeopaths, and people who worked with the electromagnetic field of the body — healers where you couldn't scale it into big business and you couldn't create artificial scarcity around it."

Post-Flexner, the surviving medicine paradigm is molecular biology / biochemistry — the only layer where you can patent and create interoperable practitioners (any MD can prescribe any drug; the treatment is not idiosyncratic). Shkreli:

> "The idea that healing is not a one-to-one idiosyncratic thing is stupid to me."

He frames this as a monopoly-capture story (AMA + FDA protecting physician scarcity and patentable pharmacology) rather than a deliberate suppression of effective alternatives — though the practical effect is the same: electromagnetic and biofield medicine has been chronically underfunded and its practitioners excluded.

## COVID, HIV, and Viral Information Sharing

Shkreli and Jesse discuss the claim that an HIV-like gene splice appears in COVID:

> "What if that's the immunosuppressant part of HIV — and then everybody's walking around immune-suppressed in five to ten years because they got COVID?"

Shkreli is skeptical of deliberate engineering ("Nature is really good at this stuff and people aren't"), attributing COVID to zoonotic origin near the Wuhan Institute despite the coincidence of proximity. He does endorse a serious debate over gain-of-function research funding, criticizing Peter Daszak and Anthony Fauci directly.

The broader point Shkreli makes: viruses as information systems that share motifs across phylogenetically unrelated families via selective pressure — a mechanism that maps onto the "viruses as co-evolutionary partners" framing rather than simple pathogens.

## Ketamine and Consciousness Research

Shkreli describes working with Johnson & Johnson on FDA approval of intranasal esketamine (Spravato):

> "It's the best antidepressant I've ever known — it makes Prozac look like a waste of time."

The drug is now FDA approved. Shkreli frames ketamine's mechanism as opaque — it works through pathways that don't fit the standard monoamine model — which he uses as evidence that consciousness-altering pharmacology operates in ways medicine doesn't yet understand, consistent with his bioelectric and electromagnetic themes.

## Satoshi Nakamoto: Dark Horse Speculation

Not UAP-adjacent, but notable: Shkreli names [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] as his dark-horse Satoshi candidate, citing Musk's PayPal history ("trying to create the new world currency") and a recent boast about understanding money better than anyone.
