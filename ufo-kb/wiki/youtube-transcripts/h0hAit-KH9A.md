# The Wall Street Journal Is Lying About UFOs

Solo commentary episode. [[jesse-michaels]] responds to the Wall Street Journal article "Pentagon Disinformation that Fueled America's UFO Mythology" by reporter [[joel-shectman]], arguing it is itself a disinformation product.

## Headline Claims

- [[susan-goff]], a primary source for the WSJ article, is a lead practitioner in [[booz-allen-hamilton]]'s psychological operations consulting practice for the DoD; her LinkedIn lists "strategic communication and psychological operations" as core skills — a conflict of interest the article does not disclose.
- The WSJ's two-pronged explanation for all UFO mythology — secret EMP tests on US nukes, and Air Force hazing rituals — is physically and historically incoherent: EMP technology capable of the claimed effect did not exist until the early 1970s; the missiles at Malmstrom came back online (EMPs cause irreversible damage to electronics); SAC itself paid Boeing to investigate the shutdowns and was "extremely puzzled."
- [[robert-salas]]: "It would have been irresponsible and unthinkable for the US Air Force to jeopardize the operational status of these weapons. The EMP generator equipment would have involved a prolonged installation process in plain sight of our security team."
- [[carl-nell]] co-authored an op-ed (with a former cabinet-level official) expressing frustration at lack of civilian oversight of UAP programs; the WSJ opinion section refused to publish it for no stated reason — while simultaneously publishing Shectman's pro-suppression article.
- [[david-spergel]] (head of the Simons Foundation; NASA UAP working group chair) is the WSJ's de facto UFO adviser, per managing editor [[lena-bell]]; his stated position is indistinguishable from 1950s debunkers like [[donald-menzel]]: UFOs are space trash, light reflections, and temperature inversions.
- [[sean-kirkpatrick]] (AARO) lobbied against the [[uap-disclosure-act]] — the bill that would mandate civilian congressional oversight — before it went to a vote.
- [[donald-menzel]], the preeminent 1950s debunker, held extreme NSA/CIA/Navy clearances and was caught destroying astronomical records by astronomer [[dorit-hofflight]]; in 1953 he halted Harvard's sky survey monitoring the entire sky.
