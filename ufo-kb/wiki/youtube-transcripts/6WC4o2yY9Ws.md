# US Fighter Pilots Witness UFO (ft. Ryan Graves)

[[ryan-graves]], former [[us-navy|US Navy]] F/A-18 Super Hornet pilot, describes in detail what his squadron witnessed off the coast of Virginia Beach / NAS Oceana during a 2014–2015 deployment — now the most-documented post-Nimitz military UAP series. He also discusses the landmark [[2023-congressional-uap-hearing|2023 congressional UAP hearing]], [[david-grusch]]'s crash-retrieval claims, aviation safety implications, and private-sector efforts to detect UAP. [[jesse-michaels|Jesse Michaels]] co-hosts from Austin.

## Headline Claims

- **UAP sightings were routine, not rare.** Graves's squadron detected objects daily for "a few weeks" in their working area off the Virginia coast — three to seven objects at a time, operating 0–1.2 Mach, perfectly stationary in 120-knot winds, in the open-ocean airspace used by commercial aviation.
- **The cube-in-sphere morphology.** A pilot in Graves's squadron nearly collided with an object that passed within ~50 ft of the cockpit; he described it as "a dark gray or black cube inside of a clear sphere," approximately 5–15 ft in diameter. About half a second room of fighter pilots at a later event immediately recognized this description: "Oh yeah, the cubes and spheres."
- **[[2015-gofast|GOFAST video]] was four objects in formation.** Graves's close friend and WSO (weapons system officer) — the person who recorded the GOFAST video — privately told Graves that there were actually **four objects in line-abreast formation**, each about a mile apart, not a single object. The four were maintaining precise relative positioning, implying inter-craft digital coordination.
- **Five unreported near-miss incidents.** After one pilot filed a hazard report, a squadron safety survey revealed four *additional* near-miss incidents had gone unreported due to stigma and career concerns.
- **AESA radar (APG-79) was the detection breakthrough.** The new actively-electronically scanned array radar replaced the mechanically-scanned APG-73; the objects were not visible on the older radar and were initially assumed to be AESA software bugs. Triple-sensor correlation (APG-79 radar + ATFLIR + AIM-9X missile seeker) confirmed physical objects.
- **UAP objects appeared to mask their IR signature** — emitting energy uniformly across their surface rather than showing the concentrated heat plumes of conventional jet propulsion, making them appear as undifferentiated blurs on FLIR.
- **Grusch's crash-retrieval claim is credible.** Graves met Grusch before and at the hearing. He does not have personal access to corroborate the programs, but finds nothing in Grusch's background inconsistent with honesty, and regards his "I can tell you the location, get me in a SCIF" statement as the posture of someone highly confident in their claims.
- **Private sector is the path forward.** Graves has founded a space-situational awareness camera company (for non-traditional orbital trajectories) that will commercialize UAP detection. He argues academia is structurally unable to lead this work.
