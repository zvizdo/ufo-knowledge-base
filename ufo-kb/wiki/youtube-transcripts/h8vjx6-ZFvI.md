# Joe Rogan Experience #1645 - Christopher Mellon

180-minute conversation with [[chris-mellon]], former Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (Clinton and [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] administrations). Mellon frames his involvement as a personal threshold crossing after discovering that credible UAP data was systematically not reaching Pentagon leadership or Congress — not through suppression, but through institutional inertia and reporting stigma.

## Chris Mellon Background and Pentagon Career

- Mellon spent approximately 20 years in government intelligence roles: [[senate-intelligence-committee|Senate Intelligence Committee]] staff, work for Senator William Cohen (Armed Services), then four years in [[pentagon|the Pentagon]] under Secretary Cohen after Cohen became Secretary of Defense
- Stayed on under Secretary Rumsfeld; all positions were intelligence and security related
- Mellon comes from the prominent Mellon banking family; his institutional connections and family status meant he had access to briefings and officials that most Pentagon civilians did not
- After government service, became involved with [[tom-delonge]]'s [[to-the-stars-academy]] alongside [[lue-elizondo]]
- TTSA in Mellon's account: a vehicle for bringing classified-program insiders (Elizondo, Mellon, others) together with cultural figures and scientific partners to accelerate public UAP disclosure; the metamaterial acquisitions were one concrete output
- At time of recording, Mellon no longer holds an official TTSA role — stays in touch informally and continues advocacy through personal Congressional contacts
- Mellon's personal threshold: meeting Elizondo, who introduced him to Navy pilots describing East Coast UAP activity from 2015 onward; after this Mellon "threw the flag and said this is unacceptable"; his subsequent disclosure advocacy was driven by genuine outrage at institutional failure, not career opportunism

## The Institutional Blind Spot: How UAP Data Missed the Pentagon

- Mellon's central claim: during his Pentagon tenure he was not seeing data suppressed — he was seeing data not reported at all; "the problem was non-reporting, not active suppression at my level"
- An AWACS aircraft commander told Mellon directly: "yeah, we saw some really weird stuff but we weren't telling anybody about it — we tore up the files"
- NSA personnel told Mellon they kept private logs of anomalies and did not turn them over to replacements — destroyed on retirement
- A Navy pilot friend "called me up with his hair on fire" to report a UFO flying around a plane at his base that then landed — this kind of incident "was not going up through channels"
- [[norad]] was not being notified of UAP activity occurring right off the U.S. coast
- CIA Directorate of Science and Technology conducted extensive analysis of sightings by military pilots on reconnaissance missions and found "a plausible explanation for what they had seen that was not extraterrestrial"; published an article on the findings; Mellon describes it as "a good piece of research" — this is the one case where he saw data dealt with rather than ignored
- DOD memo routing to the Secretary of Defense requires approximately 14–17 different offices to sign off; any office asking a question halts the entire process; the package then joins thousands of others in queue
- Mellon describes the resulting situation: "we have blinded ourselves — we've done this to ourselves and we're playing catchup now"
- Key institutional consequence: the Secretary of Defense himself, Congress, and senior policy makers "were not hearing or seeing any of these reports — they were going into the ether"
- Stigma as the primary mechanism: "people were afraid to report it"; pilots knew ridicule awaited; this fear, not active suppression, explains why data never reached leadership
- After learning about the East Coast 2015 encounters from Elizondo and the involved Navy pilots, Mellon introduced Elizondo to two direct reports of General [[james-mattis|James Mattis]]
- Those aides, after several briefings and meetings with pilots, concluded something real was happening — but chose not to brief Mattis himself, fearing he would be "politically contaminated from even taking a briefing"; they were protective of his reputation and stature
- Mellon's personal turning point: meeting Elizondo and learning the full scope of unreported incidents — after which he "threw the [expletive] flag and said this is unacceptable" and became an activist on the issue

## DOD Inspector General Investigation

- Shortly before or at time of recording, DOD announced the Inspector General would formally investigate how UAP sightings "fell through the cracks for years"
- The IG investigation framing: how were pilots flying around seeing these objects on radar, yet nobody up the chain of command — "the Secretary of Defense himself, Congress" — was being notified
- Mellon calls the announcement "long overdue" and "stunning" given the documented evidence available
- This IG investigation is structurally distinct from the UAP Task Force report due to the Senate: the IG focuses on institutional process failure, not on cataloguing UAP incidents; Mellon treats them as complementary but non-overlapping efforts
- The announcement's implicit concession: DOD is acknowledging that something significant was being seen and was not being reported — without yet saying what it was
- Mellon's framing of the central institutional failure: nuclear missile shutdowns at Malmstrom, multi-sensor engagements like Nimitz, and mass sightings all generated data that should have compelled policy attention; the IG investigation asks why it did not

## 2004 Nimitz Tic-Tac Encounter

- Mellon describes the Nimitz case as "the most compelling case" due to convergence of multiple independent sensor systems and witnesses with no contradictions among accounts
- Witnesses across multiple platforms: Commander [[david-fravor]] and his wingman (F-18 #1, pilot + WSO = 2 crew), a second F-18 with two more crew; radar operators on [[uss-princeton|USS Princeton]] (Aegis cruiser); radar operators on E-2C Hawkeye providing sporadic tracking
- [[uss-princeton|USS Princeton]] radar had been tracking the objects "for weeks" before the key encounter; objects descended from approximately 80,000 feet off the California/Mexico coast
- On the key intercept day, the objects descended from ~80,000 ft to near sea level — described as arriving at 50 feet over the ocean — "in a split second," less than one second
- Fravor arrived to find roiled white water over "what looked like a reef" — but there was no reef in that location; something was also present beneath the surface
- The Tic-Tac object was hovering at approximately 50 feet over this disturbed water when Fravor arrived
- Fravor maneuvered to engage; "the object reacted to his presence, turned around, viewed him, and then started taking countermeasures"; it maintained a dominant position throughout — "it could easily have done what it wanted to them; they had no chance of getting behind it or getting the upper hand"
- The object knew the pilots' pre-planned rendezvous CAP point before they arrived — it departed the engagement and was waiting at the CAP point when [[chad-underwood]] flew there afterward
- Underwood filmed the Tic-Tac on FLIR at the CAP point; his weapon system could not achieve a lock — the object appeared to be actively jamming or interfering with the targeting system
- The Scientific Coalition for UAP Studies ([[scuap]]) conducted a mathematical and engineering analysis of the FLIR video: estimated G-forces at "five times greater than any human-built aircraft could withstand — it would shred any aircraft, any missile, any rocket that we've ever built"
- No sonic boom was produced despite estimated speeds of "thousands of miles an hour"; no heat signature, no wings, no exhaust, no apparent fuel consumption
- When Fravor and his wingman landed, the intelligence officers greeted them by playing the Men in Black song and appearing with tin foil hats — the debrief was entirely dismissive
- Mellon personally spoke with all pilots involved and with Princeton radar operators; "it's all perfectly congruent, it all hangs together, nobody's contradicting anybody else's story"
- A near-midair collision safety report was filed by the Navy — a matter of public record
- Mellon on Fravor: "a very respected guy, not the type of person who makes up wacky stories"

## Chad Underwood and the FLIR Video

- [[chad-underwood]] flew the second F-18 sortie on the same day to the CAP point where Fravor had lost the object; Underwood was not in the first intercept
- Underwood filmed the FLIR/Tic-Tac video; on a podcast, Underwood stated the acceleration was instantaneous — "no ifs, ands, or buts"; Underwood had thousands of hours of cockpit time
- The FLIR video is forward-looking infrared radar — not standard optical video; it measures thermal emission, not reflected light
- Underwood could not get a weapons lock; Mellon confirms "there was interference" with Underwood's systems at the CAP point; this is consistent with active countermeasure behavior rather than passive radar cross-section characteristics
- A submarine was accompanying the Nimitz carrier battle group and was dispatched to search the waters under the engagement area; it detected nothing — despite the roiled water Fravor observed above when he arrived
- Mellon's interpretation: whatever this technology is "has very unusual properties" — capable of moving underwater while remaining undetectable to submarine sonar, and supersonic in air without producing a sonic boom
- The Go Fast video: released in the Washington Post several months after Gimbal and FLIR (which came with the December 2017 NYT story); DoD authenticated it; the [[scuap]] analysis and debunker [[mick-west]]'s balloon counterargument are both treated in depth

## Gimbal Video and Unreleased Footage

- Mellon describes the [[2015-gimbal|Gimbal video]] as "probably the most compelling" video on its own due to emotional pilot reaction and the additional detail that a V-shaped fleet of UFOs was maneuvering near the filming aircraft off-screen
- One pilot's audio during the Gimbal encounter: "look dude there's a whole fleet of them out there" — referring to the off-screen V-formation of UAPs
- The V-formation veered away and flew off; those objects are unidentified
- The Gimbal object was flying into a headwind of approximately 120 knots — definitively ruling out balloon explanations
- The [[2015-gimbal|Gimbal video]] was released via a proper review process with documentation stating "approved for public release"; public affairs was not looped in and "got their nose out of joint" but the formal approval documentation existed
- Mellon says a few individuals inside the system were "very concerned" about national security and were willing to circumvent normal channels because the issue was going nowhere
- There exists an unreleased video Mellon describes as "unclassified but not released": a UAP passes very close between two Navy pilots — one is "freaking out"; Mellon calls it emotionally compelling but not necessarily more evidentially compelling than the Nimitz case
- Go Fast video: released in the Washington Post a few months after Gimbal and FLIR; DoD-authenticated; [[mick-west|Mick West]] argued the object was a ~130 mph balloon; Mellon dismisses this interpretation, noting West "has never been in one of these planes, he's never operated these sensors, and he just dismisses these people"

## UAP Technical Capabilities and Propulsion Theory

- Tic-Tac performance envelope: no sonic boom at supersonic speeds, no heat signature, no exhaust, no wings, no apparent fuel consumption, unlimited time on station, instantaneous acceleration, radar jamming capability, foreknowledge of classified flight plans
- People in close proximity to UAP objects have reported a "hum or vibration like a Transformer"; occasionally a "swooshing sound"; never a sonic boom
- Alcubierre drive theory: manipulates spacetime in the immediate vicinity of the craft; would explain why occupants don't experience G-forces (they're not being subjected to acceleration — the spacetime around them is moving); also explains the absence of sonic booms (atmosphere opens in front rather than craft penetrating it)
- Mellon notes an academic paper published shortly before the interview challenged Alcubierre theory, but still finds the framework useful for understanding the anomalous performance data
- [[lue-elizondo|Elizondo]] introduced Mellon into [[pentagon|the Pentagon]] to show him classified UAP videos; Mellon was angry to have been previously denied access as a senior official; Elizondo believes that anger sparked Mellon's Congressional disclosure advocacy

## Metamaterials and Physical Evidence at TTSA

- At a TTSA gathering, Mellon physically handled a material on the table — described as silverish, lightweight, and peculiar; no one could identify a conventional industrial counterpart
- TTSA was purchasing alleged UAP materials from collectors and researchers in order to scientifically test them
- One material's claimed provenance (displayed as on-screen text during the interview): came from "the bottom of a wedge-shaped craft in the late 1940s"; the original finder's son passed it to a researcher who brought it to TTSA
- Composition of one sample: 26 alternating layers — 1–4 microns dark bismuth alternating with 100–200 microns silver-magnesium-zinc alloy; each of several pieces was formed with a tapered curvature; sourced described as "a [[us-army|US Army]] source"
- Theoretical function: the material acts as a waveguide for microwave radiation — it may enhance microwave emissions passing through it; the craft's skin may have simultaneously functioned as an antenna; this would make the entire outer surface an active electromagnetic system rather than passive structure
- No conventional industrial counterpart for the 26-layer bismuth/silver-magnesium-zinc composite has been identified; the manufacturing process for such fine alternating layers was not available to any known industrial sector in the late 1940s
- [[jacques-vallee]] has materials from 15–20 different alleged UAP incidents, including one from an explosion in Brazil; as of the interview, these were in peer review and expected to publish in a peer-reviewed journal
- Vallée's claim on some materials: they appear to have been engineered "not at the molecular level but at the atomic level"; recreating them with current technology "would cost billions of dollars"
- Isotopic ratio analysis: some samples show isotopic ratios sufficiently different from anything in the solar system to suggest they were "born in a different sun or star"; all heavy elements in our solar system share a common isotopic fingerprint from a single supernova event — anomalous ratios suggest extrasolar origin
- Mellon's personal position: "completely agnostic" on whether materials are exotic or of terrestrial origin; considers the Nimitz case more compelling evidence than the materials

## Bob Lazar, Element 115, and Area 51

- Mellon visited [[area-51|Area 51]]; saw [[department-of-defense|defense department]] experiments and training activities; did not see flying saucers
- Mellon's assessment of Lazar: "curious and interesting" but "a little skeptical"
- Lazar described element 115 as central to the craft's propulsion in 1989 — at the time, element 115 was purely theoretical; it was later confirmed detectable in particle accelerators (Mellon agrees when Rogan suggests ~2013)
- Lazar's described propulsion mechanism (bending gravity / manipulating spacetime in the craft's immediate vicinity) matches the Alcubierre-style framework used to explain Tic-Tac performance
- A friend of Mellon's claims to know Lazar's supervisor at the facility; that person described Lazar's job as "checking radiation on badges — nothing more"
- Lazar falsified educational records (MIT enrollment); Rogan says Lazar told him off-air there is a classified government explanation involving MIT that Lazar asked Rogan not to discuss publicly
- Lazar's name does appear on Los Alamos National Laboratory employee rolls; authorities initially denied this; Lazar went to Los Alamos with [[george-knapp]] and knew the building layout and personnel
- Lazar brought friends to witness test flights — he knew the location and schedule; they were arrested; Mellon notes this is "hard to explain if he made everything up"
- Mellon dined with Lazar and [[jeremy-corbell|Jeremy Corbell]]; spoke for several hours; Lazar told Mellon off-air about working on a classified government project at MIT that "can't be documented" — Mellon declines to share details on air

## Nuclear Nexus: Malmstrom and Missile Shutdowns

- UAPs have been documented turning off nuclear missiles; Congress has never formally asked [[pentagon|the Pentagon]] to address this — Mellon calls this "stunning" and "long overdue"
- At [[malmstrom-air-force-base]], the UAP interference with ICBMs was documented; a local sheriff received so many civilian calls about UFOs in the area that he co-authored a book documenting what was happening — civilian sightings clustered spatially and temporally around missile shutdowns
- Retired officers came forward in uniform, with documentation confirming their assignments and positions, to testify about missile shutdowns — yet no official confirmation exists; Congressional committees have not called them despite their on-record availability
- Mellon's framing: the ICBM capability is "the crown jewels of our freedom and independence — the ultimate insurance policy militarily"; UAPs demonstrating the ability to disable them is among the most consequential findings in the entire UAP file
- The nuclear nexus connects: Malmstrom shutdowns + Defcon 2 Australia communication facility hover + Pantex jellyfish UAP between warhead facilities + 18/18 nuclear-capable vessel incidents (Corbell/Knapp) = a consistent pattern of NHI operational interest in nuclear weapons systems that has not triggered any formal government inquiry
- Mellon proposes that one of the first questions a new UAP oversight body should answer is whether the nuclear nexus is purposeful (NHI interest in limiting or monitoring human nuclear capability) or coincidental (UAPs happen to appear near high-security installations regardless of type)
- The distinction matters for policy: if UAPs are monitoring nuclear weapons for strategic purposes, that has profound implications for deterrence theory and the balance of power

## J. Allen Hynek, Project Blue Book, and the Michigan Swamp Gas Episode

- [[j-allen-hynek]] was the [[us-air-force|Air Force]]'s scientific consultant who went to Michigan after a wave of civilian sightings and famously attributed them to swamp gas
- Hynek was described as "very embarrassed" and "badly burned" by the experience; it changed him — he eventually became "very critical of [[project-blue-book]]"
- Congressman Gerald Ford, who represented the Michigan district, "got incensed" along with the local population over the swamp gas explanation and pushed Congress to take an interest — one of the few moments the UAP issue reached Congress
- Congress took a "fleeting interest" after the Michigan episode and then moved on

## Phoenix Lights and Mass Sightings

- [[fife-symington]], former Governor of Arizona and a former [[us-air-force|Air Force]] officer, stated after the fact that he personally witnessed the [[1997-phoenix-lights|1997 Phoenix Lights]] object — described it as not a plane, not flares; a triangular craft at least one football field long, possibly several football fields
- The [[1997-phoenix-lights|Phoenix Lights]] sighting path: appeared to originate in Nevada, entered northwestern Arizona, flew over Phoenix, continued south; hundreds if not thousands of people along the entire pathway reported it
- A military exercise dropping flares that night muddied the waters; authorities attributed some lights to flares; Symington and others reported something separate from and prior to the flares
- Rogan and Mellon discuss the distinction: the lights in the Phoenix video hover rather than drop, inconsistent with standard dropped flares; they discuss flares suspended by balloons as an alternative without resolving it
- Other mass sightings mentioned: Italy soccer match (~10,000 fans in a stadium; game came to a halt); Brazil; Australia (thousands of witnesses and dozens of school children, parallel to Zimbabwe)
- Brazil 1952 UFO overflights: the capital overflown; 23 UFOs reportedly seen simultaneously; multiple fighter jets scrambled; front page news; Mellon draws parallel to 1952 Washington DC overflights

## J. Allen Hynek and Project Blue Book (Extended)

- Hynek eventually changed his view publicly; started as a debunker and ended as a critic of the [[us-air-force|Air Force]] investigation methodology; became one of the most credible scientific voices in UFO research precisely because of his prior [[us-air-force|Air Force]] affiliation and initial skepticism
- [[project-blue-book]] concluded there was nothing of national security concern and no advanced technology — Mellon says the forthcoming UAP task force report cannot credibly repeat those conclusions given the multi-sensor evidence now in the public domain
- The pattern Mellon draws: authorities designate phenomena as non-threatening (swamp gas, balloons, flares) to suppress public and congressional interest; each episode eventually unravels; the cycle repeats without institutional reform
- A US majority leader or Speaker of the House on a rare Cold War visit to the Soviet Union saw a flying saucer from his train and others in his delegation also witnessed it; this account went into [[project-blue-book|Project Blue Book]] files — even senior political figures making UFO observations did not produce policy action
- Mellon describes [[project-blue-book|Project Blue Book]]'s fundamental flaw: it was designed to explain cases away rather than investigate them; Hynek's embarrassment at Michigan was the natural result of applying debunking pressure to a real phenomenon

## Betty and Barney Hill Abduction

- Mellon watched [[betty-hill]] appear on an old TV program (a truth-or-lies format) in which she was polygraphed live and "passed with flying colors"
- The hypnotic regression in the Hill case was conducted by a military person who was professionally trained, did not believe in [[the-phenomenon-fox|the phenomenon]], and "was not trying to lead them to that outcome" — Mellon gives this more credibility than later hypnotic regression cases
- Mellon's credibility distinction: the Hill regression was conducted by a skeptic using professional methodology (not seeking to confirm); later regression practitioners often believed and used suggestive techniques, undermining the value of subsequent cases
- [[angela-hill]], UFC fighter and granddaughter of [[barney-hill]], disclosed her ancestry to Rogan after a podcast recording — Rogan had not known until afterward; he describes the discovery as "one of the craziest things ever"
- The Hill abduction (1961) is described as the first extensively documented abduction case in the US; their account established the template for many subsequent abduction narratives (examination table, beings with large eyes, detailed memory returning under regression)

## Travis Walton Abduction

- [[travis-walton]] was an Arizona logging company worker; on the evening of the incident, he was struck by a beam from a UAP and went missing for five days
- Six or seven co-workers witnessed the UAP and saw the beam strike Walton; a full manhunt was conducted with no results; no explanation for his whereabouts during the five days was ever found
- The co-workers who reported to police immediately after were initially treated as murder suspects; the eventual return of Walton resolved the manhunt but not the mystery
- Walton's account (per the book Mellon read): he woke on an examination table, lashed out at beings examining him, ran into a hallway, ran into another room, encountered "very Nordic-looking individuals" who calmed him down and escorted him; he then woke next to a road hundreds of miles from where he had last been seen
- The Walton event is the basis of the 1993 film "Fire in the Sky"
- Mellon met Walton in person with two of his sons; asked him questions; Walton gave "quick, rapid answers without pausing," which Mellon found natural and credible — he explicitly contrasts this response pattern with what he would expect from rehearsed deception
- Mellon's assessment: "nothing contradicts his story that we have, but it's not possible to confirm it"
- Multi-witness credential: the six or seven co-witnesses represent unusual corroboration for an abduction case; their independent testimony to police under suspicion of murder makes the core event (beam, missing person) difficult to attribute to fabrication

## Cash-Landrum Radiation Incident

- The [[1980-cash-landrum-incident|Cash-Landrum case]]: a family in Texas (Dayton, Texas; described as "a couple or three hours west of here" from Houston area) encountered a UAP blocking a road; they stopped and got out of their car
- Physical effects: severe radiation poisoning requiring hospitalization; hair loss; paint on their car was damaged/fried; medical documentation exists
- They sued the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]], assuming it was a secret government project; DoD "swore up and down it wasn't theirs" and the DoD effectively lost — they could neither explain what it was nor provide compensation
- Mellon places the incident in "the 70s or 80s" — uncertain of exact year
- Mellon cites it as among the strongest physical-evidence cases: "people are visibly affected, physically ill, red marks on their skin, and medical reports to document it"
- The case establishes a pattern relevant to [[ufo-nuclear-nexus]]: UAPs appear to have physical radiation properties when operating at close range over human subjects; this matches other physical-effects cases (Soviet pilot incidents, Colares Brazil)

## Jackie Gleason and Nixon at Homestead

- Comedian and UFO researcher [[jackie-gleason]] confided to UFO researcher [[larry-warren]] (eyewitness to Rendlesham Forest UFO incident) that President Nixon drove him to a heavily guarded building at the "very far end" of Homestead [[us-air-force|Air Force]] Base
- Nixon and Gleason were described as close friends; Nixon reportedly drove Gleason himself without aides or Secret Service entourage — an unusual level of informality for a presidential visit to a secured military facility
- Security personnel "just sort of moved back as they passed"; they went through several labs before entering a restricted section Nixon described as containing wreckage from a flying saucer
- Inner chamber quote (Gleason via Warren, published in the book "UFOs Among the Stars" by Timothy Green Beckley): "there were six or eight glass-topped Coke freezers... inside them were the mangled remains of what I took to be children. Then upon closer examination I saw that some of the other figures looked quite old. Most of them were terribly mangled as if they had been in an accident."
- Gleason was reportedly deeply shaken afterward; his drinking worsened following the experience
- [[beverly-gleason|Beverly Gleason]], Jackie's wife, independently told the same story to Esquire magazine in 1974 (Mellon says it was described as a publicity story for her upcoming autobiography) — independent corroboration from a source with no obvious motive to fabricate the Homestead detail
- Gleason was a serious UFO researcher; he built a house ("The Mother Ship") with no right angles, constructed by ship builders in an airplane hangar and moved to property in Westchester County, New York; built in 1959; was for sale in 2018 for $12 million; reportedly sold in 1976 for $150,000
- The story's credibility argument: Beverly's independent corroboration to Esquire, Gleason's documented serious UAP interest predating the alleged visit, and the consistency of the account through multiple tellings elevate it above standard UFO "legend"

## Harry Reid, AATIP, and Congressional Inaction

- After the December 2017 New York Times story revealing [[aatip]] and releasing the three videos, "almost nobody in Congress asked for a briefing" — Mellon describes this as stunning
- [[harry-reid]] is described as a "big believer" in UAPs; Mellon knows Reid "but not well"; Reid is credited with putting funding behind AATIP from the Congressional side — "he's tried to solve this problem from the Congressional side in terms of the lack of interest of the US government by putting some money on it, which created this ATIP program"
- Reid's AATIP program had a task force writing a report for the Senate; the report was due "next month" relative to the recording date; Mellon says it will not be able to repeat [[project-blue-book|Project [[project]]-blue-book|[[project-blue-book|Blue Book]]]]'s conclusions
- Mellon's assessment: the forthcoming UAP task force is "tiny — a few guys" and he is concerned about what happens when it sunsets if no enduring structure is created to replace it
- The Maui Space Surveillance Complex: an intelligence committee colleague made an oversight trip to Hawaii and visited the facility; Mellon implies the facility's capabilities are relevant to UAP tracking but were not being used for that purpose
- Congress and NASA have agreed looking for "techno signatures" of alien technology in distant space is entirely plausible — yet apply a different standard to UAP in Earth's atmosphere; Mellon cites this double standard as intellectually incoherent

## Proposed Office of Strategic Anomaly Resolution

- Mellon has specifically proposed to government contacts the creation of an "Office of Strategic Anomaly Resolution" — an office within existing entities, not a new cabinet department on the scale of Energy or Agriculture
- The office would handle both UAP findings and [[2016-havana-syndrome|Havana Syndrome]]–type events — treating them as branches of the same anomalous intelligence problem requiring interdisciplinary analysis outside normal classification barriers
- Modeled conceptually on DOE national labs: flexible enough for academia, NASA, and military to collaborate without fighting over [[classification-system|classification levels]]; not subject to the normal interagency approval bottleneck
- Mellon's immediate policy recommendation to a hypothetical president: identify who needs to be "put on the carpet" to get to the truth of what has been retrieved or concealed; he frames this as a chain-of-accountability problem rather than a legislative problem
- Mellon believes the CIA and [[us-air-force|Air Force]] are "most likely" where a crash retrieval program would reside if one exists; he says he has "ideas of where to go and who to talk to"
- He explicitly invokes the Church Committee model from 1975 — the Senate investigation that exposed CIA/FBI abuses — as the appropriate template for the oversight structure needed; the Church Committee's power came from subpoena authority and witness protection, not voluntary cooperation

## Reporting Surge and Detection Gap

- In the ~12 months before the interview, [[mufon|MUFON]] saw approximately a 20% increase in UAP reports
- Mellon cites possible causes: COVID-19 lockdowns forcing more people outside looking at the sky; proliferation of smartphones with cameras; and possibly more genuine activity
- [[nuforc|NUFORC]] (National UFO Reporting Center) and [[mufon|MUFON]] both showing noticeably more video and photographic submissions than 10 years prior
- Mellon identifies multiple existing DoD/IC systems whose databases have never been searched for UAP signatures:
  - Ballistic Missile Early Warning System (BMEWS): optimized to look for very specific kinds of things like ICBMs; "clutter" data (anything not ICBM-profile) is filtered before display to operators but retained in databases — never analyzed for UAP patterns
  - Space-Based Infrared System (SBIRS): designed to detect missile launches via heat signature; databases not searched for UAP signatures
  - Global Acoustic Monitoring System: built to detect nuclear weapons tests through atmospheric low-frequency vibration; also detects meteors and bolides; Mellon has "heard rumors that it detects other things they haven't been able to explain"; nobody has searched it for UAP; Mellon has specifically suggested this to government contacts — "nobody has done it yet"
  - Sea-Based X-Band Radar (SBX): precision tracking radar; potential UAP tracking capability not utilized
  - DARPA Space Telescope: named as existing capability not being mined
  - DoD Space Fence: lists of objects in orbit, including ones that "come out of orbit, go back to orbit" and remain unidentified
- Mellon's framing: "we're paid for this stuff already, why don't we look — we got the data, let's take a look"
- Commercial AI-driven overhead imagery now enables near-daily global mapping; algorithms can rapidly search all imagery for a specific profile or signature — Mellon sees this as an untapped investigative tool
- For the Nimitz case specifically, Mellon suggests a focused search of "a few square miles" around the precise lat/long intercept coordinates is feasible with bottom-scanning sonars
- Maui Space Surveillance Complex: an intelligence committee colleague visited on an oversight trip to Hawaii; Mellon implies this facility has relevant UAP tracking capability

## Credibility Assessments: Mellon's Evaluative Framework

- Mellon's standard for the Nimitz case: multiple independent sensor systems (radar, FLIR infrared, multiple aircrews); perfect viewing conditions (broad daylight, mid-afternoon); no contradictions among all witnesses interviewed; a near-midair collision safety report on public record
- He applies a consistent credibility hierarchy: multi-sensor/multi-witness convergence (strongest) > physical medical evidence ([[1980-cash-landrum-incident|Cash-Landrum]]) > live polygraph ([[betty-hill|Betty Hill]]) > in-person assessment of witness credibility (Walton) > single-witness anecdote
- On [[travis-walton|Travis Walton]] specifically: "quick, rapid answers without pausing" when Mellon questioned him; this naturalness Mellon treats as credibility signal distinct from rehearsal
- Mellon explicitly contrasts the [[betty-hill|Betty Hill]] hypnotic regression with later cases: the Hill regression was conducted by a skeptic using professional methodology, not someone seeking to elicit confirmation; later regressions often conducted by believers with suggestive technique
- On Lazar: Mellon finds the story "curious and interesting" but remains skeptical; the strongest corroborating facts are (1) Los Alamos employee roster, (2) building and personnel knowledge verified by Knapp, (3) element 115 description years before synthesis, (4) schedule knowledge consistent with bar-source hypothesis — none conclusive individually
- Chilean military UAP sighting mentioned: Mellon says a case off Chile's coast was ultimately explained as a drug plane exuding fuel — an example of a case that initially looked anomalous but resolved conventionally; Mellon uses this to show he applies genuine critical evaluation, not blanket credulity

## Havana Syndrome and RF Weapons

- US government employees in Havana, Cuba and elsewhere began experiencing headaches, loss of energy, and other symptoms consistent with targeted RF/electromagnetic radiation from a distance — the [[2016-havana-syndrome|Havana Syndrome]]
- Mellon attributes the attacks to Russia, which has developed RF/electromagnetic radiation weapons for decades and used analogous devices on the US Embassy in Moscow for collection purposes
- Cuba provides deniability and "freedom of action" for testing such weapons; Russia can direct operations through Cuban proxies without direct attribution
- More recent incidents were being reported near the White House in DC at the time of the interview
- Mellon's friend "Mike Swick" (described as working for the Secret Service) told him about Soviet listening devices installed in the US Moscow Embassy that were powered by kinetic energy from the building's movement in the wind — analogous to automatic watches; Mellon notes he may have "garbled some details, Mike told me it a long time ago"
- During construction of the new US Embassy in Moscow, antennas and unusual shapes were found embedded in concrete forms; construction was halted; cost hundreds of millions of dollars; compromised infrastructure included pipes, wires, and embedded surveillance architecture
- The "Great Seal Bug" (Soviet passive listening device given to Ambassador Averell Harriman on August 4, 1945, concealed in a carved wooden Great Seal) is cited as historical precedent; Mellon considers it a precursor to RFID technology
- Mellon frames [[2016-havana-syndrome|Havana Syndrome]] and UAP as two branches of the same anomalous intelligence problem — hence his proposed "Office of Strategic Anomaly Resolution" would address both

## Bob Lazar Context: Los Alamos Corroboration

- Lazar's name appears on Los Alamos National Laboratory employee rolls — initially denied by authorities; [[george-knapp|George Knapp]] accompanied Lazar to Los Alamos where Lazar knew the building layout and personnel
- Possible source for Lazar's knowledge of test flight schedules: a bar near the test site where scientists, aviators, and facility workers socialized; Mellon heard Lazar frequented this bar and picked up schedule information
- Lazar brought friends to witness UAP test flights; they were arrested; the fact that he knew the location and approximate schedule is "hard to explain" if Lazar simply invented the story

## Avi Loeb and 'Oumuamua

- [[avi-loeb|Avi Loeb]], described as chief of astronomy at Harvard, assessed 'Oumuamua at 91% likely a disc shape, 10× more reflective than comparable objects, and moving faster than solar gravity alone would predict
- Mellon noted that an alternative explanation had emerged that "many scientists think clears this up" — he does not name it on air; this is offered as an example of healthy scientific revision, not debunking
- Mellon uses the 'Oumuamua case to illustrate the precession-of-Mercury principle: anomalies in science are where breakthroughs occur; the scientific community's willingness to take 'Oumuamua seriously as a possible artifact should be applied to UAP
- Rogan and Mellon discuss: Congress and NASA have both agreed that looking for "techno signatures" of alien technology in the cosmos is entirely plausible — yet the same logic is resisted when applied to UAP in Earth's atmosphere, a contradiction Mellon finds irrational
- The SETI double standard: billions spent looking for radio signals from light-years away; nothing spent systematically investigating objects demonstrating anomalous capabilities in US airspace

## 1973 Defcon 2 / Submarine Communication Facility

- Mellon relays a story heard "the other day from a foreign correspondent": during the 1973 Yom Kippur War, when the US went to Defcon 2, a UFO appeared over a "Davy communication system for submarine-launched ballistic missiles" in Australia and hovered for approximately half an hour
- Mellon describes this as unverified — from a secondhand foreign source — but notes it fits the nuclear nexus pattern documented at Malmstrom and elsewhere
- The story parallels the documented Russian Defcon-adjacent ICBM activation case and the Malmstrom shutdowns: UAPs appearing at nuclear command-and-control nodes during periods of heightened military alert

## France, Russia, China: International UAP Programs

- France has had an official UFO investigative office "for probably decades": a small office with a small budget; analysts take the top six cases per year from police and military reports, bring in scientists and engineers, and publish findings
- France has at times had more UAP reports per capita than the United States
- Mellon: "the stigma that we have doesn't exist at least to that degree over there — they're more objective, scientific; their UFO groups tend to be less fantastical and more focused on science"
- Both China and Russia have active, government-connected UAP research groups; Mellon believes these are allowed or encouraged to exist primarily for intelligence collection — "they read our papers and they may well listen to this podcast"
- [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] has developed a nuclear-powered cruise missile with "incredible range and maneuverability" that the US has validated through testing; Mellon dismisses speculation that pyramid-shaped UAPs over USS Russell were Russian ("I don't think I've heard anything about triangular objects" from Russia)

## Anomalies in Science: Mellon's Epistemic Framework

- Mellon uses the precession of Mercury as his central analogy: Newton and Einstein's theories couldn't explain it; it was a "weird outlier" until general relativity emerged and "perfectly explained it"
- His argument: "we can learn a lot from anomalies in science — that's one of the ways breakthroughs most often occur"; UAPs are an anomaly in the atmosphere worth systematic attention on the same epistemic grounds
- On debunkers: "they start with the premise that it couldn't possibly be any of those things so the facts are wrong"; no debunker has ever produced an alternative conventional explanation (e.g., "it turned out that was United Airlines flight blah blah blah")
- On his personal decision to engage publicly: "I knew people would be making jokes about me behind my back... but you just have to do the right thing at the end of the day"
- On Congress: Mellon was "stunned to learn that almost nobody in Congress asked for a briefing" after the December 2017 New York Times article revealing AATIP and releasing the three videos

## Mellon's Personal Nuclear Scare

- Mellon was one of a small number of officials told there was a nuclear weapon in New York City about to detonate, and another unlocated weapon headed to Washington DC (where Mellon lived with his family)
- He quietly relocated his family but could not tell anyone; the source ultimately proved wrong
- Described as "a very surreal experience" — offered as evidence of what it is like to operate in an intelligence environment under extreme uncertainty with incomplete information
- This anecdote is deployed by Mellon to contextualize the credibility standard applied to high-stakes intelligence: officials act on incomplete, potentially wrong information routinely; the question is not certainty but systematic analysis

## Lazar's Craft: Physical Description and S-4 Details

- Lazar called the craft he worked on "the [[s4-sport-model|sport model]]"; a detailed model of it is visible on Rogan's desk throughout the interview
- The craft was described as having no apparent controls; Mellon says Lazar described it as "biometrically locked to the NHI" — essentially unusable by humans because the interface was designed for non-human biology
- Element 115's function (per Lazar): generates a gravity wave that the propulsion system amplifies and focuses — the spacecraft effectively bends spacetime in its immediate vicinity rather than accelerating through it
- Mellon notes that element 115 (moscovium) was "purely theoretical" in 1989 when Lazar made his claims; a more stable isotope of element 115 was later synthesized, though Lazar had claimed a specific stable isotope existed
- The model on Rogan's desk is described as accurate to Lazar's descriptions of the interior layout, viewing port positions, and reactor location

## Ariel School Zimbabwe and John Mack (Extended)

- Mellon recounts that [[john-mack|John Mack]] was not only a Harvard psychiatrist but a Harvard faculty member; his investigation into the [[ariel-school|Ariel School]] incident extended to interviews with many of the child witnesses; he appears in the documentary "[[the-phenomenon-fox|The Phenomenon]]"
- The children's drawings produced after the encounter showed similar forms independently — small beings with large eyes — without coordination between witnesses; this is treated by Mack (and Mellon) as corroborating independent witness agreement from a population (young children in rural Zimbabwe) with no prior exposure to Western UFO iconography
- Mack was reportedly subjected to Harvard academic review for his work on abduction cases; the university reportedly considered whether his research was compatible with Harvard's standards; he survived the review; Mellon mentions this as an example of institutional stigma operating even at elite academic levels
- Mellon draws a structural parallel to the mass sighting cases in Italy (soccer stadium, ~10,000 witnesses; game halted) and Australia: large numbers of independent witnesses reporting simultaneously makes conventional explanations (hoax, misidentification, mass hysteria) progressively harder to sustain
- The Zimbabwe incident's specific UAP-relevance: reported landed craft, beings emerging, telepathic-style communication reported by multiple children; consistent with close-encounter reports from higher-credibility cases like [[1955-kelly-hopkinsville]] in terms of entity behavior description
