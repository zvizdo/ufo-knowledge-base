## Sheehan's Refusal of the Security Clearance — Strategic Architecture

The refusal of security clearances is not incidental but central to Sheehan's strategy. He explains the architecture:
- Any person who holds a security clearance is **bound by the terms of that clearance** for life, even after leaving government service
- Clearance holders can be prosecuted for disclosing classified information regardless of the public interest value of that disclosure
- A lawyer who holds a clearance and represents UAP whistleblowers faces a direct conflict: the clearance binds the lawyer to secrets while the representation requires them to advocate for disclosure of related secrets
- [[lue-elizondo]], [[david-grusch]], and other whistleblowers who held clearances have had to navigate exactly this tension — disclosing under specific statutory whistleblower provisions rather than simply speaking freely

By never holding a clearance, Sheehan preserves his ability to:
- Discuss publicly anything he learns through unclassified channels (witnesses, non-government documents, foreign sources)
- Advise clearance-holding clients without himself being bound by the clients' clearance terms
- Testify before Congress without being subject to "but you're cleared and can't say that" objections
- Speak freely in venues like this podcast about his firsthand experience (Madison Building, Scientology files, Oscar Wolf) because none of these involved classified access in a formal legal sense

The paradox: much of what Sheehan knows is highly sensitive and practically restricted, but none of it is technically classified — he arranged his entire career so that sensitive knowledge arrives through channels that don't bind him with formal secrecy obligations. This is deliberately constructed legal architecture.
