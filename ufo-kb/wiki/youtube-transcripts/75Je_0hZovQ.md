# The CIA's Mind Control Program (Ft. Tom O'Neill)

American Alchemy episode. [[jesse-michaels]] interviews [[tom-oneill]], investigative journalist and author of *Chaos: Charles Manson, the CIA, and the Secret History of the Sixties*. O'Neill spent 20 years (1999–2019) investigating the Manson murders and their relationship to [[mk-ultra]], discovering that the official prosecution narrative actively suppressed evidence of CIA and government involvement.

## MK-Ultra: Overview

[[mk-ultra]] originated in the late 1940s / early 1950s as a **defensive** program — U.S. intelligence learned that the Soviets, Chinese, and North Koreans had developed brainwashing techniques for extracting confessions and programming POWs. The CIA sought countermeasures, particularly for downed airmen captured behind enemy lines.

The program rapidly evolved into an **offensive** capability when researchers discovered how effective the techniques were. Ultimate goals:
- Create a **programmed assassin** — someone hypnotized to kill with no memory of being programmed
- Achieve **mass conversion** — controlling groups of thousands through media and other technologies
- **Memory manipulation** — implanting false memories and removing true ones using LSD, sodium pentothal, hypnosis, and other drugs

The program operated on non-consenting subjects in prisons, with service people, and through "Operation Midnight Climax" — CIA safe houses in San Francisco and New York City where prostitutes lured men in, dosed them with LSD through two-way-mirror observation rooms.

**Exposure and cover-up:**
- [[richard-helms]] ordered the files destroyed in 1973–74 as Watergate threatened to expose all CIA operations
- [[john-marks]], a State Department official, found surviving financial files in 1974 and took them to Congress
- Congressional hearings followed: the Church Committee (longest), plus Kennedy/Inouye hearings
- The CIA's public posture: "it didn't work, we were stupid to try it, we're sorry" — Congress accepted this; O'Neill argues it did work and the false-confession posture protected ongoing capability

## The Tom O'Neill Investigation

O'Neill was assigned a 1999 magazine piece on the 30th anniversary of the Tate-LaBianca murders. His entry point was the question no one had answered: *how did Manson get young women to commit savage murders in under a year, with no remorse?* That question led him to [[mk-ultra]].

### The Official Narrative (Helter Skelter)

Prosecutor [[vince-bugliosi]] established the "Helter Skelter" motive: Manson believed the Beatles' White Album (specifically "Helter Skelter" and other songs) contained coded messages prophesying a race war. He ordered his followers to kill in a way that would implicate the Black Panthers and accelerate the war. The murders on August 8, 1969 killed:
- [[sharon-tate]] — eight months pregnant, wife of director Roman Polanski (away at time)
- Abigail Folger — Folger coffee heir
- Jay Sebring — celebrity hair stylist
- Voytek Frykowski — Polish filmmaker
- Steve Parent — 19-year-old visitor

### Jolly West and the Haight-Ashbury Connection

[[jolly-west]] ran research at the **Haight-Ashbury Free Medical Clinic** in 1967 using LSD on hippies. This is the same time and place where [[charles-manson]] began building his group. O'Neill found West's papers at UCLA and discovered West was:
- Writing to CIA head of MK-Ultra [[sidney-gottlieb]] (under an alias — Gottlieb was called "the poisoner in chief")
- Conducting experiments that paralleled his arrival in San Francisco and Manson's arrival there
- Traveling with Manson as both moved from San Francisco to Los Angeles

**Key document:** A 1955 West report to the CIA stating he had developed the technology to implant false memories and remove true memories using drugs and hypnosis — **permanently**. This paragraph was **removed** from the version submitted to Congress; the CIA re-drafted his document without it.

West's other intersections with key figures:
- Became [[jack-ruby]]'s psychiatrist: spent ~one hour alone with Ruby in his county jail cell, then held a press conference declaring Ruby had suffered a psychotic break from which he would never recover. Ruby subsequently could not string three coherent sentences together and died three years later
- His specialty included **inducing insanity in a person without their knowledge**
- The Warren Commission was never informed West had a conflict of interest as an active CIA asset
- UCLA named an auditorium after him; he founded the first Neuroscience Center at University of Oklahoma

### Roger Smith: The Parole Officer

[[roger-smith]], Manson's parole officer, was simultaneously a criminology student at Berkeley and a drug researcher whose specialty was **adolescent gang members who became violent while using LSD and amphetamines**. This is the exact profile he oversaw as parole officer.

### The Transformation of Manson

Pre-1967: Manson was a 5'4" petty criminal of modest charisma, repeatedly caught for minor offenses.

1967–1969 (the Haight-Ashbury period, under West's orbit): Manson became a "sex god cult leader guru" who converted young women to murder within a year.

O'Neill frames this transformation — from obscure criminal to MK-Ultra's ideal product (a programmed cult leader capable of commanding group violence) — as the central evidentiary argument.

### The "Stranger in a Strange Land" Framework

Manson, supposedly illiterate, structured his group around Robert Heinlein's 1961 novel *Stranger in a Strange Land*:
- He named his first son Michael Valentine (the novel's Martian protagonist)
- He called Roger Smith "Jubal" (the novel's protective lawyer figure)
- The novel involves communal wives, a prophesied race war, and a leader building a new civilization

The implication: someone read or summarized the novel for Manson, possibly while he was under drug influence, and it became the narrative framework for the Family.

### Reeve Whitson: The CIA Informant Inside the Family

[[reeve-whitson]] was a mysterious figure who told friends and family he "worked for the government but couldn't say who for." Only shortly before his death did he tell a small number of people (including his daughter and lawyer) that he had been running an undercover infiltration of the Manson Family for a government agency. His dying regret: he could have prevented the murders. He stated he was at the crime scene the night of the murders — after the killings, before police arrived — and physical evidence supports someone else was at the property that night.

[[vince-bugliosi]] systematically removed all these peripheral CIA-connected figures from his prosecution narrative.

### Operation Chaos and COINTELPRO

Two parallel programs aimed at destroying the left:
- **[[operation-chaos]]** (CIA, 1967, San Francisco): infiltrate and neutralize the counterculture and Black militant movements
- **COINTELPRO** (FBI, J. Edgar Hoover): same mission, domestic

Hoover's November 1968 memo to the LA field office: white supporters of the Black Panthers must be made to fear that when the revolution comes they will be "lined up against the wall and slaughtered." Eight months later, five white people were slaughtered in the house that embodied liberal Hollywood's support for the Panthers — the Tate house.

O'Neill's thesis: the Manson murders functionally served the goals of Chaos/COINTELPRO by:
1. Destroying the public perception of hippies as peace-loving ("the day the '60s ended")
2. Making the white liberal establishment fear the counterculture they'd been supporting
3. Causing widespread terror that led to social rejection of the entire hippie movement

### Other MK-Ultra Subjects Named

- **[[whitey-bulger]]**: discovered late in life (while in prison) that the medical experiments he'd volunteered for in the 1950s were MK-Ultra brainwashing experiments
- **[[ted-kaczynski]]** (the Unabomber): voluntarily entered an MK-Ultra experiment at Harvard
- **Owsley Stanley**: Grateful Dead chemist who distributed LSD at Haight-Ashbury concerts; O'Neill has a growing file suggesting he was a CIA asset knowingly distributing CIA-supplied LSD
