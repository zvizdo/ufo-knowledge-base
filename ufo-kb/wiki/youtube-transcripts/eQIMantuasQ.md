# Why The CIA Ran A Psychic Spy Program

American Alchemy episode with two guests: [[paul-smith]], a former CIA [[stargate-program]] remote viewer based in Cedar City, Utah, and [[herb-metz]], a parapsychologist who spent over a decade at the [[princeton-parapsychology-lab]] (PEAR) studying random event generators (REGs). The episode covers the operational history of Stargate, the PEAR lab REG experiments, and the philosophical implications for physicalism.

## Headline Claims

- **Paul Smith firsthand — USS Stark precognition**: In 1987, Smith was tasked to "describe whatever is most important to know about in the next few days." He described a vessel resembling a US destroyer being struck by two airborne cylinders with stubby wings that made roaring sounds — leaving the vessel on fire and listing. That weekend, the USS Stark was hit by Iraqi Exocet missiles. He had the session locked in his safe before the attack.
- **PEAR lab REG data**: Over thousands of trials, human observers with a set intention can produce statistically significant skews in the output of quantum-random event generators. The data shows a clean directional correlation: subjects who try to produce more 1s get more 1s; those targeting 0s get more 0s; those targeting even distribution get even distribution.
- **Transmission model of consciousness**: Both Smith and Metz endorse the position that the brain is a receiver/transducer of consciousness, not its generator — the TV analogy: pulling out a plug distorts the picture, but the TV is not generating the program. This directly attacks physicalism.
- **Paradigm suppression is structural**: The dominant scientific paradigm actively excludes competing models not through disproof but through control of publication organs. Parapsychology has received less than ~$30M globally in 100 years — less than the cost of a single particle accelerator.
- **Sheep/goat effect** ([[gertrude-schmeidler]]): Believers in psychic phenomena ("sheep") consistently outperform skeptics ("goats") in parapsychology studies. Crucially, the experimenter's own orientation also affects results — making a priori skeptical methodology self-refuting.
- **Ingo Swann's quark detector session** (1972): [[ingo-swann]]'s accurate remote viewing of a shielded quark detector's internal readouts — without touching or being near the machine — prompted high-level defense department attention and directly triggered the CIA to launch [[stargate-program]].
- **Transcranial magnetic stimulation as amplifier**: [[herb-metz]] proposes that TMS applied to inhibitory brain regions could enhance subjects' ability to affect REGs — the brain actively self-regulates and suppresses psi effects.
