# Joe Rogan Experience #1315 - Bob Lazar & Jeremy Corbell

[[joe-rogan]] hosts [[bob-lazar]] and [[jeremy-corbell]] for a 135-minute session that became one of the most-watched UFO interviews in modern history. Lazar recounts his S4 experience in unusual detail, Corbell authenticates the [[wilson-davis-memo]] on camera, and Rogan frames Lazar as the canonical figure the entire modern disclosure movement is "playing catch-up to."

## Headline Claims

- **Lazar (firsthand)**: The [[s4-sport-model]]'s reactor produced a gravitational field strong enough to physically repel hands — no heat signature under any load, no magnetic field detected — violating known thermodynamics. Nothing Earth-made can do this.
- **Lazar (firsthand)**: Russian scientists were present at S4 in the late 1980s during the early project phases; after an unspecified "big discovery" by another team, the Soviets were permanently expelled from the base.
- **Lazar (firsthand)**: One of the nine craft at S4 had what appeared to be projectile damage — a hole punched through the edge of a very flat disc, suggesting either weapons testing or a hostile encounter before acquisition.
- **Lazar (firsthand)**: [[project-looking-glass]] was concerned with "time — any effects of time in the craft" — specifically "small, intentional distortions of time" that were being explored as part of a weapons program alongside [[project-sidekick]].
- **Corbell (investigative)**: After 30 years, Corbell located [[mike-thigpen]] — the specific security clearance officer Lazar had named — via Facebook/Google image matching through his children. Thigpen confirmed to Corbell in three phone calls that he performed security clearances for the base in 1989 and remembered Lazar.
- **Corbell (investigative)**: The [[wilson-davis-memo]] is authentic — "I'm in a position to know and it is a real document." The meeting at [[egng]] Special Projects involved an admiral being denied access by private industry to a SAP containing non-terrestrial materials.
- **Corbell (investigative)**: The $22 million attributed to AATIP actually went to [[aawsap]] — the "mother program" that was primarily a government-funded contract to study [[skinwalker-ranch]]. [[aatip]] was an auxiliary military effort.

## Bob Lazar's Origin Story — New Details

### Edward Teller Introduction (1982)

Lazar built a jet-powered Honda that appeared on the front page of the Los Alamos lab newspaper. The day that issue ran, [[edward-teller]] was visiting Los Alamos to give a lecture. Lazar went early, found Teller leaning on a brick wall reading the article, introduced himself — "I'm the guy you're reading about" — and they spoke briefly.

Years later, after Lazar had left Los Alamos and started other businesses in Las Vegas, he sent resumes back into the scientific community. One went to Teller, referencing their meeting. Teller remembered him and provided a reference to someone at [[egng]], initiating the S4 recruitment process.

### EG&G Special Projects McCarran Interviews

The job interviews took place at **EG&G Special Projects** at McCarran Airport, Las Vegas. The job description was deliberately vague: "advanced propulsion" in a "remote area," with an unusual schedule — some days on, some days off, potentially two weeks on and two weeks off. Lazar recognized this as normal Nevada Test Site working patterns.

### First Day: Janet Airlines and Paperwork

First day was uneventful: flew [[janet-airlines]] (Las Vegas → Area 51) and processed two to three hours of security clearance paperwork. No craft, no briefings, nothing unusual.

## The S4 Facility and Craft

### First Contact With the Craft

On a subsequent visit, the hangar doors were open as Lazar's bus arrived. He walked in and saw the disc — and initially thought: "Oh my god, this finally explains all the flying saucer stories — this is just an advanced fighter." Then he slid his hand along the surface and was immediately reprimanded by an armed guard: "Keep your eyes forward and your hands at your side."

### The Reactor Demonstration

Lab partner [[barry-castillo]] ran the reactor demonstration. The device — a hemisphere about the size of a basketball on a metal plate — produced a gravitational field when the hemisphere was placed on the base tower. Barry challenged Lazar to touch the sphere. He could not: "It would push my hands away just like two like poles of a magnet." Lazar's instant recognition: "Nothing that does that [exists]."

**Thermal signature test**: Infrared cameras (requiring liquid-nitrogen cooling in the 1980s) showed the reactor never exceeded ambient temperature regardless of load — **it produced zero waste heat**, violating the first law of thermodynamics.

**Magnetic test**: No magnetic field detectable around the reactor at any load level.

These two violations of known physics confirmed to Lazar this was not human technology.

### Reactor Activation Mechanism

Placing the hemisphere on the central tower activates the reactor; removing it shuts it down (load-sensing). No wiring connects sub-components — they operate by proximity alone. Lazar: "The stuff is borderline magic."

### Cyclotron Model for Reactor Base

The team's best hypothesis: the square base of the reactor functions as a miniature cyclotron — a circular particle accelerator — bombarding the Element 115 fuel with protons. The resulting antimatter cascade generates the gravity wave. They could not fully understand the mechanism.

### Prior Scientist Death

Lazar knew his lab partner Barry had a previous partner who died during reactor analysis — an "unannounced nuclear test" that was actually an attempt to cut open an operating reactor, apparently with a plasma cutter. Lazar's inference: the program has been cycling through scientists on a multi-decade timeline, making incremental progress then pausing when a roadblock is hit.

## Craft Interior Description

Lazar was allowed inside the craft once. Key observations:

- **Scale**: ~52 feet in diameter, designed for occupants roughly half human height (~3 feet). Lazar at 5'10" could only stand upright at the very center.
- **Aesthetics**: Everything a single dark pewter color, no right angles — "as if someone took a model of wax and heated it just for a short time so everything melted." All seams and junctions have a radius of curvature.
- **Seats**: Three seats equidistant around the central reactor. Three large rectangular objects (also equidistant) at the same level — no buttons, no lights, same color as everything else.
- **Three levels**: Main level (seats/consoles); middle level (gravity amplifiers below the rectangular objects); lower level (gravity emitters — "like a trash can hanging on a pipe") — three of each.
- **Top level**: Lazar believes it handles navigation/positioning via planar sensor panels around the craft that look like portholes but are simply black panels.
- **Archways**: Structural arches that could become transparent (similar to "smart glass"), allowing viewing out.
- **Honeycomb hatch**: The only component Lazar understood intuitively — a folding honeycomb-sheet hatch providing access between levels. Applied pressure from above (compressed honeycomb) is strong; from the side, it collapses, creating the opening. "The only thing in the craft that made absolute sense to me."
- **No controls visible**: No control panels, no buttons, no screens, no wiring.

## The Nine Craft

All nine craft at S4 had different shapes:
- The "Sport Model" — the one Lazar worked on, smooth disc, ~52 ft
- A "jello mold" — with rippled sides
- A very flat disc like a straw hat — sitting on its edge with apparent **projectile damage** through the edge (a hole, as if something was fired through it; either weapons testing on it, or it arrived that way)
- Others at too far a distance to characterize

The nine craft had identical reactors. Lazar's inference: they were manufactured, not independently crashed. "You don't find nine with identical reactors."

**On origin**: One of the craft may have come from an **archaeological dig** — Barry alluded to this, though Lazar cannot confirm it was the Sport Model specifically. This implies at least one craft is ancient rather than recently crashed.

## Russian Scientists at S4

During the early phase of the project — while Lazar was there in 1988–1989 — Soviet scientists were present at S4. Barry referred to them as "the commies." At some point after their arrival but apparently during Lazar's time or just after, **another team (not Lazar's group) made a significant discovery**. After that discovery, the Russians were permanently expelled from the base. Lazar does not know what the discovery was.

## Flight Tests

### First Observed Test

Dennis Mariani (S4 supervisor) invited Lazar and Barry to observe a test. The Sport Model was already outside the hangar. Key observations:
- **Liftoff**: A **blue corona discharge** (high-voltage brush discharge) appeared at the bottom during liftoff — "a bluish glow" with a slight hissing sound
- At approximately 5–10 feet altitude, both the discharge and the hissing **stopped instantly**, and the craft hovered in complete silence
- Maneuvers: left, right, down — simple movements only
- Communication: Operator used a regular VHF radio to communicate with whoever was inside. Radio waves should bend around the gravity envelope and not penetrate — Lazar and Barry spent time puzzling over why this worked at all. Lazar's explanation: the heart-shaped gravity distortion around the craft (produced by the waveguide appendage at the top) warps light and radio waves around it — normally rendering the craft invisible from below and making radio communication impossible. How they communicated remained unexplained.

### High-Performance Tests (Wednesdays)

Lazar obtained the test flight schedule. All high-performance tests were on Wednesdays (statistically lowest traffic on nearby highways). High-performance tests went above the mountain range and involved radical maneuvers. Lazar: "Nothing can move like that" — like a laser pointer moving on a wall with no apparent inertia or atmospheric resistance.

### Getting Caught (Third Observation)

Three separate trips to watch tests from outside the base perimeter. First two: successful. Third time, Lazar's group arrived with a motor home (his admission: "the stupidest thing you could possibly do"). They joked carelessly about the base, not realizing security guards with night-vision scopes were standing 20 feet away in pitch darkness. A guard dropped his scope; it rolled toward Lazar's group. Lights on: they were surrounded.

Consequence: Debriefed next day at Indian Springs Air Force Base. Security had phone transcripts of Lazar's wife's affair (they'd been monitoring his calls). They confronted him with the transcript. That night, he was let go — but realized his situation had become serious. Shortly after, he contacted [[george-knapp]].

## Disclosure and Retaliation

### Going Public

George Knapp recorded Lazar's story as insurance; as events accelerated, Knapp asked Lazar to go public. On the day of the scheduled 5 PM broadcast, Lazar reversed his decision at the last moment. Knapp physically wrestled the tape from him and aired it anyway. That evening, Lazar received a call from Dennis Mariani: "Have any idea what we're going to do to you now?" — the last contact Lazar had with Mariani.

### Retaliation Pattern

Post-disclosure consequences documented by Lazar and Corbell:
- IRS audits of Lazar and associates
- Security clearances pulled for friends and colleagues still working at Tonopah Test Range
- Education records erased: no MIT enrollment records, no Caltech records, birth certificate disappeared
- Employment records at Los Alamos denied, then partially recovered via a 1982 phone directory and a newspaper article naming "Los Alamos physicist Bob Lazar"

Corbell: George Knapp tracked the erasure attempts in real time and documented them across years of investigative reporting.

## Corroborations Discussed in This Episode

### Mike Thigpen (Newly Revealed)

[[mike-thigpen]] was the specific security clearance officer Lazar named early in his account. For 30 years, Thigpen ghosted Knapp (who named him publicly on the news, causing Thigpen to "drop his fork" at dinner). Corbell found Thigpen via Facebook/Google image matching through his adult children. In **three phone calls**, Thigpen confirmed:
- He performed security clearances for the S4/Area 51 facility in 1989
- He remembers Bob Lazar
- He almost agreed to go on camera (ultimately declined)

A handwritten note with Thigpen's signature was left for Lazar at a friend's house during the clearance investigation process — Corbell has located it.

### Dr. Krangle

[[dr-krangle]] came forward and stated: "I was in security briefings with Bob Lazar the physicist at Los Angeles." He went on record with Corbell. Other individuals who could corroborate remain silent because they are still employed at the facility.

### Biometric Hand Scanner (S4)

At S4 access control, a device read the bone lengths inside workers' fingers using transmitted light (before the era of fingerprint scanners). The device had a glass plate with a hand outline and pins; you placed your hand in, a bright light illuminated through the bones from above, a sensor underneath read the unique bone-length pattern, and your badge ejected. Lazar described this publicly in the 1980s and was widely ridiculed as fabricating science fiction. Journalist [[tyler-rogoway]] found documentary evidence that similar devices were used at **Tonopah Test Range** and possibly Area 52 during the same period — early biometrics validated by independent source.

### Gravitational Wave Detection (LIGO, 2015–2016)

Corbell cites the first observation of gravitational waves — September 14, 2015 (announced February 11, 2016) by the LIGO/Virgo collaboration — as validation of Lazar's core claim that gravity is a wave and not a particle (graviton model was dominant in 1989). Lazar: "I had about a 50-50 chance" — deflecting the significance, but acknowledging it.

## The Wilson-Davis Memo (Corbell Authentication)

[[jeremy-corbell]] authenticates the [[wilson-davis-memo]] on camera, explicitly: "I'm in a position to know and it is a real document." Key points from his read:
- The memo documents a meeting at **EG&G Special Projects** between Admiral [[thomas-wilson]] (then-sitting admiral) and a scientist employed by [[robert-bigelow]]'s organization
- Wilson sought access to a Special Access Program (SAP) involving non-terrestrial materials reverse engineering; he was **denied access by private industry**
- The program is cycled: make some progress, reach a material-science bottleneck, mothball it for ~10 years, restart when science catches up — consistent with Lazar's account of prior workers
- The document dates to approximately 2001–2002 and connects the Bigelow AAWSAP/NIDS research lineage to the EG&G contractor network Lazar was embedded in

## AAWSAP / AATIP Clarification

Corbell clarifies the common public misunderstanding:
- The $22 million congressional allocation went to **[[aawsap]]** (Advanced Aerospace Weapon Systems Applications Program) — the "mother program" run through [[robert-bigelow]]'s BAASS under a DIA contract
- AAWSAP's primary mandate was studying [[skinwalker-ranch]] — scientists there witnessed "vehicles come through like a space in the sky"
- **[[aatip]]** (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program) was a separate, auxiliary military program dealing with pilot and radar encounters (e.g., Fravor's Tic-Tac case)
- The terminology shift (UFO → AAV → UAP) is deliberate: DIA documents used "Advanced Aerospace Vehicles" (AAVs) — Hillary Clinton's "UAP" signal on Jimmy Kimmel was a deliberate public-facing terminology alignment

## Tic-Tac / Gimbal Connections

### Tic-Tac Water Disturbance

[[david-fravor]] observed not just the Tic-Tac above but a disturbance on the water surface below — an apparent USO. Corbell: some analysts believe the Tic-Tac system itself was causing the underwater disturbance. Fravor observed the [[2004-nimitz-tic-tac]] event over a section of ocean where the surface was visibly disturbed. The craft later appeared at Fravor's cap point (60 miles away) within ~60 seconds of radar cycle time.

### Gimbal Roll Maneuver

Corbell analyzes the [[2015-gimbal]] video: the craft makes a characteristic **roll maneuver** — rotating belly-first toward its direction of travel — consistent with how Lazar describes the S4 craft operating. The craft flies "wheels first" (belly-forward), not nose-first like conventional aircraft. The Gimbal video's thermal signature also shows a pocket of cold air around the propulsion source — no heat signature from an operating system, mirroring the zero-heat-output Lazar observed from the S4 reactor.

### Lazar on Gravity-Propelled Flight

Lazar explains why craft operating on gravity propulsion are indifferent to medium (air, water, space): the gravitational envelope renders the craft effectively separate from the local physics environment. Inertia does not affect it; atmospheric drag does not apply; transitioning between air and water involves no meaningful physics change. The Fravor observation of the Tic-Tac descending from 80,000 feet (the ceiling of the SPY-1 radar) to 50 feet in less than a second is consistent with this.

## Project Looking Glass — Time Effects Confirmed

Lazar explicitly confirms [[project-looking-glass]] on camera: "It had to do with time — any effects of time in the craft. I don't think we're talking about making a time machine like in science fiction, but we're talking about small distortions, intentional distortions of time and how that can be used — as part of a weapon program."

This connects to Fravor's Tic-Tac observation that the craft appeared at his next waypoint **before Fravor himself knew where his next waypoint would be** — Lazar suggests this could be consistent with time-perception effects from the gravity field.
