# "We Have Antigravity UFOs!" (ft. Nick Cook)

## Headline Claims

- **The 1956 G-Engines Are Coming article** named the top US aerospace companies (Martin, Lear, Bell, Convair) as confident they could build anti-gravity aircraft within years; Martin VP [[george-trimble]] predicted success on a Manhattan Project timeline. The same companies then went completely silent by the early 1960s — strongly implying classification rather than failure.
- **George Trimble's refusal** to speak with Cook — relayed through a Lockheed PR contact — was described as terrified, representing a pivotal "if I wasn't interested before, I am now" moment in Cook's investigation.
- **The 1971 Australian JIO memo** ([[1971-australian-jio-memo]]) names [[j-robert-oppenheimer]] and the same aerospace companies as deeply involved in anti-gravity research at the CIA's behest, lists six academic institutions, and asserts that [[project-blue-book]] was a deliberate front to conceal a real program.
- **Thomas Townsend Brown's electrogravitics** was likely transferred to Northrop and incorporated into the B2 stealth bomber via Floyd Odlum (sole investor in Brown's Guidance Technologies and majority owner of Northrop before its merger). Aviation journalist [[bill-gunston]] stated publicly the B2's performance data doesn't add up given its engines, then invoked Brown's capacitor work — then dropped the story, citing the Tower of London.
- **Ben Rich** (Skunk Works director) told Cook in direct interview that Skunk Works once contemplated burying stealth technology entirely — burying it in a metaphorical warehouse "like Raiders of the Lost Ark" — and made his famous ESP/all-points-in-space-time statement when asked how the technology to "take ET home" would work.
- **Ning Lee** was personally interviewed by Cook at a 2003 Washington DC conference; she went off-grid "within about a year" after their meeting, following receipt of a DOD contract and security clearance.
- Cook witnessed the **ASTRA** designation at the top of a Skunk Works org chart during a visit to Lockheed's Palmdale headquarters — then was immediately escorted out. A later Skunk Works director confirmed this incident was well-known internally.
- **The Aurora hypersonic aircraft** is argued to be real based on: US Geological Survey sonic boom tracking toward Area 51, [[chris-gibson]]'s North Sea sighting (Royal Observer Corps observer), British MOD documents, a $2B line item in a late-1980s budget, and [[george-muellner]]'s retirement-day puzzle about a "gap" between SR-71 and satellite coverage that "something" filled.
