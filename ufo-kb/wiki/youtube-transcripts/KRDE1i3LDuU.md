# Plato's Cave & UFOs: Consciousness Is Key (ft. James Madden)

[[jesse-michaels]] interviews [[jim-madden]] — philosopher, professor, and author of *[[unidentified-flying-hyper-object]]* — in a dense conversation applying Plato's Republic, Heidegger's technology critique, Aristotle's natural theology, and German Idealism to the UFO phenomenon, the limits of disclosure, and the structure of human consciousness.

## Who Was Plato — Madden's Revisionist Reading

- Plato was Socrates' student; his given name was a nickname — "Broad" or "Big" (broad-shouldered) because he was the most accomplished wrestler of his generation in Athens
  - Most of his writings are dialogues; Socrates is the main character but does not always speak for Plato
- The Republic is set at a festival that classical scholars date to when Plato would have been a young child — the characters (Glaucon, Adeimantus, Thrasymachus) were all dead by the time he wrote it, having been on opposing sides of the post-Peloponnesian War political struggle
  - "What Plato's depicting is this moment where everybody was together behaving civilly for the last time — and then it all fell apart"
- **The real topic of the Republic is death, not justice:**
  - The dialogue opens with an old man claiming the just don't need to fear death — then he leaves when pressed to define justice
  - The book ends with the Myth of Er — a story about the afterlife
  - "If you ask me what the Republic is about, it's really about death — and the human condition in facing death"
  - Justice is the secondary frame; answering "what is justice?" is how they try to address the prior question of whether death should be feared
- **Plato was not a Platonist:**
  - Philosophy 101 caricature (eternal Forms, pure allegory, systematic doctrine) is a misreading
  - There is a lot of "put on" in the dialogues — Plato is testing whether the reader gets it
  - [[leo-strauss|Leo Strauss]] canonized the esoteric/exoteric distinction for Platonic reading: is Plato's public text concealing a coded message?
  - Madden: even Strauss's own texts may have been written in coded fashion — the regress never ends
- **Circular founding problem:** the ideal city requires a philosopher-king, but producing a philosopher requires an ideal city
  - Plato has Socrates say: in OUR city (the ideal one), the philosopher would owe education to the city and should return — but the ideal city isn't going to happen
  - Implication: in any actual human political situation, the philosopher should NOT return to the cave; it would be futile

## The Cave Allegory — Mechanics

- Prisoners chained in a cave, facing forward; all they see are shadows dancing on the wall — what would today be called consensus reality
  - The shadows are "rightish but not fully right" — they correspond to real things (puppets) but are filtered through a fire, not direct light
  - Nobody knows the difference; it would be impossible to separate yourself from this consensus reality from within it
- "Voilà, somebody gets loose" — Plato never explains how the initial liberation happens; it is not caused by the prisoner
  - The liberated person turns to see: a wall behind them, people behind the wall with puppets on sticks, and a fire casting light onto the wall
- Climbing out of the cave is arduous, painful, and iterative:
  - The former prisoner must leave, adjust, return a little, adjust more — working their way toward the sun
  - The fire inside is a type of light/knowledge but not full knowledge; the sun is the source of all earthly fire
  - For the Greeks: all heat, all fire on earth ultimately derives from the sun — the prisoner is seeking the source of that fire
  - Can only glimpse the sun — Plato calls it "the Good"; you can never point to it because it is the source by which everything else can be seen; it is the horizon against which things are judged, not an object in front of the horizon
- Returning to the cave: the freed philosopher stumbles around as if blind; the prisoners think he's a fool; eventually they kill him (→ Socrates)
- Push and pull: someone outside must be guiding the liberation — "there has to be someone outside pulling us through"
  - Plato argues education doesn't just happen; people are disposed to stay in illusion, not leave it; someone has to be pulling

## The Noble Lie / Good Myth

- At a certain point in the Republic, Socrates concludes that the guardians must be told "good lies" (or "good myths") about the afterlife — stories that create behavioral compliance in those who must run the state
- The entire Republic ends with such a myth: the Myth of Er — a good lie about the afterlife
  - Madden's reading: Plato dragged you through the same educative process the book describes; "the whole book tricks you into following that very process"
  - "Everyone has to be lied to in this process — the guardians transmit the lie too"
- Plato's thesis: since any natural city will collapse due to greed and selfishness, no ideal city can ever be legitimately founded — the lie is therefore an infinite regress

## The Myth of Er — Memory as Access to Truth

- Er is a virtuous soldier who dies in battle, enters the afterlife, witnesses the system of rewards and lot-drawing for next lives, and uniquely does NOT drink from the River of Lethe (Forgetfulness) before returning
  - All others sleep beside the river and forget what they've seen before returning to temporal life; Er comes back with full memory
  - The most virtuous in the afterlife are indifferent to what lot they draw — because they know they'll be okay regardless; their virtue will carry them through whatever circumstances
- Madden's interpretation: truth is more like MEMORY than discovery
  - In the Meno, Socrates demonstrates that an uneducated slave can derive geometric truths through questioning alone — all knowledge is recollection of what we already had
  - "A harp reminds you of your lover but doesn't resemble them at all" — some earthly things remind us of transcendent truth without representing it
  - Our relationship to ultimate truth is more like recollection than like empirical investigation

## Eleusinian Mysteries — Plato's Possible Source

- Eleusis: a site 13 miles NW of Athens, considered the spiritual capital of Greece
  - Greek leaders and Roman emperors participated; widely held to have been essential to the fabric of society
  - Alcibiades was in serious trouble for talking about the Mysteries at an unauthorized occasion
- The initiates drank the kykeon — [[brian-muraresku|Brian Muraresku]] argues (*The Immortality Key*) that the kykeon contained an ergot-derived LSD-like compound (hallucinogenic fungus)
  - Plato, Socrates, and most prominent Athenians almost certainly participated in the Mysteries
  - Madden: "I have no reason to rule that out. This has been around religion everywhere. I'd be surprised if something like that wasn't going on."
- The Mysteries as a "cheat code to the goal line" — initiates got a direct glimpse of the truth; then spent the rest of their lives trying to think their way back to it
  - [[david-grusch|David Grusch]] on this type of experience: "The symbol rate is too slow — you literally can't communicate it in words"
  - How do you show the light by which we see other things? You end up speaking in riddles and allegories like prophets

## The UFO as Cave-Breaking Phenomenon

- Madden's origin story: 2021, watching X-Files with his kids during the first Pentagon press conference — "I guess Mulder was right"
  - Studied all the books; the UFO became a "great thought experiment about — we could be really wrong about what the actual nature of being is"
  - The more he thought about it, the more it connected with philosophy of mind and cognitive science: "Hell yeah, we really could be missing something that was concretely right there all along"
- [[carl-jung|Carl Jung]]'s *Flying Saucers: A Modern Myth of Things Seen in the Skies*:
  - Jung started the book treating UFOs as a psychology of collective anxiety — post-WWII nuclear fear expressing itself in the going metaphors of the era (airborne technology)
  - As the book goes on, "it's getting harder for him to dismiss it though" — by the end: "And yet they're on radar. And yet they leave tracks."
  - Jesse: "[[diana-pasulka|Diana Pasulka]] had the same experience — started writing *Heaven Can Wait* about UFOs as modern religious phenomena and ended up going down the rabbit hole in real time as you read it"
- The cave as a feature, not a bug, of human cognition:
  - Cognitive science supports the idea that the mind works AS MUCH by learning what to ignore as by learning what to attend to
  - "We're constantly carving a cave out. The stuff we do see is there — I'm not an idealist. But there's always more there."
  - Irreducible human contribution to perception: once admitted, teasing out the human from the non-human becomes the thorniest problem in epistemology

## The Umwelt and Perceptive Apparatus

- Jakob von Uexküll's concept of Umwelt — the perceptual world specific to each organism; different creatures inhabit different perceptual realities carved from the same physical world
  - [[eric-weinstein|Eric Weinstein]]: science is "umwelt hacking" — a continuous process of expanding the perceptive apparatus
  - Madden: our umwelt is widening through science and humanities, but always tethered to the same evolutionary origin point
- Human perceptive limits: 400–700nm of EM spectrum; narrow decibel range; these are hard constraints, not choices
  - "To say we are measurement sensors limited inside of a larger reality is not controversial" — Jesse
  - [[ken-wilson-physicist|Ken Wilson]] (physicist): higher energy output accesses more ontological truth; phase transitions in materials reveal properties invisible at lower energies
  - Strong anthropic principle: physics constants comporting to human observer limits is not evidence of many tries — "physics itself is comporting to our perceptive limitations"
- [[william-james|William James]]' dog-in-a-library analogy: humans are not unlike dogs running around a library during a high-level intellectual conversation
  - The dog hears the sounds, smells the room — all really there — but it's a carve-out from something much richer; the conversation can't possibly mean anything to it
  - We could easily be cellular automata in a larger system; Pavlov/Skinner show how easily humans can be conditioned
- Three Body Problem (Dark Forest trilogy) / Flatland: beings on a 2D plane perceive 3D bullet holes as inexplicable craters; "they're not discrete holes made by shooters they can't even perceive"
- Two exits from the cave:
  - (1) Building your own perceptive apparatus — cultivating consciousness, personal epistemological work; very hard, no formula
  - (2) Physics that breaks the cave's boundaries — a firecracker in the cave (like nuclear weapons in the 1940s)
  - But even (2) must eventually run on human cognitive hardware; "it's still going to come back to modes of cognition developed for paleolithic ancestors"

## Heidegger's Question Concerning Technology

- Essence of technology isn't devices or screen time — it's an ATTITUDE toward being
  - The attitude: asking nature "what can I extract from you?" rather than "how can I work with you?"
  - Something happened in early modernity (no committee decided this) that caused humans to begin asking extractive questions of nature
  - Medieval windmill: works when the wind blows — forces you to work WITH nature on its terms
  - Hydroelectric dam: stores and deploys nature's energy on OUR terms — nature as resource to be extracted
- Technology as "oblivion to being":
  - We risk only seeing the world in terms of how we can access and use it
  - Heidegger was horrified the first time he heard the phrase "human resources" — eventually we'll see ourselves in technological terms of accessibility and use
  - Not worried about Soylent Green literally — but worried that we lose a means/end view of EVERYTHING including ourselves
- Heidegger vs. Plato:
  - Plato: active dialectical educational process can get you to enlightenment; there's a curriculum
  - Heidegger: more passive — "build a temple and hope someone shows up"; you can't engineer your way to transcendence
  - Madden leans more toward Heidegger here
- UFO as "knocking at the door":
  - Haidegger's framing: we can't force transcendence; we can only build a receptive structure for it to show up
  - UFOs = something knocking, something trying to remind the human of something — whether you use Plato's dialectical process or Heidegger's receptive waiting

## Prometheus Bound — Humans on Rails to Destruction

- Sophocles' Prometheus Bound: Hephaestus (god of smithing) chains Prometheus to a rock
  - The irony: Prometheus gave humans all the arts, including smithing — he is being bound by the very technology he enabled
  - Force (speaking for Zeus) mocks him: "Your name means foreknowledge — didn't you see this coming?"
  - Prometheus: "I did see it coming. I wanted this."
  - Prometheus also says, later in the myth: "I hate all gods."
  - He predicts: a child of Io's line in the 13th generation (Hercules) will overthrow Zeus — "a kind of death-of-god assertive humanism"
- The Faust/Prometheus pattern: humans as technological animals always diving into the edge; Madden reads this as "humans on rails to a kind of self-assertive self-destruction"
- [[manhattan-project|Manhattan Project]] parallel:
  - Oppenheimer gives three different answers for why he wanted to do it across different interviews — technology was running him, not the other way
  - Teller: "It's going to happen so we might as well build it ourselves" — pure technological determinism
  - UFOs spike in visible reality exactly when nuclear weapons are first tested: "maybe it's like a firecracker going off in the cave"
  - "Does something super unprecedented happen when we develop perceptive apparatus through high energy output to see who's messing with us? Does that just unravel reality?"
- Collective suicide became possible in the 1940s for the first time in human history — "that has to change us in ways none of us have really dealt with in our unconscious"

## Disclosure Politics — Madden's Aloof Stance

- Madden has become "self-consciously aloof" to disclosure drama
  - [[chris-ramsey|Chris Ramsay]] ([[area-52|Area 52]]) told Jesse: "I'm not really into disclosure. I'm into UFOs." — Madden endorses this orientation
  - Either way, there's a low-probability conspiracy to accept:
    - Version A: this has been covered up for 75+ years (unlikely)
    - Version B: now suddenly powerful people want us to believe it (also unlikely)
  - "So either way you've got a low prima facie probability conspiracy you're going to have to take"
- Why would top government/military/science figures systematically signal the American population in a multipolar new Cold War context? "That seems crazier than the inverse — that this has been going on for thousands of years"
- Hume's argument about miracles applied to whistleblower testimony:
  - A witness can speak a falsehood without lying — they may have been deceived themselves
  - Testimony probability must be weighed against the improbability of the claim; meaningful comparison is often impossible
  - Tic Tac: Fravor and Dietrich are seemingly very reliable witnesses; but "I only know Bin Laden was shot because of testimony that came through media through reliable Pentagon officials" — same epistemic structure
- Disclosure would never fully land:
  - Evolution and Big Bang have been taught in public schools for generations; not everyone believes them
  - "You could have something become educational orthodoxy and it doesn't necessarily move the needle for everyone"
  - Real disclosure prediction: a weird, surprising bottom-up event — NOT a top-down government announcement from official authority figures

## The Aristotle Connection — NHI Perfectly Compatible

- Aristotle's cosmology: the planets ARE intelligences; the entire cosmos is organized by an "unmoved mover"
  - "The idea that things are managed by non-human intelligence for Aristotle is apparent in his view"
  - UFOs as envoys, von Neumann replicators managing earth from a higher AI: compatible with Aristotelian framework
- Aristotle on religion (end of his *natural theology*):
  - "Our most ancient ancestors were not wrong to posit a divinity surrounding nature... BUT it got co-opted into stories told for legal purposes"
  - Religion can go two ways: (1) co-opted into legal/political control; (2) intuitive revelation running ahead of scientific/philosophical proof
  - "Intuition can be out in front of what we can prove and guide the inquiry"
- If the sun is conscious (Sheldrake's paper argues yes), and if certain organizational structures reliably produce consciousness wherever they appear, then we get a universe suffused with consciousness without anything "irresponsibly spooky"
  - [[roger-penrose|Roger Penrose]]'s Orchestrated Objective Reduction (Orch OR): quantum consciousness via microtubules
  - [[rupert-sheldrake|Rupert Sheldrake]]'s morphic resonance: inherited learnings transcending genetic inheritance; once Roger Bannister breaks the 4-minute mile, others follow quickly (Banister Effect)

## The Monolith, Science as Mythology, and Re-enchantment

- 2001: A Space Odyssey monolith — hermeneutic reading: the monolith IS a cinema screen (aspect ratios match the cinematic format of the era)
  - "The monolith is the noble mythology of the future"
  - Diana Pasulka's point: media shapes our understanding of what we see; maybe they literally ARE presenting UFOs in the form of the going metaphors
  - Vallée: if we believe in space aliens enough, they'll appear that way — an egregore model where consensus reality is a collapsing function
- Science as modern mythology:
  - "Science is the most sacred thing we hold — it has a founding myth (Big Bang), rituals (lab coats), a priestly citadel"
  - Honest placebo studies: a placebo works even when you tell the patient it's a placebo, as long as someone in a lab coat administers it in proper packaging
  - Credential-recognition bypasses critical thinking: once someone establishes a Q-clearance credential, "whatever you say next, they believe"
- Re-enchantment (Max Weber's "disenchantment of the world"):
  - The Richard Dawkins/New Atheist era seems to be ending; Gen Z is coming online in a period of re-enchantment vs. Millennials who came online during the Dawkins craze
  - Healthy openness to enchantment vs. unhealthy openness to mystification: "I worry the swing could go to the irrational — any enchantment will do now"
  - In UFO world: some people go "pro-UFO" and become defensive believers — "this is real, period"; this is a political campaign, not truth-seeking
- Hegel and the hermetic tradition:
  - *Hegel and the Hermetic Tradition*: Hegel was contemporaries with Jacob Böhme (mystic); rumors of Rosicrucian involvement; Madden: "I'm not surprised if he had some dabblings of that sort"
  - [[jacques-vallee|Jacques Vallée]] named his 1970s working group "The Invisible College" — a Rosicrucian concept, associated with Robert Boyle; "less than subtle hint"
  - Madden: even if Hegel had esoteric affiliations, happy to take his public texts at face value; private dimensions don't necessarily change how we read the work

## Protocols for Ascending the Cave

- Madden's answer: "Nothing fancier than what you have in basic Greek ethics — don't allow irrational passion to run your life; know thyself"
  - Aristotle's Nicomachean Ethics as the protocol
  - "It's like the original protocol was a kind of life"
- Life vs. psychedelics:
  - Life is like surfing — well-earned, catching each wave
  - Psychedelics are like snowboarding — you start at the top of the mountain and must survive all the way down
  - "There are no shortcuts. There's no cheat code. Though the experience can be a legitimate tool for specific therapeutic contexts (PTSD veterans, etc.)"
- On the contemplative life:
  - "If you make a commitment to living a contemplative life, you worry more about what's true — that removes static from the background so you can hear things"
  - "Increasing signal-to-noise ratio — that's what discernment is"
  - Synchronicities: Scylla and Charybdis — put ALL faith in synchronicities and you end up in a solipsistic loop; impute NO meaning and you miss genuine signal; it's an art, not a science
- Nietzsche: first line of *Genealogy of Morals* preface — "We knowers are least well known to ourselves" — a direct challenge to Socrates' "know thyself"
  - "With the UFO topic you need to be open to how your own unconscious could be gaming you for this — 100%"
  - "The dirty little secret in all of ufology: everybody has some autobiographical thing or reason for being into it"
- Simulation theory critique:
  - Postmodern simulation theory is "nihilistic — play Grand Theft Auto, nothing matters; I can think of no better metaphor to prepare people to have bad lives"
  - Platonic version: more meaningful — you might be in a somewhat video-game-like situation BUT there's another level and it involves goodness; "that gives you a reason to live well here"

- What would Plato make of AI? Madden: "He would mistrust the whole deal. He would say 'Welcome to your cave, man.'"
