# Why Jonah Hill Made A Documentary About My Family

[[jesse-michaels]] interviews his father [[barry-michaels]] and his godfather [[phil-stutz]] — two therapists who jointly developed a widely influential psychotherapy methodology called "The Tools." The episode is primarily biographical and psychological, but contains significant coverage of [[rudolph-steiner]]'s anthroposophy, Jungian shadow work, Jesse's retrocausality model of consciousness, and the biographical origins of Jesse's interest in fringe science and UFOs. It was taped about a year before publication but released as both men were dealing with health challenges.

## Guest Backgrounds

- **[[phil-stutz|Phil Stutz]]**: Jesse's godfather; psychotherapist; subject of Jonah Hill's 2022 Netflix documentary *Stutz*
  - Early career: Riker's Island prison psychiatrist for 5 years, where assessment under duress became the baseline for his practice
  - In his early 30s: contracted chronic fatigue syndrome (extreme constant exhaustion, barely able to get out of bed); the breakdown catalyzed discovery of [[rudolph-steiner|Rudolf Steiner]] and his subsequent therapeutic approach
  - This illness paradoxically attracted some of the most influential people in the world as patients
  - Has an almost clairvoyant ability to assess people instantly and identify where they are blocked
  - His voicemail now refers all Netflix-documentary callers to his website; still receiving 7-10 such calls per week
- **[[barry-michaels|Barry Michaels]]**: Jesse's father; Phil's longtime business partner and co-author
  - Co-developed Phil's ideas and helped communicate them publicly
  - Co-authored *The Tools* (New York Times bestseller) and *Coming Alive* (followup)
  - Worked with CEOs of billion-dollar companies, A-list actors and producers, people on the verge of self-destruction
  - Trained at Harvard; considers [[phil-stutz|Phil Stutz]] the primary influence on his practice
  - Endorses [[graham-hancock]]'s thesis that human civilization extends much further back than mainstream archaeology admits
- **Jesse's origin story**: Both Phil and Barry are credited as the childhood influences that opened Jesse to fringe science, UFOs, and the mysterious nature of reality
  - "Not only am I talking about Phil, I'm also talking about my dad… they opened me up to the prospect that life is a little more trippy than we realize"
  - Jesse also mentions he once considered joining an official UFO disclosure documentary project but chose to build independently on [[american-alchemy|American Alchemy]] instead

## The Tools — Therapeutic Methodology

- **Core premise**: Therapy should give patients something actionable every session, not just facilitate open-ended talking
  - Phil's rule: "I couldn't let somebody leave my office without something — whether I had to make it up on the spot, whatever it is"
  - Traditional psychoanalysts criticized this as "demanding immediate gratification" — Phil rejected that framing as "a liar and an idiot"
  - Goal-setting is what differentiates progress from "masturbatory" self-examination: "Am I trying to stop losing my temper? Stop putting myself down? Small goals anchor the work in reality."
- **The shadow as the most powerful tool** (Phil's current view):
  - Shadow = "the unwanted inferior side of your personality — whatever you feel most ashamed about or embarrassed by"
  - Practice: talk to your shadow daily; let it talk back; develop an actual relationship with it
  - The shadow knows things you don't: Phil's story — when stumped with a non-talking patient, he asked his shadow "what do I do?" — the shadow said "look at his eyes"; Phil saw a phantom tear, said aloud "I'm sensing immense sadness," and the patient burst into tears and opened up completely
  - Unpredictability is the shadow's strength; unlike other tools, the shadow's direction cannot be predicted and yields surprising insights
  - Shadow and reversal of desire are complementary, not competing: the shadow can say "don't do the reversal today — take a walk, smell the roses"
- **Reversal of desire**: A tool for getting yourself to do things you avoid; not described in detail but framed as enabling positive agency ("I can get myself to return this email, make this phone call")
- **Loss processing**: A tool for letting go of investment in specific results — to be used either before or after a desired outcome fails to materialize
  - "Most human beings get too invested in getting results — they hedge their bets because they're afraid of not getting what they want, and then when they do get the result it's never as satisfying as they thought it would be"

## Jungian Shadow Work in More Depth

- Jungian framework underlying the tools: the Shadow is the version of yourself you most want to hide from the world
  - Unlike classical cognitive behavioral therapy, the tools incorporate Jungian archetypal symbolism
  - Jesse's interpretation: listening to the shadow produces synchronicities — the Butterfly Effect where small irrational impulses (run around the block, go to a random part of LA) lead to unexpected catalytic encounters
- **Differentiating shadow from demonic impulse**: Phil's answer is unavoidably subjective — "you have to go on instinct and create your own value system; it can't be based on proof"
  - The distinction matters because the shadow can be a great source of wisdom OR it can be the mind satiating hedonic needs
  - Phil's pyramid: Faith (bottom) → Action → Confidence (top); faith as an active choice not based on proof
- **Self-examination vs. narcissism**: "It becomes masturbatory when the purpose is no longer to have a positive impact on the people around you — when it's self-examination for its own sake"
  - Jesse's critique of modern coastal self-help: "a menu of eight things you do for yourself, none of which quite work, and you're still unhappy"
  - Phil agrees: "any body that says it's only bad is wrong, and anybody that says it's only good is also wrong"

## Rudolph Steiner's Anthroposophy

- **Who he was**: Turn-of-the-century Austrian philosopher; emerged from the Theosophical world but founded his own branch called **anthroposophy** — "empirical inquiry into spiritual phenomena"
  - Born 1860; also the father of organic farming, indicating a rigorously empirical dimension alongside the spiritual
  - Phil Stutz is a self-described "blind follower" of Steiner and considers him the defining influence of his worldview and practice
- **Key Steiner insight**: He "takes a psychological view of the human being but embeds it in a larger epistemology that includes spiritual forces surrounding the human being"
  - The only philosopher-psychologist Phil has studied who truly examines the interplay between surrounding spiritual forces and internal life
  - His evidence base was internal/experiential, not physical, which is both the limitation and the point: "you can internally test out some of the stuff"
- **The beehive as model**: Steiner was fascinated by bees; considered the beehive one single organism, not a conglomeration of individuals — compared favorably to wasps (every wasp-thing is separate)
  - The beehive represents what modern society lacks: a holistic, organistic model
- **Steiner's 100-year predictions** (~1920s, about 100 years later = now):
  - Education will become "almost impossible" due to narcissism getting so out of control that no one listens to anyone else
  - Strong leaders will become very hard to find; pseudo-strong leaders (but not truly strong) will dominate
  - Prediction was specific to narcissism as the root cause — Phil considers this fully accurate as of 2023
- **Steiner on Karl Marx**: Steiner said "the Devil was dictating to Karl Marx what to put in those books" — specifically the post-*Das Kapital* work
  - Phil's critique of Marx: Marx understood the creation of value through work but believed in self-regulation (the workers, once informed, would automatically correct the system); "there are no self-regulating systems," so once the concept of labor value emerged, "the bad guys said 'oh ——, here's real value, we're going to slurp it up'"
- **Steiner on Christ**: Not a teacher but a "man of action"; crucifixion was an active choice with karmic implications
  - Christ's function: he freely chose to go through crucifixion and by doing so "forgave the karma of the entire human race" — not individual sins but the collective karmic burden
  - Christ as the third factor between two poles: Ahriman (bureaucratic, dull, the Politburo-type evil) and Lucifer (manic, performance-oriented, self-referential — crashes eventually)
  - Steiner carved this himself: a wooden sculpture in Basel, Switzerland — Christ standing between Ahriman and Lucifer, containing both from interacting with each other
- **Horizontal/vertical tether metaphor**: Barry's addition
  - Horizontal = movement through time (willful forward motion)
  - Vertical = "there's something much greater than me going on here at all times"
  - "You become a living cross" when holding both simultaneously — Barry's interpretation of the Christian cross as a symbol for this dual responsibility
  - Connected to [[rene-girard|Rene Girard]]: horizontal tether = mimesis (imitative nature of human beings); vertical tether = the Divine/Holy Spirit within
- **Decline of the West reference**: Jesse mentions Oswald Spengler, René Guénon, and Julius Evola as "gestalt big history thinkers into esotericism" who all predicted Western decline within a few hundred years

## Jesse's Consciousness and Reality Model

- **Retrocausality**: Jesse's own working model of reality is "probable futures and probable pasts with the present as a handshake between them"
  - The present is a "collapsing function" of dimensions where time is not a factor
  - At the sub-atomic scale, time breaks down — entanglement may be explained by retrocausality
  - [[john-archibald-wheeler]] (mid-century physicist): in the delayed-choice double-slit experiment, detecting the electron mid-flight *retroactively* changes its behavior from the moment it was shot — retrocausality confirmed at quantum scale
  - Jesse: "I think the present is almost like the instantiation of the demiurge metaphor — how we perceive it is a product of our epistemology"
- **Vertical vs. horizontal tether as a model for human agency**:
  - "I'm moving horizontally through time — and in each present moment I stay tethered to something greater than myself"
  - Free will has "some relevance" as to how we take what comes from the past and move into the future; not complete control but not zero
  - Conscious awareness of this tension produces "ongoing coherence, ongoing purpose"
- **Body as pre-cognitive sensor**: Jesse and Phil both discuss somatic signals — feeling "strong" or "weak" in a situation before intellectual analysis catches up
  - "Your body articulates it before you even do intellectually"
  - Phil: "When I walk into a situation and suddenly feel something is off in the room — my body is sensing something I'm not even aware of"

## Miscellaneous Philosophical Content

- **Nietzsche and compassion-madness**: Phil tells the story of Nietzsche running out of his Vienna apartment to throw his arms around a horse being flogged by a coachman — never recovered from that moment
  - Connected to Tesla falling in love with a pigeon near Bryant Park in his final years
  - Phil: "There's something about the deep empathy you can feel toward a living being not of your own species that comes to encompass a kind of beauty in the world that's hard to bear"
- **The Nietzsche quote**: "A man is measured by how much truth he can accommodate without descending into madness"
- **The Death Drive (Freud)**: Phil describes Freud at 65 declaring his prior framework didn't work and renaming the core problem the "death instinct" — "the unstoppable irrational entropy in the human race; an instinct to repeat over and over something that erodes your identity because you can never win"
- **The ideal session**: Phil's goal is for every patient to leave with "marching orders — I'm going to do this this week, and I'll report back next week on how it went"
- **Edward Bernays / psychoanalysis as marketing**: Jesse mentions Bernays (Freud's nephew) bringing psychoanalytic ideas to America and creating modern consumerism; Bernays's influence on Nazi propaganda
  - Phil agrees: "The more generic and interoperable something is, the easier it is to scale and monetize — that's the whole ethos of the modern medical system"
  - The fault line: state/consumerist therapy vs. empowering the patient (which Phil considers "subversive to any power structure")
- **Phil's Law of Attraction critique**: The most common problem — it doesn't acknowledge evil; "no matter how many Jews in 1930s Germany created a vision board, they were going to get slaughtered if they stayed"
  - The hidden arrogance: assumes what you want "matters that much in the larger scheme of the universe"
