## Vallée at SRI and ARPANET

Vallée joined Stanford Research Institute in the early 1970s as part of the early internet research team — what was then ARPANET.

> "We had engine number three on the internet at SRI in California. By the time I joined them there were like 30 machines already."

He was there before [[hal-puthoff]] and [[russell-targ]]. SRI's primary funding came from Xerox, IBM, AT&T, and the Bank of America — corporate technology research. When the vice president of SRI came to Vallée's office in 1974, closed the door, and said Vallée's published UFO work might jeopardize SRI's contracts:

> VP: "Should we jeopardize the research we do for Xerox and IBM and AT&T and the Bank of America to do some research on psychic things? I said: well, what I could do is write up the reasons why in science you have to take chances."

Vallée wrote the VP a memo by the next day making the scientific case for taking research risks. The parapsychology program proceeded.

## Remote Viewing at SRI (Puthoff and Targ)

In approximately 1974, [[hal-puthoff]] and [[russell-targ]] came to SRI with a proposal to do parapsychology research — "which had never been done" at SRI. Their framing was explicitly physicalized: treat psychic phenomena as physical quantities, design tests "tied to physical quantities, not just psychology or parapsychology."

They brought in [[ingo-swann]] — an artist from New York who was "very uncomfortable in California" and "loved New York, loved the animation of the city." Swann was already extraordinarily gifted as a remote viewer; the SRI team provided scientific controls.

### The Coordinate Remote Viewing Origin Story

Swann's coordinate remote viewing system emerged from a conversation with Vallée about computer memory addressing. Vallée explained to Swann:

> "A computer deals with data three ways: direct memory — you know the address inside the machine; indirect addressing — you point to the address of the address somewhere else; and the rest of the world, which is too big to put inside the machine. The machine goes to get information from somewhere else — maybe the Library of Congress — and derives the address, brings it into memory, and works with it."

Swann's reaction: "That's it. That's what I need." He then designed the coordinate remote viewing system (longitude/latitude as the "indirect addressing" to the target location). This architectural insight from computer science became the foundation of the [[stargate-program]]'s operational protocol.

## Vallée's Personal Remote Viewing Experience

Vallée participated in structured RV sessions with Swann over approximately a year, though he had a job elsewhere and came in two days a week. His first major experience:

> "I get there at 8:30 in the morning. We close the door. He gives me a set of coordinates — longitude, latitude, somewhere. I get very cold right away and I get dizzy. I have to grab the table. I'm not drawing anything. Ingo says: 'Jack, what's wrong?' And I said: 'Ingo, I don't know where you're sending me but I'm cold, I'm trembling, I'm afraid of falling.' He said: 'You're on top of a peak in the Andes.'"

Swann confirmed the location: a mountain peak whose coordinates had been derived from an aerial photograph. Vallée had been physically transported to the location through sensory impression. He describes this as being "precipitated instantly to the place."

Vallée attributes the quality of his RV experiences partly to his trust in and admiration for Swann, and partly to structural factors of the experiment — openness to the process rather than skepticism.

## Ingo Swann Finds Russian Spacecraft in Africa

The SRI remote viewing program was used for military intelligence applications — primarily to "look at developments in the Soviet Union." But one use case was recovery of lost Soviet hardware:

> "They found lost spacecraft in the middle of a jungle in Africa. [Remote viewed] by Swann. No, it was Russian. So they found it through remote viewing."

Swann's description of the spacecraft's location was precise enough that the CIA/military was able to locate the crash site. This is presented by Vallée as a documented operational success of the remote viewing program, not a theoretical result.

## Patton and Battlefield Intuition as Remote Viewing

Vallée discusses the mental state required for military remote viewing success, drawing on General George S. Patton:

> "Patton was extraordinary. He demonstrated battlefield intuition again and again. There are interesting books from his lieutenants. To be a general in a World War — what is at stake, the consequences of everything you do — that's a state of mind very different from ordinary perception."

Vallée implies Patton was operating in a remote-viewing-adjacent cognitive state; the same faculty that produced Swann's coordinate successes was present in extreme military decision-makers operating under life-and-death conditions.

## Trinity Book Republished — Three Egg-Shaped Cases

Vallée and [[paola-harris]] co-authored *[[trinity-best-kept-secret]]*, documenting the [[1945-san-antonio-trinity-crash]] near White Sands. The first edition was criticized by a researcher who identified historical records that hadn't been consulted. The second edition:

> "We've republished the book. It covers three cases in all. In all three cases the object is egg-shaped, the size of a medium-sized truck."

The three cases are:
1. **Trinity / White Sands (1945)**: Witnesses [[remy-baca|Remy Baca]] and [[jose-padilla|Jose Padilla]] (ages 7 and 9); father and son went inside the craft; recovery observed for 10 days afterward; DOE/AEC track
2. **Socorro case / New Mexico (1964)**: See [[1964-socorro]]; police officer; FBI helped preserve traces; Vallée has the complete official file
3. **Valensole, France (1965)**: A lavender farmer at 5 AM; French government investigated with five agencies; beings communicated with witness in a way that "transcended his life" — giving a sense of "bigger meaning for life"

### Cross-case correlations Vallée identifies:
- All three: egg-shaped craft
- All three: beings 3–3.5 feet tall, humanoid
- All three: beings **breathe air** (no breathing equipment observed)
- All three: physical traces that could be analyzed
- All three: witness paralysis with maintained consciousness ("motor nerves inhibited but awareness intact")

Vallée's central challenge: "What kind of extraterrestrial comes here and breathes the air? We don't go to the Moon without breathing equipment. How do we know these things breathe at all?"

## White Sands Motor Pool Official Report (Never Published)

A key document Vallée found in the second Trinity investigation: a transcript of instructions from the head of the motor pool at White Sands to his team.

After the Socorro incident, the motor pool head was driving home across the desert with his family when his car stopped and his radio stopped working. A bright object was nearby. When it receded, the car restarted. The next day:

> "He gets his stuff together and says: 'You're going to take this car apart. I want to see every screw and every piece and every level — all on the floor. Test it and tell me what changed or if anything.' They couldn't find anything."

The official military report confirmed the car failure but found no mechanical cause. Vallée: "That report was never published. It nails the whole thing — this was not a balloon, not an hallucination." The motor pool head was not the primary witness but his documented mechanical investigation constitutes independent corroboration.

## Valensole (1965) — Communication Claim

The French farmer's Valensole encounter was investigated by five French government agencies:

> "He was growing plants to make perfume — expensive crop. He goes to the field at 5 AM because it's going to be very hot by afternoon. He encounters an egg-shaped craft with short beings."

Vallée visited the site with a French government diplomat (a woman with Ambassador rank who had a vacation home in the area). The witness told Vallée what he thought might happen again — he was sworn to secrecy about the specific content. But he said there had been communication with the beings:

> "There was communication with the beings when he was there. It transcended his life. There was something outside — a bigger meaning for life. Not about Divinity or God specifically — but the other side of life."

Seven creatures across the three cases are described as humanoid, breathing air, seemingly understanding the witnesses.

## UAP Materials Analysis with Garry Nolan

Vallée worked for years at Stanford on astrophysics with **[[peter-sturrock|Dr. Sturrock]]** — a solar physicist who died at over 100. Sturrock had published isotope analysis of Brazilian UFO material and donated all his collected UAP samples to Vallée when he retired. Stanford's UAP connection thus ran: Sturrock → Vallée → [[garry-nolan]].

Vallée gave Nolan all his samples — only the reliable ones from well-documented cases:

> "I gave him essentially all my samples — the ones I could relate to reliable cases, because I don't want to give him junk."

### The Red-Deposit Sample

One sample in particular: fits in the palm of a hand; multiple colors; interesting red/incrusted deposits on it. Vallée required all labs to sign a disclaimer not to scrape the deposits. Every lab that received it scraped the interesting deposits anyway.

### Isotope Analysis Results

> "We could redo the analysis — not the chemical analysis but the isotope analysis. Dr. Nolan and I took it to the lab. There was no special change in the isotope ratios. If there was [a change], that would indicate somebody had manipulated the isotopes — which is not a hoax."

The absence of isotope anomaly means the material is consistent with natural terrestrial origin but cannot be ruled out as manufactured. Nolan's Stanford instruments include machines he invented himself; results to be published as they go. Vallée presents this as ongoing scientific work, not proof of NHI origin.

### Nolan on UAP as Aerospace Forensics

Nolan's institutional framing for the Stanford work: studying "unusual materials with potential relevance to aerospace forensics." Vallée notes: "He doesn't talk about UFOs. People read between the lines."

## BAASS Pentagon Template and Limits of Official Programs

In the BASS/BAASS project ([[aawsap]]) of [[robert-bigelow]], funded by the [[dia|Defense Intelligence Agency]], the Pentagon gave investigators a template of what to look for:

> "Trajectory, composition, luminosity, radiation — those are the things the Pentagon wanted. Well, those are the same things you'd look at if you were analyzing a Russian aircraft. Is that really relevant scientifically?"

Vallée's critique: the official UAP investigation framework is built to identify adversarial hardware (Soviet/Chinese origin analysis), not to understand non-human intelligence. The Pentagon template is reverse-engineered from air defense analysis, not scientific inquiry into an unknown phenomenon.

His personal data:

> "My data is 10 times bigger than the stuff they talk about from the Pentagon. It's real data from people I can go see, people I can find out exactly what happened."

## Raytheon FLIR Memo to the Navy

After the [[2017-nyt-uap-story]] published the Nimitz FLIR footage, Raytheon sent the Navy an official memo:

> "Those things you've published — it was taken with one of the devices we sold you to put under the wing of your aircraft. It's not a camera. It's not a photographic camera. You gave us specifications for what you wanted us to build — you wanted something that could measure the temperature of the exhaust of an enemy aircraft that you're going to shoot down."

Vallée describes the memo as "official with a little touch of humor." The FLIR pod was designed to paint the heat signature of a Mig or Russian aircraft — not to photograph unidentified objects. The resulting "image" (not photograph) captures thermal data, not visual details. Rogan pulls up the FLIR1/Tic-Tac image on screen; Vallée and Rogan discuss it.

## Radar Spoofing Technology and the Tic-Tac

A Silicon Valley contact with a "distinguished Army career" told Vallée about a commercial-grade radar-spoofing device (30-year-old technology):

> "You can put it in the nose of your Piper Cub and fly around Los Angeles — it acquires the characteristics of all the radars in the area. Then you program it and can make your Piper Cub look like a B-52 20 miles away. You can redirect the air defense to another place."

On the Tic-Tac's apparent signal-blocking behavior (a pilot claim from the Nimitz incident):

> "I don't know how far this directional-radar-manipulation technology has gotten in the last 30 years. The guy I was talking to was telling me about technology from 30 years ago — and it blew my mind."

Vallée's point: when analyzing what pilots reported about the Tic-Tac's behavior, one must account for 30-year-old electronic countermeasure capabilities that the pilots themselves may not have been cleared for. Not a dismissal — an investigative caution.

## Grays as Artificial Life — Nolan Discussion

Vallée and [[garry-nolan]] discussed whether the Gray entities could be artificial life forms rather than biological extraterrestrials:

> "They had two eyes, a small nose, small mouth. They had no breathing equipment but were functioning normally — biologically. Couldn't they possibly be some sort of artificial life — some sort of creation — instead of a biological entity?"

Vallée notes that [[bob-lazar|Bob Lazar]]'s "[[s4-sport-model|sport model]]" has made the classic Gray with large eyes and flying saucer the iconic image in popular culture; this makes it hard to evaluate pre-Lazar descriptions that had more morphological variety (tall albinos, dwarfs, various types):

> "There's an embarrassment of riches in what people see. Tall albino almost-humans with large eyes, Grays, dwarfs. [[small-grays|Gray aliens]] are so iconic a flying saucer — that's a copy of the sport model from Bob Lazar's adventures."

## Soviet Dwarf/Humanoid Program (CIA Intel)

Vallée recounts CIA intelligence about Soviet efforts to create smaller human forms:

> "The CIA was looking into rumors that the Russians in the 50s, before Sputnik, were trying to create a humanoid dwarf that could pilot a spaceship — because they didn't expect to have the energy for a big rocket. It turned out you can't get tiny people to power their ships."

Separately: Russia was said to be trying to create a human-chimpanzee hybrid for warfare. Vallée presents these as documented CIA intelligence assessments, not speculation. Context: if Russians were trying to engineer small humanoid beings for practical space travel reasons, then "short humanoid creatures in a spacecraft" is not automatically evidence of non-human intelligence — it could be evidence of secret human programs.

## Federico Faggin — Integrated Circuit and Out-of-Body Experience

Federico Faggin is the Italian-American physicist who invented the integrated circuit (and the Intel 4004 microprocessor). Vallée knows him personally as a peer in Silicon Valley:

> "He's a physicist from Italy with a PhD from Italy. He's recognized as the father — or one of two or three people — who invented the integrated circuit. For a long time he was celebrated in that capacity. What he didn't say is that he had seen the design outside of his body one night — and that he had had multiple experiences outside of his body."

Faggin apparently had a spontaneous out-of-body experience in which the integrated circuit design appeared to him from an external source. He has since spoken publicly about consciousness research, writing books about the relationship between consciousness and the physical world. Vallée describes him as "one of my mentors in venture capital and high tech."

Vallée notes there is no manual for inducing such experiences:

> "There isn't much — there are a couple of books that mention steps you can take but it seems to be pretty much up to the individual."

## Vallée's Personal Contact Experiences (Previously Undisclosed)

Vallée describes experiences he "never wrote about — not that I wanted to hide them, but I wanted to understand the context before I did." He discloses them here (in a book in progress):

### The Redwood Forest Home

In the late 1970s, Vallée and his first wife Shanine bought property in Northern California in the Redwood Forest. They owned it for 18 years, used it for weekend astronomy. The last night of ownership, after everything was moved out:

> "My wife is still there because we had a car going back to the city. The last night — everything has been moved out, no curtains, nothing — and a light started filling the house. Blue, sort of UV-white-blue, ultraviolet."

The light was not a car on fire. It illuminated all details — grass, leaves on trees. Vallée: "Maybe they were there the whole time and just didn't want to show themselves. Or maybe they just wanted to say goodbye. Or maybe they were always there."

His wife Shanine had previously seen, in the observatory bedroom, two small lights moving along the walls. She woke Vallée but they were gone.

### Vallée's Direct Contact

Vallée refers separately to having been "precipitated instantly to the place where this being was" — a contact encounter he experienced directly but writes about carefully, wanting context first. He says: "There were a couple of things that I never wrote about, not that I wanted to hide them."

## Magnetoreception and Atrophied Psychic Abilities

New research suggests humans have a magnetic field detection ability similar to birds — atrophied from disuse:

> "Human beings may have the ability to detect the magnetic field the same way birds do when they fly south. Human beings have this ability but perhaps it's something that we have ignored so long it's atrophied — or maybe it's diffused not in the brain itself but in the organism."

Polynesian navigators in Tahiti: Vallée found books in Tahiti about these navigators — "special people, gifted" who could navigate vast ocean distances without instruments. He treats this as evidence that the faculty existed and has been culturally eroded by written language and media.

## Autistic Children Telepathy — Double-Blind Results

Rogan describes a podcast about nonverbal autistic children demonstrating psychic ability in controlled conditions:

> "Dozens of cases on video: people in other rooms look at objects, the child — completely locked off, can't see them at all — will say and write down what those objects are, colors, numbers, in sequence, very accurately."

Some teachers of these children have learned to receive and transmit information similarly, even though the teachers are neither nonverbal nor autistic. Vallée responds that certain Silicon Valley companies are now invested in this type of research. He frames it as evidence of a perceptual faculty that exists in humans but is suppressed, undeveloped, or accessible only to some.
