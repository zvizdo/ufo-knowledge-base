# NASA Apollo 17 Mission Transcript (1972)

Mission air-to-ground voice transcript excerpts from Apollo 17 (launched 1972-12-07; commander [[gene-cernan]], LMP [[jack-schmitt]], CMP Ron Evans). Three anomalous-observation sequences extracted from routine mission dialogue.

## Observation 1 — Rotating Distant Object (~MET 02:18:47)

CDR [[gene-cernan]] reported a bright object well outside the spacecraft debris field, at approximately 10–12 Earth diameters distance, positioned at 11 o'clock relative to Earth:

> "It is a bright object, and it's obviously rotating because it's flashing. It's way out in the distance... It's apparently rotating in a very rhythmic fashion because the flashes come around almost — almost on time."

> "I just want to emphasize that it's definitely not — not one of these particles that tends to look like a star out there. It's something physical in the distance."

Cernan also described two distinct flash types per rotation: one bright, one dull. Mission Control took the report seriously: they asked Cernan to give gimbal angle references and attempted to locate the object optically.

LMP [[jack-schmitt]] provided the most likely mundane identification: the S-IVB upper stage, which would display two flash-intensity cycles as it rotated (broad side = bright flash; engine bell = dull flash). By ~MET 02:20:56, Schmitt said he believed the two distant rotating objects were "SLA panels" or the S-IVB from a different angle. Mission Control never confirmed optical acquisition.

**Assessment**: Probable S-IVB stage, per crew. Cernan's initial report — before Schmitt's identification — is the clearest instance of a trained astronaut perceiving an unidentified rotating object as potentially non-spacecraft. The two-flash rhythmic pattern is consistent with a spinning spent rocket stage.

## Observation 2 — Cosmic Ray Phosphenes During Sleep (~MET 02:18:42)

CDR Cernan reported visual phenomena while trying to sleep:

> "I definitely saw some of these — I saw some of the same peripheral horizon-type things... and I also saw some sets of the streaks. And probably the one most imposing thing I remember is — the last one I remember before falling asleep — was the fact that there was a very bright spot that flashed right between my eyes like a very bright headlight — like a train coming at you, only with a flash."

> "No question in my mind but that they're there."

These are consistent with the Apollo Particle Percussion experiment (ALFMED): cosmic ray particles from deep space traverse the spacecraft hull and the vitreous humor of the eye, producing bioluminescent phosphene flashes in the retina. Cernan mentioned he had never seen them on his prior mission. Standard spaceflight phenomenon; UAP significance: low. However, the ALFMED data contributed to understanding of GCR (galactic cosmic ray) biological effects.

## Observation 3 — Lunar Surface Flash near Grimaldi (MET 03:15:38)

LMP [[jack-schmitt]] observed a brief flash on the lunar surface north of Grimaldi crater while in lunar orbit:

> "Hey, I just saw a flash on the lunar surface! It was just out there north of Grimaldi. Just north of Grimaldi. You might see if you got anything on your seismometers, although a small impact probably would give a fair amount of visible light... It was a bright little flash right out there near that crater... a thin streak of light."

Mission Control responded "Okay, we'll check" on the seismometers. The flash is consistent with either a small meteorite impact (producing visible light on impact) or a Transient Lunar Phenomenon (TLP). No seismometer confirmation is recorded in this transcript excerpt.
