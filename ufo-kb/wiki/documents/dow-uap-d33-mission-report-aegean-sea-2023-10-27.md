# AARO MISREP D33 — Aegean Sea UAP, 90-Degree Turns at 80 MPH, 2023-10-27

## Significance

D33 is the most kinematically anomalous MISREP in the gov-release batch. **Multiple 90-degree turns at 80 MPH** at low altitude (just above the ocean surface) cannot be performed by any known conventional aircraft or unmanned system without catastrophic structural failure. A standard-gravity turn at 80 MPH requires a turn radius of approximately 100–200 feet; an instantaneous 90-degree turn at that speed at sea level represents many tens of g's. The form field records this as "SHARP 90 DEGREE TURNS" under UAP Maneuverability Observations.

Two additional factors compound the significance. First, the UAP is described with the **identical phrase** as D35 two days later (27 October vs 29 October 2023) by the **same unit at the same base**: "SEEMINGLY CIRCULAR, TOO SMALL TO MAKE OUT DETAILS." The D33-D35 pair constitutes the most tightly clustered repeated UAP observation by a single unit in the batch — same words, same description, same area, same 33 SOS / 27 SOW detachment at Larissa AB, 48 hours apart. Second, both D33 and D35 were lost from the FMV feed during active observation (D33 at 0038Z, 3 minutes after contact; D35 at 0811Z), extending the sensor-evasion pattern to this same unit twice in 48 hours.

The observer assessment of BENIGN and "NO" for intelligent control contrast strikingly with the reported behavior. The 90-degree-turn capability in a solid, circular object at 80 MPH over sea level is directly relevant to quantitative UAP performance analysis.
