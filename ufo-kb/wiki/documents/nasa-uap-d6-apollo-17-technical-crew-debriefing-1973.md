# NASA Apollo 17 Technical Crew Debriefing — MSC-07631

MSC-07631; debriefing conducted January 4, 1973; originally CONFIDENTIAL with 90-day auto-declassification provision; reviewed by VS on 5/6/2026. Partial OCR recovery — only partial page 1 and title page text is extractable; bulk of document content was not recoverable.

## Extractable Observations

- **Evans (CMP)** — Re-entry fireball, rendezvous window: described seeing "a tunnel with a bright spot in the middle of the tunnel. Way down the tunnel, way back behind, I could see the fireball." Atmospheric optics re-entry description; not classified as UAP.
- **Cernan (CDR)** — Stated the only "unusual sighting" during landing/recovery was when CMP looked out the window and saw the recovery aircraft carrier superstructure, saying "Oh, we've got a tin can with us." Humor, not UAP.
- **Schmitt (LMP)** — Reported cosmic ray light flashes "just about continuously during the whole flight when we were dark adapted." One flash "thought was a flash on the lunar surface." During the ALFMED (Apollo Light Flash Moving Emulsion Detector) experiment with blindfolds on, all three crewmen reported "just no visible flashes"; after blindfolds removed, flashes resumed. This is standard cosmic ray phosphene documentation and confirms the ALFMED methodology worked as designed.

## Significance

Thin partial-OCR recovery. The Schmitt flash observations are the sole item of marginal UAP-adjacent interest: a flash perceived as occurring on the lunar surface could theoretically indicate an external light source, but the document treats it as a cosmic ray phosphene within the ALFMED experimental context. No UAP claims are made by any crewmember.
