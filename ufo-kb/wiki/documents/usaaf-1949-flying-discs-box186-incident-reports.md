# USAAF — Flying-Disc Incident Reports under Box 186 (1948–1950)

A declassified USAAF/USAF records collection (Box 186, Record Group 341.319.1) containing approximately 30 incident reports and administrative correspondence documenting sightings of "flying discs" forwarded to Air Materiel Command, [[wright-patterson]] AFB under Flight Service Regulation FSR 200-4 (2 November 1948) and the foundational reporting letter signed by [[cp-cabell]] (6 February 1948). Period covered: November 1948 through January 1950. Classification levels span SECRET, CONFIDENTIAL, and RESTRICTED across individual reports.

## Administrative Structure

All reports route to [[wright-patterson]] AMC Attention: MCIAXO-3 (later MCIAKO-3), with simultaneous copies to HQ USAF Director of Intelligence. The 6 February 1948 Cabell letter — signed by [[cp-cabell]] as Brigadier General, Chief, Air Intel. Req. Div. — is reproduced verbatim in Box 186, establishing direct routing bypassing ordinary channels. An October 26, 1948 HQ USAF TWX signed under Maj. Gen. Kuter notified all AACS units that "research reveals groups of sightings occur at periodic intervals with beginning of new interval imminent" — acknowledging a cyclical pattern and expanding reporting scope to include sightings outside the US and Alaska, with emphasis on photographic evidence.

## Incident Reports by Region

### Continental US — Aviation Personnel

- **Jackson, MS, 1 January 1949** — Tom Rush (ex-AAF pilot) and Mrs. Willette Rush (private pilot) in a Stinson civilian aircraft sighted a 60-ft cigar-shaped object with short stubby wings, dark blue/black, at 1,500 ft — helicopter-like sound, initially 200 mph accelerating to 500+ mph heading west then southwest. Maxwell FSC submitted EEI form; AP reported a second sighting 40 miles south later that evening, same pilot.
- **Chanute AFB, IL, 8 December 1948** — Sgt Doty and Sgt Montag (weather observers) observed a round white object moving from NE to SSW at ~15° upward angle, crossing 35° of sky in 2–3 seconds, leaving a trail. "Did not appear to be a falling star." SECRET. Signed sworn statements submitted.
- **Martinsburg, WV area, 11 December 1948** — C-45F at 9,500 ft on 125° heading; Capt. Legg (Air Rescue Service) observed a red-to-blue-to-red light 20 miles ahead, appeared stationary at 10,000–11,000 ft; faded like a flare burning out. Witnesses included Capt. Cassidy and S/Sgt Turner.
- **Fairfield-Suisun AFB, CA, 3 December 1948** — PFC McFarland and Sgt DeLafayette in control tower observed a brilliant round white light (runway-light intensity) at 500–1,000 ft, speed ~400 mph, slowing to ~200 mph abreast the tower, then climbing almost vertically to ~20,000 ft. [[project-sign]] EEI form filed. "Under absolute control at all times." No exhaust, no sound, no radar contact. Observer GCT score 143. CONFIDENTIAL.
- **Mountain Home/Nampa, ID, 24–25 July 1949** — Harry Clark (airport manager) observed 7 delta-wing objects with dark circular dome structure at 3,000–5,000 ft; executed 180° turns with no banking. SECRET.
- **Columbus, OH, 19–20 July 1949** — luminous flying-wing shape with humming (not rushing) sound; photographer with jet experience.
- **Boston, SW, 10 June 1949** — 1st Lt Kirshbaum in T-6: white tubular object ~100 ft long at 30,000 ft, stationary directly ahead. CONFIDENTIAL.
- **Sandberg Pass, CA, 28 February 1949** — USAF Lt Smith in T-11: "circled by jet-propelled blimp" at 500 mph, 9,000 ft. RESTRICTED.

### Aviation — Airline Captains

- **Portland/Troutdale/Mt. Hood, 30 July 1949** — Capt. Thrush (Northwest Airlines) observed 2 red + 1 white lights that outran his Martin 202 at 210 mph. SECRET.
- **Spokane, WA, 26 July 1949** — civilian commercial pilot observed 8 dish objects larger than a B-29 in formation. RESTRICTED.

### Multi-Witness Ground / ATC

- **Seattle, WA, 22 August 1949** — 3 National Guard AC&W controllers and 2 CAA ATCs tracked a 75–100 ft circular object at estimated 600 mph. RESTRICTED.
- **Medford, OR, 8–10 August 1949** — 7 AACS and CAA personnel observed 1–7 shiny objects in varying formations; theodolite used for tracking. CONFIDENTIAL.
- **Columbus, OH, 1 August 1949** — 3 CAA controllers observed a white object at 30,000+ ft for 2 hours. RESTRICTED.
- **Elko, NV, 2 May 1949** — Marvin Small (CAA radio operator) observed 3 flying discs ~30 ft at 14,000 ft, 300–400 mph. CONFIDENTIAL.
- **Griffiss AFB area, NY, 20 September 1949** — Lt Col Ruhl in C-45 at 43°40'N 74°55'W observed cylindrical object with exhaust trail 2x its length. CONFIDENTIAL.
- **Kansas City, KS, 6 January 1950** — two stationary spheres observed 10–15 minutes by Bendix Aviation employees (one a pilot).

### Alaska and Arctic

- **[[1949-kodiak-alaska-ufo|Kodiak, AK, 8 April 1949]]** — US Marshal Paul Herring, Lt. Cdr. Shepard (USN), and 2 civilians observed a greenish-blue object at 2,500 ft estimated 1,500 mph. ONI report on the case evaluated "probable." CONFIDENTIAL.
- **Fort Worth, TX, 13–14 April 1949** — Carswell FSC officers reported a silver ball at 1,000+ mph with exhaust 3x its size showing star-shaped figures. "Definitely not jet or meteor." CONFIDENTIAL.
- **Goose Bay, Newfoundland, 21 February 1949** — slow-running engine sound SW at high altitude, 15–20 min, no visual. Newfoundland Base Command commentary: "activity in Greenland last summer may have shifted to North American continent."
- **Newfoundland, 11 February 1949** — burning object at 1,000 ft with 200-yard flaming exhaust; faded then a second burst; civilian witnesses Wells and Burns. SECRET.
- **Indian House Lake, Canada, 18 January 1949** — bright lights, "travelled exceedingly fast"; deduced meteor by local personnel; Goose Bay relay to AACS.
- **Edmonton, Alberta, 17 November 1948** — 1st Lt. Toomey (C-47) and S/Sgt. Blink (radio operator) observed bright flaming orange egg-shaped object with tail at ~18,000 ft in shallow dive, top of overcast. Continental MATS Detachment, Edmonton.

### International

- **Fort Chimo/Crystal 1, Quebec, 18 March 1949** — SAC message explicitly routing through "PROJECT SIGN": red light at 10,000 ft, 200 mph W-to-E, 3 minutes, 4 reliable witnesses. SECRET.
- **[[1949-san-andres-islands-disc|San Andres Islands, Caribbean, 6–7 February 1949]]** — Capt. McBride (S.S. Antigua, United Fruit Line) tracked 3 disc objects by sextant; silver→yellow→red color shift. 6th Weather Squadron investigated two Russian 300-ton sloops (Omar and Blesk) in the area as possible source.
- **[[1949-hickam-afb-flying-disc|Hickam AFB, HI, 4 January 1949]]** — Capt. Stoney (Asst. S-2) observed round flat white oscillating disc for 28 minutes; "under absolute control at all times"; no propulsion visible; departed at 25° magnetic heading climbing out of sight. S-2 officer commented "extremely cool and level-headed." SECRET. Investigation ruled out updraft hypothesis.
- **Japan, 25 January 1949** — Multiple weather stations (Fukushima, Iida, Suwa, Nagano, Takayama, Gifu) simultaneously observed a large pale glow traveling NW to SE, illuminating ground objects; 3–5 minutes duration; thunder-like sound; forwarded through 2143rd Air Weather Wing / Far East Air Forces. Classified as possible meteor but multi-station simultaneity and 5-minute duration noted.
- **Godman AFB/Fort Knox, KY, 7 February 1949** — Theodolite tracking by Sgt. Ledbetter, PFC Dumphy, PFC Peterman, and Cpl. Rogers: bright rotating object (green/yellow/red) at azimuth 242.5°, elevation 3.6°, disappearing SW after bright flashes at 0310. Exactly 13 months after the [[1948-mantell-incident]] at the same location.

## Cross-Cutting Patterns

- **Cabell directive chain**: C.P. Cabell's Feb 6, 1948 letter — reproduced in Box 186 — is the governing authority for all reports; all route to AMC MCIAXO-3 (later MCIAKO-3). This is the same directive the [[fbi-62-hq-83894-ufo-investigations-1947-1968]] documents separately.
- **Theodolite trackings**: Godman/Fort Knox (Feb 1949) and Medford OR (Aug 1949) — independent military personnel using precision optical instruments, consistent with the Box 7 theodolite tracking pattern.
- **Periodic wave acknowledgment**: The Oct 26, 1948 HQ USAF TWX explicitly states "research reveals groups of sightings occur at periodic intervals with beginning of new interval imminent" — an official US government recognition of recurring UAP waves consistent with the [[1947-ufo-wave]] pattern.
- **Project Sign explicitly named**: Fort Chimo (Mar 1949) and Fairfield-Suisun (Dec 1948) EEI forms both name "PROJECT SIGN" in routing headers, with Fairfield-Suisun using the standardized Project Sign EEI form.
- **International scope**: Canada (7 reports across Fort Chimo, Goose Bay, Newfoundland, Edmonton, Indian House Lake), Pacific (Hickam, Japan), Caribbean, and Alaska — demonstrating that the phenomenon was not geographically restricted to US airspace during the [[project-sign]] / early [[project-grudge]] transition period.
- **Caliber of witnesses**: Includes an S-2 intelligence officer, a US Marshal, a USN lieutenant commander, airline pilots (Northwest Airlines), ex-AAF pilots, professional weather observers, CAA controllers, and military ATC personnel — systematically high-credibility per [[credibility-frameworks]].
- **Soviet vessel investigation**: Caribbean sighting (Feb 1949) prompted investigation of two Russian sloops in the area — direct [[cold-war-uap-nexus]] instance where UAP was explicitly correlated with possible Soviet activity.

## Headline Claims

1. Oct 26, 1948 HQ USAF TWX (Maj Gen Kuter): "research reveals groups of sightings occur at periodic intervals with beginning of new interval imminent" — official US military acknowledgment of a cyclical UAP wave pattern.
2. C.P. Cabell's Feb 6, 1948 directive — reproduced verbatim in Box 186 — established direct routing to AMC bypassing ordinary channels; the same Cabell later became CIA Deputy Director.
3. Kodiak, AK (April 1949): object at estimated 1,500 mph corroborated by US Marshal and USN officer; ONI evaluated the case as "probable."
4. Godman AFB/Fort Knox (Feb 7, 1949): theodolite tracking of a rotating green/yellow/red object — 13 months after Mantell's fatal pursuit at the same location, suggesting repeated activity at a nuclear-adjacent air base.
5. Caribbean (Feb 1949): Capt. McBride tracked 3 disc objects by sextant; US military explicitly investigated two Soviet sloops (Omar and Blesk) as possible source.
6. Fairfield-Suisun (Dec 1948): control tower observers filed Project Sign EEI form; intelligence officer evaluated object as "under absolute control at all times" with GCT-143 observer; weather service ruled out updraft hypothesis.
7. Mountain Home/Nampa ID (Jul 1949): 7 delta-wing objects with dome structure executing 180° turns with no banking — geometry inconsistent with any 1949-era experimental aircraft.
