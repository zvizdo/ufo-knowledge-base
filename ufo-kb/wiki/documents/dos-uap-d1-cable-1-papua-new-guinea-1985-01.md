# DoS Cable D1 — Papua New Guinea Inquiry Re Overflights (January 1985)

**MRN:** 85 PORT MORESBY 199. DTG: 280000Z Jan 85. From: AMEMBASSY PORT MORESBY. To: USCINCPAC HONOLULU HI IMMEDIATE; info to 43 SW ANDERSON AFB GU IMMEDIATE, SECSTATE WASHDC, AMEMBASSY CANBERRA, AMEMBASSY JAKARTA. Classification: LIMITED OFFICIAL USE. PURSUE Release 01. Released in full by John Powers (Acting-Director DoS) 12/25/2026.

## Incident

**Date:** Evening of January 24, 1985  
**Location:** Over Papua New Guinea; key radar contact near Angoram (~4°S, 144°E)

**PNG National Intelligence Organization (NIO) inquiry**: On January 28, 1985, the US Embassy Port Moresby received an informal inquiry from the PNG NIO about reported sightings of **high-altitude, high-speed aircraft** over PNG on the evening of January 24. Local residents in Wewak had been frightened by the overflights, prompting the Provincial Premier to call a public meeting attended by the **Prime Minister** (who was in his electoral district that weekend).

**Reports included**: Fast-moving objects with lights, contrails, and noise.

**Most credible report (per NIO)**: An **Air Niugini commercial pilot** who had just taken off from Wewak enroute Port Moresby — his **radar picked up aircraft flying south to north at high altitude and high speed** when he was over Angoram (approximately 4°S, 144°E).

**Visual sightings from multiple points around PNG**:
- 1 aircraft moving **north to south** at 1900 local
- **6–8 aircraft traveling south to north** at 2200 local (contrails visible from various points)

## US Government Response

Based on Embassy records and a telephone consultation with 43rd Strategic Wing at Anderson AFB (Guam), the Embassy told NIO: **"We knew of no B-52 overflights and no US aircraft in PNG airspace on January 24."**

The cable requests USCINCPAC confirm this statement and provide "any light you might throw on these reports." The Embassy acknowledged the information was "very sketchy" and sources were unsure of the directions.

## Significance

1. **Radar confirmation**: An Air Niugini commercial pilot radar-confirmed high-altitude/high-speed contacts — not merely visual; NIO placed credence in this specific report.
2. **Formation**: 6–8 aircraft in a south-to-north trajectory at 2200 local is consistent with an organized formation flight, not natural phenomena.
3. **USCINCPAC IMMEDIATE routing**: The Embassy sent the cable to USCINCPAC (US Pacific Command) and 43rd Strategic Wing at Andersen AFB Guam at IMMEDIATE precedence — indicating the sightings were considered potentially sensitive enough to warrant urgent military-command query.
4. **US denial on record**: The Embassy documented that the US formally denied having any aircraft in PNG airspace — the cable to USCINCPAC asks them to confirm this. Whether confirmed is not recorded in the released document.
5. **Political impact**: The sightings were significant enough to draw the PNG Prime Minister to a public meeting.
