# SHAEF Foo Fighters and German Armament Investigation

Declassified WWII document package: SHAEF G-2 message chain (January–March 1945) investigating the "foo fighter" phenomena reported by Allied combat aircrews over occupied Europe. Includes firsthand sortie reports from the [[415th-night-fighter-squadron|415th Night Fighter Squadron]], institutional responses from SHAEF, the UK Air Ministry, and USSTAF, and a comparison with German "Flak Bombe" / "Foo Fighter" documentation.

## 415th Night Fighter Squadron — Sortie Reports (December 1944 – January 1945)

The [[415th-night-fighter-squadron|415th NFS]] (XII TAC) compiled chronological sortie reports constituting the most detailed firsthand pilot documentation in the package. GCI radar consistently reported "negative" on bogey aircraft in the area for every incident.

- **14–15 Dec 1944** (Erstein vicinity): Large red light at 2,000 ft, travelling ~200 mph eastward. Not identifiable as any known German aircraft.
- **16–17 Dec 1944**: Five to six flashing red and green lights in a "T" formation; followed the aircraft after crew turned toward the lights.
- **22–23 Dec 1944** (Magenau vicinity): Two lights rose from the ground, leveled off, and **"stayed on the tail [of the aircraft] for 2 minutes"**; described as **"appearing to be under perfect control at all times"**; emitted orange glow.
- **23–24 Dec 1944**: Reddish flames at ~10,000 ft; one glowing red object "shot straight up, then did a wing over and went into a dive, disappearing."
- **26–27 Dec 1944**: Yellow streaks of flame and red balls of fire making **"distinct lines somewhat like arrows"**; one light came within 100 ft of the aircraft; **"peeled off"** during evasive action but then **continued to follow for 5 minutes** before "pulling up rapidly and going out of sight"; crew reported feeling propwash.
- **1–2 Jan 1945** (North of Strasbourg/Saverne): Crew reported seeing "Foofighters" — first use of the term in the sortie log.
- **14–15 Jan 1945** (Ingweiller): Large orange glow approximately 5 ft diameter at 6,000 ft.
- **29–30 Jan 1945**: Foofighter with amber lights 1,000 ft away, following the aircraft; disappeared when aircraft turned into it.

The term "Foofighters" is credited in the 30 January 1945 endorsement as "the name given these phenomenon by combat crews of this Squadron."

## Institutional Investigation Chain

- **16 January 1945 — XII TAC report**: Forwarded 415th NFS encounters to higher command: *"We have encountered a phenomenon which we cannot explain; crews have been followed by lights that blink on and off changing colors etc. The lights come very close and fly formation with our planes."*
- **11 February 1945 — SHAEF G-2** (Air Commodore Grierson to USSTAF and Air Ministry): *"It would seem that there must be something more than mere imagination behind the matter"* — formally requested joint investigation.
- **17 February 1945 — ADI(K) response** (Wing Commander S.D. Felkin): Proposed **"Flak Bombe"** (per ADI(K) Report 562/1944, paragraphs 128–129) as the probable explanation; also cited Me.262 as a possible explanation.
- **13 March 1945 — UK Air Ministry response** (Group Capt. E.D.M. Hopkins, Ref 111/45/DDI2): **"The whole affair is still something of a mystery and the evidence is very sketchy and varied so that no definite and satisfactory explanation can yet be given."**
- **18 March 1945 — SHAEF closure letter** (Grierson to First Tactical Air Force): Forwarded Air Ministry conclusion; incident chain closed with the mystery unresolved.

## Cylinder Object — 4 March 1945 (AI.320 Signal, IX TAC/Quesada)

A separate, distinct encounter was reported by IX TAC: pilots encountered an **aluminum-colored cylinder approximately 12 ft long and 1 ft in diameter** floating at 9,000 ft over occupied territory. Crew attacked the object — it **partially deflated**, produced **red flame without smoke**, but **did not disintegrate**. Photographs were attempted but unsuccessful. This morphology (cylinder, near-rigid, not destroyed on attack) is distinct from the luminous sphere/ball reports of the 415th NFS.

## Intelligence Assessments

- **ADI(K) "Flak Bombe"**: German flak-weapon explanation cited in ADI(K) Report 562/1944 — designed to harass or damage aircraft using proximity-detonation effects. Considered the official best-hypothesis at closure.
- **Me.262**: Proposed as a possible source of the faster-moving light phenomena given its novel jet exhaust signature; Grierson's letter mentions this explicitly.
- **Post-war outcome**: Post-war investigation of German records found no documented Flak Bombe program that matched the 415th NFS encounter profile, and German pilots were reporting identical phenomena — ruling out attribution to either side.

## Significance

This document package represents the **primary-source institutional record** of the Allied foo fighter investigation at the command level. Key features:
1. **Firsthand sortie reports with specificity**: Named dates, locations, flight altitudes, relative distances, response behaviors (lights following after evasive action, propwash felt).
2. **GCI radar null**: In every incident, pilots queried GCI radar control and received "negative" responses — no conventional aircraft tracked in the area.
3. **Official acknowledgment of mystery**: Air Ministry and SHAEF both formally stated the phenomenon was unexplained; the investigation closed without resolution.
4. **Cylinder object attack outcome**: Pilot-attacked object partially deflated but did not disintegrate — incompatible with any known German flak weapon or aircraft.
