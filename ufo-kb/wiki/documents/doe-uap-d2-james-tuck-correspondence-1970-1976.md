# DOE-UAP-D002 — James L. Tuck Correspondence

A three-document correspondence bundle from the personal/research files of [[james-l-tuck]] (Los Alamos physicist) regarding UFO/UAP phenomena, released by the [[doe|DOE]] under DoW PURSUE Release 02 (2026-05-22). Archive index "A-83-001-2-1." Personal names redacted under FOIA b(6); the file is otherwise unclassified. Coverage runs from 1970 (the two dated letters) through circa 1976 (third letter references McCampbell's *UFOLOGY* published 1976).

## Document Inventory

1. **11-23-70 — handwritten letter to "Mr. Tuck" from "BL" (b)(6)** — page 1–2 of the PDF. Light blue letterhead. Reports that during 1948–1951 several sightings of "green lights" were made at Los Alamos, usually 9:00–11:00 pm in the [[jemez-mountains|Jemez Mountains]]: "green lights weaving in and out of mountain peaks." All sightings reported to the **Protective Force Headquarters** and "should be a matter of record on their logs." Also reports one daytime instance of "five objects flying over Los Alamos in the afternoon… flying from southeast to northwest and appeared to be flying in formation"; a redacted (b)(6) Protective Force member was one of five-or-six witnesses. Identifies a second (b)(6) name to check with. Suggests Tuck obtain the Protective Force logs for time/date confirmation. Signed "Good Luck. (b)(6)".

2. **16 December 1970 — typed letter from James L. Tuck (signed "James L. Tuck", JLT:gaa)** — page 3 of the PDF. Header code "P-DO." Addressed to (b)(6) at U.S. Army Engineering School, Fort Belvoir VA 22060, Department of Mechanical and Technical Equipment. Tuck requests "the recipe that was used for the simulated atomic bomb demonstrations" referenced in their telephone conversation that day. Stated interest: "the large atmospheric vortices which are produced as reported in the book *Scientific Study of Unidentified Flying Objects* by Dr. Edward U. Condon" — i.e., [[condon-report|the Condon Report]] (1968).

3. **November 28 (no year, c.1976+) — letter to "Jim" from (b)(6)** — page 4 of the PDF. Says: "Mindful of your interesting report on ball lightning, I am enclosing comments on same by a UFO believer, James M. McCampbell, in his *UFOLOGY*, 1976, appearing on the new non-fiction shelf at the Mesa Library, call number 629.1338 M125u. His chapter FLIGHT AND PROPULSION strengthens my conviction that Einstein, while seemingly straying from the main current of physical research in his later years, was on scent like a bloodhound when he persisted in trying to lock in on a unified field theory."

## Headline Claims

- **Los Alamos 1948–1951 green-light corpus is primary-source-documented** in the DOE archive: a Los Alamos staff member (Protective Force-adjacent) confirms in writing in 1970 that "several sightings of green lights were made at Los Alamos" during 1948–1951, "usually during the early part of the night, nine to eleven, and were usually in the Jemez Mountains" — sightings "weaving in and out of mountain peaks." This is **direct documentary corroboration** of the [[green-fireballs]] / Project Twinkle corpus from inside the Los Alamos security organization itself, not a secondhand account.
- **Protective Force logs are alleged to contain the time/date records** of the 1948–1951 Los Alamos green-light sightings. The 1970 letter writer asserts that all sightings were reported to and logged by the Protective Force Headquarters; this would be a tractable archival target for any future declassification request specifically pursuing those logs.
- **Daytime formation sighting at Los Alamos (date unknown, c. 1948–1951)**: one previously-undocumented (to public KB) daytime UAP report — "five objects flying over Los Alamos in the afternoon… from southeast to northwest… appeared to be flying in formation" — witnessed by 5–6 people including at least one Protective Force member. Distinct phenomenology from the green nighttime lights; potentially adds a daytime cluster to the Los Alamos UAP record.
- **James L. Tuck was an active UAP-curious physicist**: a senior Los Alamos scientist (group leader for Project Sherwood fusion work) wrote in December 1970 to the US Army Engineering School at Fort Belvoir requesting the recipe for "simulated atomic bomb" demonstrations because he was investigating large atmospheric vortices reported in the Condon Report — an internal-research-driven attempt to reproduce / model the vortex phenomenology Condon catalogued. Tuck also produced "an interesting report on ball lightning" referenced in the 1976 letter. This places a Manhattan Project veteran inside the post-Condon-Report scientific UFO-research community.
- **The Tuck file was sequestered in DOE archives for >50 years**: archive index "A-83-001-2-1" implies a 1983 archival accession of Tuck materials (Tuck died 1980). The release in 2026 marks the first public surfacing of Tuck's UFO-related correspondence.

## What's Revealed

- **Witness identity for the green-fireball corpus** (page 1–2): a previously-anonymous Los Alamos Protective Force-adjacent witness in writing testifies to multiple 1948–1951 green-light sightings in the Jemez Mountains range west/northwest of Los Alamos townsite. This corroborates [[lincoln-lapaz|Lincoln LaPaz]]'s Project Twinkle investigation from a Los Alamos-site-internal viewpoint and confirms that the sightings were operationally reported up the security chain rather than dismissed.
- **Protective Force was the receiving agency** for green-light reports at Los Alamos (page 1–2). The Los Alamos "Protective Force" was the AEC/LANL security organization (predecessor to today's NNSA Office of Secure Transportation / LANL Security Operations). That a security force was receiving and logging UAP reports — rather than the scientific staff — is itself a notable institutional detail.
- **Tuck's vortex-research lane** (page 3): Tuck cites the [[condon-report]] specifically for its discussion of "large atmospheric vortices" and requests the Army Engineering School's "simulated atomic bomb" recipe to (presumably) reproduce vortex generation experimentally at scale. The "simulated atomic bomb" referenced is most likely the HE-only test bomb used at Camp A.P. Hill / Fort Belvoir for tactical demonstration; the recipe Tuck wanted was the explosive composition and geometry that produced the characteristic mushroom-cap vortex.
- **Tuck's ball-lightning lane** (page 4): the 1976 letter writer references Tuck's existing "interesting report on ball lightning" — confirming that Tuck (already historically known as a ball-lightning researcher per published scientific literature) was actively producing internal reports on the phenomenon while at Los Alamos. The writer also engages with James M. McCampbell's *UFOLOGY* (1976) chapter "Flight and Propulsion" and explicitly endorses Einstein's late-career unified-field-theory work as relevant to UAP propulsion — a researcher-level inside-DOE endorsement of the unified-field-theory-as-UAP-physics-foundation thesis.
- **Archive provenance**: index "A-83-001-2-1" implies the Tuck correspondence was accessioned into DOE archives in 1983, three years after Tuck's death (1980). The file label "P-DO" on the December 1970 outgoing letter is most likely Los Alamos's "P Division — Director's Office" file code (P Division = Physics).

## What's Redacted

- **Personal names** of all three correspondents (the 1970 Protective Force witness; the 1970 Fort Belvoir recipient; the 1976 letter writer) and the third-party "(b)(6)" Protective Force member identified as a daytime-sighting witness — all redacted under FOIA Exemption b(6) (clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy). Tuck's own name is fully visible as signatory of his own outgoing letter.
- **Signature blocks** on the 1976 letter — visible signature redacted under b(6).
- The (b)(6) redactions are appropriate; the witnesses are private individuals not voluntarily public. The redaction is the standard FOIA personal-privacy treatment and does not protect substantive content.

## Open Questions Raised

- **Where are the Protective Force logs?** The 11-23-70 letter directs Tuck to "obtain the Protective Force logs" for date/time records of the 1948–1951 sightings. Whether Tuck did so, whether the logs survived, and whether they remain in DOE/NNSA archives (or were destroyed under standard DOE records-retention schedules) is the most actionable archival lead in this document.
- **Did Tuck get the atomic-bomb-simulation recipe?** No follow-up correspondence from Fort Belvoir is in this release. Whether Tuck went on to reproduce the Condon-Report vortex phenomenology experimentally at Los Alamos, and whether any results were published or archived, is unresolved.
- **Why did Tuck care about both ball lightning AND UAP vortices?** Tuck is documented in the open scientific literature as a serious ball-lightning researcher (his published proposals included experimental reproductions of ball lightning via shaped-charge methods). The DOE-released correspondence shows him simultaneously interested in atomic-bomb-derived atmospheric vortices and the Condon Report's UAP catalog. This puts Tuck in a small set of senior physics-establishment figures actively cross-referencing nuclear-effects research and the UAP corpus in the early 1970s — a lineage worth tracing forward.
- **Who is "BL"?** The handwritten 11-23-70 letter is signed with what appears to be initials "BL" at the top (under the "P-DO" header isn't on this page; the "BL" appears next to the date stamp). Identifying the witness would be a high-value cross-reference for any future archival pull.
- **Why did the third letter writer reference Einstein's unified field theory?** The November 28 letter's endorsement of late-career Einstein as "on scent like a bloodhound" with unified-field-theory ties the McCampbell *UFOLOGY* chapter on propulsion to a serious physics-research program — implying the writer (and presumably Tuck) considered unified-field approaches to gravity-EM coupling a legitimate UAP-physics target as early as 1976. This pre-dates the Sol Foundation / TTSA-era reintroduction of unified-field-theory-as-UAP-propulsion by ~45 years.
