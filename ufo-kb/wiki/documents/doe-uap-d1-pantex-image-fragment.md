# DOE-UAP-D001 — Pantex Unidentified Object Incident Report (Image Fragment)

The released DoW PURSUE Release 02 PDF contains only **pages 5 and 6 of an original 6-page** "Pantex Unidentified Object Incident Report" produced by Consolidated Nuclear Security, LLC (the M&O contractor for the [[pantex-nuclear-facility|Pantex Plant]]). The narrative pages 1–4 of the original report are absent from the release; all extractable text is restricted to page headers, footers, and the redaction marker block. Marked UCNI ("Unclassified Controlled Nuclear Information"); a single (b)(3) (UCNI) FOIA-exemption redaction block covers the lower half of page 5 (alongside the source image from the Ground Surveillance Radar Tower).

## What's Actually in the Released Fragment

- **Page 5 of 6** — header: "Pantex Unidentified Object Incident Report" / "Image from Ground Surveillance Radar Tower." The image area on this page is largely blank with a small red circle in the upper right marking a dot-sized object presumably overhead; the lower image area is covered by a grey (b)(3) (UCNI) redaction block.
- **Page 6 of 6** — header: "Sandia National Labs Enhanced Images of the Object." Shows two enhanced renderings of an object: an upper image of a dark roughly-hexagonal mass with a thinner projecting element below it (suggestive of a top-heavy or "spinning top" shape, though heavily blurred), and a smaller lower image with similar geometry.
- All other text content (the narrative incident description, witness accounts, sensor logs, timestamps, location coordinates) is absent because pages 1–4 were not released.

## Headline Claims

- A formal "Pantex Unidentified Object Incident Report" exists in the [[doe]] / [[pantex-nuclear-facility|Pantex Plant]] document trail — generated by the M&O contractor [[consolidated-nuclear-security-llc]] using Ground Surveillance Radar Tower imagery, with image enhancement performed by [[sandia-national-laboratories]]. The institutional pipeline (Pantex → CNS LLC → Sandia → DOE → AARO/DoW) is itself a significant disclosure.
- The unidentified object was captured on **ground surveillance radar tower imagery** at the most sensitive US nuclear weapons assembly/disassembly facility, then routed to Sandia for image enhancement — implying that the object was both real enough to warrant analysis and atypical enough that the radar tower's native image was insufficient for identification.
- The released fragment is **only the image-evidence appendix** (pages 5–6 of 6); the narrative report (pages 1–4) was withheld, presumably because it contains location/timestamp/sensor specifics protected under the AEA UCNI framework or because it would identify the specific radar tower / facility coordinates.
- Sandia's enhancements show an object morphology distinct from typical aircraft (the dark hexagonal/octagonal mass with thinner projecting element does not match standard drone, balloon, or aircraft silhouettes), though the enhancement artifacts and absence of scale references prevent firm shape identification.

## What's Revealed

- **Document existence**: A formally-numbered "Pantex Unidentified Object Incident Report" exists in CNS LLC's UCNI-controlled record system. Independent of the [[george-knapp]]-reported "jellyfish UAP" Pantex incursion (currently sourced only to podcast testimony per [[pantex-nuclear-facility]]), DOE has now released evidence that a parallel (or possibly identical) UAP incursion at Pantex was formally documented and image-evidence-preserved.
- **Sandia analytical role**: [[sandia-national-laboratories]] performed the image enhancement; consistent with Sandia's role as the DOE-affiliated national lab with photo-analysis capability and as the Pantex Plant's design-agency partner.
- **UCNI classification**: The document is marked UCNI under DOE 10 CFR 1017 ("Identification and Protection of Unclassified Controlled Nuclear Information") rather than at SECRET or higher; this means the underlying content is treated as nuclear-facility-sensitive but not formally classified national security information. The b(3) exemption invocation in the redaction block ties to the statutory UCNI framework (the FOIA b(3) exemption requires a separate statute mandating non-disclosure; UCNI cites 42 USC 2168).

## What's Redacted

- **Pages 1–4 of the original 6-page report**: entirely absent from the release. These would normally contain: cover page, date/time/location, incident summary, witness accounts, sensor readings, response actions, analyst conclusions.
- **Page 5 lower half**: grey redaction block marked "(b)(3) (UCNI)" — covering presumably either the un-enhanced ground surveillance radar image's surrounding context (terrain, facility structures, security elements) or supplementary annotation.
- **All dates and identifiers**: no incident date, report date, report number, or witness identifiers visible.

## Open Questions Raised

- **Is this the [[george-knapp]] "jellyfish UAP" incident or a separate Pantex incursion?** The Knapp account (per [[pantex-nuclear-facility]] page) describes a jellyfish-shaped object passing between warhead holding facilities 3 and 4 with IR invisibility, thermal visibility, and weapons jamming — a sophisticated active-countermeasure profile. The DOE-released image shows an object that is not obviously jellyfish-shaped (the dark hexagonal mass + projecting element below is more conical/top-heavy), but image enhancement artifacts and absence of orientation make morphology comparison unreliable. **Most likely interpretation given current evidence: probably a different Pantex UAP incident**, because (1) Knapp's source described the object as IR-invisible — incompatible with capture by a Ground Surveillance Radar Tower that would presumably use both radar return AND thermal/IR sensing, while the DOE document does NOT note any sensor-mode anomaly; and (2) the Knapp account does not mention an enhanced-image package or Sandia involvement, both prominent in the DOE release. **Flagged as open question** — the released fragment lacks the narrative content needed to resolve.
- **What date?** Pantex (then operated by Mason & Hanger) transferred to Consolidated Nuclear Security, LLC management in **July 2014**; CNS retained management through at least 2020 with extension awarded. The CNS letterhead means the incident occurred during the CNS management period — **2014 or later**. Beyond this, no date can be inferred from the released fragment.
- **Why release only the image plates?** Standard FOIA/declassification practice is to redact within a complete document, not to release only image appendices while withholding the underlying narrative. The release pattern suggests an explicit decision to release "evidence of the incident's existence" while protecting narrative content under UCNI.
- **What was the radar return like?** The "Image from Ground Surveillance Radar Tower" page heading is unusual — most security cameras are EO/IR not radar imagery; this implies the source image is from a synthetic-aperture or multi-mode ground radar that produces images. Pantex's perimeter sensor suite is itself UCNI-protected; the radar tower may be part of the [[uap-camera-evasion-problem|standard nuclear-site multi-sensor perimeter]].
