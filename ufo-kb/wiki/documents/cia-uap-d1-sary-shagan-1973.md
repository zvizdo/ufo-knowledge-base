# CIA-UAP-D001 — Intelligence Information Report FIRK-311/01638-77

CIA Directorate of Operations Intelligence Information Report on the [[sary-shagan-test-range]] (Soviet ABM and air-defense weapons testing range, Kazakhstan SSR), based on HUMINT from a former Soviet citizen acquired in Germany. Date of Information: November 1972–November 1973; Date Distributed: 20 December 1977. The report is a routine military-intelligence summary of range layout, unit designators (V/Ch 03080 regional HQ; V/Ch 03142 warhead checkout unit at Site 4) and weapons-system specifics for the [[sa-2-system-75|System-75 (SA-2)]] surface-to-air missile and the [[abm-1-galosh-system-300|System-300/Aldan (ABM-1 GALOSH)]] anti-ballistic missile. Two paragraphs at the end break the otherwise-conventional pattern: rumored Soviet laser-weapon research with "powerful antennas," and a firsthand UAP observation by the source at Site 7 in late summer 1973 — [[1973-sary-shagan-green-uap]]. Classification: CONFIDENTIAL // WARNING NOTICE — SENSITIVE INTELLIGENCE SOURCES AND METHODS INVOLVED. Released through Department of War PURSUE Release 02 with "Approved for Release 2026" stamp.

## Headline Claims

- A former Soviet citizen working at [[sary-shagan-test-range|Sary Shagan]] personally witnessed a "sharp (bright) green circular object or mass in the sky" west of Site 7 at ~70° elevation on one evening in late summer 1973; within 10–15 seconds it widened into "several green concentric circles" before color faded over minutes. Silent. No conventional explanation offered by the source or by the CIA field officer.
- The same defector reports that "according to hearsay, experiments involving laser weapons were being conducted at an unknown location at the range" using "powerful antennas." This is one of the earliest US-IC paper trails on the Sary Shagan laser program later confirmed as the [[terra-3]] DEW research site (5K17 / Omega complex). The document treats it as rumor, not first-hand observation.
- Warhead specifics: System-300/Aldan (ABM-1 GALOSH) warheads were ~2 m long, 80–100 cm dia, ~400 kg, contained black cassettes 45×12 cm housing metal balls (shariki) — a fragmentation-type warhead. System-75 (SA-2) warheads carried ~100 grey cassettes (30×7 cm) of 200–300 metal balls each (≤1.5 cm dia). Source had no knowledge of nuclear loadings but opined "all systems could be equipped with nuclear warheads."
- Unit designators: V/Ch 03080 (regional headquarters), V/Ch 03142 (Site 4 warhead checkout unit); System-300/Aldan missiles processed at Site 4 then transferred to Site 35 for launch. These are intel artifacts useful for cross-referencing with subsequent Sary Shagan reporting.

## What's Revealed

- **[[sary-shagan-test-range|Sary Shagan]] range structure (paras 1–9, redacted)**: Body text describes facilities, work areas, security fencing, regional HQ (V/Ch 03080), warhead checkout unit at Site 4 (V/Ch 03142). Most narrative detail is redacted in the released version; only the framing summary on page 1 and paragraphs 10–14 survive.
- **System-300/Aldan = ABM-1 GALOSH (para 10)**: Field comment explicitly identifies the Soviet-named system as the NATO-designated ABM-1 GALOSH; source did not know whether Aldan was an alternative name for System-300 or a related missile. "According to rumor, the missile was for antimissile defense (protivo raketnaya oborona — PRO)."
- **Warhead specifications (para 11)**: see Headline Claims.
- **Laser-weapons rumor (para 13)**: "According to hearsay, experiments involving laser weapons were being conducted at an unknown location at the range. Supposedly, the tests involved powerful antennas (no further details)." Source did not observe the laser site directly.
- **UAP observation (paras 14, page 3)**: Source was watching a Canada–USSR sport competition on television at Site 7 in late summer 1973 (consistent with the September 1972 Summit Series rebroadcasts, or a 1973 hockey/figure-skating contest; the exact event is not specified). Stepped outside; observed an unidentified "sharp (bright) green circular object or mass in the sky," west of site, ~70° elevation, altitude undetermined but "would have been higher than cloud level" per source's belief. "Within 10 to 15 seconds of observation, the green circle widened and within a brief period of time several green concentric circles formed around the mass. Within minutes the coloring disappeared. There was no sound, such as an explosion, associated with the phenomenon."
- **Field officer assessment**: "Source had no opinion as to what this phenomenon was. There was no resultant rumors. Source could not provide any further details." The CIA case officer offered no own assessment.

## What's Redacted

- Source's name, agency identifier, and field-number block all blanked.
- Paragraphs 1–9 covering range facilities, work areas, security fencing detail are present but the released image shows mostly blank space in the body of the report — heavy line-by-line redaction.
- Page 2 of the released bundle is mostly blank (redacted body content between paragraphs 4 and 10).
- Page 4 of the original is absent from the released PDF (only pages 1, 2, 3, 5 are present in the war.gov release — page 4 either redacted in full or not included). The released PDF jumps from para "Site 7" cover to para 10 missile-warhead specifics, skipping any para 5–9 content.
- "Field No." block redacted. No `b(N)` FOIA exemption codes are stamped — the document predates the 1996 EO 12958 review marks; classification is by reporting officer per E.O. 11652.

## Notable Quotes

- "According to hearsay, experiments involving laser weapons were being conducted at an unknown location at the range. Supposedly, the tests involved powerful antennas (no further details)." (para 13)
- "On one evening in late Summer 1973, Source observed an unidentified phenomenon at Site 7. While watching a sport competition between Canada and the USSR on television, he stepped outside for some air and observed an unidentified sharp (bright) green circular object or mass in the sky." (para 14)

## Open Questions Raised

- **Was this an early Terra-3 laser test?** The CIA report has the laser-weapons rumor and the green UAP observation on the same page. Sary Shagan's later-documented [[terra-3]] complex used powerful lasers (the 5K17 / Omega systems) for ABM research; a green concentric-ring optical phenomenon over the range is consistent with a high-altitude laser-air-ionization test, but no laser system at Sary Shagan in 1973 is publicly documented as having the visible-light output described. The CIA officer makes no such connection in the released text.
- **What is "Site 7"?** The numbered-site designators at Sary Shagan are used inconsistently across declassified reporting. "Site 7" here is the source's observation post; whether it corresponds to the later-known Test Site 35 (launch site for ABM-1) or a separate housing/admin compound is not stated. V/Ch 03080 is the regional HQ; V/Ch 03142 is at Site 4.
- **Why released in 2026?** The "Approved for Release 2026" stamp and inclusion in PURSUE Release 02 imply the document was held back through normal CIA declassification review cycles (25-year review under E.O. 13526 would have triggered in 2002; 50-year automatic in 2027). Release as part of PURSUE rather than via routine declassification suggests AARO/DoW prioritization for UAP content over the otherwise-conventional missile-range reporting.
- **Why so heavily redacted in 2026?** The released document removes facility detail that is now public knowledge (Sary Shagan layout has been extensively analyzed in open-source publications since the 1990s). Redactions may follow standard practice rather than current-information-protection rationale.
