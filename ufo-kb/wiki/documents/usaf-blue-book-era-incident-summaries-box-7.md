# USAF Blue Book Era Incident Summaries — Box 7

Three-volume collection of 233 standardized "Check-List — Unidentified Flying Objects" incident report forms spanning July 1947 through January 1949, produced under [[project-sign]] (USAF's first formal UFO investigation program, operational 1947–1949). Released in PURSUE Release 01. Reports are classified RESTRICTED, CONFIDENTIAL, or SECRET at time of creation. Each form captures: date/time, location, observer qualifications, object description (shape, color, size, altitude, speed, maneuvers), evaluation (A-1 through D-5 reliability rating), and investigator notes. Many include supplementary narratives, sketches, and correspondence.

This document constitutes the primary evidentiary corpus of [[project-sign]] — the actual incident forms that drove the contested "[[estimate-of-the-situation]]" debate within USAF in 1948.

## Volume 1: Incidents 1–100 (July 1947 – Early 1948)

### Muroc AFB Cluster (Incidents 1–4), July 7–8, 1947

Four sightings at [[edwards-air-force-base]] (then Muroc Army Air Field, Rogers Dry Lake) by active military personnel within 24 hours of each other:

- Capt. Strapp, Rogers Dry Lake: flat disc-shaped object at estimated 300–400 MPH, 8,000 ft — evaluation B-2
- Major Wise (test pilot): silver disc at high altitude in steady level flight with no propulsion system visible
- 1st Lt McHenry: disc-shaped object with no visible propulsion
- All rated military observers; cluster represents the earliest multi-witness military cluster in Project Sign records and one of the most institutionally significant given the installation's test-flight mission

### Portland, Oregon (Incidents 5–8a), July 4, 1947

Police officers and civilians observe 3–6 disc-shaped objects over downtown Portland during daylight Fourth of July:
- Sgt Cross, patrolman Lissy, patrolman Ellis, officers McDowell and Patterson — rated law enforcement witnesses
- Consistent shape descriptions; objects in loose formation

### Godman Field / Fort Knox — Mantell Death (Incidents 33–33g), January 7, 1948

Seven sub-cases documenting the death of National Guard pilot [[thomas-mantell|Thomas F. Mantell]] while chasing a UAP — the first aviation fatality attributed to UAP pursuit in US military records:

- P-51 pilot Mantell (NG flight leader) crashed after climbing to pursue "metallic object of tremendous size" — stationary white object visible simultaneously from multiple locations across Kentucky
- Control tower witnesses at Godman Field: Col. Hix (base CO), Lt. Orner, T/Sgt Blackwell, Capt. Carter, Capt. Duesler
- Object tracked from Elizabethtown to Owensboro; multiple civilian ground reports corroborate across wide geographic range
- Mantell's last radio transmission: described object as "metallic and of tremendous size"
- Aircraft debris field analyzed; no recovery of object
- Evaluation contested between Venus hypothesis (official eventual position) and unknown; the Venus explanation is geometrically implausible for the reported daylight visibility

### Incident 83 — Film Evidence, July 9, 1947

David I. Johnson (AT-6 pilot), Boise, Idaho: observed round black object execute a barrel roll; **8mm film footage taken** — one of the earliest claimed film records in Project Sign; civilian aviation-rated observer.

### Lockbourne AAB Cluster (Incidents 30–32), January 7, 1948

Concurrent with Mantell pursuit at separate installation: red-to-amber luminous object with exhaust trail at Lockbourne Army Air Base, Ohio, observed for 35 continuous minutes by multiple military witnesses on installation — independent data point ruling out the Godman object as singular.

### Norcatur / Spacecraft Hypothesis Letter, February 1948

Sky explosion near Norcatur, Kansas; civilian observer Markham wrote directly to Army Chief of Staff proposing the object was "a spacecraft from the moon." Dr. [[lincoln-lapaz]] provided skeptical response. Documents the direct public-to-command reporting channel and LaPaz's role as scientific gatekeeper even before the green fireball wave.

## Volume 2: Incidents 101–172 (Spring–Fall 1948)

### Memphis Multi-Object (Incident 117), May 7, 1948

50–60 shiny high-altitude objects over Memphis; [[j-allen-hynek]] and [[lincoln-lapaz]] both consulted — documents the earliest collaboration of the two figures who would define the scientific response to UAP in the Blue Book era.

### Berlin Sighting — German Missile Expert (Incident 118), March 28, 1948

Dr. Ormans, identified as a former **Rheinmetall-Borsig guided missile expert**, among the observers of an unidentified aerial object over Berlin; his expertise made his report of particular intelligence interest given active USAF investigation into whether German/Soviet experimental craft were responsible for US sightings.

### Norway/Denmark/Sweden Fireball Wave — Peenemünde Direction (Incident 133), February 20–26, 1948

Multiple fireball reports originating from the direction of **Peenemünde** (former German V-weapon research facility); investigator notes association with Rjukan heavy water production facility in Norway. Documents USAF's active tracking of the Peenemünde nexus as a potential Soviet-exploitation vector — a key pillar of the [[cold-war-uap-nexus]] theory competing with the extraterrestrial hypothesis within Project Sign.

### Selfridge Field Formation (Incident 134), May 28, 1948

Lt Kokolonis (Selfridge Field): 5 silvery-gold disc-shaped objects in stepped stern formation, estimated 500+ MPH, 300–400 ft in size; corroborated by M/Sgt Ernest Davis on ground. Military rated observer + ground corroboration.

### Chiles-Whitted Encounter (Incident 144), July 24, 1948

The most consequential case in Project Sign for the extraterrestrial hypothesis:

- Eastern Airlines DC-3 crew: Capt. [[clarence-chiles|C.S. Chiles]] and First Officer John Whitted, 20 miles SW of Montgomery, Alabama, 0230 local
- Cigar-shaped object approximately 100 ft long with **two rows of windows** (upper and lower deck), blue fluorescent glow beneath fuselage, red-orange flame exhaust trail at the rear
- Observed at 5,000 ft; object pulled up sharply into cloud upon closest approach
- Passenger Clarence McKelvie (also aboard) corroborates from different position in cabin
- Multiple independent corroborating sightings same night at 0230
- This case directly informed the Project Sign "Estimate of the Situation" (1948 classified report concluding interplanetary origin), subsequently rejected by Gen. Hoyt Vandenberg and ordered destroyed

### Fielding Lake, Alaska (Incident 145), July 9, 1948

72nd Reconnaissance Squadron pilots 1st Lt Caramia and Carpenter: approximately 20 grayish-black spheroid/disc objects in shotgun-blast dispersion pattern at 500+ MPH, immediately below cloud layer. Distinctive **buzzing sound** described as "like a group of jet aircraft." Rated military pilots in reconnaissance squadron.

### San Acacia Dam — Nuclear Corridor (Incident 146), July 17, 1948

M/Sgt Carter (armament technician, "A" reliability rating) and M/Sgt Harvey, 65 miles south of Albuquerque near nuclear installations:

- 7 circular metallic objects in V, L, and Q formation variations with transitions between formations
- Pulsating light source; estimated 1,500 MPH if reported altitude of 20,000 ft was accurate
- Location in the Albuquerque–Kirtland–Sandia nuclear corridor replicates the [[ufo-nuclear-nexus]] pattern documented in the FBI case file; "A"-rated observer provides maximum credibility weight for the nuclear-proximity correlation

### Propeller-Shaped Object with Controlled Descent (Incident 151), July 29, 1948

James W. Toney and Robert Huggins, Indianapolis: **propeller-shaped object** 6–8 ft in length at 30 ft altitude, moving at 25–30 MPH, aluminum-colored, no sound. Object had **cup-shaped protrusions on upper side**. Most significantly: object **descended in a controlled manner into a wooded area**; ground search found no physical trace. First documented controlled-descent case in the corpus — not a ballistic trajectory.

### Hamel, Minnesota — Ground Trace Evidence (Incident 162), August 11, 1948

Jerome Leuer (age 10) and Benne Leuer (age 8), Hamel, Minnesota: a **1-ft metallic disc landed in their back yard**, spun, produced a steam-whistle sound, then maneuvered around tree branches and telephone wires before shooting northeast. **Ground impression found** — dented earth and crushed rocks beneath where the object hovered and rested. Washtub placed over spot to preserve evidence; [[fbi]] and local postmaster notified. Represents the earliest confirmed [[landing-trace-evidence]] case in the Project Sign record. Dismissed due to observer age, but physical trace evidence is independent of witness credibility.

### Gorman Dogfight (Incident 172), October 1, 1948

[[george-gorman|George F. Gorman]] (North Dakota Air National Guard F-51 pilot; civilian occupation: construction manager), Fargo, North Dakota:

- 27-minute aerial engagement with a small luminous orb over Hector Airport
- Object consistently faster than F-51 Mustang at full throttle; executed tighter turns than the fighter could achieve even at corner velocity
- Made repeated evasive passes; demonstrated apparent awareness of Gorman's pursuit vector by reversing course each time he closed
- Witnessed simultaneously by Hector Airport tower controllers and ground observers
- Investigation officially rated "possible weather balloon" but maneuvers described are irreconcilable with balloon behavior; Gorman's dual-occupation background (rated pilot + civilian professional) elevated credibility assessment

## Volume 3: Incidents 173–233 (September 1948 – January 1949)

### Los Alamos Proximity — Stationary Disc (Incident 175), September 23, 1948

A. Ruble Angier (licensed pilot since 1928, neon sign manufacturer), Santa Fe: 100–150 ft circular flat white disc stationary at 25,000–30,000 ft, observed near **Los Alamos** for 15–20 continuous minutes; no exhaust, no sound, no rotation. Companion Fairchild gave conflicting description. The 15-20 minute stationary hold at altitude directly above the Los Alamos weapons facility is the critical data point; consistent with the UAP-surveillance-of-nuclear-sites pattern LaPaz was independently documenting.

### Honolulu Multi-Witness (Incident 178), October 18, 1948

Maj Robert C. Drum (rated USAF pilot), Honolulu: silver disc observed in 4 separate events over 10 minutes; corroborated independently by wife and 8-year-old daughter.

### Fukuoka F-61 — Active Radar Evasion (Incident 183), October 15, 1948

F-61 Black Widow interceptor crew (pilot plus radar observer) operating out of Fukuoka, Japan:

- Tracked UAP on airborne radar through **6 separate close-approach attempts** over 1+ hours
- Each time F-61 closed to approximately 12,000 ft, object **suddenly accelerated and dove** out of radar range — consistent pattern across all 6 attempts
- Investigator's written conclusion: "The object may have been aware of F-61's location — possibly carrying radar detection equipment"
- Object demonstrated **near-vertical ascent** capability
- Speed "much greater than F-61 at full throttle"
- First case in the corpus where an investigator explicitly hypothesizes UAP active radar-detection capability; 6 consistent repetitions rules out chance coincidence

### Goose Bay / Newfoundland Radar Series (Incidents 188, 195, 196), October–November 1948

Multiple Ground Controlled Approach (GCA) radar contacts at Goose Bay, Labrador and Fort McAndrew, Newfoundland:
- Objects making landing-approach maneuvers tracked on GCA scope — suggesting navigational awareness of approach paths
- One object characterized as "too small for aircraft, too large for bird" at 60 MPH
- Radar-only contacts with no visual correlation — earliest multi-station radar-only UAP cases in the corpus

### Andrews AFB Chase (Incidents 207, 207A, 207B), November 18, 1948

Lt Kenwood Jackson and Lt Henry G. Combs, Andrews AFB:

- 15–18 minute pursuit of oval wingless object
- Object executed active **evasive action** including tight circles that Combs could not match even with flaps fully deployed
- Speed varied between 80 and 600 MPH within the same engagement — controlled speed modulation, not ballistic
- **Vertical climb** capability demonstrated during evasion
- Combs closed to 300–400 ft and turned landing light onto object: "very dull gray glow, oblong shape, no wings"
- Ground corroboration: Sgt Kushner observing from base perimeter confirmed sighting
- Object eventually departed NE at 500–600 MPH

### Project Sign Named — Primary Source (Incident 213), December 3, 1948

Col. Clingerman's letter within the file explicitly names "Project Sign" while requesting information about ram jet test flights — primary-source confirmation of the investigation's operational designation at this date.

### New Mexico Green Fireball Wave (Incidents 223–231), December 5–13, 1948

Nine-incident cluster documenting the first [[green-fireballs]] wave over New Mexico — the primary Project Sign documentation of the phenomenon that would drive [[project-twinkle]]:

- Incidents 223/223a: Capt. Goade (pilot) witnesses two green fireballs in succession
- Incident 224: [[fbi]] Special Agents Stahl and Neef (both rated pilots — exceptional dual qualification) personally witness a green fireball at 13,500 ft — independent law enforcement and aviation credentialing on a single sighting
- **Incident 227**: [[lincoln-lapaz]] personally witnesses the December 12 fireball near Bernal, New Mexico, with multiple additional witnesses present; produces **9-point scientific analysis** concluding these are NOT meteoric:
  1. Horizontal flight paths — meteors follow curved atmospheric-entry arcs; these do not
  2. Anomalously low altitude: 8–10 miles vs. typical 40+ miles for ordinary meteors
  3. Velocity 3–12 miles/sec — slower than meteors, faster than V-2 rockets or jet aircraft
  4. No detonation sounds despite low altitude (expected for metallic objects at near-sonic speeds at that altitude)
  5. Appear at full brightness instantly — unlike gradual brightening of meteors entering atmosphere
  6. Directional preference from north — inconsistent with random meteoric distribution
  7. Curious temporal association with meteor shower peaks — possible deliberate camouflage with natural phenomena
  8. Vivid green color = copper salt spectral emission at λ≈5218 Å — anomalous for natural meteors
  9. 2–3 second duration — longer than ordinary meteors, shorter than genuine meteorite falls

  LaPaz's explicit note: the December 12 path "passes almost centrally across the Los Alamos reservation."

## Cross-Cutting Patterns

**Intelligence reliability rating system**: All 233 reports use A–D (source reliability) × 1–5 (information reliability) grading per [[classification-system]] methodology (A-1 = completely reliable source, completely confirmed; D-5 = reliability cannot be judged, truth cannot be judged). Military officers rated B-2 or A-2; civilian witnesses typically C-3 to D-4. The grading system is applied consistently across all three volumes.

**Nuclear installation proximity**: Multiple high-quality sightings cluster near nuclear facilities — Muroc (early atomic test flight support base), the Albuquerque–Kirtland–Sandia corridor (Incidents 146, 175, 227). LaPaz's explicit finding that the December 12 green fireball path "passes almost centrally across the Los Alamos reservation" (Incident 227) was the direct scientific basis for Project Twinkle's 1950 establishment.

**Radar-confirmed cases**: Incidents 183 (Fukuoka airborne radar), 188, 195, 196 (Goose Bay/Newfoundland GCA radar) represent the first documented airborne and ground-radar intercept attempts; Incident 183 is the first case where an investigator explicitly hypothesizes UAP radar-detection capability.

**Maneuverability signature**: Cases 33 (Mantell — high altitude stationary, then rapid vertical departure), 144 (Chiles-Whitted — sharp pull-up into cloud), 172 (Gorman — consistently outmaneuvered F-51), 207 (Andrews — 80–600 MPH variable speed, tight circles, vertical climb) document a consistent maneuverability signature across 18 months of Project Sign reporting.

**Physical trace evidence**: Incidents 151 (controlled descent into wooded area with no debris found) and 162 (Hamel ground impression — dented earth, crushed rocks) are the only physical trace cases in the corpus.

**German/Soviet weapons nexus**: Incidents 118 (Rheinmetall-Borsig missile expert witness, Berlin) and 133 (Peenemünde-directional Norwegian fireball wave) document active USAF tracking of the hypothesis that post-war Soviet exploitation of German V-weapon technology explains the phenomenon — the main competitor to the extraterrestrial hypothesis within Project Sign.

## Headline Claims

1. **Mantell death (Incidents 33–33g)**: US Air Force P-51 pilot killed in pursuit of "metallic object of tremendous size" — first aviation fatality attributed to UAP pursuit; official Venus hypothesis is geometrically implausible for the reported conditions.
2. **Chiles-Whitted windowed craft (Incident 144)**: Commercial airline pilots independently describe two rows of windows on cigar-shaped object at 5,000 ft — directly drove the Project Sign "Estimate of the Situation" (interplanetary conclusion) that Gen. Vandenberg ordered destroyed.
3. **Fukuoka F-61 sensor-aware evasion (Incident 183)**: UAP evaded airborne radar intercept 6 consecutive times with identical break-away maneuver; USAF investigator explicitly wrote that the object may have carried radar detection equipment — 1948 primary-source documentation of apparent adversarial sensor awareness.
4. **LaPaz 9-point non-meteoric analysis (Incident 227)**: Dr. [[lincoln-lapaz|Lincoln LaPaz]]'s personally witnessed December 12, 1948 fireball near Bernal, NM; his analysis rules out meteoric origin on 9 independent grounds; explicitly notes the December 12 path "passes almost centrally across the Los Alamos reservation."
5. **Hamel landing trace (Incident 162)**: 1-ft metallic disc lands in back yard, produces ground impression (dented earth, crushed rocks); FBI notified; physical trace evidence independent of child-witness credibility — earliest confirmed landing trace in Project Sign records.
6. **Gorman 27-minute dogfight (Incident 172)**: Rated ANG pilot engaged luminous orb that consistently outmaneuvered F-51 Mustang at full throttle for 27 minutes over civilian airport with tower and ground corroboration.
7. **Project Sign named (Incident 213)**: Col. Clingerman's December 3, 1948 letter is primary-source confirmation of the project's operational designation.
