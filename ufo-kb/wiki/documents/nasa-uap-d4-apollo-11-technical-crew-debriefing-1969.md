# NASA Apollo 11 Technical Crew Debriefing (1969)

Two-volume technical crew debriefing for Apollo 11, the first crewed lunar landing mission. Prepared July 31, 1969 by NASA Mission Operations Branch / Flight Crew Support Division. Participants: [[neil-armstrong]] (CDR), [[buzz-aldrin]] (LMP), [[michael-collins]] (CMP). CONFIDENTIAL / GROUP 4; declassified ~1981. Source provenance: gov-release / PURSUE Release 01.

## Observation 1 — Unidentified Object in Translunar Coast (~1 day out from Moon)

[[buzz-aldrin]]: "The first unusual thing that we saw I guess was 1 day out or something pretty close to the moon. It had a sizeable dimension to it, so we put the monocular on it."

Crew called Mission Control; was told the S-IVB upper stage was **6,000 miles away** — ruling it out as the observed object. All three crew observed it:

- **Naked eye (Aldrin)**: "L shape"
- **Naked eye (Armstrong)**: "like an open suitcase"
- **Sextant out-of-focus (Aldrin/Armstrong)**: "appeared to be a cylinder" / "two rings" / "two connected rings"
- **Sextant focused (Collins)**: "hollow cylinder — you could look right down in its guts. It was tumbling and, when it came around end-on, you could look right down in its guts."
- **Sextant refocused (Collins)**: "replaced by this open-book shape. It was really weird."

[[michael-collins]]: "We really don't have a conclusion as to what it might have been, how big it was, or how far away it was. It was something that wasn't part of the urine dump, we're pretty sure of that." Collins speculatively proposed spacecraft debris origin (Mylar, dislodged high gain antenna part) but acknowledged the shape was inconsistent with either. No crew member identified it. A high gain antenna anomaly occurred around the same time period.

**Assessment**: Unresolved in the debriefing. This is the only verbatim, primary-source firsthand account of the Apollo 11 crew's unidentified object observation — not hearsay, not ham-radio intercept, but the official debrief.

## Observation 2 — Cabin Light Flashes During Coast

[[buzz-aldrin]] observed flashes inside the darkened cabin, approximately 1 per minute, during translunar and transearth coast:

- Spaced "a couple of minutes apart" initially; later more frequent (~1/min on average)
- Later: "double flashes at points separated by maybe a foot" — and "a line with no direction of motion"
- Aldrin's on-orbit interpretation: "some sort of penetration of some object into the spacecraft that causes an emission as it enters the cabin itself. Sometimes it was one flash on entering. Possibly departing from an entirely different part of the cabin."
- Double flashes: "entry and then impact on something such as the struts"
- "It did give you a rather funny feeling to contemplate that something was zapping through the cabin."

[[neil-armstrong]] (final night, ~50 observations in 1 hour): "It could be something like Buzz suggested. Mainly a neutron or some kind of an atomic particle that would be in the visible spectrum."

**Modern context**: These are now understood as **cosmic ray phosphenes** — high-energy charged particles (protons, heavy nuclei) passing through the astronaut's retina and triggering a light sensation. Apollo 11 was among the first systematic reports of this phenomenon; subsequent crews specifically documented it in debriefings.

## Section 21 — Visual Sightings (Vol. II)

**21.4 Translunar/Transearth**: Aldrin reported imagining Mars was appearing from the dark portion of the Moon — he and Armstrong assessed it was an optical illusion immediately adjacent to the lunar horizon.

**21.5 Lunar Orbit** (post-ascent, approaching CDH burn): Aldrin observed "a fairly bright light source" as the Earth came up above the lunar horizon, initially unidentified. Tentative identification at the time was unclear. After observing a similar appearance near Earth on transearth coast, Aldrin revised his interpretation:

> "When putting the monocular on the light source, it appeared as though it was the reflection of the Sun from a relatively smooth body of water such as a lake. I think we've revised our initial conclusion as to what the source of that light was."

Aldrin still noted: "I still think it's an unusual phenomenon, at that distance, to see so bright a source of light." A directed laser at the Moon was considered and ruled out (no one owned up to having beamed it).

## Significance

1. **Primary source for the "L-shaped object" claim**: The debriefing is the only official transcript of the crew's unidentified translunar object observation. It confirms all three crew members saw it, the S-IVB was definitively excluded (6,000 miles away), and no conclusion was reached. This contradicts the common skeptic dismissal that the crew explained it as the S-IVB.
2. **Unresolved shape-ambiguity**: The object's shape shifted with observation method (L / open suitcase / cylinder / two rings / hollow cylinder / open book) — morphological ambiguity consistent with a tumbling object of unknown geometry.
3. **Cosmic ray phosphene documentation**: The cabin flashes are the earliest known systematic astronaut debriefing of cosmic ray phosphenes. Armstrong's "neutron or atomic particle" hypothesis was essentially correct.
4. **Comparison with Apollo 17**: Both missions observed a distant unidentified rotating/uncertain object shortly after leaving Earth; Apollo 17's was later attributed to the S-IVB (which was 6,000+ miles away for Apollo 11 too, ruling it out), and Apollo 11's was never resolved.
