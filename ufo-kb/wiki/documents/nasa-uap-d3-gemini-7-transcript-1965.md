# NASA Gemini 7 — UAP Sighting Transcript, December 1965

PAO release commentary tape of communications between Gemini 7 crew and Houston Mission Control, December 5, 1965. Released as NASA-UAP-D3 (PURSUE Release 01). Source PDF prefix `255_` is a filing artifact — not related to the COMETA 255-series.

## Observation

At approximately 4 hours 24 minutes into the Gemini 7 flight, Frank Borman (CDR) called out: **"A BOGEY AT TEN O'CLOCK HIGH."**

Mission Control responded: *"Gemini 7 — is that the booster or is that a natural sighting?"*

Borman clarified: **"WE HAVE DEBRIS UP HERE — THIS IS AN ACTUAL SIGHTING."**

Three distinct objects were visible to the crew simultaneously:

1. **The "bogey"**: Unidentified object at ten o'clock high; Borman explicitly separated it from both the booster and the particle field
2. **The Titan II booster**: Described by Lovell as "a brilliant body in the sun against a black background with trillions of particles on it" — tumbling, slowly, at approximately two o'clock position ahead of the spacecraft
3. **A particle cloud**: "Very, very many — hundreds of little particles" passing the spacecraft to the left at 3–4 miles distance, heading into polar orbit

## PAO Release Context

The tape was dubbed off the master tape at Mission Control specifically because it contained "references to sighting not only some particles but as well as an unidentified object plus the booster." The PAO release identifies the "third and unidentified object" as "a brown bogey" — a description that is the official characterization but provides no mechanism for what the object was.

## Significance

1. **"BOGEY" terminology**: Borman used military aviation terminology for an unidentified aircraft — not "particle," "debris," or "artifact." His follow-up explicitly distinguished the bogey from both the booster and the particle field
2. **Mission Control's question**: Houston's immediate question — "is that the booster or is that a natural sighting?" — indicates NASA had an existing protocol for distinguishing debris from anomalous observations in 1965
3. **PAO release**: This tape was released as a UAP-relevant document; it was dubbed and played for press specifically because of the unidentified object reference — NASA itself chose to flag it
4. **Ambiguous resolution**: The "brown bogey" characterization in the PAO commentary is not a definitive identification — it is a description, not an explanation
