# NASA Skylab Technical Crew Debriefing, 1973

Compiled technical crew debrief from all three Skylab crewed missions: SL-2 (June 30, 1973), SL-3 (October 4, 1973), and SL-4 (February 22, 1974). Prepared by NASA Lyndon B. [[johnson-space-center|Johnson Space Center]] Training Office, Crew Training and Simulation Division. FOIA-exempt at publication; declassified under PURSUE Release 01.

## SL-2 Debrief — Cosmic Ray Phosphenes (June 30, 1973)

SL-2 crew (Conrad, Kerwin, Weitz) systematically documented the light-flash phenomenon first reported on Apollo 11:

- **All three crew confirmed seeing them**; frequency "waxed and waned" — "two or three per minute" at peak; possibly correlated with South Atlantic Anomaly passage
- **Morphology**: spots/sunbursts (most common) and streaks (less frequent); primarily in peripheral visual field ("very few in the central visual field")
- **Eye isolation**: Weitz isolated flashes primarily to his left eye; Conrad could isolate to one eye with concentration; Kerwin could not isolate
- **Entrance/exit streaks**: Conrad and Weitz both observed what appeared to be a particle entering one side of the eye and exiting the other — "bing-bing, it seemed like it was one side of the eyeball, and then the other side"
- **Distinguished from cabin lights**: crew learned to distinguish cosmic ray flashes from actual fire sensor winks; Kerwin: "they're not a hallucination"
- **Operational decision**: "We didn't feel it was operationally necessary for anybody to know about it right now" — suggests they were documenting this for the first time in detail

This is the second documented NASA astronaut crew report on cosmic ray phosphenes after [[nasa-uap-d4-apollo-11-technical-crew-debriefing-1969]]. The SL-2 debrief provides more systematic data: frequency rate, eye isolation attempts, and morphological distinctions.

## SL-3 Debrief — Rotating Unidentified Object (October 4, 1973)

**Primary source documentation for the Skylab red-light encounter.** Approximately one week before SL-3 splashdown (August/September 1973), crew observed an unidentified rotating object in a very similar orbit:

**[[owen-garriott|Owen Garriott]]'s account** (detailed description, on record):
- "A bright reddish object was out there" — first noticed by [[jack-lousma]] in the wardroom window
- "Much brighter than Jupiter or any of the other planets"
- Tracked for "5 to 10 minutes" until sunset
- **Rotation period**: brightness varied with a "period of almost exactly 10 seconds" — observed rotating
- **Orbital proximity**: went into darkness 5 to 7 seconds after Skylab; Garriott deduced it was "not more than 30 to 50 nautical miles from our location"
- **Relative position stability**: drifted only "10 to 20 degrees during the course of that 10-minute interval" from the wardroom window — indicating a very similar orbit
- **Reddish color**: maintained even when well above the horizon, before atmospheric coloration should apply
- **Never re-observed**: "We never saw it on any earlier or succeeding orbits" — despite the expectation that a nearby satellite in a similar orbit would appear again
- Garriott requested identification: "We'd be quite interested in having its identification established. It's all debriefed in terms of time on channel A, so the precise timing and location can be picked up from there."

**[[alan-bean|Alan Bean]]** (co-witness, SL-3 commander): "We tried monitors and everything on it but we could never make it into anything other than a bright light." Confirmed it was never seen again.

**[[jack-lousma|Jack Lousma]]** (first to notice it): "It never did take the shape of an object but it was always brighter than any other star or planet in the night sky. It was much brighter."

> ⚠ Date correction: [[1977-skylab-ufo]] was dated "~1977" based on [[kevin-knuth]]'s recollection of Alan Bean's informal account. The primary source debriefing dates this event to September/October 1973 (SL-3 mission). Bean commanded Skylab 3; the debrief was October 4, 1973. Knuth's "~1977" is a misremembering of the mission year.

## SL-4 Debrief — Lights with Definite Motion (February 22, 1974)

Gerald Carr (SL-4 commander): "On occasion we saw some lights flashing outside with very definite motion relative to ours. We presumed that they were other pieces of Skylab, or possibly other satellites. We reported our two or three sightings of that kind as soon as they occurred." Edward Gibson confirmed some appeared to be tumbling (oscillation of light flashes). Carr noted they "found it very interesting to be able to see other objects up there with us."

These were assessed by crew as likely Skylab debris or satellites — lower evidentiary value than the SL-3 object, but documented contemporaneously via dump tape.

## Significance

1. **Primary source for [[1977-skylab-ufo]]**: Garriott's verbatim on-record description is the only official documentation of this event — Bean's account to Knuth was informal/hearsay. D7 supplies the specific details (10-sec rotation, 30-50 NM distance, 10-min duration, 5-7s delay into darkness)
2. **Cosmic ray phosphene continuity**: SL-2 debrief extends the Apollo 11 phosphene documentation to a second crew, with more systematic characterization (frequency, morphology, eye isolation)
3. **Multi-crew coverage**: three distinct Skylab crews over 8 months each document anomalous visual phenomena, establishing a persistent pattern across the station's operational life
4. **Identity never provided**: Garriott explicitly requested identification via mission record; the debrief was marked FOIA-exempt — and no identification was ever publicly provided
