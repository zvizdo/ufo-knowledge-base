# USAF — Modeling of Unlikely Space-Booster Failures in Risk Calculations (1996)

RTI Report No. RTI/5180/77-43F. Final technical report prepared for the [[45th-space-wing|45th Space Wing]] (AFSPC) Safety Office, [[patrick-air-force-base|Patrick AFB, FL]], and the 30th Space Wing (AFSPC) Safety Office, [[vandenberg-afb|Vandenberg AFB, CA]]. Contract FO4703-91-C-0112. Authors: James A. Ward Jr. (Research Triangle Institute, Cocoa Beach FL) and Robert M. Montgomery (ACTA Inc., Torrance CA). Classified: Unclassified, distribution restricted to US Government agencies and contractors. Released in PURSUE Release 01.

## Purpose and KB Relevance

The report develops a framework for modeling "Mode-5" launch failures — unlikely rocket malfunctions that cause debris or a still-thrusting vehicle to deviate substantially from the intended flight path and potentially impact population centers miles uprange or far off the flight line. This framework was used in USAF's program DAMP (facility DAMage and Personnel injury) for launch-area risk assessments at [[cape-canaveral]] (Eastern Range) and [[vandenberg-afb]] (Western Range).

**UAP attribution context**: This document was released in the PURSUE Release 01 UAP disclosure batch because it contains the most comprehensive publicly available database of anomalous launch-vehicle behavior from the US military ranges 1946–1996. Its Appendix D catalogs every Atlas, Delta, Titan, and Thor launch from the Eastern and Western Ranges with classified failure-response mode classifications — data directly relevant to distinguishing genuine UAP observations from anomalous missile/rocket launches visible to ground observers, pilots, and radar operators. The companion document is [[dow-uap-d49-vandenberg-launch-summary-1958-2000|dow-uap-d49]], which provides a focused Vandenberg-specific launch history through 2000.

## Six Failure-Response Modes (Program DAMP)

- **Mode 1**: Vehicle topples at or shortly after liftoff; propellants deflagrate at launch point
- **Mode 2**: Loss of control at/shortly after liftoff; destruct transmitted within 6–12 seconds; debris limited to launch area
- **Mode 3**: Vehicle fails to pitch-program; near-vertical flight while thrusting; terminated by destruct at "straight-up time" (30–70 seconds at [[cape-canaveral]])
- **Mode 4**: Vehicle flies within normal limits until malfunction terminates thrust or causes breakup; debris impacts near flight line — the most common failure mode (~80%+ of all failures)
- **Mode 5**: Vehicle may impact in any direction within energy capabilities; decreasing probability as angular deviation from flight line increases; models unlikely sustained off-nominal thrust — the primary source of objects observed far from the flight line
- **Mode NA**: Anomalous behavior affects mission success without increasing ground risk (e.g., unplanned orbit)
- **Mode 6** (normal): Separated stage impacts modeled as bivariate-normal around mean impact point

## Failure Probability Data (1996 Estimates)

Overall failure probabilities for representative configurations:

| Vehicle | Flight Phase 0–1 (liftoff to 1st-stage cutoff) | Flight Phase 0–2 (through 2nd stage) |
|---------|------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Atlas   | 0.022 (2.2%)                                   | 0.031 (3.1%)                         |
| Delta   | 0.010 (1.0%)                                   | 0.013 (1.3%)                         |
| Titan   | 0.040 (4.0%)                                   | 0.064 (6.4%)                         |

For mature systems, Mode 4 accounts for ~86% of all failure responses (flight phases 0–2); Mode 5 accounts for ~8%. Mode 5 absolute probability for Atlas in flight phases 0–1: **0.00223** (1 in 449 launches); for Titan: **0.00405** (1 in 247 launches).

## Appendix D — Launch and Performance Histories (1946–August 1996)

The primary UAP-attribution dataset. Contains classified failure-response-mode assignments for every launch of:

- **Atlas**: 532 total flights cataloged (Eastern and Western Ranges); 1957–1996; configurations include Atlas ICBM, Atlas D/E/F, Atlas LV-3, Atlas II, IIA, IIAS; complete narrative failure descriptions for each anomalous flight
- **Delta**: 232 total flights (Eastern Range); 1960–1996; configurations include Thor-Delta, Delta D, Delta II, Delta GOES; failure narratives from Pad 17 [[cape-canaveral]]
- **Titan**: 337 total flights; configurations from Titan I through Titan IV; 1959–1996; Vandenberg and Cape Canaveral
- **Thor** (excluding Delta): 85 Eastern Range flights

For each launch: date, vehicle configuration, success/failure classification, flight phase of any anomalous behavior, failure-response mode (1–5, NA, or blank). Represents the most complete declassified record of US military and government satellite launch anomalies available — critical for ruling out launch-vehicle sources for UAP reports from near [[vandenberg-afb]] and [[cape-canaveral]].

## Mode-5 Shaping Constants (Optimum Values)

The Mode-5 density function uses two shaping constants (A and B):

| Vehicle | Breakup qα    | B         | A    |
|---------|--------------|-----------|------|
| Atlas   | 5,000 deg-lb/ft² | 5,000,000 | 6.30 |
| Delta   | 5,000        | 4         | 3.50 |
| Titan   | 5,000        | 1,000     | 3.50 |
| LLV1    | 5,000        | 1,000     | 2.75 |

A = 3.45, B = 1,000 (traditional 45 SW/SE values for Atlas) — launch-area risks changed by only ~10% vs. optimized constants, indicating the methodology is robust to moderate parameter variation.

## Cross-Cutting Significance

- **Total sample**: 1,186 flight tests across Atlas, Delta, Titan, and Eastern-Range Thor; the most statistically comprehensive US launch failure database as of 1996
- **Mode 5 rarity**: Only ~8% of failures produce off-nominal trajectories (Mode 5); the overwhelming majority (Mode 4) impact near the flight line — meaning objects observed far off the flight line or moving uprange are genuinely anomalous relative to known launch failure patterns
- **Vandenberg dual role**: Both a launch site and UAP reporting hotspot; this report's Vandenberg-specific data enables retrospective assessment of whether historical UAP reports from the California coast match known launch failure trajectories
- **Classification → disclosure**: Distribution restricted "to protect administrative/operational use data" when published (1996); release in the PURSUE UAP batch implies declassification authority concluded the failure probability data no longer requires restriction from public UAP research
