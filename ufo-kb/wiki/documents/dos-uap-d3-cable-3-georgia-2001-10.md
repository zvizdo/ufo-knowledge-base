# DOS UAP Cable 3 — Georgia: 'UFOs Over Georgia' Airspace Incident, October 2001

US Embassy Moscow cable (MRN: 01 MOSCOW 13169; DTG 300000Z OCT 01). Classification: CONFIDENTIAL. Declassified and released in full, 2/25/2026 (John Powers, Acting Director, DoS). Routed to SECSTATE, Embassy Tbilisi, USUN, Embassy Baku, Embassy Yerevan, USOSCE, SECDEF, JOINT STAFF.

## Subject: Russia Uses "UFO" as Diplomatic Cover for Georgian Airspace Violations

Despite the title, this cable documents no UAP observations. Its UAP significance lies entirely in the **diplomatic use of "UFO" as a deniability mechanism**.

**Background**: On October 28–29, 2001, Georgian authorities accused Russian planes of violating Georgian airspace and bombing positions in the **Kodori Gorge**, Abkhazia. Russia denied involvement.

**The "UFO" language (Para 3)**: Russian MFA Georgia Desk Chief Tereoken, denying any Russian planes were in the area, told the Embassy: reports of planes over the Kodori Gorge "might as well have been about **'UFOs.'**" He added that Moscow "does not have the technical capability to determine whether there were foreign planes in the region."

**Embassy Comment (Para 8)**: Ambassador Vershbow wrote: "It is hard to accept official Russian denials that Russian planes were not involved. **To posit that they could be UFOs would be humorous if it were not for the seriousness of the violations.** Most likely the Russians want to keep the pressure on the Georgians and the Chechens in the Gorge." The Embassy assessed Russian denials as "a traditional Russian penchant to avoid an awkward admission with a bold lie."

## Context (Off-UAP Content)

The cable primarily covers:
- Russian military withdrawal from Gudauta base (one trainload departed, two more loaded and awaiting Abkhaz permission)
- ~600 Russian troops remaining; Moscow insists on keeping ~340 as "guards"
- Georgian Parliamentary Speaker Zvania's Moscow visit: positive tone, "no practical outcome"
- Upcoming CIS summit (Nov 30–Dec 1, Moscow); possible Shevardnadze–Putin margins meeting

## Significance

1. **"UFO" as diplomatic deniability tool**: The first documented instance in the DoS UAP cable series of "UFO" being invoked not as an observation claim but as an ambiguity vehicle — Russia used it to claim that unidentified airspace intruders are inherently unknowable, pre-empting accountability.
2. **Embassy's counterframing**: Vershbow's "humorous if not serious" comment documents US diplomatic awareness of the pattern: "UFO" can be deployed to deflect legitimate airspace accountability.
3. **PURSUE Release inclusion**: This cable was released as part of PURSUE Release 01 (the same UAP-focused release batch as genuine UAP sighting cables), suggesting DoS archivists flagged "UFO" keyword presence regardless of context.
4. **Post-9/11 Russian-Georgian tensions context**: The October 2001 timing (6 weeks after 9/11) coincided with Russia using the "global war on terror" framing to characterize Chechen militants in Georgia — the UFO deflection was part of a broader information strategy.
