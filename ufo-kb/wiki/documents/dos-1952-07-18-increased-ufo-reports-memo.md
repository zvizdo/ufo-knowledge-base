# NASC Memo — Maxwell Hunter: "Thoughts on the Space Alien Race Question" (July 1963)

**Date correction**: Source file slug reads "1952" but the document is unambiguously from **July 18, 1963** — references Mariner II (1962), Lunik III (1959), and Project Ozma (1960). The source PDF `59_214434_sp_16_7.18.1963.pdf` filename is accurate; the "1952" designation in the slug is a processing artifact. Memo to Robert F. Packard, Office of International Scientific Affairs, Department of State, from Maxwell W. Hunter II, Member, Professional Staff, National Aeronautics and Space Council. Subject: "Thoughts on the Space Alien Race Question." Classification: OFFICIAL USE ONLY.

## Content

Hunter was prompted by the occasional question during **BNSP (Basic National Security Policy) Task I** deliberations: what policy to follow if an alien intelligence is discovered. He offers a frank three-scenario framework:

**Scenario 1 — Chemical Martians**: A solar system civilization (e.g. Martian) with only chemical rocket capability. Hunter analyzes Mars-to-Moon travel as energetically feasible via chemical rockets. Discusses the 19th-century discovery of Phobos and Deimos as potentially artificial space stations. Discusses three "hints" of lunar intelligence (gas outgassing from Alphonsus crater, infrared hot spots, failure of lunar/planetary probes of two spacefaring nations) — noting these would be "broad hints" if scientists were less confident. If discovered, "current national policy would be made to order for the situation."

**Scenario 2 — Nuclear civilization**: A race with nuclear-propulsion space capability equivalent to Earth's best understanding. Hunter estimates half-to-three-quarters-speed-of-light ships are imaginable. A 200,000-year expansion from the galactic center could populate the entire galaxy. "Not nearly as tame as the previously hypothesized chemical Martians, and our policy would need to be revised accordingly."

**Scenario 3 — FTL civilization**: "Scientifically abhorrent" — if Einstein is only an approximation. If encountered, "our policy had better be to negotiate fast, because the implications of their far better understanding and control of the fundamental forces of nature would be obvious."

**Conclusions**: Hunter acknowledges the probability of finding an alien intelligence is finite and "perhaps should not be completely ignored." Key policy action: identify which scenario as fast as possible. "A policy of the immediate burying of all Terrestrial hatchets would likely be in order." On readiness: "no one of consequence is going to take this rubbish seriously unless it happens. At that point, our policy will be determined in the traditional manner of grand panic."

## Significance

1. **1963 US government alien contact policy analysis**: A NASC professional staff member formally advising the DoS on the policy implications of alien contact — during the same period BNSP Task I was being deliberated
2. **Three-tier framework**: Hunter's chemical/nuclear/FTL classification of potential alien civilizations maps closely to modern UAP capability-assessment frameworks (e.g. AARO's "propulsion agnostic" analysis)
3. **"Grand panic" conclusion**: Despite serious analysis, Hunter's conclusion is that no real preparation is possible — echoing the Robertson Panel's dismissal but from a policy-planning angle rather than a debunking angle
4. **BNSP nexus**: The deliberations on BNSP Task I apparently included discussions of alien contact as a policy scenario — indicating this wasn't purely speculative but was formally tabled in US national security deliberations in 1963
