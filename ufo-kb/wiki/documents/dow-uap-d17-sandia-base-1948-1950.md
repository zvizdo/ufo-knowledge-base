# DOW-UAP-D017 — Sandia Base AFSWP File 333.5: Unidentified Flying Objects (1948–1950)

The most substantial document in PURSUE Release 02 by far: a **116-page consolidated correspondence file** from the [[afswp|Armed Forces Special Weapons Project (AFSWP)]] Classified Documents Library at [[sandia-base]], Albuquerque, New Mexico, under File No. 333.5 — "Unidentified Flying Objects." The file covers correspondence from approximately April 1949 through 1950 (with material referencing observations from December 1948 onward), including scientific reports by [[lincoln-lapaz|Lincoln LaPaz]] and [[w-d-crozier|W. D. Crozier]] on attempts to recover physical material from the [[1949-new-mexico-green-fireballs|1949 New Mexico green fireballs]], the founding-era documentation of the [[17th-osi-district]] Office of Special Investigations UFO collection program (December 1948 standing-up of the New Mexico aerial-phenomena reporting plan), and accompanying military-administrative correspondence on Sandia Base security and Strategic Air Command special-weapons coordination that was filed alongside in the same classification series.

The file was declassified under authority NND 58378 and NW 91526; released by DOE/DOW as part of PURSUE Release 02 on 2026-05-22. The file is foundationally important because it is **the institutional paper trail of the original USAF/AFSWP UAP collection program at Sandia Base, 1948–1950**, predating and feeding into [[project-twinkle]] (the formal Air Force investigation of the green fireballs). Together with [[doe-uap-d2-james-tuck-correspondence-1970-1976]] (Los Alamos site-internal recall) and [[fbi-62-hq-83894-ufo-investigations-1947-1968]] (parallel FBI investigations), it forms the **primary documentary corpus for the foundational US-government UAP collection apparatus** during the 1947–1951 wave.

4. **Pages 8–11 — 17 August 1949 — Lincoln LaPaz → Colonel Doyle Rees** — Subject: "Anomalous Luminous Phenomena. Sixth Report (attempts to collect volatilization products from green fireballs)." Key claims: (a) the 30 January 1949 green fireball left no recoverable fragments despite searches; (b) Crozier and Seely's dust-collection work was undertaken on LaPaz's recommendation; (c) the unusually large 100-micron copper particles in the 25 July Socorro Campus collection were initially suspected to be from the New Mexico School of Mines Assaying Building roof, but identical particles found in open-country collections (San Antonio, Carthage, Bingham NM) refuted that explanation; (d) copper indications in the 14 July 1949 collection (10 days BEFORE the 24 July green fireball) raise the possibility of "an unobserved or unreported green fireball occurring in the Socorro neighborhood shortly before July 14th"; (e) **LaPaz's headline conclusion**: "if future more detailed work shows that the numerous copper particles found by Dr. Crozier and Mr. Seely are indeed floating down from green fireballs, then the fireballs are not conventional meteorites. Copper is one of the rarest of the elements found in meteorites… nothing could exceed in importance attainment of a definite conclusion in regard to the origin of the copper particles." Recommendations: (1) airplane dust-collection flights at >40,000 ft beneath green fireball paths (Crozier opined a B-36 or B-50 should be made available); (2) ground-level dust collections along the well-determined earth-trace of the 30 January 1949 fireball.

11. **Pages 63–67 — Inclosure 3: 23 May 1950 — Lincoln LaPaz "Seventh Report" letter to Lt Col Doyle Rees, Commanding Officer 17th District OSI** — Subject: "Anomalous Luminous Phenomena (Seventh Report)." This is the **most substantively important scientific document in the entire 116-page file**. Content:
    - **¶1 — recapitulates the 10 differences between December 1948 green fireballs and typical meteors** (first listed in LaPaz's Second Report of 20 December 1948):
      (1) **Horizontal nature of the paths** — "Genuine meteors are rarely observed to move in horizontal paths."
      (2) **Very low altitude** — "in sharp contrast from the genuine meteors for which heights of the order of 40 or more miles are normally observed."
      (3) **Velocity much less than meteors** (yet greater than V-2 rockets, jet planes, or conventional flares).
      (4) **No noise** despite penetration to very low levels (with 1950-05-23 note: "Possible exceptions to the noiselessness of green fireballs are the incidents of 1949, January 30, and 1949, December 4.").
      (5) **Instant full brightness** (vs. genuine meteors which begin as thin hair lines and brighten near end of path).
      (6) **Strongly biased compass directions** — green fireballs come "from the north half of the sky" disproportionately (vs. uniform-distribution meteors).
      (7) **Curious association with well-known meteor showers** despite no meteor shower producing such bright green fireballs naturally — "**This relationship might indicate an attempt to render the green fireballs less conspicuous by causing them to appear only when there is considerable meteoric activity.**" *(Note the verb "attempt" — implies intelligent agency.)*
      (8) **The remarkably vivid green color is rarely observed in genuine meteors. By laboratory test this peculiar color seems to be identical with that given off by copper salts in the blowpipe flame. If this identification is correct, the wave length of the radiation from the green fireballs is near λ = 5218 Å.** *(LaPaz's spectroscopic copper-salts identification — foundational.)*
      (9) **Duration 2-3 seconds** — longer than visual meteors (0.4-0.5s) but shorter than meteorite falls (5-30s).
      (10) **No train of sparks or dust cloud** — sharply contrasts with meteoric fireballs that penetrate to similar low altitudes.
    - **¶2 — 72 green fireballs accepted in the category at the time of writing**, less than 5% of total unscreened reports.
    - **¶3 — 11th distinguishing item added**: "**the maximum frequency of sighting of green fireballs (occurring at approximately 2030) coincides in time with neither the frequency maximum for ordinary meteors (occurring at approximately 0300) nor the frequency maximum for meteorite falls (occurring at approximately 1600).**"
    - **¶4 — most green fireballs sighted 5pm-11pm MST** with a secondary maximum around 2am MST.
    - **¶5 — Soviet-missile-from-Urals hypothesis explicitly proposed**: "**Some significance may attach to the fact that the time interval... extends from about 7 a.m. to 1 p.m. in the Ural region of the USSR. Since missiles moving with velocities of the order of those found for the green fireballs for which real path determinations have been possible would travel from the southern Urals to New Mexico in less than 15 minutes, a possible interpretation of the concentration of sightings... is that the green fireballs result from guided missiles launched from bases in the Urals in the morning hours before cloudiness due to convection or blinding afternoon dust storms can interfere with non-radar tracking, such as has been used by the Optical Trajectory Section at White Sands Proving Ground.**"
    - **¶6 — concentration on Friday-Monday days** of the week, particularly Saturday/Sunday.
    - **¶7 — temporal substitution observation**: "There has been a distinct decline in the number of green fireball sightings during the last two months, within which the number of so-called 'flying saucer' incidents in this region has attained an all-time high."
    - **¶8 — Joseph Kaplan + von Karman both disclaim US-experiment explanation**: "Although I have recently received from Dr. Joseph Kaplan of the Scientific Advisory Board a letter containing the statement 'Frankly, I don't know of any U. S. experiments that would result in the appearance of these unconventional objects, **and neither does Von Karman**'..." *(Theodore von Karman was the chief scientific advisor of the USAF Scientific Advisory Board.)*
    - **LaPaz quotes his 20 February 1950 letter to Dr. P. H. Wyckoff** (Chief, Atmospheric Physics Laboratory, Base Directorate for Geophysical Research):
      > "The fireballs reported on fall into one of two categories: Those of the first category (the majority) are meteoritic falls of unusual, but certainly not of impossible, magnitude, frequency and other characteristics; **those of the second category (the minority) are U. S. guided missiles undergoing tests in the neighborhoods of the sensitive installations they are designed to defend.** **This interpretation of the latter category is the one that I proposed in answer to a question raised by Dr. Teller at the first Los Alamos conference on February 17, 1949.**"

S. origin, then certainly intensive, systematic investigation of these objects should not be delayed...
