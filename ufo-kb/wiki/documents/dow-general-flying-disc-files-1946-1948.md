# General Flying Disc Files 1946–1948

Two-volume declassified file collection (NND 760191) covering US Army Air Forces and early USAF internal correspondence on the "flying disc" phenomenon from 1946 through mid-1948. Vol 2 covers 1946–1947 material from AMC and Air Intelligence headquarters; Vol 1 covers January–June 1948. The files document the institutional response to the 1947 disc wave, the founding of [[project-sign]], and the investigation of the [[horten-brothers]] hypothesis.

## Vol 2 — 1946–1947 Documents

### Twining Memo (23 Sep 1947)

[[twining-memo]] (23 Sep 1947) — [[nathan-twining]], Commanding General AMC, responds to [[george-schulgen]]'s collection request. Conclusions: the phenomenon is "something real and not visionary or fictitious." Object characteristics described: flat circular or disc-shaped, approximately 50 ft in diameter, metallic or light-reflecting surface, no trail except occasionally with steep banks, absence of sound at close range, level flight above 300 knots (observed), extreme rates of climb, evasive action when approached. Some had windows. Recommends data-sharing with AEC, JRDB, RAND, NEPA, Air Force SAB, and NACA. Projects RAND and AMC to cooperate.

- Twining explicitly states no crashes or material have been recovered — "lack of physical evidence in the shape of crash recovered exhibits which would undeniably prove the existence of these objects."
- Report forwarded through Schulgen to Army Air Forces Washington and eventually to the FBI.

### LeMay Confirms No US Program (29 Aug 1947)

[[curtis-lemay]], Deputy Chief of Air Staff for Research and Development, signed the 29 August 1947 routing form confirming "The Army Air Forces has no research project with the characteristics described" — i.e., no US secret program matches the disc reports. Signed concurrently by Lt. Col. Munson, Maj. Wickerson, and Col. Westenbaker.

### Loedding LD-2 Flying Disc Drawing (24 Sep 1947)

Col. [[harold-mccoy]] (AMC T-2 Intelligence, Deputy CG) transmitted to USAF Washington a drawing of the Loedding LD-2 flying disc — a patent by AMC aeronautical engineer [[alfred-loedding]] — along with the Royal Aircraft Establishment Technical Note AERO 1703 on Horten tailless aircraft. Key intelligence note:

- A US Military Attaché report from Moscow (9 Jun 1947) indicated 1800 aircraft based on the Horten VIII design — "six engine pusher, 131 ft wingspan, 33,000 lb gross weight" — were being built for Soviet bomber use, jet-propelled. Reference report V-32291-S.
- The T-2 report "German Flying Wings Designed by [[horten-brothers|Horten Brothers]]" (F-SU-1110-ND) was separately transmitted.

### Garrett Collection Request (22 Aug 1947)

Lt. Col. [[george-garrett]] (Collection Branch, Air Intelligence Requirements Division) sent a routing form to Army R&D asking for confirmation that no US program matches disc characteristics. The response arrived in the 29 Aug 1947 LeMay memo above. Garrett also forwarded the complete sighting file to AMC for photostating in Sep 1947.

### Mrs. Merchant Theory

A report investigated and dismissed: the theory that Russian discs were fired from Mexico aimed at US atomic installations. No corroborating evidence found.

## Vol 1 — 1948 Documents

### Project SIGN Founding Letter (30 Dec 1947)

Gen. [[laurence-craigie]] signed the founding directive for [[project-sign]]: "priority 2A, security classification restricted, Code Name SIGN." The letter instructs AMC to collect, collate, and evaluate atmospheric phenomena reports. This is the primary founding document for the first formal USAF UFO investigation.

### Cabell Policy Directive (27 Feb 1948)

[[cp-cabell]] (Chief, Air Intelligence Requirements Division) issued the directive formalizing AMC Wright-Patterson as the sole USAF collection agency for flying disc reports. Key elements:
- All Air Force installations within CONUS and Alaska to report directly to AMC (ATTN: MCI)
- AMC authorized direct contact with all installations
- Standardized reporting format required: location/time, weather, witness names/occupations, photographs, number/shape/size/color/speed/heading/maneuverability/altitude/sound/exhaust trail

### Fighter Aircraft Alert Proposal (Feb–Mar 1948)

McCoy proposed maintaining fighter aircraft at continuous alert status across CONUS to intercept disc reports. This was rejected by both the Director of Plans and Operations (S.E. Anderson) and by Cabell's branch as "unfeasible" — too costly in aircraft/personnel, impossible without complete radar coverage, and impractical given that reports came primarily from civilian sources.

### Horten Brothers Interrogation (24 Feb 1948)

Cabell memo states: "This Directorate has been advised that contact has been made with the Horten Brothers." Collection Memorandum No. 7 (21 Jan 1948) was forwarded as the basis for EUCOM G-2 interrogation. Results to be forwarded when received. The Horten interrogation was thus ongoing through early 1948 — a direct follow-up to the Loedding/Horten hypothesis raised in Sep 1947.

### Sighting Reports Forwarded to AMC (Jan–Jun 1948)

Multiple field sightings routed through Garrett's office to AMC Wright-Patterson:
- **Hobson, Ohio (8 May 1948)**: Round phosphorescent object, apparent 9-inch diameter from ground level, altitude 6–8 miles, moving east (90° heading), phosphorescent trail. Reported by New York Central System railway workers (Ben Rupe, Earl Roush, Bob White, C.K. Hite) to FBI Cleveland, forwarded via Eleventh Air Force.
- **Bakersfield, CA (5–8 Mar 1948)**: Two objects fell to earth southwest of Bakersfield with smoke and debris trailing; one appeared on fire with red/black smoke; search parties, aircraft, and rescue units found nothing.

## Chain of Command Structure (1947–1948)

The document makes visible the flying-disc reporting chain:
- Field units → Base A-2 (intelligence) → Air Defense Command / numbered Air Forces
- FBI field offices → FBI Washington → forwarded to AAF Washington
- AAF Washington (AFOIR, [[george-garrett|Lt. Col. Garrett]]) → AMC Wright-Patterson (MCI/TSDIN)
- McCoy (T-2) ← primary collector and analyst at AMC
